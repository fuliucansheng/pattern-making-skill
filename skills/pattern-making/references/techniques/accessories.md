# PART 1. GLOVES

## 1.1 What a glove is, and how to recognize the construction on a photo

A glove is five tubes (four fingers and a thumb) joined to a palm/back body, cut from a material that
stretches across the hand. A fitted leather glove is NOT a drawn outline of a hand: it is a trank with
straight parallel fingers, plus small pieces (fourchettes, thumb, quirks) that give the fingers their depth.

Recognize on a photo:

| What you see | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Seams running along both sides of every finger, from the web to the tip | Fourchettes (strips between fingers) |
| One seam on the little-finger side, from tip to cuff | Single closing seam of a one-piece trank |
| A seam on the index finger on the thumb side, ending at the thumb web | The two index halves of the trank sewn together (no fourchette on that side) |
| Thumb seam running from the tip to a V-shaped corner at the thumb base | Bolton thumb (thumb and quirk in one piece) |
| Small diamonds or triangles at the base of each finger and at the thumb base | Quirks |
| Three rows of stitching or ribs on the back of the hand | Points (also called silking or embroidery) |
| Seams visible on the outside, stitches bridging two butted edges | Outseam |
| Smooth outside, no stitches visible | Inseam, glove turned right side out |
| Overlapped edge with one row of stitching | Piqué |
| Cuff with a slit on the palm side and 1 to 4 buttons or a snap | Opening (mousquetaire or button glove) |
| Cuff turned in with a second row of stitching | Self hem or facing |

## 1.2 Anatomy and vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trank (trunk) | Main body: palm, back and the fronts and backs of the four fingers, cut in one piece with a single side seam [S: Dreamstress, Koch] |
| Back / palm | The two halves of the trank, side by side. The back is slightly wider than the palm |
| Slit (slash) | Cut between two fingers. It stops at the crotch point (a dot on the pattern) |
| Crotch point | End of a slit, at the web between two fingers |
| Fourchette | Forked strip forming the sides of two adjacent fingers. Also "fork" or "forge" [S] |
| Fourchette arm | One half of the fork, belonging to one finger. One pattern per arm |
| Single / double fourchette | One strip per finger side, or an arm pair joined at the base. One-piece or two-piece fourchettes are both accepted by the Canadian specification [S: RCMP] |
| Quirk (querk) | Small diamond or triangle at the base of a fourchette or thumb. Gives movement and room, mainly in thick or low-stretch leather [S] |
| Gouch (gusset hole) | Thumb hole in the trank [S: Koch] |
| Tongue | V-shaped point at the top of the thumb hole. The corner of the thumb piece sits in the V [S: Koch] |
| Bolton thumb (English thumb) | Thumb piece and its quirk cut in one piece [S: Dreamstress, Cornelia James]. Yeovil's glossary words it as the quirk cut with the back and palm. Sources differ; this sheet uses the "thumb with its quirk" definition |
| Quirk thumb | Plain thumb piece plus a separate quirk sewn at its base |
| French thumb / set-in thumb | Round or oval thumb cut in one piece with no quirk, set into a round hole. Used in expensive gloves [S: Yeovil]. Trade names vary by factory |
| Points | Decorative lines on the back of the hand. Historically extensions of the fingers that improved shaping [S] |
| Silking | Stitching of the points, usually three rows on a two-needle machine [S: RCMP] |
| Outseam (overseam) | Seam where the stitches pass over two butted edges. Seam shows outside |
| Inseam | Glove sewn inside out, then turned |
| Piqué | One edge laps over the other and is sewn with one row [S: Yeovil] |
| Half-piqué | Piqué on the back of the fingers, inseam on the palm side [S: Yeovil] |
| Prixseam (PXM) | Outseam variant made on a special machine with horizontal stitches [S: Yeovil] |
| Binding | Strip of leather or tape folded over a raw edge (cuff, opening) |
| Facing | Separate band turned to the inside at the cuff edge |
| Self hem | Cuff edge folded over, 6 mm in the Canadian specification [S: RCMP] |
| Wrist length / gauntlet / opera | Glove ends at the wrist; cuff flares over the sleeve; glove reaches above the elbow (about 16 buttons) |
| Button length | Traditional unit: about 1 inch (25.4 mm) of arm length per button, counted from the base of the thumb [S: Cornelia James]. Makers differ, so confirm in millimetres |
| Table cut | Cut by hand or die on a table from leather that has been stretched out in length, so fingers do not lengthen [S: RCMP] |
| Pairing | Matching two gloves, tacked together at the side seam |

A fourchette fork joins two arms at the base. Arm lengths differ because neighbouring fingers have different
lengths, so each arm has its own pattern.

## 1.4 Materials and stretch direction

- Dress gloves: lambskin, kidskin, cabretta, nappa. Thickness 0.45 to 0.6 mm for women, 0.8 to 1.0 mm for men [S: RCMP].
- Suede, pigskin, deerskin for work and driving. Fabric: stretch knit, fleece, stretch velvet, doe-suede knit [S: Koch, Shapes of Fabric].
- Leather stretches mostly across the width of the hand (about 15 to 25 percent for fine skins) and little along
  the fingers. Put the fingers along the low-stretch direction of the skin and the trank width along the stretch.
- Pull the length stretch out of the skin before cutting, otherwise the fingers lengthen after wear [S: RCMP].
- Cut the thumb with its circumference along the stretch direction. Cut each fourchette with its length along
  the low-stretch direction.
- Knit fabric: 2-way or 4-way stretch of at least 3/8 to 1/2 inch over a 2 inch gauge (about 20 to 25 percent) [S: Koch].
  Cut the knit with the stretch across the hand.
- Raw-edge-friendly fabrics (wool knit, leather, suede) allow 3 mm allowances without finishing.
- Mirror pieces: a glove pair needs left and right tranks and thumbs. The slash of the thumb and the slash of the
  gouch point in opposite directions [S: Koch]. Do not flip a fourchette arm unless its edges are symmetrical.

## 1.5 Sizing

### Size and French inch

- A glove size is the hand girth in inches, measured around the palm at the knuckles without the thumb.
  Size 7 is a 7 inch (177.8 mm) girth. Sizes go in half steps (12.7 mm of girth per half size).
- The French inch (pouce) is 27.07 mm. A French line is 1/12 inch (2.256 mm). The Canadian specification
  states trank widths in inches and lines on a French ruler [S: RCMP].
- Women's sizes run 6 to 9.5 or 10, men's 7.5 to 12 or more.

| Size | Girth (English inch) mm | Trank width stretched fully, women, RCMP [S] mm | Trank width stretched fully, men, RCMP [S] mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 152 | 182.7 (6 in 9 lines) | |
| 6.5 | 165 | 196.2 (7 in 3 lines) | |
| 7 | 178 | 209.8 (7 in 9 lines) | |
| 7.5 | 191 | 223.3 (8 in 3 lines) | 243.6 (9 in 0 lines) |
| 8 | 203 | 236.9 (8 in 9 lines) | 257.2 (9 in 6 lines) |
| 8.5 | 216 | 250.4 (9 in 3 lines) | 270.7 (10 in 0 lines) |
| 9 | 229 | 263.9 (9 in 9 lines) | 284.2 (10 in 6 lines) |
| 9.5 | 241 | 277.5 (10 in 3 lines) | 297.8 (11 in 0 lines) |
| 10 | 254 | 291.0 (10 in 9 lines) | 311.3 (11 in 6 lines) |

- The same specification states the glove is "table cut" at the size plus 18 lines (40.6 mm). A size 8 is cut
  9 in 6 lines (257 mm) wide. These are work-glove die widths with generous stretch. They are not a fitted
  dress-glove trank width: use section 1.7 for fitted gloves.
- Stitch density 3.5 to 4 stitches per cm, outseam stitching 0.8 to 1.5 mm from the edge, backtack 13 mm at
  starts and ends, self hem 6 mm [S: RCMP].
- If the wearer falls between sizes, take the larger size; fit is driven by girth first, then finger length.

## 1.6 Hand measurements to take

Measure on the bare hand, fingers together, thumb relaxed. Draw around the hand as a check, but never use the
outline as the pattern.

| Code | Measurement | How | Example size 7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| G | Hand girth | Around the palm at the knuckles, thumb excluded, tape snug [S] | 178 |
| Wr | Wrist girth | At the wrist crease | 150 |
| HL | Hand length | Wrist crease to tip of the middle finger, palm side [S] | 175 |
| PL | Palm length | Wrist crease to the crease at the base of the middle finger, palm side | 98 |
| h_I, h_M, h_R, h_L | Crease heights | Wrist crease to the base crease of each finger, palm side | 94, 98, 94, 85 |
| F_I, F_M, F_R, F_L | Finger lengths | Base crease to tip, palm side [S] | 66, 77, 69, 55 |
| Cf_I, Cf_M, Cf_R, Cf_L | Finger girths | Around the middle of the proximal phalanx | 56, 58, 54, 48 |
| T_o | Thumb outer length | Web (thumb to index, back of hand) to thumb tip, along the outer edge [S] | 60 |
| T_p | Thumb palm-side length | Thumb base crease to tip, palm side [S] | 45 |
| T_g | Thumb girth | Around the thumb at the fit point; at the fit you want, not too snug [S] | 62 |
| Web_T | Thumb web height | Wrist crease to the thumb-index web, palm side | 80 |
| A_f, A_w | Forearm girths | At the planned glove end, and at one point halfway between wrist and end | per glove length |
| E | Wrist to end | Wrist crease to the planned glove end | per glove length |

Tip height of each finger above the wrist crease: Ti = h_i + F_i. For the middle finger this equals HL.

## 1.7 Trank draft, step by step

Coordinates: x runs left to right, y runs up from the wrist line (y = 0) to the fingertips. The trank is drawn
as two halves side by side, fold at the thumb side:

```
 back half (left)                       palm half (right)
 L    R    M    I    |    I    M    R    L        <- finger order, left to right
 little ring middle index | index middle ring little
                        x = Wb   (centre)
```

The centre line is a fold below the index crotch (point C0) and is a slit above it. The two index halves are
two separate half-fingers placed side by side. They are sewn together on their outer (thumb) side. They must
never be drawn as one wide paddle across the fold. The only free long edges that receive the closing seam are
the two outer sides (little finger sides).

### Step 1. Width of the trank

| Material | W (cut width at finger bases, no allowance) |
|---|---|
| Fine leather, cross-stretch 15 to 25 percent, dress glove | G + 0 to +4 |
| Medium leather or suede | G + 10 to +20 |
| Knit, slight stretch, medium weight | G + 10 [S: Shapes of Fabric] |
| Woven or no-stretch material | G + 25 to +30 |

Back and palm split [S: Shapes of Fabric]: back Wb = W/2 + 4, palm Wp = W/2 - 4. The back is slightly wider because it
covers the knuckles and the palm side rests flat.

Example (dress leather): W = 180, Wb = 94, Wp = 86.

### Step 2. Fourchette width and finger widths

Fourchettes add width to each finger, so the trank finger widths are not the full finger girths.

1. fw_mid = (Cf_I + Cf_M + Cf_R + Cf_L - W) / 6. This is the average width of one fourchette arm at mid-finger.
   Example: (56 + 58 + 54 + 48 - 180) / 6 = 6.0 mm.
2. Trank width of each finger (back + palm) = Cf_i - n_i x fw_mid, where n_i is the number of fourchette arms
   on that finger: index 1, middle 2, ring 2, little 1.
   Example: I 50, M 46, R 42, L 42. Sum = 180 = W, which closes the system.
3. Split each finger between back and palm in the ratio Wb : Wp.
   Back: I 26.1, M 24.0, R 21.9, L 22.0 (sum 94.0).
   Palm: I 23.9, M 22.0, R 20.1, L 20.0 (sum 86.0).

Alternative when finger girths are not measured [S: Shapes of Fabric, knit glove, W = 200]: back
I 28, M 26, R 24, L 26 (sum 104); palm I 26, M 24, R 22, L 24 (sum 96). Scale to the actual Wb and Wp.
The source gives the little finger the middle finger's width because the middle finger has a fourchette on both
sides while the little finger has one. Check each width against the measured finger girth before using it.

### Step 3. Finger x positions (example)

| Finger | x start | x end |
|---|---|---|
| Back L | 0.0 | 22.0 |
| Back R | 22.0 | 43.9 |
| Back M | 43.9 | 67.9 |
| Back I | 67.9 | 94.0 |
| Palm I | 94.0 | 117.9 |
| Palm M | 117.9 | 139.9 |
| Palm R | 139.9 | 160.0 |
| Palm L | 160.0 | 180.0 |

Fingers are straight, parallel and vertical. Slits are zero-width lines at the x values above. Do not fan the
fingers out and do not leave gaps between them: the fourchettes supply the space between fingers.

### Step 4. Fingertip heights

Ti = h_i + F_i + e_tip, with e_tip = 3 to 6 for leather (finger tip ease and tip closure), 3 for knit [S].
Example, e_tip = 5: I 165, M 180, R 168, L 145.

- Use the palm-side finger lengths on the back as well. The back gets ease because fingers bend toward the palm [S: Shapes of Fabric].
  Do not shorten the back.
- Draw each finger top flat. The tip is rounded in the next step, by corner radius not by a pointed shape.
- Tip rounding: corner radius r = 0.35 to 0.45 x finger width, tip depth about 0.3 x finger width. The
  fourchette arm ends where the rounding starts, tapering to 3 to 4 mm.

### Step 5. Slits and crotch points

Crotch height = (lower of the two neighbouring crease heights) + 4 on the palm. The back slits are 5 mm deeper (lower
y) than the palm slits so the longer back-side fourchette edge fits [S: Shapes of Fabric, Koch: slit lengths differ
between back and palm]. The centre slit (index to index) ends at C0.

| Slit | Palm crotch y | Back crotch y |
|---|---|---|
| Index to middle (I\|M) | 98 | 93 |
| Middle to ring (M\|R) | 98 | 93 |
| Ring to little (R\|L) | 89 | 84 |
| Index to index (centre, C0) | 80 (= Web_T) | 80 |

The centre slit depth below the fingertip is Ti_I - 80 = 85 mm. This is the index outer seam length.

Edge length of a finger edge = Ti - crotch y. These are the lengths that the fourchette arms must match
(see 1.9). Example, palm: index inner (C0) 85, index outer (I\|M) 67, middle left 82, middle right 82,
ring left 70, ring right 79, little 56. Back: little 61, ring left 84, ring right 75, middle left 87,
middle right 87, index left 72, index right (C0) 85.

### Step 6. Sides, flare and cuff length

1. Start each side at the outer edge of the little finger at the little crotch height (back L at x = 0, palm L at x = W).
2. Draw ONE straight line from there to the glove end (y_end, see table below). No kink at the wrist: the line
   continues unchanged below the wrist line. A bend at the wrist makes the glove twist on the hand.
3. Cuff width at the end of the glove: Wend = A_end + ease, where A_end is the forearm girth at the planned end. Ease 0 to 10 for
   stretch leather, 10 to 20 for non-stretch. The hand must pass through the cuff: if the cuff at the wrist is narrower than
   about G + 25 (fist with thumb folded), add a wrist opening (see 1.12) or use elastic.
4. Flare per side f = (Wend - W) / 2 over the distance from the little crotch to the end.
5. Cuff bottom edge: straight, square to the centre line.
6. Body below the index crotch: the centre line C0 down to the cuff bottom is the fold. Add a notch at the wrist line.

Glove end, button convention [S: Cornelia James: about 1 inch per button counted from the thumb base] and the web
height Web_T = 80 mm. Glove end y = Web_T - buttons x 25.4 (negative = below the wrist crease). Forearm girths are
typical values for a size 7 hand [T]:

| Length | Buttons | Glove end measured from thumb web | End below the wrist crease (example) | Forearm girth at end (example) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wrist | 1 to 2 | 25 to 51 | 55 to 29 above the crease | 150 |
| Bracelet / 3/4 | 3 to 4 | 76 to 102 | 4 above to 22 below | 150 to 155 |
| Mid-forearm (mousquetaire) | 8 | 203 | 123 below | 190 |
| Elbow | 12 | 305 | 225 below | 225 |
| Opera | 16 | 406 | 326 below | 250 |
| Gauntlet (driving, riding) | n/a | 100 to 180 below wrist | flares to 230 to 280 round the cuff | 230 to 280 |

Makers use 1 inch to 1.33 inch per button, so always state
the length in mm and check it on the wearer's arm. Take the forearm girth at the end and halfway; the sides are one
straight line between the finger-base start point and the end width. If the forearm is not linear, add one
intermediate point and use two straight segments, never a curve with a wrist kink.

### Step 7. Allowances

| Seam | Allowance in the pattern |
|---|---|
| Outseam, leather | 0.8 to 1.5 (stitch placed 0.8 to 1.5 mm from the edge) [S: RCMP]. Dies include this |
| Inseam, leather | 3 to 4 |
| Knit, hand or machine seam | 3 [S: Shapes of Fabric] |
| Piqué | 1.5 to 2 (lap) [T] |
| Cuff self hem | 6 [S: RCMP] |
| Cuff with facing | 3 on the facing seam, facing width 20 to 25 [T] |
| Thumb and hole, knit | 3 around both [S: Shapes of Fabric] |

Gloves have no seam allowance larger than 4. Draw net and add the allowance in the die or cutting file. In CLO3D send
the net outline; CLO3D adds its own allowance.

## 1.8 Thumb hole (gouch) and Bolton thumb: drafting

What real Bolton patterns show (published leather glove patterns, for example The Shapes of Fabric and
historical glove-making workshops), and what the engine's `block glove` reproduces. Do not improvise this
shape: an elliptical tip with straight sides, a tulip with horns or a narrow V are all wrong and fit badly.

### Thumb hole

| Feature | Rule | Example, size 7 (G = 178) |
|---|---|---|
| Position | In the palm half, its inner side running along the fold (1 to 6 mm from it), under the index finger | |
| Top | About 40 mm under the finger crotch points of the palm | |
| Size | About 3.5 cm wide and 5.5 to 6 cm long for a size 7, scaled with the hand girth | 35 x 57 |
| Bottom | Round, towards the wrist, with a notch | |
| Flap (tongue) | The top edge is a V pointing down into the hole. The triangle of leather above the V is a flap | V depth about 9 |
| Slit | A curved cut from the top corner of the hole away from the fold, rising to a dot about 10 mm above the top of the hole. The cut frees the flap, which stays attached only on the fold side and can lift | slit about 17 |

Mark the dot of the slit, the V point and the bottom notch.

### Bolton thumb

| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| Overall shape | A wide oval, about 0.72 times as wide as it is tall, folded on its long axis. Height about 1.7 x the thumb's outer length (about 100 mm for a size 7) |
| Top | Two rounded lobes separated by a cleft on the fold axis, cleft depth about 15 % of the height. Folded, the two lobes are sewn together: they make the rounded tip of the thumb |
| Width | The full width at each height is the round of the thumb there; widest around mid-height (thumb plus the fleshy base) |
| Slit | A cut from one side edge, at about 40 % of the height from the top, curving inward and down to a dot. The flap of the trank goes into this slit: it gives the thumb its movement |
| Notches | On the other side edge, two notches (single and double) bound the part that is sewn to the two edges of the flap's V. A notch at the bottom of the oval matches the bottom notch of the hole |

### Matching (sewing correspondence)

| Thumb edge | Sewn to |
|---|---|
| Lobe edges, from the cleft down to the slit (one side) and to the first notch (other side) | Each other: the tip seam |
| Between the single and the double notch | The two edges of the flap's V |
| From the double notch round the bottom to the slit | Round the hole, bottom notch to bottom notch |
| The two lips of the thumb slit | The two lips of the trank slit (body side and flap side) |

Thumb edges are 2 to 4 % longer than the hole edges they meet (ease). Re-measure every section after cutting
the slits; expect one or two toiles before the thumb is right.

### Quirk thumb variant

- Draft the thumb as a plain tapered piece (no heart lobes): width w_t at the base end, straight sides, elliptical tip. Hole drawn as an oval,
  not a drop, without tongue.
- The quirk is a separate diamond: width 14 to 18, height 16 to 20 [T]. It is sewn at the base of the thumb, in the
  crotch between thumb and hand.
- Use when leather is thick or has little stretch [S].

### Set-in (French) thumb

- Oval hole in the palm (width 0.17 x G, length 0.25 x G).
- Thumb piece is a single elongated oval folded on the axis, with the same perimeter rule as above and no V [S: Yeovil].

## 1.9 Fourchette drafting

The fourchette gives the finger its depth. One arm per finger side, as many patterns as there are arms per pair.

Arms per hand (the second hand mirrors them) and pair totals:

| Finger | Arms per hand | Arms per pair | Fork it belongs to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Index | 1 | 2 | index-middle |
| Middle | 2 | 4 | index-middle and middle-ring |
| Ring | 2 | 4 | middle-ring and ring-little |
| Little | 1 | 2 | ring-little |

Per arm:

1. Length (back edge) = back finger edge length from Step 5. Palm edge length = palm finger edge length; it is 5 mm shorter [S: Shapes of Fabric: the back-side arm is 5 mm longer].
2. Draw a straight tapered strip with the back edge longer. Cut the base end at a slant, or curve the back edge slightly, so both edges meet the
   finger edges exactly [S: Shapes of Fabric].
3. Width at the base fw_b = 1.3 x fw_mid. Width at the tip fw_t = 0.6 x fw_mid. Example, fw_mid = 6: base 8, tip 3.5.
   Knit gloves sample [S: Shapes of Fabric]: 15 at the base, 5 at the top.
4. Tip: the arm stops where the tip rounding starts. Cut across at the finger tip, shape one side to the contour of the finger and ease the other side
   in a gentle slope starting about 25 mm down [S: Koch].
5. Notch at the base (centre of the fork) and 1 notch at mid-finger.
6. Pair the two arms of a fork at their bases: a short seam of 4 to 6 mm at the web, or one strip folded in half and stitched across the fold [S: Koch].
7. Optional quirk at the web: diamond of 8 to 10 mm side set on a point, or triangle 10 x 12 [T].

Worked example, back edge / palm edge lengths and widths:

| Arm | Back edge | Palm edge | Base width | Tip width |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Index (inner, middle side) | 72 | 67 | 8 | 3.5 |
| Middle (index side) | 87 | 82 | 8 | 3.5 |
| Middle (ring side) | 87 | 82 | 8 | 3.5 |
| Ring (middle side) | 75 | 70 | 8 | 3.5 |
| Ring (little side) | 84 | 79 | 8 | 3.5 |
| Little | 61 | 56 | 8 | 3.5 |

Index outer edge: no fourchette. The two index halves are sewn directly together, length 85.

## 1.10 Completing the draft: checks

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| Finger circumference | Back + palm + arm widths at mid-finger = Cf_i (+ stretch ease) |
| Trank width at finger base | Back + palm = W |
| Sum of finger widths per half | Back = Wb, palm = Wp |
| Slit side edges | Match the matching fourchette arm edge within 0.5 mm |
| Thumb perimeter | Thumb base edges = P_g |
| Side lines | One straight line from little crotch to glove end |
| Cuff width | Hand passes through or an opening is drawn |
| Notches | Fingertips, crotch points, wrist, thumb hole (3), fourchette base and mid |
| Grain | Fingers parallel to the low-stretch direction |

## 1.11 Sewing order and seams

Hand glovers sew with small even stitches 3.5 to 4 per cm (machine) and use outseam for leather. Order [S: Koch, Shapes of Fabric, RCMP]:

| Step | Operation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cut and mark the pieces. Cut the slits and the thumb hole exactly | Leather is cut on dies or table cut with a cutting knife |
| 2 | Silking (points) on the back of the trank | Three rows on a two-needle machine; ends pulled through to the flesh side and tied [S: RCMP] |
| 3 | Make up each fourchette fork | Join the two arms at the base, trim the fold [S: Koch] |
| 4 | Make up the thumb: fold on the axis, seam the long side from tip to the slash | Leave 1/16 inch (about 1.5 mm) at each end for the allowance [S: Koch] |
| 5 | Set the thumb into the gouch | Start at the tongue, align the slash edge, then go round the base. Corner C into the V [S: Koch] |
| 6 | Sew the fourchette forks to the fingers, working from index to little finger | Align the fork centre with the crotch point, pin along both finger edges, check that nothing stretches [S: Koch, Shapes of Fabric] |
| 7 | Close the finger tips and the index outer seam, then the little finger edge and the side seam down to the cuff | The side seam is the single closing seam |
| 8 | Hem or face the cuff | Self hem 6 mm; where the palm has an opening, add the reinforcing strip [S: RCMP] |
| 9 | Attach snaps, buttons, loops; tack the pair at 13 mm from the end of the side seam | [S: RCMP] |
| 10 | Finishing: damp the gloves and lay them off on heated hand forms, brush | [S: RCMP] |

Never sew to the extreme edge at the start and end of a seam: leave one allowance so the next seam can start
there [S: Koch].

In the knit version [S: Shapes of Fabric]: thumb into the hole first, then fourchettes pair by pair, index to little finger with 3 mm seam
(the base of each finger has about 1 mm of allowance, so sew it in several passes), the middle finger sewn in two halves and closed at the tip after the
fourchette is attached, then the little finger edge and side seam.

### Seam types

| Seam | How made | Use | Allowance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outseam (overseam) | Edges butted, stitches pass over both [S: Yeovil], 0.8 to 1.5 mm from the edge [S: RCMP] | Leather work gloves, sport gloves, sheepskin; one row of stitching | 0.8 to 1.5 |
| Prixseam | Outseam with horizontal stitches, made on a special machine [S: Yeovil] | Better dress and fashion leather gloves | 1.2 to 2 [T] |
| Inseam | Sew inside out, turn | Fabric, thin leather, lined gloves; leaves no stitches visible | 3 to 4 |
| Piqué | One edge lapped over the other, one row of stitching | Thin kid on dress gloves | lap 1.5 to 2 |
| Half-piqué | Piqué on the back of fingers, inseam on the palm [S: Yeovil] | Dress gloves | as above |
| Whipstitch / brosser | Hand overcast | Sport, hand sewn | 1.5 to 2 |

## 1.12 Variants

### Elastic shirred wrist

- Cuff band cut as a straight band: length = wrist circumference x 1.5 to 1.8 (gather ratio), height = 2 x elastic width + 6.
- Casing: fold the band, stitch 6 mm elastic (flat, 5 to 8 mm wide) inside, elastic length = wrist girth - 15 to 25 (relaxed).
- Or sew 3 to 4 rows of shirring elastic thread at 6 mm spacing, with a bobbin tension adjustment.
- Cuff opening width without a slit = fist circumference (G + 25) + 10.

### Openings

| Opening | Spec |
|---|---|
| Mousquetaire slit | Slit on the palm side at the wrist, about 75 mm (3 inch) long [S: Cornelia James glossary], 2 to 4 buttons, loops or button holes, a 6 mm facing |
| Snap | Four-piece snap on the inside wrist, double leather reinforcement behind the snap [S: RCMP] |
| Press stud / button | Place on the palm-side flap, overlap 18 to 22 mm, reinforce with an extra strip |
| Zipper | Short 100 to 120 mm zipper in the cuff seam |

### Lining

- Lining pieces from the shell patterns, reduced by 1.5 to 2.5 mm at the sides and 3 mm at the fingertips so the lining does not buckle [T].
- Sew the lining inseam. Slip the lining into the shell inside out and tack it at the cuff and at the finger tips, or hem it at the cuff and pull the fingers
  through. Cut lining stretch across the hand.
- Fleece and fur linings: loosen the shell by 1.5 mm per finger edge.

### Knit and mittens

| Type | Method |
|---|---|
| Sewn knit gloves | Draft as in 1.7 with W = G + 10, seam allowance 3, and a rib or elastic wrist |
| Mitten | Two identical pieces (back and palm). Width = G/2 + 12 (stretch) or G/2 + 20 (no stretch). Length = HL + 20. Top: semi-ellipse, depth = 0.4 x width. Thumb: a separate piece, width = T_g/2 + 8, length = T_o + 10, with the same rounded top. Thumb hole on the palm side at 35 mm from the cuff and 25 mm from the side |
| Mitten with gusset thumb | Add a gusset: a diamond of width 35 and height 70 |
| Fingerless glove | Tube: width = G + 10, length = wrist crease to the finger base crease - 5 + hem. Thumb hole: oval 20 x 35 at the thumb web height. Hem the top 10 mm. Or use the trank draft cut off at the finger base crease, with a 10 mm hem |
| Half finger / flip top | Fingers cut at the first joint, with a mitten flap |

## 1.13 Faults and fixes

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Fingers fan out or gaps between fingers | Fingers drawn as a traced hand | Draw straight parallel fingers; the slits close; fourchettes make the space |
| Two index fingers merge into one wide paddle | Centre fold drawn through both indexes | Keep the centre slit down to C0; sew the index halves together on their outer side |
| Plain drop thumb hole | Tongue missing | Add the V tongue at the apex; thumb piece corner C into the V |
| Thumb twists or pulls | Perimeter mismatch, thumb hole not in the palm | Match P_g to the lobe edges; hole within the palm half, tilted toward the centre |
| Kink or bulge at the wrist | Side line bent at the wrist line | One straight line from the little crotch to the glove end |
| Fingers too tight on the back | Back finger lengths shortened | Use palm-side lengths on the back; make back slits 5 mm deeper and back arms 5 mm longer |
| Webs tear | No quirk, slit too shallow | Add quirks; deepen the slits; sew the web with small stitches and a back tack |
| Glove too long in the fingers after wear | Length stretch not pulled out | Pull the stretch out of the skin before cutting; fingers in the low-stretch direction |
| Cuff too tight to put on | Cuff narrower than the fist | Wend, opening or elastic |
| Lining buckles | Lining same size as shell | Reduce lining by 2 mm and tack at the tips |
| Seams rub the fingers | Seam allowance too wide, seam inside | Trim to 1.5 to 3 mm, use outseam or piqué |

## 1.14 CLO3D notes for gloves

- Mesh: particle distance 4 to 6 mm. Collision thickness 0 (the glove otherwise sits off the hand). Use a fine mesh only on the fingers.
- Draw the glove as 2 trank pieces (or 1 trank), 2 Bolton thumbs, 12 fourchette arms as separate pieces. Sew each slit edge to the matching fourchette arm
  edge with segment sewing, keeping the length ratio 1:1.
- Fingers: avatars rarely have finger arrangement points. The user usually places the glove pieces by hand and positions the fingers during simulation
  (using Pin or freezing the avatar fingers). Place the trank around the hand from the wrist.
- Fold the thumb piece on its axis with a fold line; sew it with its straight edge and mark N1, N2, N3 as sewing points.
- Fabric: leather preset with a low stretch. Add a small bend stiffness. Fingertip closing seams are tiny; sew at the highest mesh density.
- Verify menu names in your CLO3D version.

# PART 2. HATS

Head measurements: H = circumference above the eyebrows and ears, around the widest part (women 540 to 580, men 570 to 620, children 480 to 540).
Ease: +10 to +15 for a lined woven hat, 0 to -10 percent for a knit. Arc F-B = front to back over the crown (about 0.5 x H + 20).

## 2.1 Bucket hat

What: soft hat with a conical crown and a sloping or flat brim, no peak.
Photo cues: round flat top, straight slanted sides, brim of constant width all round, seams around the crown base.

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Crown top | 2 (outer, lining) | Circle |
| Crown side | 2 | Cone frustum, seam at the back |
| Brim | 2 (top, under) | Ring or ring sector |
| Interfacing | 1 brim, optional | Fusible interfacing |

Formulas (net):

1. Bottom circumference of the crown Cb = H + 10.
2. Crown height h (side, vertical) 70 to 90. Top circumference Ct = Cb - 2 x h x tan(theta) x pi. Use theta = 6 to 10 degrees for a normal bucket. Radii:
   Rb = Cb/(2 pi), Rt = Ct/(2 pi).
3. Crown top disc radius = Rt.
4. Crown side is a frustum: slant s = sqrt(h^2 + (Rb - Rt)^2). Unfold the cone: outer radius Lout = s x Rb/(Rb - Rt), inner radius Lin = Lout - s,
   sector angle alpha = 360 x Rb / Lout degrees. Add the back seam allowance.
   Example, H = 570, Cb = 580, h = 80, theta = 8 degrees: Rb = 92.3, Ct = 580 - 70.6 = 509.4, Rt = 81.1, s = 80.8,
   Lout = 663.3, Lin = 582.5, alpha = 50.1 degrees (recomputed; an earlier version gave 672 / 591 / 49.4).
   (A simple alternative is a straight band H + 10 long and 80 high, narrowed 15 mm per side at the top.)
5. Brim width B 40 to 60 (droopy 65 to 70). Flat brim: ring with inner radius Rb, outer radius Rb + B. Drooping brim at angle delta below the horizontal:
   sector angle theta_b = 360 x cos(delta), inner radius ri = Cb/(2 pi cos(delta)), outer radius ro = ri + B. delta = 15 degrees gives 347.7 degrees; 25 degrees gives 326.3 degrees [T, derived].
6. Seam allowances 10 (hat), 6 on the brim edge if topstitched.

Sewing order: crown side seam, crown to top, brim seam (a ring cut in 2 or 4 sectors), layer brims right sides together and stitch the outer edge, turn, press, topstitch rows
6 to 8 mm apart (3 to 5 rows stiffen the brim), attach brim to crown, attach lining crown, finish with a sweat band or bias tape.

Faults: crown does not meet the brim (inner radius mismatch: compare arc lengths), brim collapses (add interfacing or rows of stitching), too loose (reduce by 10 and add a stitched tuck at the nape).
CLO3D: brim fabric with higher bend stiffness; sew the brim sectors with 1:1 segment sewing; mesh 5 mm; place the hat on the head with Pin to avoid slipping.

## 2.2 Baseball cap, six panels

Photo cues: six gores meeting at a button on top, stiffened front, curved visor, back strap or band.

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Panel | 6 (2 front panels interfaced) | Gore |
| Visor | 2 fabric + 1 stiff board (plastic or buckram) | Curved |
| Sweatband | 1 | Strip |
| Lining panels | 6 optional | |
| Button | 1 covered | |
| Back strap or eyelets | 1 | |

Draft a dome as a sphere with circumference H (ease +10 total):

1. Panel base width bw = (H + 10)/6.
2. Panel length S = (H + 10)/4 (along the surface from the bottom edge to the top point). A lower-profile cap: use S x 0.92.
3. Panel width at distance s from the top: w(s) = bw x sin(90 degrees x s / S). Compute at every 10 mm along the panel and join the points with a smooth curve.
4. Cut the tip: at the top cut the point square at 12 to 15 mm width so the button covers it.
5. Front panels: add a centre seam only if the pattern has a 6 panel with seam in the front; the front panel is stiffened with fusible buckram.
6. Visor: inner edge follows the head curve; chord (width) = 2 x bw = 190, depth of the curve at the centre 20, overall visor length from the front 65, outer edge arc of radius 150 to 170. Draw it by arc, then draw 2 pieces plus the stiffener (the stiffener is smaller by 6 on all sides).
7. Sweatband: strip (H + 10) x 35, folded in half; the finished band is (H) long.
8. Back opening: sew the back panels' seam leaving a 30 to 40 mm gap and fix the strap.
9. Seam allowance 10. Topstitch seams at 3 mm.

Sewing order: interface the front panels, sew panel pairs from the base up to the top, join the pairs, top-stitch each seam, sew the visor (two fabric layers with stiffener, stitched in rows 8 mm apart), attach the visor to the front panels, add the sweatband, add the button, back strap.
Faults: cap sits too high (panel too long), puckered tip (gores not tapered with sin curve), visor flips up (too curved).
CLO3D: dome from 6 panels simulates well; use mesh 5 mm; stiffen the front panels and visor with higher bending and weight.

## 2.3 Beret

Photo cues: flat circular disc, slight dome, a small stalk at the centre, an inner band, sits tilted.

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Top (outer) | 1 | Circle, diameter D_o |
| Under (inner) | 1 | Ring: diameter D_o, centre opening D_i |
| Band | 1 | Bias or knit strip |
| Lining | optional | |

1. D_i = (H + 10)/pi (for H = 570: 184).
2. D_o = 260 to 290 for an adult (the larger, the slouchier). Typical 270.
3. The under piece: the same outer diameter as the top; the opening D_i. The ring is sewn to the top along the outer edge.
4. Band: strip 25 wide x (H + 10), folded in half, sewn to the opening.
5. Optional stalk: a roll of 25 x 25.

Sewing order: stitch top and under pieces right sides together along the outer edge leaving a gap, turn, press, topstitch near the edge, attach the band to the opening, tack the stalk.
Faults: beret sags (outer circle too large), tight at the opening.
CLO3D: set the opening to a head circumference; low stiffness; let it drape on the head.

## 2.4 Beanie (knit, cut-and-sew)

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Crown gore | 6 (or 4) | Pointed arch |
| Cuff | 1 | Band |

1. Knit stretch 40 to 60 percent. Beanie circumference Cb = H x 0.85 to 0.9.
2. Gore (6): base width Cb/6, length = 0.24 x H (crown height 120 to 140), the shape is a pointed arch (the top is a point).
   Cuff: a straight band, cut height 90 to 110 (folded in half), length Cb.
3. Alternative: rectangle (flat tube), width = Cb/2 and height 220 to 250, with a seam at the top (folded in half) or gathered on a cord.
4. Allowance 8 (stretch seam), use a ball point needle and a stretch stitch.

Faults: too loose (increase negative ease), wavy cuff (stretch not matched).
CLO3D: use a knit fabric preset with stretch; mesh 5 mm.

## 2.5 Fedora brim (basics)

Photo cues: pinched crown with a centre dent, ribbon band, brim of 55 to 65 with a slight up-turn at the back.

1. Head oval: the head opening is an oval (long axis a, short axis b = 0.82 a). With H = 580: a = H/5.73 = 101, b = 83 (Ramanujan perimeter).
   Opening length = 2a, width = 2b.
2. Crown: oval tip (top) with the same oval, crown side height 105 to 120. Crown opening slightly larger than the head (+10 ease).
3. Brim: a flat oval ring whose inner oval = crown base; width 55 to 65, with a wired edge (millinery wire). Cut a brim with a back and front difference: front 60, back 55.
4. Ribbon: grosgrain 25 to 35 wide x (H + 30).
5. Sweatband: leather or cotton 25 x H.

Faults: brim sags (wire or stiffener missing), oval too round.
CLO3D: model the crown as a single deformed cylinder; the felt shaping is not reproducible by simulation; use stiff fabric with high bending.

# PART 3. BAGS

Measurements: finished width W, height Ht, depth D (gusset). All values net.

## 3.1 Tote (flat)

Photo cues: two rectangles, two handles, no gusset or boxed corners.

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Body | 2 (or 1 on the fold) | W x Ht |
| Handles | 2 | strap |
| Lining | 2 | same size, 10 shorter |
| Interfacing | 2 | fusible fleece |

1. Body width = W + 20 (allowance), height = Ht + 20.
2. Handle: finished width 25 to 40; cut width = 4 x finished width (edges folded to the centre, then folded again), length: hand handle 300 to 350 (drop 100 to 140), shoulder 560 to 650 (drop 230 to 280).
3. Place handles 70 to 120 from the side seams and stitch with a box-X for reinforcement.
4. Seam allowance 10; top hem 40 (fold twice 20).

Sewing order: interface, make handles, sew handles on the bag front and back, sew body side and bottom seams, sew lining (leave a 120 turning gap), insert the lining into the bag right sides together, sew the top, turn, topstitch.

## 3.2 Gusseted bag and boxed corners

Boxed corner: from a flat bag with bottom corners, cut a square of side c = D/2 at each bottom corner, then stitch the cut edges together: the depth is D = 2c.

1. Boxed-corner panel (front and back): width = W + 20, height = Ht + c + 20, with c = D/2 cut from each bottom corner.
2. For a gusseted bag: front and back panels W x Ht; gusset strip width D, length = 2(Ht - r) + (W - 2r) + pi x r for a U shape with corner radius r (r = 0 gives W + 2 Ht).
3. Round the lower corners with radius r = 20 to 40 on the panels, and the gusset follows the same path length.
4. Lining as above, 10 shorter, with a patch pocket 150 x 180.

Faults: gusset length mismatch (measure panel perimeter), baggy corners (interface the bottom).
CLO3D: use a stiff fabric with high bending, add pressure or use pins to inflate the form.

# PART 4. BELTS

Photo cues: strip with buckle, one end pointed or rounded, holes at 25 spacing.

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Belt strap | 1 leather or 1 folded fabric | |
| Loop (keeper) | 1 | |
| Buckle | 1 | |

1. Finished width 25 to 40 (jeans 30 to 38, dress 20 to 25). Check the buckle inner width = belt width + 1 to 2.
2. Length = waist (at the belt line) + 150 to 180. Size = distance from the buckle fold to the middle hole.
3. Holes: 5, spacing 25, centre hole at the size, first hole 15 from the tip. Hole diameter 4 to 5.
4. Buckle end: fold over 40 to 50 and stitch with 2 rows. Tip: pointed (60 degrees) or round.
5. Fabric belt: strip width = 2 x finished + 20, fold the long edges toward the centre, interline with stiff interfacing.
6. Curved waistband belt: the upper edge is shorter than the lower; for a width w and a curve of angle phi (radians) the difference is w x phi. Draw it by arcs.

Faults: loose (too few holes), buckle bar too tight (strap too thick; reduce thickness on the fold).
CLO3D: draw as a strap piece with high stiffness and a thickness fabric; position on the avatar with a belt line.

# PART 5. NECKTIES AND BOW TIES

## 5.1 Necktie

Photo cues: long strap widening to a blade, narrow neck section, tail, bias grain (stripes diagonal), a bar tack at the back, a keeper loop.

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blade (large end) | 1 | Bias |
| Centre or tail | 1 or 2 | Bias; joined by 45 degree seams |
| Interlining | 1 | Wool/cotton cut on the bias |
| Tipping | 2 | Lining at both ends |
| Keeper loop | 1 | |

1. Finished length 1450 (145 cm). Width of the blade 80 to 95 (slim 50 to 65). Neck width 30 to 40. Tail end 50 to 60.
2. Profile [T]: width at the blade end 90, constant to 200, taper to 35 at 800 from the blade tip, constant 35 to 1150, widen to 55 at the tail end (1450).
3. Cut on the true bias (45 degrees). Join the pieces with seams at 45 degrees (the stripe pattern continues). Seam allowance 10, press open.
4. Interlining is cut as the finished shape minus 3 mm all round, also on the bias.
5. Fold in three: the edges fold to the centre and are slip stitched (loosely) with a continuous thread, leaving slack so the tie stretches.

Faults: tie twists (not true bias), neck width too wide, tie too short for a large neck (lengthen by about twice the extra neck girth).
CLO3D: set the grain at 45 degrees; mesh 5 mm.

## 5.2 Bow tie

| Piece | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bow | 1 rectangle | Folded double |
| Knot cover | 1 strip | |
