# Sewing and construction techniques

Reference for choosing a technique from a photo or a design brief, and for giving the pattern the right
allowances, notches and extra pieces. All values are millimetres or centimetres. Values are typical ranges
from home-sewing, couture, tailoring and industrial practice; where sources disagree the range is given and
the item is marked "varies". Always test on a scrap of the real fabric.

## 0. How to read this sheet

- **Net outline** is the sewing line. **Allowance** is added outside it (see `finishes.md` for the default
  table). In CLO3D send the net outline only: CLO3D adds its own allowance.
- **Pattern needs** lists what the pattern must carry: allowance, notches, drill holes, extra pieces,
  grain direction.
- Allowance rule of thumb: the allowance must hold the construction. Count: seam line to cut edge = space
  for folds, trims and stitching. Never trim the pattern to the minimum; industrial 1 cm is only valid
  with a folder, a seaming overlock or a binder; use 1.5 cm where alterations or hand finishing are expected.
- Notch every point that must match: ends of gathers and ease, pocket openings, dart legs, pleat folds,
  zip stops, button positions, hem folds, centre lines, balance points.
- Stitch length: wovens 2.5 mm (10 to 12 stitches per inch, 4 to 5 per cm); topstitch 3 to 3.5 mm; basting
  4 to 5 mm; gathering 3.5 to 4 mm; knits 2.5 to 3 mm with stretch stitch.

## 1. Seams

### 1.1 Seam types

| Seam | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain (open or pressed to one side) | No visible stitching on face; allowances pressed open or to one side | 1 to 1.5 cm; notches | Right sides together, stitch on the line, finish edges (overlock, zigzag, pinking, binding), press open | Default for all woven garments, most linings |
| French seam | Narrow rounded seam on the wrong side, no raw edge visible inside | 1.5 cm | Wrong sides together, stitch 9 to 10 mm from edge, trim to 3 mm, press, fold right sides together on the first stitching, stitch 6 mm from the fold | Sheer and light fabrics, silk, lawn, chiffon, baby clothes. Not for curves tighter than a gentle bust curve or bulky fabric |
| Hairline (narrow French) seam | Very fine seam, 2 to 3 mm finished, hardly visible | 1 cm | As French seam: first pass 5 mm, trim to 2 mm, second pass 3 mm from the fold. Hand or machine | Organza, voile, sheer blouses, couture. Not for stressed seams |
| Flat-felled seam | Two parallel rows of topstitching on face, one on the wrong side; flat folded edge | 1.5 cm on both pieces for a 6 mm fell; 2 to 2.5 cm on the fell side for a 10 mm fell | Wrong sides together, stitch; trim the under allowance to 6 mm (for a 10 mm fell trim to 10 mm); fold the wide allowance over it, turn under the raw edge, edgestitch. Industrial: folder attachment with 4-needle machine (stitch 401 or 301) | Jeans, workwear, shirts side seams, sportswear, reversible garments. Medium fabrics; too bulky for heavy cloth; not for tight curves (use mock felled) |
| Mock flat-felled seam | Looks like a felled seam from the face (two rows) but the wrong side shows a stitched allowance | 1.5 cm | Plain seam, press allowances to one side, overlock or pink together, topstitch two parallel rows through all layers (or 2-needle coverstitch) | Curves, heavier fabrics, shirts and jeans yoke when speed matters |
| Welt seam | One row of topstitching 6 to 10 mm from the seam on the face | 1.5 cm; one allowance can be reduced | Plain seam, press both allowances to one side, trim the lower one to 6 mm, topstitch through the upper allowance only | Flat reinforcement, tailored trousers, heavier cloth, curves |
| Lapped seam | One edge visibly overlaps the other; topstitched on top | Top piece: fold under 1 to 1.5 cm; under piece: 1 to 1.5 cm or none if the overlap is a design line | Press the top edge under, lay it on the seam line of the under piece, edgestitch or stitch with a 1 to 2 mm stitch width | Yokes, patch seams, interfacings, felt, leather, non-fray fabrics (cut edge lap: 0 allowance) |
| Slot seam | Two folded edges with a strip underneath, gap visible | 1.5 cm each plus an under strip 4 cm wide | Fold each edge back along the seam line, baste onto a strip centred under the gap, topstitch both folds | Decorative; contrast strip; tailored |
| Bound seam (Hong Kong finish) | Each raw edge wrapped in a bias strip, 6 mm wide, visible when the garment is open | 1.5 cm; no extra cut except strips | Plain seam. Strip cut on the bias 2.5 to 3 cm wide (allowance + 6 mm). Stitch to the right side of the allowance 6 mm from the edge, wrap over the edge, stitch in the ditch from the right side, or edgestitch | Unlined jackets, coats, unstructured garments, couture, any ravelling fabric; replaces overlock |
| Piped / corded seam | A narrow rounded cord at the seam, on the face | 1.5 cm; piping flange 1.5 cm sandwiched, trim to 1 cm | Make piping (1.5.3), baste it to the right side of one piece with the cord on the seam line, put the other piece on top and stitch with a zipper foot on the first stitching, closer to the cord | Edges, yokes, cuffs, armholes, upholstery, shaped contour lines |
| Princess and curved seams | Long convex-concave seam from shoulder or armhole to the hem | 1 to 1.5 cm; many notches; one on the bust and one on the waist at minimum | Stitch the concave piece (bust) onto the convex one, with the concave piece on top, clipped; start at the waist and stitch up and down to keep the grain; press over a ham, clip or notch the concave allowance | Fitted bodices, dresses, jackets |
| Overlock (serged) | Three or four looped threads on the edge, no folding | 0.6 to 1 cm; the knife trims 3 to 5 mm | See 1.4 | Knits, ready-to-wear |
| Topstitched seam | One or two rows of stitching on the face next to the seam | Same as the base seam | Seam pressed to one side, topstitch at 3 to 8 mm | Detail, jeans, tailoring |
| Seam with stay tape | Narrow tape in the seam, not visible | Notch at the tape ends | Tape (1 cm woven or twill) stitched in the seam | Shoulder seams, waist seams, hem edges that stretch |
| Self-bound / flat seam | Looks like a Hong Kong seam but made with the allowance itself | 2 cm on the wider edge | Fold the wide allowance twice around the narrow one and stitch | Unlined jackets, linen |

### 1.2 Seam allowance by seam type and machine

| Seam or machine | Pattern allowance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Plain seam, lockstitch 301, home sewing | 1.5 cm (5/8 inch) | Standard of home patterns; room to alter |
| Plain seam, industrial lockstitch, ready-to-wear | 1 cm | Industry standard; use 1.5 cm on alteration seams |
| Overlock 504 (3 thread) | 0.6 to 1 cm | Blade removes 3 to 5 mm; the stitch width is 4 to 5 mm |
| Overlock 514 (4 thread, 2 needles) | 1 cm | Light-medium wovens and knits; seaming strength |
| Safety stitch 516 or 515 | 1 cm | Wovens: chainstitch plus overedge in one pass |
| Coverstitch 406 or 602 | 0 for join (butt seam); hem 2 to 2.5 cm | Seam finish for knits and flat seams |
| Flatlock | 0.6 to 1 cm, trimmed to 3 to 5 mm | Cut edges butted or overlapped |
| French seam | 1.5 cm | Total of 9 mm plus 6 mm |
| Flat-felled seam | 1.5 cm both sides (6 mm fell); 2 to 2.5 cm on fell side (10 mm fell) | Needs a folder to be 1 cm industrial |
| Hong Kong | 1.5 cm | Strip is extra |
| Welt seam | 1.5 cm | |
| Seam with zip | 1.5 to 2 cm | See section 5 |
| Curved neckline or armhole | 0.6 to 1 cm | Small allowance for clean turning |
| Leather, glued and topstitched | 1 cm | See 1.5 |
| Knit, 1 cm | 1 cm | Do not use less than 0.6 cm unless overlock trimmed |

### 1.3 Notching and clipping

| Case | Treatment | Pattern needs |
|---|---|---|
| Convex curve (outer curve, collar edge, pocket corner) | Notch: cut small V wedges out of the allowance to remove bulk | Allowance 0.6 to 1 cm; notch depth up to 3 mm from the stitching |
| Concave curve (neckline, armhole) | Clip: cut straight snips towards the stitching, 2 to 3 mm short, every 1 to 2 cm | Allowance 0.6 to 1 cm |
| Corner | Cut diagonally across the corner 2 to 3 mm from the stitching | Tip needs 1 to 1.5 cm; square tip |
| Inside corner (square neckline, pocket mitre) | Clip to within 1 to 2 mm of the stitching at the corner | Reinforce with a small interfacing patch or stay stitch |
| Pattern notches | 5 to 6 mm cut INTO the allowance (never past the seam line) in the pattern; single notch front, double back, triple for sleeves per `finishes.md` | At every match point |
| Grading (layering) allowances | Trim each allowance to a different width, 3 mm apart: the one nearest the face is longest | Allowance 1 cm minimum; see facings |

### 1.4 Seams for knits

| Technique | Recognise | Pattern needs | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overlock 3 or 4 thread (504 / 514) | Looped thread edge, stretchy | 1 cm | T-shirts, jersey, side seams, sleeve joins |
| Safety stitch (516) | A chainstitch line next to the overlock | 1 cm | Wovens and stable knits that bear stress |
| Coverstitch (602, 406) | Two or three parallel rows on face, loopers on the back | Hem 2 to 2.5 cm | Hems, neckbands, cuffs, topstitched seams |
| Flatlock (607) | Flat seam with ladder pattern on one side and loops on the other | 0 to 0.6 cm, cut edges butted | Activewear, base layers, no-bulk joins |
| Stretch stitch / lightning stitch on a home machine | A zigzag-like straight stitch | 1.5 cm trimmed to 1 cm | Home sewing without overlocker |
| Narrow zigzag (3 mm wide, 2.5 mm long) | Narrow zigzag line | 1 cm | Home sewing fallback |
| Twin needle | Two rows on face, a zigzag cover on back | Hem 2 to 2.5 cm | Home hems and neckline topstitch |
| Stabilising tape | Clear elastic or twill tape in the seam | 1 cm; notches | Shoulder seams, waist seams, necklines that must not grow |

Rules: use a ballpoint or stretch needle; do not stretch while stitching; set the differential feed for
stretch (1 to 1.5); thread with the same fibre; stitch parallel to the cut edge. Pattern: place seam lines
on the stretch direction; keep all long seams matched with notches every 15 to 20 cm because knits grow.

### 1.5 Seams for leather and faux leather

| Technique | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glued and topstitched | Flat seam, two rows of topstitching 3 to 6 mm either side of seam | 1 cm; 1.5 cm on collars and edges | No pins. Apply leather contact cement to both allowances, let dry, press with a hammer or roller, then stitch with a heavy needle (leather, size 90 to 110) at 3 to 4 mm stitch length. Do not backstitch more than once |
| Plain seam, pressed open and glued | Hardly visible seam | 1 to 1.5 cm | Stitch, tap flat with a rubber mallet, glue allowances open, topstitch if desired |
| Lapped seam | Overlap, no allowance on the top piece | Top piece: 0 allowance (cut edge as seam line); bottom: 1 cm | Glue the top piece onto the bottom, edgestitch; common on leather |
| Saddle stitch (hand) | Two needles, one thread, a stitch that does not unravel; slanted stitch | 3 to 4 mm stitch marks | Hand sewing with pricking iron; strongest seam, holds if one stitch breaks |
| Welt and double topstitch | Flat or standing seam | 1.5 cm | Trim one allowance, stitch both |
| Binding seam | Bound edge | 0 raw edge (cut + bind) | Leather binding, bias tape 2.5 to 3 cm |

Rules: no pins (they leave permanent holes), use paper clips, wonder clips, double-sided tape or glue; use a
Teflon or roller foot; no unpicking (holes stay); stitch length 3 to 4 mm. Raw edges of leather do not
fray: hems and edges can be left cut (0 allowance), burnished, edge-painted, or turned and glued (1.5 to
2.5 cm). Corners need mitres or cut-out relief.

## 2. Hems and edge finishes

| Hem | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turned and stitched (double-fold) | One or two rows of stitching on face | 3 to 4 cm skirt, 4 cm trousers, 2 to 3 cm sleeve, 1.5 cm shirt tail | Finish raw edge, fold up once, press, edgestitch; or fold twice (1+1 cm) | Shirts, casual, kids |
| Blind hem (hand or machine) | No visible stitching on face; invisible hem line | 3 to 4 cm; finish the raw edge first | Fold up, stitch just under the turned edge catching a thread of the garment with a blind hem foot or hand slip stitch | Skirts, trousers, dresses, tailored garments |
| Catchstitch hem | Invisible, hemline slightly elastic | 4 to 5 cm | Hand catchstitch on a taped edge | Wool, jackets, stretch |
| Rolled hem (3 mm, machine or overlock) | Very narrow tight roll, 2 to 3 mm | Allowance 0.6 cm, none if hemmed on edge | Overlock with rolled hem setting or a rolled hem foot | Chiffon, scarves, edges on ruffles |
| Baby hem (narrow hem, 6 mm) | Narrow double fold, 3 to 6 mm, visible stitching on the face | 1 cm (turn 5 mm twice) | Stitch 5 mm from the fold, trim the fold, turn again and stitch (or use a hemmer foot) | Silk, lingerie, circle skirts, ruffles |
| Hand-rolled hem | Tiny rolled edge with hidden slip stitches | 0.6 to 1 cm | Roll and whip stitch | Couture silk scarves, bridal |
| Faced hem | Shaped facing sewn to the hem, nothing visible | Facing piece cut on the hem curve, 6 to 8 cm wide, seam allowance 1 cm | Sew facing to hem right sides together, turn, press, catch it down | Curved or shaped hem, jackets with a curved front, heavy or bulky fabrics |
| Bias faced hem | A bias strip turned in | Strip 3 to 4 cm wide cut on the bias | Strip sewn at 0.6 to 1 cm, turned, hand stitched | Circles, curves, scalloped edges, bulky fabrics |
| Horsehair braid hem | Hem stands out in a stiff wave | Braid width 2.5 to 10 cm; hem edge allowance 0.6 to 1 cm | Sew braid to the right side of the hem edge, fold to the inside and slip stitch; fabric may need a facing | Bridal, ball gowns, skirts with structured flare |
| Circle and bias hem | Hem on a circle or full flare | Hem allowance 0.6 to 1.5 cm (narrow); grain at 45 degrees | Hang the garment 24 hours (up to 48 on bias), then mark the hem with a level, trim, finish with a rolled or baby hem | Circle skirts, bias dresses, flared sleeves |
| Knit hem, coverstitch or twin needle | Two parallel rows | 2 to 2.5 cm | Fold up, press, topstitch from the right side with a coverstitch or twin needle; stabilise with tape | T-shirts, jersey, sportswear |
| Raw edge (leather, felt, neoprene, scuba, bonded fabrics) | Cut edge left | 0 | Cut clean edge, edge-paint or burnish; check that the edge does not curl | Leather, felt, scuba, laser cut |
| Shirt-tail hem | Curved hem with tiny double fold | 1 to 1.5 cm | 5 mm fold twice; stitch at 3 mm | Shirts, blouses |
| Jeans hem | Chainstitch hem with roping | 3 cm; double fold 1.5 | Fold twice, chainstitch | Jeans, workwear |
| Lining hem | Hem of lining with jump hem, shorter | Lining shorter by 2 cm than the shell; hem 2 cm | Baby hem or double fold | Jackets, skirts |

Hem rules: let a garment hang before hemming if the grain is on the bias or very flared. Curved hems
need an allowance narrower than 2.5 cm (or the fold will not lie flat); ease the extra with a gathering
thread. Hem depth becomes narrower as the sweep increases: straight 4 cm, A-line 3 cm, flared 1.5 cm,
full circle 0.6 to 1 cm. Mark the fold line with a notch at the side seams.

### 2.1 Hem corners and vents

| Case | Pattern needs |
|---|---|
| Vent, slit or kick pleat | Extend the hem allowance and mitre: fold the vent allowance (2.5 to 3 cm wide) and stitch diagonal at 45 degrees |
| Mitred hem corner (jacket front, vent, sleeve vent) | Allowance equal to the hem depth on both edges; cut the mitre off to 1 cm of the corner |
| Sleeve hem with a vent | Hem 3 to 4 cm, vent seam folded back |
| Hem at an angle seam (kick pleat) | Hem allowance trimmed at an angle so that it fits the angle of the seam |

## 3. Facings and bindings

| Technique | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shaped facing | Inside shaped piece, no visible stitching, edge turned in | Facing cut from the garment pattern: same shape as the edge, 5 to 8 cm wide (neckline 5 to 6 cm, armhole 4 to 5 cm); 1 cm allowance; same grain; interfaced; notches at shoulders | Join facing at shoulders, finish outer edge, sew to garment right sides together, grade, clip, understitch 2 mm from the seam on the facing, turn, tack at seams | Necklines, armholes, waistlines |
| All-in-one (combination) facing | Neckline and armhole faced with one piece, no visible shoulder seam | One piece covering neck and armhole; shoulder seams are left open until after the facing is turned | Stitch neckline and armholes, turn through the shoulder opening, close the shoulders | Sleeveless dresses and tops |
| Extended (grown-on) facing | Front edge folded back, same fabric | Add the facing width (4 to 8 cm) beyond the centre front along the fold; interface the fold; notch the fold line | Fold, press, turn; edge finish at the end | Jackets, shirt fronts (placket), skirt openings |
| Separate front facing | A separate piece on the front edge | Same shape as the front edge, 5 to 8 cm wide, 1 cm allowance | As shaped facing | Jackets, coats, cardigans |
| Bias facing (bias binding as facing) | Narrow stripe visible only inside | Strip 3 to 4 cm wide cut on the bias; 1 cm | Sew strip to edge, press to inside, stitch down | Curves with little bulk, sheer fabrics |
| Single bias binding | Strip seen on both sides, one raw edge turned under | Strip cut on the bias, width = 4 x finished width (finished 6 mm needs 2.5 cm); allowance of the garment edge 0 (the strip adds) | Stitch strip to the edge (garment right side to strip right side) at the first fold (6 mm), wrap over, edgestitch or hand stitch | Armholes, necklines, hems on lightweight fabric |
| French (double) binding | Double thickness binding, clean edge | Strip cut on the bias 6 x finished width (finished 6 mm = 3.8 cm; add 0.5 to 1 cm for bulky fabrics); fold in half lengthwise | Stitch to the front at the first fold (equal to the finished width), turn to the back and slipstitch on the seam line | Jackets, quilts, children's wear, edges that need strength |
| Piping | Narrow cord rounded edge between two pieces | Cord 2 to 5 mm; bias strip width = cord circumference + 2 x 1.5 cm (3 mm cord needs 4 cm); piping sandwiched in a 1.5 cm seam | Cover cord, baste, sew with a zipper foot | Edges, seams, cushions |
| Flat piping (no cord) | A narrow folded edge between pieces | Strip 2.5 to 3 cm wide folded in half and inserted | Fold, baste, sandwich | Contrast edge |
| Bias tape (pre-folded) | A narrow band folded around the edge | Band 1.2 to 2.5 cm wide finished at 6 to 12 mm | Slip over the edge and stitch | Seam finishes, edges |
| Elastic binding | Binding with elastic | Bias strip and elastic ratio per section 4 | See elastic below | Swim, lingerie |
| Self-fabric binding | The garment edge wrapped in own fabric | As bias strip | As above | Edges of reversible garments |

Facing rules: facing is cut with the same grain as the garment; interface fusible weft to the facing; the
facing is usually 0.3 to 0.5 cm narrower than the garment at the shoulder, or the shoulder seam has to
match; understitch curved facings 2 to 3 mm from the seam; tack the facing at the shoulder seam and CB
with a thread or a small stitch; trim the corners; the facing edge should be finished (overlock,
pinking, turn and stitch).

Bias: cut at 45 degrees. A strip stretches while stitching: do not pull. Join strips on the straight grain
with a diagonal seam (seam line at 45 degrees) to reduce bulk. Pre-shape the binding with steam on
curves.

## 4. Shaping

### 4.1 Darts

| Dart | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Straight dart | Triangle sewn into the garment, point toward bust or hip | Legs equal length; notches at each leg; drill hole 1 cm before the point; allowance clean; dart point 2.5 to 3 cm short of the apex | Fold right sides together, stitch from the wide end to the point, run off the end of the fold, tie off. Press flat, then press the dart direction: waist darts toward the centre, bust darts down, shoulder darts toward the neck, elbow darts down |
| Double-pointed (fish-eye) dart | Diamond shaped dart | Two points; mark the waist line; notches at the legs and at the widest point | Stitch from point to point through the widest part; clip at the waist line (about 1 cm) to let it lie flat; press toward the centre |
| Contour dart | Curved dart following the body | Curved legs; notches along the curve | Stitch as straight, clip curved parts |
| Slashed dart | Cut open and pressed flat | Allowance 1.5 cm each side of the dart | Cut along the dart centre to 1.5 cm of the point, press open; stitch with an overlock or bind |
| Dart converted to seam (princess) | A seam in place of a dart | See 1.1 | |
| Dart tuck / released dart | Soft tuck, no point | Tuck depth equals dart intake | See 4.2 |

Dart rules: darts are trued so the allowance matches after folding (see `finishes.md`); the point is not
backstitched, it is knotted; heavy fabric darts are cut open and pressed flat; in fine fabrics press with a
paper strip under to avoid imprints; bust dart ends 2.5 to 3 cm before the bust point; waist darts end
8 to 10 cm below waist at the hip (front) and 12 to 14 cm (back).

### 4.2 Tucks and pleats

| Technique | Recognise | Pattern needs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pin tucks | Very narrow tucks 1.5 to 3 mm wide, stitched through the fold | Fabric consumption = 2 x tuck width + 2 mm gap; sew before cutting the piece (tucks are made on a block of fabric, then cut) | Fine fabrics, blouse fronts, baby clothes |
| Tucks (wider) | Folds stitched from the top, 0.6 to 2.5 cm | Consumption 2 x depth for each tuck | Stitch from the right side or from the wrong, press to one side |
| Release tucks | Tucks stitched part way, open below (or above) | Mark the stitch end with a drill hole; consumption as tucks | Shaping at the waist, shoulder, cuffs |
| Knife pleat | Folds all in one direction | Pleat depth D (fold to foldback); fabric per pleat 2 D; pleats mark in three lines (fold, foldback, edge); notches on each | Skirts, kilts; fix with topstitching at the waist or with tape; press sharp |
| Box pleat | Two folds opposite, meeting at the back | 4 D per pleat | Skirt, shirt back yoke |
| Inverted pleat | The opposite of box; folds meet at the face | 4 D per pleat | Skirts, sleeves, shirt back |
| Kick pleat | A pleat at the hem of a straight skirt | Extension 10 to 15 cm tall at the hem (from the hem to 20 to 25 cm up); the underlay is cut as a piece | Straight skirts for walking ease |
| Accordion pleat | Many tiny folds, each 6 mm | Special heat-pressed fabric; pattern allows 3:1 | Chiffon skirts |
| Sunray pleat | Pleats radiating | Cut as a circle; widths vary by length | Special |
| Pleat from yoke | Several pleats at the waist | Notch every fold | Trouser fronts (forward pleats), skirts |
| Trouser pleat | One or two folds below the waistband, pressed toward the pocket | Pleat depth 2.5 to 4 cm; add 5 to 8 cm to the front width | Tailored trousers |

Pleat rules: add the consumption to the width of the piece; maintain the grain in the fold; stitch the
pleat edge for 8 to 10 cm below the waist (stitch-down) to control; press pleats with a damp cloth;
heavier fabrics use soft pleats; grain line parallel to the fold.

### 4.3 Gathers, ruffles, shirring and smocking

| Technique | Ratio / dimension | Pattern needs | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gathers (two rows of long stitches) | Sheer, light: 2.5 to 3 : 1; light to medium: 2 to 2.5 : 1; medium weight: 1.5 to 2 : 1; thick: 1.25 to 1.5 : 1 | Allowance 1.5 cm at the gathered edge; notch at the beginning and end of gathers and at the centre; ratio width = finished width x ratio | Two rows of stitches (one on the seam line and one 6 mm in the allowance); stitch length 3.5 to 4 mm; pull the bobbin threads; distribute evenly; match the notches; stitch with the gathered side up; trim and press toward the flat piece |
| Ruffle (flounce) | Length 1.5 to 3 times the edge; strip width as design + hem | Strip: width + hem + 1.5 cm; length = edge x ratio; a circular (cut-on-curve) flounce gives drape without gathers | Hem the free edge (rolled or baby hem), gather the other, sew |
| Easing | 0 to 5 % extra (sleeve cap 2 to 4 cm; shoulder back 0.6 to 1 cm) | Two notches; ease marked | Machine ease stitch, shrink the ease with steam over a ham |
| Gathered waistband / skirt | 1.5 to 2 : 1 for skirts | See above | Skirt panel width = waist x ratio |
| Shirring (elastic thread) | Fabric width = finished width x 2 to 3 : 1; rows 6 to 10 mm apart; elastic thread on the bobbin (wound by hand, not stretched); stitch length 3.5 to 4 mm | Rectangles with the grain horizontal; allowance 1.5 cm on side seams; top edge of shirred panel with a 1.5 cm fold; shirring depth marked | Shirring is done on a flat piece, side seams after (or in the round); steam the rows to tighten; leave 1.5 cm at the ends unstitched; lightweight cotton or rayon |
| Smocking | Fabric 3 to 4 times the finished width | Straight grain, rows at 6 to 8 mm (3 mm grid); decorative embroidery | Gather with a smocking pleater or dots, embroider across the pleats, remove gathering threads | Children's dresses, yokes, cuffs |
| Gathering with cord | Zig-zag over a cord, pulled | Same as gathers | Heavy fabrics, large ruffles |

Gathers rules: heavier fabrics gather less; gathering at the waist should be placed at the sides and the
back, not the front (smooth abdomen). A pattern for gathers states the ratio on the piece and notches
every quarter.

### 4.4 Elastic casings and applied elastic

| Technique | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fold-over casing | Tunnel at the edge, with a row of stitching, elastic hidden inside | Casing width = elastic width + 3 to 6 mm ease. Allowance beyond the edge = casing width + 1 cm turn-under (or + 0.6 cm if the edge is overlocked) | Fold the allowance to the inside, stitch near the lower edge leaving a 3 to 4 cm gap, thread the elastic, close the gap |
| Separate casing | A strip sewn on | Strip cut width = 2 x (elastic width + 3 mm ease) + 2 x 1 cm; length = circumference | Sew to the edge, fold, stitch |
| Waistband elastic casing | Wide casing on a skirt | Elastic 2 to 4 cm; casing fold 1.5 cm + width | Same |
| Multi-row elastic | Several rows of narrow elastic 6 to 12 mm | Rows 12 to 20 mm apart; ratio of elastic as below | Stitch rows, thread elastic in each, or stitch elastic on |
| Applied elastic (zigzag, overlock or coverstitch) | Elastic stitched on the fabric | No casing; edge allowance 1 cm; elastic stretched 0.75 to 0.9 of its length | Quarter-mark both; stretch elastic evenly to fit; stitch |
| Elastic in a binding | A bound edge with elastic | Binding strip + elastic | Swimsuit leg openings |
| Elastic edge, lingerie (picot, plush) | Decorated stretch edge | 0 to 0.6 cm allowance | Stitch with 3-step zigzag |

Elastic length (ratio of finished elastic to the opening), indicative:

| Use | Elastic type | Cut length = body / opening measure x |
|---|---|---|
| Waistband, woven, wide (4 cm) | Woven or knit elastic, little stretch | 0.85 to 0.9 of the waist measure (plus 2.5 cm overlap) |
| Waistband, narrow braided (1 to 2 cm) | Braided elastic | 0.8 to 0.9 |
| Cuffs, wrists, ankles | Braided | wrist measure + 1.5 cm for pulling over the hand; or 0.9 of the circumference |
| Swim leg and armhole | Swim / clear elastic | 0.8 to 0.9 of the opening; 0.9 to 0.95 waist |
| Lingerie picot / plush | Narrow soft elastic | 0.8 to 0.9 |
| Clear elastic (stabiliser in seams) | Clear polyurethane | 0.75 to 0.9 of the seam |
| Fabric gathered by elastic | | Fabric 1.5 to 2 times the elastic |
| Shirring elastic thread | 1 mm round | 1 : 2 to 1 : 3 |

Add a 1.5 to 2 cm overlap when joining a loop of elastic. Elastic is quartered and matched to the
notches at CF, CB and sides.

### 4.5 Drawstrings and boning

| Technique | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drawstring in casing | Cord drawn through a tunnel with two exits | Casing width = cord width + 6 mm; casing allowance as 4.4; buttonhole or eyelet openings 1 to 2 cm at CF, reinforced | Make openings before folding the casing, sew, thread through |
| Drawstring at waist (trousers) | Cord exits at CF | Two buttonholes 1.5 cm long, 3 cm apart; interface | |
| Drawstring at hem (anorak) | Tunnel with toggle | Hem allowance 3 cm + 1 cm | |
| External ties | Ties sewn in | See ties in section 5 | |
| Boning, channels | Rigid vertical strips in seams | Channel width = bone width + 3 mm; bone length = seam length minus 1.5 cm at the top and bottom (minus 2 cm at a waist seam); allowances 1.5 to 2 cm on the seam with the bone | Stitch channel tape or the seam allowances; insert; close the ends |
| Boning, tape | Boning in a ready tape, 6 to 12 mm | Stitch the tape over the seam allowance | Spiral steel for curves, flat steel for the straight |
| Plastic boning | Light boning in collars and bustiers | Channel as for steel; cut 1 cm shorter than the channel, round the ends | Insert in channels; avoid for heavy corsetry, it takes a set with body heat |
| Busk or bones at CF (corsets) | Rigid front closure or a pair of flat steel bones each side of CF | CF cut with 2 cm allowance, or split for a busk (loops and studs side), facing strip | Busk: insert loops between CF and facing, punch studs through the other side; or two flat bones in channels either side of the eyelets |

Bone placement: princess seams, side seams, CF, CB (at the zip), and darts; never at the neckline edge
(leave 1 to 1.5 cm). Corsets need a waist tape and a busk. Support is checked at the toile stage.

### 4.6 Interfacing and its positions

| Type | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Woven fusible (cotton, polyester) | Light to heavy | Collars, cuffs, waistbands, facings: stable but follows the grain |
| Knit fusible (tricot) | Light | Stretch fabrics, jackets, knits; keeps give |
| Non-woven fusible | Light to heavy | General purpose, cheap, can bubble |
| Weft-insertion fusible | Light | Wovens, silky fabrics, shirts and blouses |
| Sew-in interfacing | Light to heavy (hair canvas, horsehair, muslin, hymo) | Tailoring, wool, silk, couture |
| Hair canvas / tailor canvas | Medium | Jacket fronts, lapels, chest piece; permanent shape |
| Horsehair canvas | Heavy | Shoulders, hems, roll lines |
| Fusible stay tape (1 cm) | Narrow | Roll lines, seam edges, shoulders |
| Interfacing with elastic | Narrow | Stretch fabrics |

Where it goes:

| Place | Type | Cut |
|---|---|---|
| Collar, upper collar and collar stand | Woven fusible medium, or sew-in | Pattern minus 3 mm at the seam allowance edges |
| Cuffs | Woven fusible, medium | Pattern minus 3 mm |
| Front bands, plackets | Woven or weft fusible | Pattern |
| Neckline and armhole facings | Light weft or tricot | Pattern |
| Waistband | Woven fusible, 3 to 4 cm wide strips or waistband interfacing | Width of the band |
| Hems of jackets and coats | Bias strip, 4 to 6 cm | Along the hem fold |
| Jacket front | Hair canvas | Cut from the front pattern, shoulder to hem; chest piece on top |
| Zip openings | Fusible tape 1 to 2 cm | Strip along the seam allowance |
| Pocket openings, welts, flaps | Fusible, light | Pattern |
| Buttonhole areas | Extra patch | 4 to 5 cm wide |
| Shoulder seams, necklines | Stay tape | On the stitch line |
| Pleat folds and roll lines | Tape | |

Interfacing is cut from the same pattern as the garment; omit 3 mm at the outer cut edges or trim the
allowance afterwards to reduce bulk. Fuse with a press cloth, steam, hold 10 to 15 seconds without sliding,
let it cool before moving. Grain of interfacing follows the grain of the piece unless a stretch is needed.

## 5. Closures

### 5.1 Zips

| Type | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps and rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invisible (concealed) zip | A seam without visible zip, pull at the top | Seam allowance 1.5 cm minimum (2 cm for heavy fabric); zip stops 1 to 1.5 cm under the waist seam; opening length = zip length minus 1 cm | Install before stitching the lower seam; press tape flat; stitch with an invisible zip foot; close the seam below the zip stop; zip tape and fabric should have notches at the zip end |
| Centred zip | Zip under a seam; two rows of topstitching at equal distance | 1.5 to 2 cm; opening notches | Baste the seam closed, press open, stitch the zip behind the seam, topstitch 6 to 8 mm each side |
| Lapped zip | One side overlaps the other; one row of topstitching visible | 2 cm on both sides (1.5 cm is a minimum); lapping side 1.5 cm | Stitch the zip to one side, fold the other over, topstitch at 1 cm; stop at the zip end with a bar tack |
| Fly zip (trouser fly) | J-shaped topstitching curve on the front of trousers | Left front extension (fly facing) 3.5 to 4 cm wide beyond the centre front; right front: fly shield, a separate piece 4 x 18 cm, cut double; zip 11 to 15 cm (women), 15 to 18 cm (men); crotch curve notched | Join the front crotch seam below the fly; attach zip to the facing; add the shield; topstitch the J curve through the left front, facing and zip; the fly lap goes on the right-hand side of the wearer for women's wear (the left over right is conventional for men; verify per pattern) |
| Separating zip | Two-way opening for jackets | 1.5 cm; facing on each front edge; zip teeth set flush with the edge | Sew each tape to the garment edge, turn the facing over the tape, topstitch if needed |
| Exposed zip | The zip teeth are visible as a decorative feature | A slit with no seam; seam allowance 1.5 cm; a facing behind the zip tape | Stitch the zip on the right side with a topstitch or a welt, finishing the ends with a bartack |
| Zip in a placket | Zip hidden under a front band (anoraks, polos) | Placket width = zip tape width + 1 cm each side; separate band or grown-on extension | Sew the zip to the band edges, then the band to the opening; topstitch |

Zip length: the opening must be long enough to put the garment on: skirt 18 to 22 cm; dress back from the
neck to below the hip (waist to hip is 20 cm for a fitted dress), trousers fly 11 to 18 cm; neckline zip
(roll over the head) 18 to 25 cm; sleeves 8 to 10 cm. A zip is always longer than the opening by the zip
stop (1 cm top, 1 cm bottom). Zip end tapes should extend into the waistband or facing allowance.

### 5.2 Buttons and buttonholes

Buttonhole length = button diameter + button thickness (+ 2 to 3 mm for thick or domed buttons, + 3 mm for
bound buttonholes, which shorten when the facing is finished). Test on a scrap with the real button.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Extension (wrap, overlap) | Extension beyond the centre front = button diameter, minimum 1.5 cm (varies: some sources use button diameter minus 3 mm for the distance of the buttonhole end from the edge; 1.6 cm from the centre front to the edge is common for buttons up to 22 mm). Women's wear: the right front laps over the left; men's: left over right |
| Button position | On the centre front line (button centre), buttonhole opposite |
| Horizontal buttonhole | Starts 3 mm beyond the centre front toward the edge; its end sits on the button when the garment is closed. Use where the strain is across: waistbands, cuffs, coat fronts, tailored garments |
| Vertical buttonhole | On the centre front, centred on the line; for narrow bands (up to 3 cm), shirt plackets, and light strain |
| First button | Half to one button diameter below the neckline seam; for a collar stand, centred on the stand |
| Last button | At least 2 button diameters from the hem; on a long shirt, 8 to 10 cm above the hem |
| Spacing | Equal spacing between the first and last: (distance) / (n - 1). Blouse 7 to 9 cm, shirt 8 to 10 cm, coat 10 to 13 cm, small buttons on cuffs 6 to 7 cm. Always add a button at the bust point and at the waist for fitted garments to avoid gaping |
| Dress or coat with large buttons | Spacing = 3 to 4 times the diameter |
| Strain points | Bust, waist, hip: place a button on each |
| Types of buttonholes | Straight machine (shirts, blouses), keyhole (jackets, thick fabrics: the round eye holds the shank), bound (welted: tailoring, coats), hand-worked (couture, wool), thread bar loop (for delicate fabrics), rouleau loop (bridal) |
| Pattern needs | Drill holes or notches at both ends of each buttonhole; interfacing in the placket; marks on the pattern |
| Shank vs flat button | Flat button on a thread shank (the shank height equals fabric thickness); thick fabric needs a shank |
| Buttons on the fly | Fly-front shirts: the concealed placket has buttons and buttonholes inside; the face shows no buttons |

Bound buttonhole: organza or fabric patch 5 cm wide and buttonhole length + 4 cm; lips 5 to 6 mm each, a
total opening of 1 cm; facing slit. Patch cut in the fabric of the garment; interface.

## 6. Pockets

| Pocket | Recognise | Pattern needs | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patch pocket | Pocket sewn flat on the surface | Rectangle or shape; top folded over 3 to 4 cm, sides and bottom allowance 1 to 1.5 cm; for a hand 14 to 16 cm wide, 15 to 18 cm deep; notches or drill holes at 4 corners | Hem the top, fold the other three sides, press (with a template), topstitch to the garment. Reinforce top corners with a bar tack or a triangle |
| In-seam pocket | Opening in the side seam; invisible from the front | Two bag pieces (shape 30 to 35 cm deep, 15 to 18 cm wide); opening 15 to 18 cm; 1.5 cm seam allowance; notches at the top and bottom of the opening on the garment and bag | Sew the bag to the garment at the seam allowance, join the side seam around the bag, leave the opening unstitched; stay tape 1 cm on the opening |
| Slant pocket (front hip pocket, trousers) | Diagonal opening at the hip | Opening 15 to 18 cm; angle 20 to 30 degrees from vertical; 5 to 6 cm at the top; pocket facing (piece) and bag (2 pieces, with the pocket facing cut from the garment fabric) | Facing + bag: sew the bag to the facing, attach the facing to the opening, stay with tape, understitch, close the bag |
| Jeans (scoop) pocket | Curved opening at the front hip, topstitched; pocket facing visible | Scoop 9 to 11 cm wide x 6 to 8 cm deep on the front; facing (yoke) cut in denim, bag in pocketing, 2 pieces; coin pocket on the facing | Sew bag to the scoop, understitch, topstitch twice; stitch facing to the second bag, close the bag, baste to waist and side |
| Welt pocket (single welt) | One welt visible, 1 to 1.5 cm high | Welt piece 2.5 cm x (length + 3 cm) double; opening 12 to 16 cm; bag pieces; interface the garment; drill holes at opening ends | Mark the opening, stitch the welt and the pocket with 6 mm lines, cut open, turn, sew the bag |
| Jetted (double welt) pocket | Two narrow lips (5 to 6 mm each), meeting in the middle; a slit | Opening 10 to 12 cm (breast), 15 to 17 cm (hip); two lips each 2.5 to 3 cm wide, cut on the grain; bag 2 pieces 18 to 20 cm; organza/ interface; notches at the ends | Mark, make two lips, stitch, cut, turn, fix the bag. For a flap: flap on top |
| Flap pocket | Flap over a jetted or welt opening | Flap width = opening length + 0 to 3 mm; flap 4 to 5 cm tall at the centre; 1 cm allowance; interface; flap cut 2 (upper and lower) | The flap is sewn into the top of the opening; the bag is attached below |
| Kangaroo pocket | Big patch on the front with two side openings | Width 30 to 38 cm, depth 20 cm, side openings 14 to 16 cm slanted; seam allowance 1 cm; top opening binding | Hem the side openings, stitch the other edges to the garment |
| Zipped pocket | Zip set in a slit, welt each side | Opening = zip length (12 to 18 cm); lips 6 mm; bag 2 pieces | Zip welts as double welt, stitch zip to the lips, attach bag |
| Hip yoke pocket | Opening along a yoke seam at the hip | Yoke seam with the opening left unstitched 14 to 16 cm; bag cut to the yoke shape, facing in self fabric | As the in-seam pocket, the bag caught in the yoke seam |
| Coin pocket | Small patch pocket on the jeans pocket facing | About 7 x 7 cm finished; top hem 2 cm | Hem the top, fold the sides, topstitch to the facing before the facing is sewn to the bag |
| Inside pocket | Jetted or welt pocket in the lining or front facing of a jacket | Opening 13 to 15 cm, 2 to 3 cm from the facing seam; lips in lining fabric; bag 2 pieces | As the jetted pocket, worked through lining and a lining-weight stay |

Pocket rules: position the pocket relative to the body (top of a hip pocket 8 to 10 cm below the waist; a
breast pocket on jackets is 10 to 12 cm long, tilted 5 to 10 degrees; patch pockets sit at the chest or
hip). Pocket bags are cut in the lining or in a pocketing fabric (cotton twill, poplin) to avoid bulk;
the bag edges are finished by French seams or double stitching to catch crumbs. Use grain parallel
to the garment on patches.

## 7. Linings, underlining, interlining and pressing

| Layer | Recognise | Pattern needs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lining (jacket, coat, skirt) | Smooth fabric inside, covers seams | Cut from the garment pattern; remove facings width from the front edge; CB pleat 1.5 to 2 cm (fold) for ease; add 1 cm at the shoulder and sleeve length 1 cm; hem 1.5 to 2.5 cm shorter than the garment (jump hem fold) | The lining is sewn to the front facing and hung free at the hem. Ease in the sleeve cap smaller |
| Bagged lining (fully lined) | Lining sewn to the garment, turned through a hole in the sleeve | Lining same as above; leave an opening 20 cm in the sleeve lining | Jackets, coats |
| Skirt lining | Slip inside the skirt | Same pattern, 3 to 4 cm shorter, no darts or smaller | |
| Half lining | Only the back and sleeve | Back and sleeves, front facing finished | |
| Underlining (backing, mounting) | Extra fabric cut and basted to each piece, seams treated as one layer | Cut as the shell; same allowance; basted along all edges | Gives body or hides seams on sheer fabrics; silk organza, cotton batiste, lawn |
| Interlining (insulation) | Batting, flannel, fleece between shell and lining | Cut the lining pattern; seam allowance trimmed 1.5 cm to avoid bulk | Winter coats |
| Interfacing | See 4.6 | Fused or sewn on the facing, collar, cuffs, plackets, pocket openings | Choose by weight: lighter than the shell |
| Stay tape | Narrow tape fused or stitched along a seam line | 6 to 12 mm wide, on bias edges, necklines, shoulders, pocket openings, zip seams | Fuse or stitch inside the seam allowance so it stops stretching |

Pressing:

| Fabric | Iron temperature | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Silk, viscose, acetate | 110 to 130 C (low) | Press cloth, no steam on delicate silk |
| Polyester, nylon | 110 to 150 C | Cloth; shiny marks |
| Wool | 148 to 160 C | Damp cloth, steam; no shine |
| Cotton | 150 to 200 C | Steam |
| Linen | 200 C | Moist |
| Velvet | Needleboard or face down on a self-fabric, low | Do not press on the pile |
| Knit | 110 to 150 C | Press without stretching |

Pressing rules: press each seam as sewn (flat first, then open or to one side) before crossing it with
another; use a ham for curves and a sleeve board for narrow tubes; press from the wrong side with a cloth;
lift the iron, do not slide on bias; shape collars, lapels and darts over a form; let the piece cool
before moving it.

## 8. Sewing order

### 8.1 Skirt (straight with zip and waistband)

1. Fuse the interfacing (waistband, zip area, hem).
2. Finish the edges of the pieces (overlock).
3. Sew the darts, press toward the centre.
4. Sew the back seam, leave the zip opening; insert the zip (invisible, centred or lapped).
5. Add pockets in side seams, if any.
6. Sew the side seams; press open.
7. Prepare the waistband; attach to the skirt waist (right side up, with the zip tapes folded in).
8. Finish the waistband: turn, stitch in the ditch or slipstitch.
9. Add the hook and eye.
10. Hem (blind hem); press.

### 8.2 Trousers

1. Fuse the interfacing (waistband, fly, pocket openings).
2. Finish all edges.
3. Darts or pleats on the fronts and backs.
4. Front pockets: slant pocket facing and bag.
5. Back pockets: welt or patch.
6. Fly: front crotch seam, fly zip, shield, J-stitch.
7. Inseam and side seams (leave top 12 cm of the side seam open if the pocket is set in the seam).
8. Join the legs along the crotch (back crotch from the fly stop to the waist).
9. Waistband: attach, belt loops (before the waistband is turned), buttonhole, button.
10. Hem; press.

### 8.3 Shirt

1. Fuse the interfacing (collar, stand, cuffs, front plackets).
2. Pockets (patch pocket) on the left front.
3. Front plackets or bands.
4. Yoke: attach the back to the yoke, then fronts to the yoke (burrito method).
5. Collar: sew and turn the collar; attach to the neckband; neckband to the neck.
6. Sleeve plackets (slit, tower), sleeve seams open; set sleeves flat into the armholes.
7. Side seams and sleeve underarm seams in one pass (flat-felled or overlocked).
8. Cuffs, attach to sleeves.
9. Hem the shirt tail.
10. Buttonholes and buttons; press.

### 8.4 Dress (fitted, lined, with a zip)

1. Fuse the interfacing (facings, zip areas).
2. Darts, princess seams.
3. Shoulder seams.
4. Neckline facing (or lining); armhole facings.
5. Side seams, leaving the zip opening; insert the zip.
6. Waist seam, if any (stay tape on the waistline).
7. Sleeves: ease, set into armholes.
8. Lining: assemble and attach.
9. Hem; press.

### 8.5 Jacket (lined, tailored)

1. Cut the canvas and interfacing; fuse or pad stitch.
2. Make the darts and the front seams; add pockets (welt, flap).
3. Sew the back seams, the side seams, and the shoulder seams.
4. Make the collar and lapel; attach.
5. Make the sleeves and set them into the armholes; shoulder pads.
6. Attach the facings; turn.
7. Make the lining; attach to the facings and hem.
8. Hem the sleeves and the jacket.
9. Buttonholes, buttons.
10. Press.
