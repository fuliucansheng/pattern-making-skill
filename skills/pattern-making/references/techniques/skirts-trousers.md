# Skirts and trousers: every construction

Technique sheet. Basic blocks are in `blocks.md`, dart pivot / slash-and-spread / circle radius in
`transformations.md`, ease in `ease.md`, finishes in `finishes.md`. This sheet gives the full drafting
logic, cut counts, sewing order, fit faults and CLO3D notes for each skirt and trouser construction.

Notation: W waist, H hip (full circumference), L finished length, a ease, SA seam allowance, CF/CB center
front/back, d dart or pleat depth, n count. Metric, half-body unless said otherwise.

Reliability of numbers: values marked (checked) were cross-checked against published drafts read online
for this sheet (see Sources). All others are typical industry/textbook ranges (Aldrich, Armstrong,
Müller & Sohn, Esmod, Bunka differ by 0.5 to 1 cm on many of them). Treat a range as a starting point,
confirm on a toile, and never quote one number as "the" standard.

---

## 0. Conventions used below

- Grain: lengthwise grain parallel to CF/CB for skirts and to the crease line for trousers.
- Cut counts: "x2" = two pieces (a mirrored pair), "x1 on fold" = one piece cut on a fold. Lining and
  interfacing are listed separately.
- Overlap direction: women's garments close left over right as worn, men's right over left. Fly flap side
  varies by brand. Match the reference garment, never assume.
- Walking minimum: hem sweep (full circumference) of at least 85 to 90 cm for a skirt that is not
  flared, otherwise add a vent, slit or kick pleat.
- Hip level: 18 to 22 cm below the waist for most adult women (use the measured value).

# PART A. SKIRTS

## A1. Straight / pencil skirt

- What: fitted skirt, hem width equal to or slightly smaller than hip, darts or seams for waist shaping.
- Recognize: parallel or slightly tapered side seams, 2 front + 2 back darts (or 1+1), CB zip, back vent
  or slit.
- Pieces: front x1 on fold, back x2 (CB seam for zip and vent), waistband x1 (or facing x2 front/back
  pairs), interfacing for band or facing. Optional lining same count.
- Draft (see `blocks.md` for the base): hip width (H + 4 to 6) / 4 per quarter, front +0.5 and back -0.5
  at the side seam. The waist-hip difference per quarter is shared between the side seam (about 1 to
  1.5 cm taken in, 3 cm at most) and the darts (1.5 to 3 cm each). Dart lengths: front 9 to 10 cm, back
  12 to 14 cm. Waist curve: side raised 0.7 to 1.2 cm, CB lowered 0.5 to 1 cm.
- Pencil taper: at the hem take in 2 to 3 cm per side seam quarter (hem circumference = H - 6 to H - 12),
  with a straight line from the hip level to the hem, or from the knee level for a narrower pencil.
  If the resulting full hem sweep is under 85 to 90 cm, a vent or slit is required.
- Hem length: a hem allowance of 3 to 4 cm (straight), 1 to 1.5 cm if the hem is curved/narrow.
- Sewing order: darts, CB seam from zip end to hem (leave vent open), zip, side seams, waistband or
  facing, lining, hem, buttons or hook-and-bar at the band end.
- Finishes: overlocked seams, pressed open; blind hem or catch-stitched hem; hook and bar at band end.
- CLO3D: sew line to line, keep darts as sewn darts (do not close in 2D), particle distance 5 to 10 mm,
  use woven fabric with a weight of 150 to 300 g/m2, tight skirts may look wrinkled at the thighs if the
  seat ease is under 4 cm in the avatar's pose.

### Back vent (single, at CB)

- Recognize: rectangular overlap at the CB hem, top layer visible, slit line rising from the hem.
- Draft: at CB, mark the vent length V (typical 15 to 25 cm from hem; minimum 15 cm for a skirt with
  a hem width of 90 cm) and vent depth (extension) of 3.5 to 4.5 cm. Add the extension on one back piece
  (the overlap) and a 1.5 to 2 cm SA on the other (underlap).
- Overlap width equals the extension + SA. The vent is cut as a continuation of the CB seam below the
  zip stop; the overlap top is mitred.
- Pieces: back x2 (one with extension, one plain); the extension is part of the piece, not separate.
- Sew: CB seam to the vent top, reinforce vent top with a bar tack or a square of stitching, press the
  vent flap, hem, miter the corner. Lining: leave an open "box" of 2 to 3 cm so lining does not drag.
- Fit fault: vent gapes or flips open: extension too narrow or the CB seam is not straight. Fix by
  widening the extension to 4 to 4.5 cm and stabilising with interfacing.

### Kick pleat (inverted pleat insertion at CB)

- What: a sewn-in pleat piece that keeps a narrow skirt closed yet walkable.
- Draft: at the CB hem width, add a separate "kick" rectangle of width 12 to 20 cm (finished 6 to 10 cm
  per side when folded) and length equal to the vent length + SA. The skirt back is then cut with a
  slit. Alternative: extend the back panel by pleat depth d (typically 8 to 10 cm) at CB from a point 15
  to 25 cm above the hem, cutting an inverted pleat and sewing the CB seam to the pleat's top.
- Pieces: back x2 plus an underlay (kick) piece x1 (cut self or lining fabric).
- Sew: seam the skirt back to the kick piece and press pleat folds; edge-stitch the pleat top as a
  reinforced inverted "V" or bar tack.
- Recognize on a photo: a short hidden underlay visible when the skirt moves, usually a straight back
  hem with an inverted pleat at CB.

### Slit (side or front)

- Same rule as a vent: a slit is an unlined opening on a seam. Length 15 to 35 cm; maximum for
  decorum is around mid-thigh unless the user wants high slits. Reinforce the top with a bar tack and
  interface the seam allowances behind the slit.
- Slit overlap allowance: 1.5 to 2.5 cm on each edge for a double-turned hem.

Fit faults (straight skirt):

| Fault | Cause | Pattern fix |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal wrinkles across the hip | Hip too tight or hip line too high | Add 1 to 2 cm total ease at the side seams; verify hip line depth against H measurement |
| Skirt rides up at the front or hem lifts | Front too short or seat too wide | Add 1 cm at the front waist and raise the hem; reduce back width at the hip |
| Smile wrinkles under the seat | Back too short at the seat or back too tight | Lengthen the back at the hem and CB; add 0.5 to 1 cm at the hip |
| Side seams swing forward or back | Side seam placed wrongly | Shift side seam so that front : back are (H/4 + 0.5) : (H/4 - 0.5) and re-balance by the dart |
| Gap at CB waist | CB too long or lacking sway | Lower CB by 1 cm and enlarge the CB dart or CB curve |
| Skirt twists | Unequal side seam lengths or grain off | Check pattern side-seam length equality and grain placement |

## A2. A-line and flared skirts

- What: skirt widening to the hem with a straight or slightly curved side seam.
- Recognize: no waist-to-hem parallelism, hem much wider than hip; sides straight from the hip down.
- Pieces: front x1 on fold, back x2 (or x1 on fold with CB seam omitted), waistband x1.
- Draft (A-line from the block): from the hip point of the side seam, draw the hem edge outward by
  (flare amount per side) = 2 to 6 cm at the hem per quarter for a light A-line, 8 to 15 cm for a
  pronounced A-line. Keep the front and back side seams equal in length.
- Draft by slash-and-spread (flared): close the waist darts (or part of them) by pivoting, cut slash lines
  from the hem to the waist points where the darts were, spread each slash by the dart value plus the
  desired flare (3 to 10 cm per slash at the hem), redraw the hem as a smooth curve (correct the pointed
  hem corners with a perpendicular at the side seam). Closed dart values are retained as waist-to-hip
  shaping.
- Hem levelling: flared skirts cut on straight grain still drop on bias areas. Cut 1 to 3 cm extra at
  the bias points, hang for 24 hours, then re-mark the hem level.
- Sewing order, finishes: same as A1, but narrow baby hem (1 to 1.5 cm) on curved hems.
- CLO3D: the outline of the hem must equal the flat pattern; check the hem curve, because a sharp corner
  at the side seam will simulate as a point.

Fit faults: rippling at the hem = hem curve length unequal across front and back; side seam swinging =
bias on the side seam (re-grain so that grain is centered on the panel).

## A3. Gored skirts (4, 6, 8, 12 panels)

- What: skirt made of n vertical panels joined with seams, each panel tapering from hip to a wider hem,
  shaped for a smooth flare.
- Recognize: vertical seams from waist to hem, equal-width panels, often with a panel grain along the
  panel's center line, a seamline on CF and CB.

Panel counts and cut counts:

| Gores | Pieces | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | front x2, back x2 (one panel pattern cut x4) | Seams at CF, CB, both side seams; CF seam commonly on grain |
| 6 | One panel pattern cut x6 | CF and CB each have a seam only if the gore is centered on them; otherwise seams at CF/CB are absent and the panels are mirror pairs |
| 8 | One panel cut x8 | Hip width per panel = (H + a) / 8 |
| 12 | One panel cut x12 | Narrow panels, useful for bias or tight hip, fine control |

- Draft (equal gores): waist width per panel = (W + a_w) / n, where a_w is 1 to 2 cm. Hip width per panel =
  (H + a_h) / n, with a_h of 4 to 6 cm. Hip level at the same depth as the block. At the hem, add the
  flare per panel F_p = (hem circumference total - H) / n, with typical hem circumferences of 1.5 to 3 ×
  H for a flared gored skirt.
- Draft step by step:
  1. Draw the grain line vertically (this is the panel center).
  2. Measure waist half-width, hip half-width, hem half-width symmetrically about the grain line.
  3. Join the waist to hip with a straight line (or slight curve), then hip to hem with a straight line
     (A-line gore) or a curve (godet-like flared gore).
  4. The waist-hip angle gives built-in shaping; no darts are needed if n >= 6.
- Bias warning: a panel with side seams cut at an angle has one edge on bias. Seams on a bias edge
  stretch; stay-tape bias seams or use the grain aligned with the panel's center.
- Sew: seams pressed open, apply stay tape at the waist, zip at CB or side, hem last.
- Fit fault: gores pop out at the hip = hip curve too sharp. Add a gentle curve to the panel from the waist to
  the hip, not a straight line.

### Godets

- What: triangular or sector pieces inserted into a slit or seam to add sweep at the hem. A godet is
  added at a seam end (seam godet) or in a slit (slit godet).
- Recognize: V or sector wedge visible at the hem, with a visible join at the top apex.
- Draft: godet height = slit height G (typical 15 to 40 cm). Godet angle at the hem 45 to 90 degrees (60
  commonly). Width at hem = G × θ (θ in radians; 60 degrees = 1.047 rad -> 1.05 G). Draw a sector with
  radius G and the chosen angle; the godet edges are straight and equal in length (G + SA). If the godet
  hem is curved, use arc length = G × θ.
- Pieces: skirt panels cut with a slit; godet x1 per slit (cut x2 for a pair of slits).
- Sew: apex reinforcement is mandatory. Pin godet to the slit starting at the apex (pivot the skirt
  slit so the corner is straight), stitch each side from the apex outward, backstitch at the apex, stay
  the apex with a small square of fusible interfacing on the wrong side.
- CLO3D: the apex is a sharp point; sew with the apex at the correct pivot or the mesh will pinch.

## A4. Circle skirts (full, three-quarter, half, quarter)

- What: skirt cut as part of an annulus. The waist edge is an arc of radius r, the hem an arc of radius
  R = r + L.
- Recognize: no side seams visible except one or two, waist is smooth with no gathers, hem is
  a wave pattern with deep folds, bias drops visible.

Radius formulas (waist circumference C = W + 1 to 2 cm, arc length of the waist edge):

| Type | Fraction of circle | Waist radius r | Angle of sector |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full | 1 | r = C / (2π) = C / 6.283 | 360 degrees |
| Three-quarter | 3/4 | r = C / (1.5π) = C / 4.712 | 270 degrees |
| Half | 1/2 | r = C / π = C / 3.142 | 180 degrees |
| Quarter | 1/4 | r = 2C / π = C / 1.571 | 90 degrees |

- General formula: r = C / θ, where θ is the sector angle in radians. Cut outer radius R = r + L + hem
  allowance (1 to 1.5 cm for a narrow hem). Seam allowance at the waist: r is the finished (sewn) waist
  radius, so the cut edge lies at r - SA, toward the center of the circle. Compute r from C first, then
  offset.
- Fabric width needed: full circle requires a square of side 2R (or two halves each of width R + SA
  across the fabric); half circle needs R across and 2R along the length.
- Pieces: full circle = 1 piece cut as 1 full on fold-fold (quarter pattern ×4 folded), or two halves
  (CF and CB seam, or side seams). Add waistband x1 and zip at CB or side.
- Grain: place the quarter's straight edge on the lengthwise grain so that the bias (45 degrees) appears at
  the center of the sector; seams on the grain edges are stable. A full circle always has four bias
  zones at 45 degrees; a half circle has two.
- Bias drop and hem levelling:
  - Bias zones stretch under their own weight by about 2 to 5 cm in 60 cm of length on typical
    drapey fabrics and less in stiff cloths.
  - Cut with extra length (1 to 3 cm at the bias zone is common) or hang the cut skirt for 24 hours
    (48 hours for viscose/crepe), then re-mark the hem level from the floor on a dress form.
  - Use a narrow rolled hem (3 to 6 mm), narrow machine hem, or bias binding. Wide turned hems
    cup and ripple.
  - Stay-tape the waist seam (1 cm tape, cut 0.5 to 1 cm shorter than the waist) to prevent the bias
    waist from stretching.
- Waist opening: the waist circumference must permit the hips to pass: unless there is an opening, C must
  exceed H or the skirt has a zip. Zip at CB or side (18 to 22 cm).
- Variants: double circle (two stacked circles), asymmetric hem (cut sectors with different R),
  circle with CF seam only.
- Sew: seam(s), waist stay tape, zip, waistband, hang, level, hem.
- Fit faults: dips at the hem = unleveled bias drop, fix by re-leveling after hanging; waist rippling =
  waist stretched, stay-tape; skirt sticks out like a lampshade = fabric too stiff for the radius, use
  a lighter fabric or a smaller fraction of the circle.
- CLO3D: use a particle distance of 5 mm for circle hems (rough meshes at 10 mm show polygonal
  edges), check the bias zones of the simulation, set the fabric stretch values (weft/warp bending) from a
  scanned or measured fabric, and let the avatar pose rest for 100 to 300 frames.

### Skater skirt

- What: short circle or 3/4-circle skirt, usually in stretch jersey, with elastic or fold-over waistband.
- Draft: as above with C reduced for knits: C = W × 0.90 to 0.95 for waist elastic, or C = W for a
  wider foldover band. L = 40 to 50 cm. Minimum hem allowance 1 cm, coverstitch or twin-needle hem.
- Pieces: skirt x1 (full) or x2 (halves), waistband x1 (cut with the stretch around, circumference
  W × 0.85 to 0.9).
- Fit fault: waist twists = waistband too long or grain off; hem curls = jersey edge, use a stable hem
  tape or a narrow hem.

## A5. Yoke skirts

- What: skirt with a shaped hip yoke, below which the skirt is gathered, pleated or flared.
- Recognize: a horizontal or curved seam about 8 to 14 cm below the waist; yoke is smooth, lower part
  has volume.
- Pieces: yoke front x1 on fold (or x2), yoke back x2, skirt front x1 on fold, skirt back x2, waistband
  or facing.
- Draft: on the block, rule the yoke line at the desired depth (typical 8 to 12 cm at the side seam, may
  be curved or angled). Close the waist darts: if the dart point lies above the yoke line, the dart is
  absorbed into the yoke seam (dart closed on the yoke piece, the lower piece has no dart). If the dart
  extends below the yoke line, close the dart to the yoke line only and move the remaining intake to the
  lower piece as ease, a pleat or flare.
- Trace the yoke (with darts closed by pivoting; the dart value transfers to the yoke seam as a spread)
  and the lower piece (with the added fullness: slash-and-spread by 1.5 to 3 times the width for
  gathers, or for flare see A2).
- Sew: stitch yoke seams (join right side to right side), gather or pleat the skirt piece to the
  yoke seam length, then press the seam toward the yoke, topstitch 2 to 6 mm.
- Fit fault: yoke gapes at the lower edge = the dart was not fully closed in the yoke; transfer more
  intake into the yoke seam.
- CLO3D: sew the yoke seam with a gather ratio set (see A7) rather than by shrinking the pattern.

## A6. Pleats (knife, box, inverted, sunray, accordion, kick)

Pleat vocabulary:

- Pleat depth d: distance from the visible fold to the underfold (the part hidden under the pleat).
- Fold line: crease line seen from the outside. Underfold line: crease on the inside where the fabric
  folds back.
- Take-up (consumption): extra fabric the pleat absorbs compared with the finished surface it spans.

Take-up maths (derived from the geometry of the fold; checked against several published pleat calculators):

| Pleat | Take-up | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Knife (side) | 2 d per pleat | Each fold stacks three layers over distance d; fabric for the pleat = finished step s + 2d; with the usual d = s, 3 s per pleat (3 × waist for fully lapped pleats). Calculators that use 2 × only apply to pleats that overlap by half. |
| Box | 4 d per pleat | Two outward folds, each taking 2d. With d = b/2 (face b, underlays meeting at the center), the pleat takes up 2 b extra, total 3 b per box. |
| Inverted | 4 d per pleat | A box pleat seen from the wrong side. Same maths. |
| Accordion | about 2 × per fold | Fully zig-zagged folds: fabric = 2 × finished width at the same pleat width. Typical ratio of flat width to finished 2.0 to 3.0. |
| Sunray (radial) | ratio about 2 to 3 at the waist | Radial folds on a circle sector. The fold depth grows from waist to hem, so the take-up is constant by ratio, not by distance. |
| Kick | 2 d + underlay | Single inverted pleat at CB, see A1. |

Skirt waist calculation (knife pleats), with n pleats:
- Finished waist (+ ease) F = W + a. Pleat step s = F / n (this is the width that the pleat spans on the waist).
- Choose depth d. d = s means pleats fully overlap each other. d < s leaves a gap.
- Pattern waist width (flat) = F + n × 2 d + (n + 1)... for seams, plus SA.
- Fabric hip width must also cover the hip: the pleat opens to hip width; stitch the pleat down to the hip
  level (typically 12 to 18 cm below the waist), then it falls free.

Box / inverted pleat skirt: total fabric width = F + n × 4 d + SA. With d = b / 2: width = F + 2 n b.

How to draft any pleat in a pattern:
1. Mark the pleat position on the pattern block (CF, center of the panel, or at the dart line).
2. Slash from the hem to the waist along the pleat line and spread by the pleat take-up (2 d for a knife, 4 d
   for a box).
3. Mark the fold lines (F), the underfold lines (U) and the stitching length (stitch-down line).
4. Fold direction: F to U in the direction of the arrow, usually the knife pleat folds toward the
   side seam at the front and toward CB at the back, so the pleats face away from the viewer.

Pleat shapes:
- Straight pleat: the same d from waist to hem.
- Tapered pleat: d decreases toward the hem (e.g., from 4 cm at the waist to 3 cm at the hem), used where
  the skirt is narrower at the hem than the hip.
- Stitched-down (edge-stitched) pleat: top of the pleat stitched to the hip line or yoke level, then
  free below.
- Pressed or pre-pleated: pleats set by heat (synthetic, polyester). Real accordion and sunray pleats
  are made by an industrial pleater with a paper template and only hold on thermoplastic fibres (polyester,
  nylon). Natural fibres require a hand steam press and will not hold wear.
- Marking: notch the F and U lines, thread-mark or chalk, baste the pleat, press from the wrong side.

Pleat in 2D CLO3D: simulate pleats with the folding arrangement tool (fold angle and fold strength) on
the pattern and sew the pattern as a flat piece with correct gather lengths. Set the pleat "fold
strength" to 0.5 to 1 for crisp, a higher bending value for stiff cloth, and use a 5 mm particle
distance so that fold edges are resolved.

Sunray: the cut sector uses a radial grid. Flat sector angle φ = ratio × C / r_w, where the ratio is the
take-up (2 to 3). Typically cut as a half or full circle, then pleated into the waist. For skirts finished
to length L, the hem edge length R × φ is 2 to 3 times the finished hem circumference. Fabric: thermoplastic
chiffon, georgette, or satin with heat setting.

Fit faults: pleats splay open at the hip = depth too small relative to hip, stitch down lower or add
depth; pleats flip at the back = fold direction opposed to the wearer's movement.

## A7. Tiered and gathered skirts

- What: horizontal tiers, each longer and fuller than the one above, joined by gathered seams; or a
  single gathered dirndl.
- Recognize: stacked flounces with a gathered seam, short top tier, widest bottom tier.
- Fullness ratios (flat width : width of the seam to which it is gathered):
  - Light cotton, voile, lawn: 1.5 to 2.0
  - Chiffon and silk georgette: 2.0 to 3.0
  - Medium-weight cloth (poplin, linen): 1.3 to 1.5
  - Heavy cloth (denim, wool): 1.2 to 1.3 (or use pleats)
- Draft (dirndl): rectangle of width = ratio × (W + a), length = L + hem + band seam allowance. Fabric
  width constraints: join several widths with side seams.
- Draft (tiered): top tier width T1 = H + a (no gathers) or ratio × the waist; tier 2 width T2 = ratio ×
  T1; tier 3 width T3 = ratio × T2. Tier heights: typically 30:35:35 percent of L, top tier shorter.
  Add SA on both long edges (1 cm for the gathered edge).
- Pieces: n tiers, each a rectangle x1 (or x2 if seamed at the sides), waistband x1.
- Sew: seam each tier closed, run two rows of gathering stitches (stitch length 4 to 5 mm, 6 mm
  and 1 cm from the edge), pull to match the tier above, pin evenly (quarter points), stitch and
  press the seam up. Overlock together.
- Fit fault: waist looks bulky = too much gathering at the waist, use a flat top tier or a yoke.
- CLO3D: use the sewing type with gather ratio for the gather seams (ratio of lengths of the two
  seam lines) and a fine particle distance (5 mm) on the gathered side.

## A8. Wrap skirts

- What: skirt with overlapping front panels closed by a tie, snap or button, without a separate zip.
- Recognize: diagonal front edge, overlap visible, tie at the waist.
- Pieces: front overlap x1 (the wrapping panel, cut on the full width), front underlap x1, back x1 on fold
  or x2, ties x2, waistband or facing.
- Draft: from the A-line (A2) or circle pattern, extend the front by the overlap amount: the overlap at
  the waist 15 to 25 cm (more at the hip), the opening line shaped as a smooth curve from waist to hem.
  The underlap piece must cover the overlap edge by at least 8 to 10 cm at the hip level so that it does
  not expose the leg when walking.
- Sew: finish the edges with a narrow hem or facing, attach waistband, make ties.
- Fit fault: opening gapes while walking = overlap too small or underlap too short; increase the overlap by
  5 cm.
- CLO3D: pin the overlap as two stacked pattern pieces, enable collision layers (set layer 1 on the
  underlap and 2 on the overlap).

## A9. Bubble and balloon skirts

- What: skirt with a puffed hem, formed by an outer shell pulled in at the hem and attached to an inner skirt.
- Recognize: rounded shape with a gathered hem band and visible pouf.
- Pieces: inner skirt (straight or tulip) x1; outer shell x2 (front and back) wider and longer than the
  inner; hem band or facing.
- Draft: inner skirt = pencil skirt with hem circumference = H or slightly less. Outer shell: waist the same as
  the inner; at the hip level width = inner × 1.15 to 1.4; at the hem, the outer is cut long by 2 × the
  puff drop p (typically 8 to 20 cm) so that the hem folds under the pouf. Outer length = L + 2 p (+ SA).
  The outer hem width equals the shell's hem circumference of 1.3 to 2 × inner hem width.
- Sew: the outer hem folds up and is gathered (or elasticated) onto the inner hem or the hem band; the puff
  is set by the folded-under allowance.
- Fit fault: pouf collapses = outer length too short or fabric too limp; add interfacing at the hem band.

## A10. Tulip skirts

- What: skirt whose hem is rounded under with overlapping curved petals.
- Recognize: curved front overlap, hem narrower than the hip with a "petal" overlap at the front.
- Pieces: front right petal x1, front left petal x1 (cut as mirror), back x2, waistband.
- Draft: from the pencil block, narrow the hem by 3 to 5 cm per side; draw a curved line from near CF
  at the waist to the hem; the petal overlaps CF by 6 to 10 cm at the hem. The two petals cross at CF.
  The back is a normal pencil skirt with a vent.
- Sew: stitch the curved edge with its facing, stack the petals, join at the side seam.
- Fit fault: petals gape = overlap not enough; add 1 to 3 cm at the CF overlap.

---

## A11. Trumpet and mermaid skirts

- What: fitted to the thigh or knee, flared below.
- Recognize: close fit to hip and thigh, sudden flare, usually panelled.
- Draft: from the pencil/straight block, choose the flare start (knee level, typically 25 to 30 cm below
  the hip line, or mid-thigh). Hip-to-knee line straight, knee width = knee circumference + 6 to 10 cm
  total (half-ease per quarter). From the flare start, slash-and-spread to the hem; total hem sweep
  2 × H up to 3 × H for a full mermaid. For smoother fall, divide into 6 to 8 gores (see A3) with the hip
  and knee widths from the formulae, and use godets or a flounce rather than a single spread.
- Pieces: 6 to 8 gores x1 each (cut n), waistband or facing.
- Sew: seams pressed, stay at the knee level, CB zip.
- Fit fault: restricts walking = knee ease too small; add 4 to 6 cm total at the knee or open a vent.

## A12. Waistbands and facings (skirts)

### Straight waistband

- Recognize: rectangular band at the waist.
- Cut: length = W + a (1 to 2 cm) + overlap (3 to 4 cm) + 2 × SA; width = 2 × finished width (3 to 4 cm
  women; up to 5 cm for high bands) + 2 × SA. Fold in half lengthwise; cut interfacing = finished width + SA.
- Pieces: waistband x1 (or x2 if split at CB), interfacing x1.
- Attach: right sides together, stitch the band to the skirt; press up, turn, stitch in the ditch from the right
  side or hand-sew the underside.

### Contour (shaped) waistband

- Recognize: curved band that follows the waist-hip curve; top edge shorter than lower edge.
- Draft: overlay the block with the waist at the top; trace the band from the waist line down the desired
  width (2.5 to 4 cm, up to 6 for a high waist). Close the darts first (or take the dart intake out of the
  band), then shape the band: the lower edge length = skirt waist length; the upper edge = waist + 0 to
  1 cm shorter. For a band that sits below the natural waist (hip-hugger), set the top at 3 to 5 cm below.
- Pieces: contour band x2 (front and back) or x4 (self + facing), interfacing x2.
- Fit fault: the band stands away at the top = lower edge too wide, curve the pattern more; the band cuts
  = top edge too tight, add 0.5 to 1 cm to the upper edge.

### Facing (shaped, no waistband)

- What: an inner lining of the waist, 5 to 7 cm wide, cut from the skirt pattern at the waist.
- Draft: trace the waist from the finished pattern down 5 to 7 cm; close darts first (or leave them and
  transfer to the facing as a dart or ease). Match the facing grain to the skirt grain. Add SA on all sides.
- Pieces: front facing x1 on fold (or x2 if CF seam), back facing x2, interfacing x1 set.
- Sew: join facing at the sides, finish the lower edge (overlock or turn and stitch), stitch facing to
  the waist right sides together, clip curves, understitch, turn, tack to the seams.
- Fit fault: facing flips out = understitch missing; the facing gapes at CB = facing is slightly longer
  than the skirt; match lengths.

## A14. Skirt lining

- What: inner skirt, same shape as the shell but with specific ease allowances.
- Pieces: lining front x1 on fold, lining back x2 (or x1 on fold with a CB pleat); no facing. Same
  grain and darts as the shell.
- Draft: copy the skirt; shorten the lining hem by 2 to 4 cm above the shell hem (so it never shows). If
  the hem sweep is large (flared skirt), the lining may be a straight A-line smaller than the shell.
  Add 1 cm ease at the CB (pleat of 2 to 2.5 cm folded and edge-stitched, called an ease pleat) and
  1 cm more circumference at the hip than the shell to relieve strain.
- Sew: lining assembled separately, open at the zip and vent; attach to the waist before the band is sewn;
  vent: lining open or finished with a small square; hem 1 cm double-turned.
- Fit fault: lining shows below the hem = lining too long; lining pulls the shell at the thigh = lining
  width too small at the hip, add ease.

# PART B. TROUSERS AND SHORTS

## B1. Classic trouser block (women, with measurements)

Reference measurements for an illustrative size (replace with measured values): waist W = 70, hip H = 96,
waist to hip = 20, body rise (seated depth from waist to seat) = 27.5 (including ease), waist to floor
(outer leg) = 103.

Definitions:
- Crotch line: horizontal line at the body rise below the waist. It is the position of the crotch point
  for both front and back.
- Hip line: horizontal at the waist-to-hip depth.
- Knee line: halfway between the crotch line and the hem line, then moved up 5 cm (checked). Many textbooks
  place the knee at half the crease length minus 5 cm; use the person's measured knee level if given.
- Crease line: the grain line, perpendicular to the waist and hem lines, centered on the leg.

Two published approaches are given below, so that an agent can compare them and see where they agree.
Both are normal; neither is "the" standard.

### Method A: grid draft with crotch extensions of H/16 and 1/8 H (checked against a published draft)

Front (half body, from point O at the front waist; steps from the published draft):

1. Crotch depth: O to A down = body rise (here 27.5), square across. Waist to floor: O to C down.
2. Hip line at the waist-to-hip depth, square across.
3. Front crotch reference E = A - (H/12 + 1.5) horizontally. The vertical through E is the front crotch
   (CF) line, carried up to the waist.
4. Front crotch point H = E - (H/16 + 0.5). For H = 96: extension = 6.5 cm.
5. Front hip width: from CF at the hip line, H/4 + 0.5 to the right (side hip point I).
6. Waist: 1 cm in from CF at the waist (point J) and then W/4 + 2.25 to the side (this includes a 2 cm
   dart, plus 0.25 ease).
7. Front dart 2 cm wide, 10 cm long, on the crease line.
8. Front crotch curve: from the waist at CF (1 cm in), to CF line, then a smooth curve to H. The deepest
   point of the curve sits about 2.75 to 3.5 cm from the diagonal at E (diagonal by waist size: 2.75 cm for
   W up to 66, 3 for 66 to 78, 3.25 for 78 to 91, 3.5 above).
9. Hem width: half the hem width - 0.5 each side of the crease line, with hem width = 0.125 W + 13 for a
   straight leg (W = 70 gives 21.75 flat per leg). Knee width = hem width + about 1.3 each side (1.5 for W 78 to
   91, 1.7 for 91 to 103, 2 above).
10. Side seam: from the waist through the hip I, the knee, to the hem, with a 0.5 cm curve at the hip. Inseam:
    from H through the knee to the hem, with a 0.75 cm inward curve at the thigh.

Back (overlaid on the front grid):

11. Back crotch reference Q: one quarter of E to A along the crotch line from E (2.4 cm for H = 96), so the
    back CB line starts further inside the front's grid.
12. Back hip width: H/4 + 1.5 from the CB line at the hip line.
13. Back crotch point: beyond the front crotch point by half of the front extension (3.25 cm for 6.5) plus
    0.5, giving a back extension of about 12 to 13 cm in total for H = 96. That is close to H/8.
14. CB inclination: from the CB hip point, rise to the waist 2 cm outward-inward shift and 2 cm raise
    (the CB line goes up 2 cm above the original waist and moves 2 cm in, relative to the vertical). That
    corresponds to a CB slope of about 6 to 8 degrees.
15. Back waist: W/4 + 4.25 (2 darts of 2 cm = 4 cm plus 0.25 ease). Darts at one third and two thirds of
    the waist, lengths 12 and 10 cm.
16. Back crotch curve depth: 4.25 (W up to 78), 4.5 (78 to 91) or 4.75 cm (over 91) from the diagonal.
17. Back leg: hem and knee widths = front + 1 cm each side (the back leg is wider than the front).
18. Hem: lowered 1 cm at the crease line on the back (a gentle curve), because the heel pushes the
    trouser back.

### Method B: Aldrich style (ratio of the hip, typical)

- Hip ease total 4 to 6 cm (small for stretch, up to 10 for wide trousers).
- Front crotch extension H/20, back crotch extension H/10 (typical; the range quoted in textbooks is
  H/20 to H/16 front, H/10 to H/8 back). Back extension is about twice the front.
- Back CB: raise 2 cm at the waist and shift 2 to 3.5 cm toward the side (the CB angle), adjusting for
  seat: more tilt for flat or round seat.
- Crease line: through the center of the width at the crotch line; knee and hem are symmetrical about it,
  front 1 cm narrower on each side than the back.
- Waist dart: front up to 2 cm, back up to 3 cm (one or two darts).
- Checks (both methods): the front and back side seams are equal; the back inseam is shorter than the front
  inseam by 0.5 to 1 cm only if the trouser has stretched the front; waist matches W + a.

Crotch length check (both methods): measure the front crotch curve (CF waist to the inseam point) and the back
crotch curve (CB waist to the inseam point). For an adult woman with H of 96, typical values are front 25
to 28 cm, back 32 to 36 cm, with a total of body crotch length + 2 to 4 cm ease. The back is longer than
the front by 6 to 10 cm.

Seam allowance and notches: SA 1.5 cm on the side and inseam, 1 cm at the crotch curve (or 1.5 with a
second seam finish), 2 cm at the hem. Notch the hip level, knee and crotch point and the
fly stop.

Pieces: front x2, back x2, waistband x1 (or contour x2), fly facing x1, fly shield x1, pocket bags x2 (pairs),
interfacing for waistband and fly.

Sewing order (standard): 1) darts and pockets, 2) fly, 3) front crotch seams (to the fly stop), 4) back
crotch seam, 5) side seams, 6) inseams, 7) waistband, 8) belt loops, 9) hem, 10) buttons, hook and bar.

Finishes: overlock the edges; press the crease with a damp cloth; hem 3 to 4 cm blind stitched or 2 cm
machine-stitched.

CLO3D: sew front to back at the side seams and the inseam, then the crotch seams; start with the avatar
in an A or T pose and let it relax; mesh size 5 to 10 mm; ensure the front/back crotch curves are
aligned so that the seam length ratio is correct (CLO will otherwise stretch or compress; match segment
lengths).

### Fit faults (trousers) and their pattern fixes

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Smile lines under the seat / drag lines to the crotch | Crotch length too short | Lower the crotch line by 0.5 to 1.5 cm (adds length on both curves) or lengthen the back crotch curve by lowering the back crotch point and increasing the extension |
| Whiskers at the front crotch (horizontal folds) | Thigh or hip too tight, front rise short | Add 1 to 2 cm to the thigh and hip width, or lengthen the front rise |
| Excess fabric under the seat (baggy seat) | Back crotch too deep or back extension too long | Shorten the back extension by 1 cm or raise the crotch line by 0.5 to 1 cm |
| Vertical fold at the front crotch (fish eye) | Front rise too long | Shorten the front rise at CF by 0.5 to 1 cm |
| Gap at CB waist | CB too straight for a hollow back | Increase the CB angle by 1 cm (shift in) and lower CB by 0.5 to 1 cm |
| Wrinkles from the crotch to the side hem (diagonal) | Hip level or crotch line too high | Lengthen the rise |
| Crease swings / hem twists | Crease not on grain | Re-draw the grain at the crease line |
| Back thigh wrinkles | Back thigh too tight | Add 1 cm to the back inseam and side seam at the thigh |
| Knees baggy | Knee too wide | Reduce the knee width by 0.5 to 1 cm each side |

### Pleated front

- Draft: replace the front dart with pleats. Slash the front pattern from waist to hem along the crease
  line (or from the center of the dart) and spread by the pleat intake: 3 to 4 cm for a single pleat, 2 × 2.5 cm
  for a double. Add 0.5 to 1 cm to the crotch width on the front to keep the pleat from pulling at the thigh.
- Pleat direction: forward pleats (toward the side seam) are the modern default; reverse pleats (toward CF)
  are traditional and flatter on the hip.
- Pleat depth: usually runs from the waist to the hip, stitched 8 to 12 cm down, then free.
- Pieces: front x2 (wider), back x2.
- Fit fault: pleats gape = intake too large for the hip; pleats pull = depth too small, add 0.5 to 1 cm.

### High-waist

- Draft: raise the waist line at the side and CF by 4 to 8 cm. Measure the body high-waist circumference
  at the new level (rib-waist measurement). The pattern width at the new waist line is the measured high
  waist + ease. To keep the hips right, continue the side seam up following the line of the waist-to-hip
  shape and add darts or pleats to take in the extra width at the top.
- The waistband (if separate): width 4 to 7 cm, contour recommended.
- Fit fault: the waist gapes at CB = CB raised too much; take out 1 cm at the CB and add a CB dart.

## B3. Jeans

- What: five-pocket trousers in denim, with yoke, flat-felled seams and topstitching.
- Recognize: curved back yoke, two back patch pockets, two front scoop pockets and a coin pocket, J-shaped
  topstitching at the fly, rivets, belt loops.

Pieces (men or women, five-pocket):

| Piece | Cut count |
|---|---|
| Front leg | x2 |
| Back leg | x2 |
| Back yoke | x2 |
| Back patch pocket | x2 |
| Front pocket bag (shell) | x2 |
| Front pocket facing (self) | x2 |
| Coin pocket | x1 |
| Fly facing (extension) | x1 |
| Fly shield | x1 |
| Waistband | x1 (or x2 with CB seam) |
| Belt loops | x5 to x7 |

- Block adaptation: use the trouser block with a hip ease of 3 to 5 cm for rigid denim and 0 to -3 cm for
  stretch denim (stretch of 15 to 30 percent). The leg is often tapered; the rise is chosen: low
  (front rise 20 to 23 cm), mid (24 to 26), high (27 to 31).
- Back yoke: close the back waist darts by pivoting them into the yoke seam. The yoke seam is a straight or
  curved line from the side seam to the CB, with the CB depth 6 to 8 cm and the side seam depth 3.5 to 5 cm.
  After pivoting, the dart intake becomes a small concave/convex shape at the yoke seam (typical
  intake 1.5 to 3 cm). The lower back piece (below the yoke) has no dart.
- Back patch pockets: 13 to 15 cm wide, 14 to 16 cm tall, upper edge straight or arched; top edge
  placed 5 to 8 cm below the yoke seam at the pocket center; the pocket is tilted slightly to follow the seat
  (CB side edge 5 to 6 cm from the CB seam at the top). Hem the pocket top with 1.5 to 2.5 cm, topstitch.
- Front scoop pocket: opening starts at the waist 6 to 8 cm from the side seam and ends on the side seam 14 to
  17 cm below the waist; the curve is a smooth scoop (depth 4 to 6 cm into the front). Pocket bag depth 20 to 25 cm.
  The facing is a piece of denim on the opening edge that is seen when worn.
- Coin pocket: 6 to 8 cm wide, 6 to 9 cm deep, placed on the right front, 1.5 to 2 cm below the waistband, 1 to
  2 cm from the opening. Reinforced with rivets.
- Fly with J-stitch: fly stitch line at 3.2 to 3.5 cm from CF on the fly facing, J-curve ends at fly
  length + 1 cm; the fly length (zip length + 1.5) is 14 to 16 cm for women, 16 to 18 for men. Fly extension
  3.5 to 4 cm. Fly shield width 6 to 8 cm.
- Flat-felled seams: SA 1.5 cm on the wide side and 1.0 to 1.5 cm on the narrow side; trim one allowance to
  3 to 4 mm; fold the wide one over it, edge-stitch; second row at 5 to 7 mm. A twin-needle or a
  two-step operation can be used.
- Belt loops: finished width 1.0 to 1.3 cm; cut 4 times the finished width (edges turned to the center);
  length = waistband height + 2 × 0.7 (tuck) + 0.6 ease. Placement: front pair at 3 to 5 cm from the
  front fly edge, side pair behind the side seam (1 to 2 cm), CB loop at the CB seam; 5 to 7 loops.
- Rivets and bar tacks: rivets at the pocket corners, bar tacks at the pocket corners, fly base, belt loop
  ends, and the crotch seam.

Typical jeans sewing order (industrial sequence):
1. Finish pocket bags; attach coin pocket and facings.
2. Attach front pocket bags to the front legs, topstitch the opening, close the bag.
3. Make the fly: assemble the zip, fly shield, J-stitch.
4. Seam the front rises up to the fly stitch.
5. Attach the back yoke to the back legs with a flat-felled seam; sew the back pockets.
6. Join the back rise with a flat-felled seam.
7. Close the outer side seams (flat-felled), then the inseams (flat-felled).
8. Sew belt loops, attach the waistband, finish ends.
9. Hem (single or double-turn, chain stitch for the vintage look), rivets, bar tacks, button.

Fit faults: back yoke gapes at CB (the dart intake lost) = the yoke seam is curved too steeply, bring the
CB down by 1 cm; "pockets pop" at the hip (front scoop opens) = hip too tight or pocket bag too narrow;
"honeycombs" are a wash and finishing effect, not a pattern defect.

CLO3D: use a denim fabric preset (150 to 400 g/m2 depending on weight), set the topstitching with the
Topstitch tool and the proper thickness, use the 3D pocket and flap as separate pieces; the pocket bag uses
a layer index lower than the front.

## B5. Leggings and stretch trousers

- What: close-fitting trousers in knit with negative ease.
- Recognize: no pockets or only slits, no fly, elastic or wide stretch waistband.
- Stretch classes: 2-way stretch (across only, 20 to 40 percent), 4-way stretch (40 to 100 percent).
- Negative ease (reduction) of the body measurement: 10 to 15 percent horizontal for 4-way stretch of 40 to 50
  percent; 15 to 25 percent for 70 to 100 percent stretch. Vertical reduction 0 to 3 percent (leave length).
  Test: stretch the fabric by 10 cm and measure; the fabric stretch percent should be at least 1.5 to 2 times
  the reduction used.
- Draft: use the trouser block with ease removed (hip width H/4 instead of H/4 + 1.5), crotch seam length
  as measured (leggings seats need the full seat length), apply the reduction horizontally to the width, then
  redraw the crotch curves in the scale so that they remain correct in length. Hem is the ankle opening
  + 0 to 2 cm.
- No-side-seam version: join the front and back pieces along the side seam (pivot or overlay at the hip
  and knee, the side seams are straight), giving a front-back wing; the leg is one piece with only an
  inseam, saving a visible seam. The crotch can be reinforced with a gusset (a diamond 12 to 15 cm long, 4 to
  6 cm wide).
- Pieces: leg x2 (front and back combined), crotch gusset x1 (optional), waistband x1 (elastic casing or
  fold-over).
- Waistband: fold-over cut = 2 × height (12 to 20 cm folded) + SA, length = waist × 0.85 to 0.90 (stretch);
  elastic width 2.5 to 5 cm, length = waist × 0.85 to 0.90.
- Sewing: ballpoint or stretch needle (size 75/11 or 80/12), stretch stitch or overlock (3 or 4 thread),
  coverstitch hem or twin needle, differential feed 0.8 to 1.0.
- Fit faults: sagging at the knee = vertical reduction too small; wrinkling at the crotch = gusset too
  small; transparent at the seat = ease too tight (fabric stretches, becoming sheer).
- CLO3D: use a high-stretch knit fabric preset with a lower stretch strength (stiffness) value; in the 3D
  window, shrink the pattern pieces horizontally (shrinkage 0.9 weft) to simulate negative ease, mesh 5 mm.

## B6. Jumpsuits and boiler suits

- What: bodice and trousers in one garment.
- Recognize: waist seam with or without belt, one-piece from shoulder to hem.
- Torso length addition: the vertical distance on the body from the nape over the shoulder to the crotch and back
  to the nape is the jumpsuit girth. The block pattern length from the shoulder at the neck to the crotch
  (front) and from the neck at the back to the crotch must equal this measurement plus a total of 4 to 6 cm
  ease (2.5 to 3 cm at each of the front and back at the waist seam; add 2 cm extra if the wearer sits a lot).
- Draft: place the bodice block and the trouser block with the waist lines level. Add the torso addition to
  the waist (raise it by 1.5 to 3 cm at CF and CB on the bodice or the trouser, usually the trouser
  top). The waist circumference can be above W (blouson) by 4 to 8 cm to allow bending. Add a
  waist casing, belt, or elastic when the garment is bloused.
- Opening: the garment must be able to pass over the hips and shoulders. Options: full-length CF or CB zip
  (zip length 50 to 65 cm), side zip plus a back zip, front button placket or wrap. If the waist circumference
  is less than the hip circumference + 4, an opening must extend below the hip.
- Pieces: bodice front/back, trouser front/back, waistband or casing, sleeves, facings or lining.
- Fit faults: pulling at the shoulders when sitting = torso too short, add 2 cm at the waist; crotch cuts
  = crotch curve short.
- CLO3D: sew the bodice to the trouser at the waist seam; simulate the pose (sit) to test the torso
  length.

## B7. Elastic waists

- What: pull-on trousers with an elastic or drawstring waist.
- Recognize: gathered waist, no zip or fly, casing at the top.
- Draft: cut the trouser with the top waist width equal to the hip circumference + 2 to 6 (so the garment goes on
  over the hips without opening), with no darts and a straight line from the hip to the waist. The waist is
  gathered by the elastic to the body waist.
- Casing: height = elastic width + 0.6 to 1 cm (ease) + the fold allowance; for a fold-over casing, height =
  2 × (elastic width + 0.3) + SA. For a 2.5 cm elastic: casing = 6.5 to 7.5 cm cut, 3.1 to 3.3 cm finished.
- Elastic length: waist circumference × 0.85 to 0.92 (comfortable), minus 2 to 6 cm in total.
- Sewing: sew the casing, leave a 4 to 5 cm gap, thread the elastic, overlap and stitch the ends, close
  the gap. Stitch through the elastic at the side seams with a zigzag to stop twisting.
- Drawstring: add two buttonholes or eyelets at the CF, with a cord 1.5 × the waist.
- Fit faults: the waist rolls over = elastic too narrow; the garment does not stay up = elastic too long.
- CLO3D: simulate the elastic by assigning the waist seam a shrinkage or by using a separate elastic strip
  with a stretch value; use a gather ratio on the casing seam.

## B8. Pockets for trousers

### Slant pocket (front hip pocket)

- Recognize: diagonal opening from the waist to the side seam on the front.
- Draft: opening starts at the waist 5 to 7 cm from the side seam and ends on the side seam 15 to 19 cm
  below the waist (the opening length 16 to 20 cm; for men 18 to 22). The line is straight or slightly
  curved.
- Pieces: pocket bag (facing side, self) x2, pocket bag (back side, lining) x2, interfacing at the
  opening, stay tape.
- Bag shape: depth 25 to 30 cm from the waist, width reaching to the center front (or pocket stay); the
  bag facing is the part of the bag visible through the opening (self fabric).
- Sew: stitch the bag facing along the opening edge right sides together, understitch, turn, topstitch (optional),
  place the back bag, stitch the bag edges (French seam or overlocked), attach the bag to the waist and
  side seam.
- Fit faults: opening gapes = opening cut off-grain; stretch it by stay-taping with a 1 cm tape.

### On-seam (side seam) pocket

- Recognize: opening in the side seam.
- Draft: opening 15 to 18 cm long, beginning 5 to 7 cm below the waist. Pocket bags are pieces sewn to the
  front and back along the seam allowance (extensions).
- Pieces: pocket bag x4 (two each side) or the bag made as an extension of the leg.
- Sew: sew the bag to the seam allowances, close the bag, seam above and below the opening.
- Fit fault: the pocket pulls = pocket not extended enough to the front; reduce by 1 cm.

### Welt / jetted (double-welt) back pocket

- Recognize: slim slit pocket(s) with a strip on top (welts) and a flap or button.
- Draft: opening 12 to 15 cm (women) or 14 to 16 cm (men), position 8 to 10 cm below the waist on the
  back, centered on the dart. The welt (jet) finished width 0.6 to 1.2 cm (each jet 0.5 cm).
- Pieces: jets x2 per pocket, pocket bag x2 (front and rear), facing, interfacing patch behind the
  opening. Flap x2 optional.
- Sew: mark the opening on the interfaced garment, sew the jet rectangles to the opening (two parallel
  lines 1 to 1.2 cm apart, ending in a square), slash and turn, stitch the bag, finish the corners with
  triangles.
- Fit faults: ends flare or pucker = slash not cut precisely to the corners; the jet gapes = opening too
  tight on the grain.

### Back patch pocket

- See B3 for dimensions. Finish top with a hem 2 to 3 cm and topstitch, attach with 2 rows of topstitching
  (6 mm and 1 cm).

## B9. Zip fly front

- What: front closure with a zip, hidden behind a flap, used in trousers, jeans and shorts.
- Recognize: curved J topstitching on one front and a flat piece (fly shield) inside.
- Pieces:

| Piece | Cut count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Right front (with the zip) | x1 | Straight CF edge |
| Left front (with the fly extension) | x1 | Extension 3 to 4 cm beyond CF at the waist, ends at the fly stop |
| Fly facing (extension piece) | x1 | Width 4 to 5 cm (finished 3.5 to 4), shaped like a J at the base |
| Fly shield (guard) | x1 | 6 to 8 cm wide, length = zip length + 2.5 to 3, rounded base |
| Zip | x1 | 14 to 18 cm for trousers (YKK type 3 or 4) |

- Draft: add the fly extension to the left front CF (width 3.5 to 4 cm from CF to the J-stitch line, plus SA).
  Mark the fly stop at the zip length + 1 to 1.5 cm below the waist. Trace the facing as a J: the straight
  edge on the CF and the curved lower end. The shield is a rectangle with a rounded lower end.
- Sew order:
  1. Fuse the interfacing to the fly facing and the extension.
  2. Finish the facing edge. Attach the zip to the fly shield (zip tape to the shield edge).
  3. Sew the left front (right side up) to the right front along the front crotch seam, starting at the
     fly stop, ending at the inseam point.
  4. Sew the zip to the right front CF edge, then to the facing.
  5. Topstitch the J on the left front with the shield held out of the way.
  6. Bar tack at the bottom of the J through all layers to anchor the shield.
- Finishes: bar tack 1.5 cm (zigzag width 1.7 mm, length 0.25 mm), tack the shield to the crotch seam.
- Fit faults: fly gapes = the extension too narrow or the crotch curve too long at CF; fly bulges =
  facing and shield too stiff, trim.
- CLO3D: the Zipper tool can be used, but for most simulations a stitched placket and a button is
  enough; the J topstitch can be created with the topstitch tool on the two fronts.

## B10. Waistband with extension, belt loops, cuffs

### Waistband with extension

- Cut length = W + 1 (ease) + extension 3 to 4 cm (the overlap at the center front) + 2 × SA; width =
  2 × finished (3 to 4.5 cm) + 2 × SA. The band is stiffened with interfacing or a non-roll waistband
  stiffener.
- For a contoured waistband, see A12.
- Attach: sew the band to the trouser waist with the extension aligned to the fly extension; fold, then
  edge-stitch the other side from the right side; close the ends with 1.5 cm SA; add hook and bar or button.
- Fit fault: the band stands away from the body at CB = the band is too straight for a hollow back; use
  a contour band or take a dart.

### Belt loops

See B3 for dimensions. For trousers: width 1 to 1.3 cm, number 5 or 7, attach before the band is completed,
with one end sewn to the trouser waist (below the band), fold up, and the other tucked into the band
fold and topstitched.

### Cuffs / turn-ups

- What: hem allowance folded to the outside, forming a cuff.
- Recognize: cuff band at the hem, pressed and tacked.
- Cuff depth c: 3.5 to 4.5 cm (4 cm classic).
- Allowance: below the finished-length line (the cuff bottom at the floor), cut 2 × c plus 1 to 1.5 cm for the
  turn-in of the raw edge. The derivation: the lower c of fabric plus the hem layer c folded back inside, and
  the flipped unit shows its right side. Check on a toile.
- Hem width: widen the hem allowance by about 0.3 × c per side (the turned-up part lies around the
  thicker leg), mitre the corner of the allowance at the seam.
- Sewing: hem is hand blind-stitched or machined, the cuff is pressed, and the cuff is tacked at the
  seams with 2 to 3 stitches (catch-stitch) so it does not fall.
- Fit fault: cuff flips down = tack missing or hem allowance too short.

## B11. Trouser lining and facing

- Half-lining (knee lining): lining from the waist to the knee, a straight-cut piece with an 8 to 10 cm
  dart ease. Pieces: front x2, back x2, cut with 1 cm ease at the hip.
- Full lining: copy the leg with reduced width (1 cm narrower at the knee) and a hem 4 to 6 cm above the
  trouser hem.
- Waist facing: 4 to 5 cm wide, same as A12.

## B12. Summary of CLO3D guidelines (skirts and trousers)

| Aspect | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sewing | Pieces sewn line to line; match segment length ratio between the sewn lines |
| Mesh | Particle distance 5 mm for gathers, pleats and narrow pieces; 10 mm for large flat panels |
| Fabric | Choose a preset close to the real cloth; set weight (g/m2), thickness, bending stiffness, stretch; for knits add stretch |
| Pleats | Folding arrangement with fold angle and strength; sew only the stitched-down region |
| Gathers | Use gather ratio in the sewing; avoid extreme ratios above 3 |
| Pockets | Separate pieces on lower layers, topstitch with the topstitch tool |
| Zippers | Zipper tool, or leave decorative |
| Poses | Simulate in A pose or relaxed pose; for sitting tests use a seated avatar pose |
| Layers | Wrap overlap and fly extension need layer indices (underlap lower than overlap) |
