# Tailoring, coats and outerwear

Reference for jackets, coats and outerwear drafted from the bodice block. Metric. Dimensions are in cm
unless stated. Half-pattern, full round or flat is stated each time.

Reliability: the geometry and the sequences below are standard trade practice (Aldrich, Armstrong,
Müller & Sohn, Esmod, Bunka, Cabrera). Numbers are typical working ranges, not laws: the books differ
by a few millimetres depending on size chart and block. Every range is meant to be confirmed on a toile
in the real fabric. When two ranges are given, the first is for fine or fitted work, the second for
loose or heavy work. Where a figure comes from a single survey or a single maker, it is said so.

Relation to the other sheets: block ease is in `ease.md`, block list in `blocks.md`, finishing in
`finishes.md`, CLO3D conventions in `clo3d.md`. Hoodies and sweatshirts are also covered by the knits
sheet. This sheet does not repeat them.

## 1. Principles shared by all garments in this sheet

### 1.1 Why the ease is larger: layers add girth

A layer of thickness `t` wrapped around a torso adds about `2 x pi x t = 6.3 x t` to the girth. Use it to
check any ease choice instead of guessing.

| Layer under the garment | Thickness t | Girth added |
|---|---|---|
| Shirt or fine knit | 0.2 to 0.4 | 1.3 to 2.5 |
| Thin jacket or waistcoat plus shirt | 0.8 to 1.0 | 5 to 6.5 |
| Tailored jacket plus shirt | 1.0 to 1.5 | 6.3 to 9.5 |
| Chunky knit | 0.8 to 1.5 | 5 to 9.5 |
| Down or wadding layer (the garment itself, loft) | 2 to 5 | 12.5 to 31 |

Same reasoning at the bicep: a coat sleeve over a jacket sleeve needs about `6.3 x 0.8` = 5 cm more
bicep girth than the jacket sleeve.

### 1.3 From the bodice block to a jacket or coat block

Start from the block with its normal wearing ease (`ease.md`: bust 8 women, chest 10 men). Let
`E_target` be the value of 1.2 and `A = E_target - E_block` the ease to add, full round. The block
quarters are two half-backs and two half-fronts, so each of the four pieces receives `A / 4` in width.

Placement of the `A / 4` of each piece (typical split, one third at the armhole/shoulder zone, two
thirds at the side seam):

| Piece | At the back width / chest width line (one third) | At the side seam (two thirds) |
|---|---|---|
| Half back | A/12 (0.5 for A = 6) | A/6 (1.0 for A = 6) |
| Half front | A/12 (0.5 for A = 6) | A/6 (1.0 for A = 6) |

Worked example, women, bust 92, jacket, `E_target` = 12: `A = 4`, each piece receives 1.0: 0.3 on the
back and chest width lines, 0.7 at the side seam. Men, chest 100, jacket, `E_target` = 16: `A = 6`, each
piece receives 1.5: 0.5 and 1.0.

Adjustments to the block by garment (half pattern, cm, all measured from the original block points):

| Adjustment | Jacket | Overcoat / trench / parka | Puffer, cocoon | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lower the underarm point | 1.0 to 1.5 | 2.5 to 4.0 | 4 to 7 | the side seam also moves out by the same amount |
| Shoulder end: raise | thickness of the pad (0.5 for a 5 mm pad) | pad thickness + 0.5 | 0.5 to 1 | note it on the piece |
| Shoulder end: widen | 0.5 | 1.0 to 1.5 | 2 to 8 (drop shoulder) | cocoon and drop shoulder: see 12.5 |
| Back neck point: raise | 0.5 | 0.5 to 1.0 | 1 | collar sits higher; follows the raised shoulder |
| Neck width (both back and front): widen | 0.5 | 0.5 to 1.0 | 1 | needed for the collar and layers |
| Front neck point: lower | 0 | 0 to 0.5 | 0 | keep the neckline closed unless the design opens it |
| Centre back waist: reduce | 1.0 | 0 to 1.0 | 0 | smooths to nothing at the underarm level |
| Dart point: drop | 2.5 | 2.5 to 4 | n/a | follows the lowered underarm |
| Hem length | design | design | design | see 1.5 |

The sleeve is redrafted after these changes (section 4). Never keep the old sleeve cap with a new
armhole.

### 1.4 Shoulder and back neck

- Back shoulder is longer than the front shoulder and eased into it: 1.0 to 1.5 for a jacket, up to
  2.0 in firm wool tailoring; 0.5 to 1.0 for coats and non-structured garments (`ease.md`, back shoulder).
- Shoulder slope: the pad raises the slope; keep the pad on the toile.
- Back neck: the jacket neckline is wider and the CB neck point higher than the shirt block, so the
  collar stand sits clear of the shirt collar. With the pad the shoulder line must stay straight
  or slightly concave from neck to end.

### 1.5 Lengths (from the nape, back, finished garment)

| Garment | Length from nape | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Waist-length bomber, cropped jacket | 50 to 62 | at or above the hip bone |
| Tailored jacket, men | about 0.41 x height, typically 72 to 78 | covers the seat; lower edge near the thumb knuckle with arm hanging |
| Tailored jacket, women | 58 to 70 | hip bone to top of the seat |
| Peacoat | 75 to 82 | upper thigh |
| Mid coat, parka | 85 to 100 | mid thigh |

| Maxi coat | 120 to 135 | mid calf |

### 1.6 Balance, grain, seam allowances

- Grain along the CB and along the front balance line (CF). Sleeves: grain on the centre line of the
  top sleeve, under sleeve parallel to the front seam.
- Seam allowances (typical, `finishes.md` for the hems): side and sleeve seams 1.5 in wool tailoring
  (so they can be let out), 1.0 elsewhere. Armhole, neck, shoulder 1.0. Hem 4 to 5 for jackets,
  5 to 7 for coats, 3 to 4 for sleeves of jackets, 5 to 6 for coats. Facing and lining edges 1.0.
- Notches: shoulder, front and back armhole balance, chest, waist, hem, pocket ends, button positions,
  roll line start, gorge, collar notch, CB, vent top.

## 2. Tailored jacket, men's and women's

### 2.1 What it is and how to recognise it on a photo

A structured, lined, usually single- or double-breasted jacket made from wool, linen or cotton
twill with a notch or peak lapel, a two-piece sleeve, a defined shoulder with pad and a sleeve head.
On a photo: lapel with a visible roll to the first or second button; a lapel and collar that do not
lie flat; a dart or side body panel; flap or welt pockets; sleeve buttons; a vent at the back.
Women's versions use darts or princess seams instead of the male front dart and side body, and may
button right over left. Single-breasted fronts: 1, 2 or 3 buttons. Double-breasted: 4 to 6 buttons in
two columns.

### 2.3 Drafting from the block

#### 2.3.1 Men's single-breasted jacket (reference size: chest 100, height 178, E_target 16)

Starting from the men's block adapted as in 1.3. Half pattern, cm.

1. Mark the waist line (WL) from the block (back nape to waist about 44 to 46).
2. Jacket length: nape to hem from 1.5 (about 74 to 76). Square the hem line across from the CB.
3. Hip line: 18 to 20 below WL. Seat line similar.
4. Waist suppression S (total, full round) from 8 to 14 for a fitted jacket, 6 to 8 for a straight
   one. Per half pattern, typical split (S/2 = 5): centre back 1.0 to 1.5, back panel at the side body
   seam 1.0 to 1.5, side body to the front panel 1.0 to 1.5, front dart or front side seam 1.5 to 2.5.
5. Back: draw CB from the nape with a gentle spine curve that is 1.5 to 2.0 in at the waist level and
   goes out at the hem by 1.5 to 2.0 (vent area). Shoulder: SP at the adapted point; back shoulder
   longer than the front by the ease in 1.4.
6. Side body (side back): from a point 1/3 down the back armhole (about 7 to 9 below the shoulder
   line, at least 6 above the armhole bottom) curving through the waist at 1/2 the back width, ending
   at the hem by flaring 1.5 to 2.5 each side. The seam is a continuous curve with no corner.
7. Front: the front dart (belly dart or fish dart) is placed at the pocket line, 1.2 to 2.0 wide,
   from the pocket height down 3 to 5 and up to a point 10 to 12 below the chest line; or replace with
   a front side seam. The side seam falls at the line where the front meets the side body.
8. Front edge: CF + wrap 2.5 for a 20 to 25 mm button (see 2.4 for double-breasted).
9. Bottom front edge: square, rounded (radius 4 to 6), or cutaway (angle from the last button).
   Rise the front hem by 0.5 to 1.0 to compensate for the belly drop (keep the hem line square on the
   body).
10. Draw the lapel and collar (section 2.5), the pockets (section 8) and the vent (2.7).
11. Finish with the sleeve (section 4) and check every seam length: the armhole of the jacket is
    measured on both parts and equals the armhole of the sleeve cap minus the cap ease.

#### 2.3.2 Women's jacket (reference size: bust 92, E_target 12)

1. Adapt the women's block as in 1.3. Move the shoulder dart into the armhole or the neck and
   close it. A tailored jacket has no shoulder dart and no armhole dart.
2. Waist suppression: waist dart or princess seams at the front, side back seam, CB shaped.
   Typical jacket waist: 4 to 8 cm of ease at the waist (bust 92, waist 74 gives a jacket waist of
   about 78 to 82 in the garment).
3. Princess line front: from the shoulder line at 1/3 of its length from the neck point
   (about 5.5 from the neck), through the bust point, to the waist then to the hem. Princess line at
   the side front from the armhole at one third of the armhole width (about 5 to 7 in).
4. Back: a CB seam (shaped 1.0 to 1.5) and a side back seam from the armhole, 1/3 down the back
   armhole at about 7 to 9, like the men's side body.
5. Button stance, front edge, lapel, pockets and sleeve as for men's with the differences in 2.4.

### 2.4 Button stance and front edge

| Item | Men, single-breasted | Women, single-breasted | Double-breasted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front button diameter | 22 to 25 mm | 18 to 22 mm | 22 to 25 mm |
| Front edge (wrap) from CF | 2.5 | 2.0 to 2.5 | 7.5 to 9 (see below) |
| 1 button | at the waist, +0 to 1.5 | at the waist | not used |
| 2 buttons | top at waist, second 9 to 10 lower | top at waist +1 | 2 to 3 pairs |
| 3 buttons | top at 8.5 to 9.5 above waist, middle at the waist | same | |
| Buttonholes | right front (men), left (women) horizontal, vertical in line with CF | | |
| Rolling point of the lapel | top working button | top working button | usually the top pair |

For a double-breasted jacket, the buttons sit in two columns 10 to 11 apart (each 5 to 5.5 from CF)
and the front edge goes 2.5 to 3 beyond the outer column: 7.5 to 8.5 from CF. A 6-button (2 to close)
has pairs at 10 to 12 spacing, a 4-button (1 to close) 12 to 14.

The stance has to be checked in the toile: the break point is at the top working button, and the
lapel roll line passes through it.

### 2.5 Lapel and collar reference (notch collar)

Draft on the front pattern after the front edge is set.

1. Break point B: on the front edge (CF + wrap) at the top button position (usually the waist or just
   above it).
2. Roll line: from B to R, a point on the extended shoulder line beyond the neck point NP, at distance
   `r = collar stand height`, typically 2.5 to 3.0 (Aldrich uses the stand height). The roll line is a
   straight line; its length is the roll line length L.
3. Lapel width w: from the roll line, perpendicular, to the lapel edge: notch lapel 7.5 to 9.5 (men),
   6.5 to 9 (women), peak 8 to 10. Draw the lapel edge parallel to the roll line, then shape it (belly
   0.5 to 1.5 convex for a soft lapel, straight for a sharp one).
4. Gorge seam: from G on the neckline to the lapel edge. Gorge height (vertical distance from the
   shoulder line to the gorge end on the lapel edge): 8 to 10 (low gorge fashionable 9 to 10, high gorge
   classic 7 to 8). The gorge line is at 90 to 100 degrees to the roll line in a normal notch collar.
5. Collar notch: cut out of the lapel edge and the collar a notch 3.0 to 3.5 deep and 3.0 to 4.0 wide
   (the notch angle is about 80 to 100 degrees in classic forms, wider in 70s styles).
6. Collar draft: reflect the roll line, set the collar stand and fall:

   | Part | Men | Women | Coat |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Stand (at CB) | 2.5 to 3.0 | 2.2 to 2.8 | 3.0 to 4.0 |
   | Fall (at CB) | 3.5 to 4.5 | 3.2 to 4.0 | 4.5 to 6 |
   | Collar neck edge length | = neckline from G to CB, measured on the garment | | |
   | Roll line (collar) | equal to L plus the neck curve | | |

   The collar neck edge is curved concave (raised at the CB by the stand) so that it fits the
   neckline when it stands up; the outer edge is longer than the neck edge by the amount that
   makes the fall turn over, which comes from the curve (the more the neck edge is curved, the more
   the collar rolls and the closer it hugs the neck). Make a sample in the real fabric.
7. Fall-off (turn of cloth): the undercollar is cut smaller than the top collar by 0.3 to 0.5 at the
   outer edge and around the points, so that the seam rolls under. The front facing is cut 0.3 to
   0.5 larger than the front on the lapel edge for the same reason. In thick coat fabrics: 0.5 to 0.8.
8. Undercollar: split at CB, bias cut, in felt (melton) or hair canvas; stretch the roll with the iron
   over a rounded form (see 2.8).

### 2.6 Peak lapel

Same as 2.5 up to step 3. The peak lapel has no notch: the lapel edge extends upward beyond the gorge
by 3 to 5 as a pointed projection, upturned at 20 to 30 degrees from horizontal, and the collar
(top collar) end is angled to meet it. Lapel width: 8 to 10. Gorge height is higher (6 to 8 for a
classic peak).

### 2.7 Vents and back hem

| Style | Overlap | Length from hem | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single centre vent | 3.5 to 4.5 (extra beyond CB on the right back) | 24 to 30 men, 18 to 25 women | right piece is the overlap, usually left over right for men |
| Double (side) vents | 3.5 to 4.0 at each side body seam | 20 to 25 | the front is cut with the side seam straight; the back with extra at the side body |
| No vent (ventless) | none | | CB seam closed; check seat ease, need the 4 to 6 extra |

The back panel of a vent is a rectangle of the overlap on one back piece (the "under" part with a
mitred corner) and a facing 4 to 5 wide on the other.

### 2.8 Sewing order, tailored jacket (hand-finished fully lined)

1. Cut shell, lining and interlinings. Mark all notches and construction lines with chalk or thread.
2. Fuse hem strips, back stay, pocket areas, facing (if fused).
3. Sew darts / side front seams; press open or to the CF, slash the dart and press open.
4. Make the pockets (section 8) on the front.
5. Make the chest piece (if canvas): pad stitch the lapel and the chest piece on the front canvas, tape the
   roll line and front edge (section 5).
6. Attach the canvas to the front (fell stitch or machine along seams), add edge tape.
7. Sew the side body seams and CB seam. Press, shrink the waist and the seam at the back armhole.
8. Shoulder seam: ease the back to the front (1 to 1.5 ease), press.
9. Make the undercollar (stretch the roll, hand finish) and the top collar.
10. Join the facing to the front, stitch the front edge, turn, press with the roll (the lapel must curve).
11. Attach the collar to the neckline: undercollar to the body, top collar to the facing, close the gorge
    with the hand.
12. Prepare the sleeves: sew front and hind seams, ease the cap by hand.
13. Set the sleeves: baste, check the hang on a form or the wearer, sew, add the sleeve head.
14. Insert the shoulder pads, fix to the armhole seam with a thread chain.
15. Make the lining (body, sleeve) and bag it (machine to the facing, lining to the hem with a jump pleat).
16. Finish the hem and the vent by hand, buttonholes and buttons.
17. Final press using a pressing ham, pressing cloth, tailor's board.

### 2.9 Common faults and fixes (jackets)

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Collar stands away from the neck | collar neckline too long, too straight | raise the curve (roll) of the neck edge; shorten the undercollar neckline by stretching the fall |
| Collar breaks too low at the back | stand too small or fall too deep | raise the stand 0.3, reduce the fall |
| Gaping lapel | gorge too long, roll line too loose, canvas not shrunk | tape the roll line 0.8 to 1.0 shorter; ease the lapel edge; shrink the front |
| Drag lines at the front armhole | pitch, round shoulder, too tight chest | forward pitch the sleeve, release the front shoulder |
| Drag lines from the back neck | back neck too tight, shoulder slope not right | lengthen the back neck, add the shoulder slope for the pad |
| Front flares at the hem | too much suppression at the waist, front balance | deepen the front dart, lengthen the CF, reshape the hem curve |
| Hem rises at the front | belly, missing front balance | add length at the CF (0.5 to 1.5) and curve |
| Cuffs twist, sleeve swings | wrong pitch or grain | rotate the sleeve cap 1 cm forward, check grain on the centre line |
| Pocket sags or gapes | bag too heavy or hanging from the opening | stay the opening with tape, balance the weight, shorten the bag |
| Pad ridge at the shoulder | pad too thick or too short | feather the pad edge, extend it into the sleeve head |
| Lining drags | lining cut without a pleat or jump | add CB pleat 1.5 to 2.0, add length 2 to 3 (section 7) |
| Cap puckers | too much cap ease | reduce ease to the limit in table 4.2 and shrink by steam |

## 4. Two-piece sleeve reference

### 4.1 What and when

Two seams (the forearm, or front, seam and the hindarm, or back, seam) divide the sleeve into top
sleeve (the larger) and under sleeve. This lets the sleeve curve at the elbow, so a tailored sleeve hangs
forward with the arm. Standard for tailored jackets and coats. A one-piece sleeve with an elbow dart is
cheaper but less refined.

### 4.2 Quantities that decide the sleeve

| Item | Jacket | Coat | Parka / puffer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bicep girth ease (above arm girth) | 8 to 12 men, 6 to 9 women | 14 to 20 | 16 to 24 |
| Cap height as a fraction of the armhole depth | 0.62 to 0.67 | 0.52 to 0.60 | 0.45 to 0.55 |
| Typical cap height | 14 to 16 men, 12.5 to 14.5 women | 12 to 14 | 11 to 13 |
| Cap ease (on armhole length) | 2.5 to 4.0 wool, 1.5 to 2.0 firm or synthetic | 3 to 5 | 1 to 3 |
| Cuff width, jacket | 14.5 to 15.5 flat (29 to 31 round) men; 12.5 to 14 women | 16 to 18 | with elastic or tab |
| Length (shoulder point to hem) | arm to wrist bone + 0 to 1, shows shirt 1.0 to 1.5 | arm + 2 to 3 | arm + 3 |
| Forward pitch of the cuff (hem line moved forward) | 1.5 to 2.5 | 2 to 3 | 2 |
| Hem allowance | 3.5 to 4.0 | 5 to 6 | 3 to 4 or a cuff |

Rule for the cap height: when the underarm is lowered by d, the armhole gets deeper and the cap height
drops. The cap length must equal the armhole length plus the ease in the table; the cap height may be
tuned by +/- 0.5 to reach that length.

### 4.3 Drafting a two-piece sleeve from the one-piece block

All values for a men's jacket sleeve with cuff 30 round. Women's: reduce by 10 to 15 percent.

1. Draw the sleeve block adapted as in 4.2 (cap height, bicep line, elbow line, cuff line, centre
   line from the cap top to the cuff).
2. Move the cuff forward by the pitch p (1.5 to 2.5): the hem line and the front seam bend forward so the
   sleeve curves.
3. Draw the front (forearm) seam: from the front underarm point F (on the bicep line) to a point on
   the cuff at 1/4 to 1/3 of the cuff width from the front edge. Between F and the elbow the seam is
   nearly straight, curving slightly in at the elbow line (0.5 to 1.0 curve).
4. Draw the hind (hindarm) seam: from a point on the cap at the back (at the back notch), through the
   back edge of the bicep line, to the cuff at the back; offset the seam 2.0 to 2.5 forward of the
   back edge of the block in the elbow area. The top sleeve overlaps it, so that the hind seam sits on the
   back of the arm and is hidden.
5. The under sleeve is the area between the front seam and the hind seam on the underarm side.
   Typical width: under sleeve about 28 to 33 percent of the bicep line width and 30 to 35 percent of
   the cuff width; top sleeve the remainder.
6. Separate the two pieces. Add 1.0 to 1.5 seam allowance.
7. Sleeve ease distribution: the top sleeve hind seam is longer than the under sleeve hind seam by
   1.0 to 1.5 and is eased into it in the elbow area. The front seams are the same length.
8. Notches: front and back balance notches, elbow, cuff, vent position (vent on the hind seam on the top
   sleeve overlap).
9. Sleeve vent: the cuff opening of 10 to 12 on the hind seam; if it works with buttons: 3 to 4, 1.0 to
   1.5 from the cuff edge, spaced 2.0 to 2.2 ("kissing" buttons touch, spaced at the button diameter).
10. Check: the hem width, the cap ease, the length of the seams, and the fit of the sleeve on the
    toile. The sleeve should hang 1 to 2 cm forward of the side seam, not twisted.

### 4.4 Sleeve head and ease treatment

Cap ease is eased in between the notches (not at the underarm), 70 to 80 percent of it between the
top of the cap and the notches. In wool it is shrunk with steam. In stable or synthetic fabrics, the
ease is kept at the lower value in 4.2. The sleeve head (bias strip of wadding or lambswool, 3 to 4
wide) supports the ease. Raglan and one-piece set-in sleeves for knits: 0 to 1 ease.

## 5. Canvas and interfacing plan

### 5.1 Three constructions

| Aspect | Full canvas | Half canvas | Fused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas | hair canvas floats from the shoulder to the hem | hair canvas from the shoulder to just below the chest, about to the pocket line | none (fusible interlining, 2 to 3 layers) |
| Chest piece | hair canvas plus felt, wool or domette, pad stitched | the same | absent or one layer of fusible |
| Lapel | pad stitched by hand, roll tape | pad stitched by hand or machine | fused, flatter roll |
| Below the canvas | free hem, hem canvas bias 4 to 6 deep | fused | fused |
| Weight and drape | best, ages well | good | lightest, stiffest, can bubble |
| Durability | highest | high | lower |
| Handwork | high (about 6 to 10 hours) | medium | low |

Fusing and canvas can be combined (called "floating canvas" when the canvas is only attached at the
seams and the stitched lines).

### 5.2 Layers in a classic men's chest (outside to inside)

1. Shell front.
2. Main canvas (hair canvas, cotton or linen warp with hair weft): shape = front, from the shoulder
   to waist or hem; cut on the straight with a dart cut and pushed together (overlapped and stitched
   with zig-zag).
3. Chest piece (hair canvas, 2 layers) sewn on the canvas, covering from the shoulder to the armhole
   bottom: 18 to 20 wide, 24 to 28 high; felt or wadding on top (1 layer, 3 to 5 mm).
4. Shoulder pad and sleeve head (section 6).
5. Interior: lining.

### 5.3 Tapes and stays

| Tape | Position | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roll line tape | on the lapel roll line | 0.8 to 1.0 shorter over 20 to 24 cm | straight cotton or linen tape 10 to 13 mm; makes the roll |
| Front edge tape | on the front edge and the lapel edge | 1.0 to 1.5 shorter in the lapel curve | prevents stretch; stitched on seam line |
| Armhole tape | on the front armhole seam | 0.5 to 1.0 shorter | holds the armhole against the stretch |
| Shoulder stay tape | along the shoulder seam | straight | stabilises |
| Neck tape | on the neckline of the undercollar | exact | |
| Back stay (back interfacing) | upper back from the armhole to 12 below the nape | cut on straight grain | |
| Hem canvas / fusing strips | hems 4 to 6 deep | bias cut | |
| Pocket opening stay | welt and flap | | 2 to 3 |

| Item | Specification |

| Soft pad (soft tailoring, unstructured) | 5 mm, thin, cut in the garment shape |

| Structured women's jacket | 10 to 15 mm |
| Coats | 12 to 20 mm |
| Extent | the pad exceeds the shoulder seam by 0.5 to 1.5 into the sleeve, and the neck side stops 1.5 to 2 before the neck point |

## 7. Linings and facings

### 7.1 Facing

| Item | Typical |
|---|---|
| Front facing width at the chest | 8 to 10 |
| Front facing width at the hem | 6.5 to 8 |
| Facing at the shoulder line | reaches 4 to 6 from the neck point |
| Facing inner edge | straight or slightly curved, passes behind the pocket and the breast welt |
| Back neck facing | 8 to 10 at CB, narrowing to 5 at the shoulder; may continue to a full back lining |
| Facing grain | along the front edge, same as the front |

The facing edge is placed 1.5 to 2.0 beyond the lapel roll in the lapel zone, so that the visible
edge of the facing is on the inside, not on the right side.

### 7.2 Lining derived from the shell

Lining is not a copy of the shell: it is cut roomier in set places, with pleats and folds.

1. Trace the shell pieces without the facing. Remove the seam allowance of the facing and add 1.0 at the
   facing edge.
2. Centre back: add a pleat of 1.5 to 2.0 depth (pleat value 3 to 4 at the fold) from the nape down to
   the waist (or to the hem), pressed to one side, held at the neck and waist with a catch stitch (the
   pleat lets the back expand when the arms reach forward).
3. Armhole and shoulder: lower the armhole by 0.5 to 1.0 and add 0.5 to 1.0 across the back, and lower the
   back neck by 0.3 to make a clean fit.
4. Width: lining fits roomier than the shell at the chest, waist and hip. A survey of menswear
   brands (Korean, see sources) reports extra space of the lining versus the shell pattern in suit
   jackets of 5.6 at the chest, 3.8 at the waist, 2.7 at the hem and bicep, and in casual jackets 2.4,
   1.3, 1.3 and 1.1; sleeve hem about 0.1. Take these as indicative orders of magnitude.
5. Length: the finished lining hem sits 1.0 to 2.0 above the shell hem edge (not below). This is
   done with a jump hem (jump pleat): cut the lining length = shell length - hem allowance - facing
   offset + 3 to 4 (a fold of 1.5 to 2.0 that opens when the wearer sits or bends).
6. Sleeve lining: cut like the shell sleeve, ease the cap 1 to 1.5 less than the shell cap, add 1 to 1.5
   at the elbow, and a jump hem of 2.0 at the cuff: lining sleeve length = shell sleeve length - hem
   depth + 3 to 4. The lining must never be tighter than the shell.
7. Pockets: the inside pockets are set into the lining front: breast (left), 14 to 15 wide with a
   double jet 1.0, positioned 8 to 10 below the armhole bottom line; a second inner pocket on the right
   or a smaller phone pocket optional.

### 7.3 Sewing the lining

Bagging: machine the body lining to the front facing and the back neck facing, join the sleeve lining
to the body lining, and set the lining to the jacket at the shoulder, hem and sleeve edges by hand or
machine ("bagging", "turning through the sleeve").

For coats the lining is hung from the facings with a CB pleat of 2.0 and a hem pleat; quilted linings,
interlinings and padding are cut from the same pattern as the lining, 1 smaller per seam (see 14.3 and 15).

## 8. Pocket constructions

Generic rule: an opening stay of fusible 2 to 3 wide on the front along the opening, pocket bags in
lining or cotton pocketing, the bag attached to the opening so that the weight hangs from the top
edge, never on the stitched corners.

### 8.1 Types and dimensions

| Type | Opening | Details | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jetted (double welt) | men's hip 15.5 to 17, breast 10 to 11.5, women 12.5 to 14 | two jets (each 0.5 to 0.6 finished, total 1.0 to 1.3), mouth closed between them | breast, trousers, inner |
| Flap (jetted with flap) | men 15.5 to 17, women 13 to 15 | flap 4.5 to 5.5 deep, 1.0 longer than... equal to the opening plus 0.3 per side | hip pocket |
| Ticket | as the flap, 1/3 narrower | smaller flap, 3.0 to 3.5 deep, above the right hip pocket | classic British |
| Welt (single welt, breast) | 10 to 11.5 | one wide welt 1.5 to 2.0 finished, bag behind, one jet | left chest |
| Patch | design | sewn on, flat with inset hem 3 to 4, corners rounded or pointed | casual, parka, denim |
| Slash / in-seam | 15 to 18 | in a seam, no stitching on the surface | coats |
| Besom | 14 to 17 | double welt without flap, can be zipped | outerwear |

Relative sizes: the jets and the flap are the same length; the flap completely covers the jets.

### 8.2 Positions (tailored jacket, to check on the toile)

- Hip pockets: placed 8 to 10 below the waist line, centred over the front dart or the centre of the
  front panel, with the front end at least 5 behind the front edge; the line of the opening parallel to
  the hem or tilted 1.0 forward as a visual correction. A hacking style is slanted by 2.5 (check the
  direction of the slant against the model).
- Breast welt (left): the mouth is about at the chest line (0 to 3 above), the inner end
  5 to 6 from the lapel roll and 7 to 9 in from the armhole; tilted so the outer end is 1 to 2 higher
  than the inner end.
- Ticket: 3 above the right hip pocket.
- Inside pockets: in the lining as 7.2.

### 8.3 Making a double-welt (jetted) pocket

1. Fuse the opening area on the wrong side (3 to 4 deep, the length of the opening plus 3).
2. Draw the pocket line (the opening) on the right side; draw the jet lines 0.5 to 0.6 each side of it.
3. Attach the jet pieces (jets of width 2.5 to 3, length opening + 3) with a rectangle of stitching
   exactly the opening length: top jet and bottom jet are stitched on the pocket line with a single
   straight line each side, 1.0 apart.
4. Cut between the two stitching lines, 1 cm from the ends to the corners, into a triangle with a
   clean point to the stitching.
5. Pull the jets and bag through to the wrong side, press, and join the ends by sewing the triangles
   (the "triangle") to the jets.
6. Finish the bag: sew the top bag to the top jet seam, the bottom bag to the bottom jet seam. Top
   bag is the facing piece in shell fabric (to avoid lining showing); bottom bag is pocketing.
7. Close the bag edges.

### 8.4 Flap pocket (with flap)

Flap: outer piece in shell, 5 deep, with the lower corners rounded (radius 1.0 to 1.5) or square;
under flap smaller by 0.3 so that it turns under; interface the outer. Flap is sewn into the top seam
of the pocket opening. The jets are made as 8.3 except that the top jet is replaced by the flap
seam. The flap needs to be 0.5 narrower than the pocket opening on each side in the canvas to ensure
it fits.

### 8.5 Patch pocket

Size (finished): hip 16 to 19 wide, 18 to 22 deep for a casual jacket; chest 10 to 12 by 12 to 14.
Corners rounded (radius 1.5 to 2.5) or pointed. Top hem 3 to 4 deep, stitched and bar-tacked at the
corners (a small rectangle or triangle for strength). Interface the top hem. Pocket placement is
marked with a notched template. In unlined patch pockets the edges are folded 1.0 and top-stitched
at 0.2 to 0.3 from the edge. Pleated patch pocket (box pleat 2 to 3) for utility jackets.

### 8.6 Ticket pocket

As the flap, but the flap is 3 to 3.5 deep and the opening is 14.5 to 16 on a jacket whose hip
pocket is 16.5 to 17.

### 8.7 In-seam, slash and zip pockets

Slash: the opening is cut in the seam; add 3 to 4 to the opening edge as a facing (cut on the front
piece or an extra). Zip pocket: a zip of 14 to 18 with the tape covered by 0.5 of the jet; stay the
opening against the stretch.

## 9. Buttonholes and button spacing

### 9.1 Dimensions

| Button | Diameter | Buttonhole length | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sleeve, jacket | 15 to 18 mm | diameter + 2 to 3 mm (17 to 21) | |
| Front, women | 18 to 22 mm | diameter + 3 mm (21 to 25) | |
| Front, men | 22 to 25 mm | diameter + 3 to 4 mm (25 to 29) | |
| Coat, large | 28 to 35 mm | diameter + thickness (the height of the button) + 2 mm | rule for thick buttons |
| Toggle (duffle) | 60 to 90 long | | rope loop or leather tab |

The general formula for a flat button is `L = D + T + 1.5 to 3 mm`, T the button thickness; for a
domed or shank button measure with the button pressed through the hole in the toile.

### 9.2 Position

- Horizontal buttonhole: the end near the garment edge is 1.5 to 2.0 from the finished edge; the
  other end (the "eye", the end that carries the strain) is at the CF line plus 0.3 to 0.5 toward the
  inside. The button then sits at the eye end when buttoned.
- Vertical buttonhole: centred on the CF line, used in plackets and thin fronts.
- Buttons are on the CF line (left front for men's jacket, right front for women's).
- On a double-breasted jacket the outer columns carry the working buttons; the inner columns carry
  the "jigger" buttons (the lining or inside button 1.0 above the first).

### 9.3 Spacing

For `n` buttons between the first and last: spacing `s = (distance first to last) / (n - 1)`.
Examples: 2-button jacket: 9 to 10 between; 3-button: 8.5 to 9.5; 4-button DB: 12 to 14; 6-button DB:
10 to 12 between pairs; sleeve buttons: 4 buttons spaced 2.0 to 2.2, the first 1.0 to 1.5 from the hem
edge (a 10 to 12 vent). In coats: the spacing is bigger, 9 to 12 and the bottom button 12 to 15 above
the hem. A button sits at the break point of the lapel (the working top button).

### 9.4 Reinforcement

Buttonholes need a stay (a fusible patch 2 to 3 wide) behind them; a cord (gimp) within the hole of
heavy fabrics. Shank buttons on coats. Place a "stay button" (a small flat button) on the inside
opposite the front button for heavy fabrics.

## 10. Overcoat

### 10.1 What it is, how to recognise it

A knee-length (or longer) coat worn over a jacket: wool melton, cashmere, tweed, flannel. Classic
forms: Chesterfield (single- or double-breasted, with a velvet collar and covered buttons), Crombie
(short collar), Balmacaan (raglan), Polo coat (belted, camel hair, patch pockets). Photo
recognition: a long, usually straight or slightly fitted body, notch lapels, set-in sleeves or raglan,
a flap or slit pocket, a back vent or no vent, two-piece sleeve.

### 10.2 Pieces and cut counts (Chesterfield, single-breasted)

Front 2, side front (or the princess) 2, back 2 (CB seam), side back 2, top sleeve 2, under sleeve 2,
top collar 1, undercollar 2 (felt; velvet optional), front facing 2, back neck facing 1, pocket flaps
2 to 4, welts 2, bags 4, chest welt 1, canvas and chest pieces 2, lining 2 front, 2 or 1 back, 4
sleeve pieces, inside pockets, interlining (optional) the same shape as the lining, shoulder pad 1
pair, hem canvas.

### 10.3 Drafting

1. Block adapted as 1.3 (coat column): ease 20 to 26, underarm lowered by 3.0 to 4.0, shoulder
   widened 1.0 to 1.5 and raised by pad plus 0.5.
2. Length per 1.5; hem sweep (full round at the hem) 130 to 160 for a straight coat; flare side
   seams 1.5 to 2.5 each from the hip line.
3. Waist suppression (fitted version) 5 to 10, none for a Balmacaan.
4. Front: CF plus wrap 3 (large buttons); DB 8 to 10. A box pleat at the CB (4 to 6) is optional.
5. Collar and lapel per 2.5 with the coat values, collar stand 3 to 4. Undercollar in melton cut on
   the bias (or straight, with a CB seam).
6. Sleeve: two-piece per 4.3 with coat values, cuff turned up or open; length arm +2 to 3.
7. Pockets: flap 17 to 19, slit pockets at the side seam are common, angled 2 to 3.
8. Vent or CB pleat: 28 to 40 for a coat that stays closed when walking.
9. Facing: 10 to 12 wide at the chest, 8 to 10 at the hem; lining hung by the facing with CB pleat 2.0.

### 10.4 Sewing order

As the jacket (2.8), with a hem, a wide hem of 5 to 7 hand-hemmed (invisible stitch),
a hem weight (chain) optional, and lining fixed to the hem with a jump pleat 3.

### 10.5 Faults

| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Drag across the back when the arms move | add 1.0 to 1.5 across the back width and a pleat or vent |
| Collar and lapel curl | shrink the undercollar, tape the roll line |
| Hem swings | add a hem weight or interfacing at the hem |
| Sleeves too tight over the jacket | check the bicep girth including the jacket sleeve (1.1) |

## 11. Trench coat

### 11.1 What it is, how to recognise it

A belted, double-breasted raincoat in gabardine (water-resistant by weave), originally military.
Recognise by: double-breasted with 10 buttons (often 6 working), wide lapel and collar, a storm
flap on the right front, a gun flap on the right shoulder/chest, epaulettes, a belt with a buckle,
cuff straps, a back yoke (rain cape or yoke), sleeve straps, a deep back vent or a box pleat,
D-rings.

### 11.2 Pieces and cut counts

| Piece | Cut | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Front | 2 | with the front yoke or with the gun flap seam |
| Back | 1 on fold (or 2 with CB seam) | box pleat optional |
| Back yoke (rain cape) | 1 | cut on the fold, lined, hangs from the shoulder |
| Back storm flap / lining yoke | 1 to 2 | |
| Storm flap (front) | 1 | right chest, attached at the shoulder and the armhole, buttoned |
| Gun flap | 1 | right shoulder/chest |
| Epaulettes | 2 pairs | 5 wide and 13 to 15 long, buttoned 2 from the armhole |
| Sleeve (top, under) | 2 + 2 or raglan 2 | |
| Sleeve strap | 2 | 4 wide, buckle or snap |
| Belt | 1 or 2 | 5 to 6 wide, length waist + 50 to 80 |
| Belt loops | 6 | fix at side seams and front |
| Collar | top 1, under 2 | stand with a throat latch, 7 to 8 total height |
| Facing | 2 | |
| Pockets (slash with flap) | 2 flap + bag | |
| Lining | front 2, back 1, sleeve 4 | optional, in cotton or removable |
| D-rings | 2 to 4 | at the belt |
| Buttons | 10 (6 working) | 22 to 28 mm |

### 11.3 Drafting steps

1. Choose a block with darts or princess seams for the fitted shape (the darts are needed to draw
   the princess seams); ease per 1.2 (trench). Lower the underarm 2.5 to 3.5.
2. Length per 1.5 (below knee): men 105 to 115, women 95 to 110.
3. Front: wrap 8 to 10 (DB) from CF, with 2 columns of buttons 10 to 12 apart.
4. Gun flap: a flap on the right front starting at the shoulder seam and covering the upper chest,
   14 to 18 wide at the shoulder, with a straight lower edge 12 to 14 below the armhole level, buttoned
   at its end. It is sewn in at the shoulder and the armhole only; the lower edge hangs free over a
   facing of the same shape.
5. Storm flap (front): a shaped flap along the right front edge, 8 to 10 wide at the chest, lying
   under the buttoned front, fixed on the shoulder side. It prevents rain from entering at the front
   edge.
6. Back yoke: a cape over the back, from the shoulder and the armhole, 18 to 25 deep at CB. Cut a
   lining yoke in the same size and attach a second layer for the double thickness. Let the yoke hang
   free from the armhole to the CB with a gap of 1 to 2 at the sleeve (rain drains out).
7. Epaulettes: a strap of 5 x 14 with a pointed end, fixed at the shoulder seam by a bar tack and
   closed at the end with a button 2 from the armhole.
8. Belt: 5 to 6 wide, 2 layers, a buckle with 2 D-rings or a slide; belt loops at the waist side
   seams and at the back.
9. Collar: stand 3, fall 5 to 6; the collar can be raised and fastened across by a throat latch
   (strap, 4 wide).
10. Cuff strap: a strap of 4 x 22 fixed at the hind seam of the sleeve, with a buckle or snap.
11. Back vent: deep, 30 to 45; or a box pleat at the CB 5 to 6 wide.
12. Raglan sleeve option: see 12.6.

### 11.4 Sewing order

Make the yoke, flaps and belt before assembly. Seams: the main seams are flat felled or French seamed
(waterproof, durable). Order: front darts/panels, flaps on the front, back (pleat), shoulder seams
with the yoke, side seams, sleeves (felled), collar, facing and lining, hem (double stitched), finish
with buttons, D-rings, epaulettes, belt, cuff straps. Top-stitch at 0.6 to 0.8 from the edge,
topstitch twice (double row) as the trench signature.

### 11.5 Finishes and faults

Finishes: flat-felled seams, bound seams inside, a stitched hem 4 to 5. Seam allowance 1.5. Lining
optional: a removable wool liner on buttons.

| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Flap lifts | stay with tape and a button |
| Yoke hangs crooked | check its grain and the shoulder seam length equal |
| Belt looks skewed | cut straight grain, edges stitched evenly |
| Rain enters the sleeve | cuff strap smaller or longer collar |

## 12. Other coats

### 12.1 Peacoat

Short (75 to 82), double-breasted, wide lapels, 6 to 8 large buttons (25 to 35 mm, often with an
anchor), wool melton, vertical or slanted hand-warmer pockets, a warm quilted lining. Recognise by:
large collar, two vertical slash pockets at the front, short length, buttoned DB with wrap 8.

Pieces: front 2 + side front 2 (optional), back 2, side back 2, top and under sleeve 2 + 2, top
collar 1, undercollar 2, facing 2, slash pocket welt 2, bags 4, lining pieces.

Drafting: ease 20 to 26 (men), 16 to 22 (women); armhole lowered 2.5 to 3.5; wrap 8; the pockets
are cut at the side front seam at an angle 20 to 30 degrees, openings 16 to 18; hem straight. Lapel
width 8 to 10, collar stand 3.5 to 4.

### 12.2 Wrap coat

No buttons, a large overlap closed by a belt or tie, an extra-wide shawl or notch collar. Recognise:
the front crosses at the waist by 20 to 30, no visible closing, belt at the waist.

Pieces: front 2 (the left front is much wider, the right goes to the side seam for the wrap), back 1
or 2, sleeve 2, collar or shawl collar 1 to 2, facing 2, belt 1, belt loops 2, pocket bags in the
side seams.

Draft: front overlap 20 to 30 at the waist beyond the CF, the front edge drawn as a soft curve from the
neck to the hem; inside tie at the waist on the left side; belt 6 to 8 wide.

### 12.3 Duffle coat

Heavy wool melton, hood, toggles (horn, wood) with loops of rope or leather, a short yoke at the
shoulders, large patch pockets, a 3/4 length. Recognise by: a large hood, 3 to 4 toggles, a
hood that lies on the shoulders, a single- or double-layer front yoke (the shoulder yoke
strap on the right). Pieces: front 2, back 1, yoke front 2 and back 1, hood 2 (+lining 2), sleeve 2 or
2+2, pockets 2 patch, toggle straps 3 to 4 (leather) and loops. Hood per section 13.

### 12.4 Cocoon coat

Rounded volume, narrow at the hem, drop shoulder, curved side seams, a funnel or standing collar.
Recognise: the silhouette is egg-shaped, the sleeves taper, the hem is narrower than the widest point
(at the hip). Pieces: front 2, back 1 or 2, sleeve 2, collar 1, facing 2.

Draft: from the coat block, slash and spread to open the side seams and the hip by 8 to 12 total
per piece at the widest point (about 18 to 25 below the waist), then bring the hem back in by 4 to 8.
Drop the shoulder by 5 to 10 (the sleeve is joined with a dropped armhole at 45 degrees), lower the
underarm by 6 to 10. The sleeves are cut narrow (cuff 13 to 15 flat) and are joined to the dropped
shoulder as a straight seam. Dartless. Reinforce the seams with a stay tape because the curved side
seams stretch.

### 12.5 Drop shoulder (cocoon, oversize)

The shoulder end is moved 3 to 10 outward; the armhole becomes a short, deep curve; the sleeve is
drawn from the lower point as a straight sleeve (cap height 3 to 8, no cap ease); the sleeve width
at the top = the depth of the armhole + 4 to 6.

### 12.6 Raglan coat

The sleeve extends to the neckline, the seam runs from the neck to the underarm. Recognise by the
diagonal seam from the collar to the armpit; no shoulder seam on the sleeve (or a shoulder dart in the
sleeve).

Draft (Aldrich method in summary):
1. Overlap the adapted block and sleeve (sleeve top at the shoulder point, the sleeve centre line
   angled 20 to 30 degrees from the vertical for a close fit, 45 degrees for a low-sleeve style).
2. Front raglan line: from a point on the neckline 3.5 to 4.5 from NP to the front underarm, curving
   slightly (1.0 hollow). Back: from 4.0 to 5.0 from NP to the back underarm.
3. Separate the raglan and the body; the shoulder dart of the block is closed and its value
   (about 1.5 to 2.0) goes to the raglan seam, or the sleeve has a dart-seam over the shoulder.
4. Add ease at the bicep per 4.2 (sleeve width at the underarm must equal the bodice lowered part).
5. Check seam lengths: sleeve front raglan equals bodice front raglan, with 0 ease.

The Balmacaan form: raglan with a notch collar, no pad, loose.

## 13. Hood drafting reference

### 13.1 Dimensions (finished, adult, typical)

| Item | Women | Men | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hood height (neck seam up to the crown, vertical) | 34 to 38 | 36 to 40 | head height about 22 to 24 + neck |
| Hood depth (front edge to back, horizontal at the brow) | 27 to 31 | 29 to 33 | |
| Neck edge length | = neckline from CF to CB of the garment, no ease or +0.5 | | |
| Face opening (front edge along the curve, half) | 28 to 34 | 30 to 36 | for a drawcord, extend 1.5 for the channel |
| Ease on width and height | add 2 to 4 on the height, 2 to 3 on the width | | other sewn examples report width ease 5, length ease 10; use as upper limits |

These figures need a toile on the intended wearer.

### 13.2 Two-piece hood (CB seam)

1. Measure N = the garment neckline from CF to CB (finished edge, seam line).
2. Draw a rectangle D wide (depth) by H high. The left edge is the front edge, the right edge the CB
   seam.
3. Neck edge: the base line of length N: the edge is curved concave (0.5 to 1.5) so it fits the neck,
   and rises 1.5 to 2.0 at the back (the nape).
4. Crown: round the back-top corner with a radius of 10 to 14 and the top-front corner with
   a radius of 6 to 8.
5. Front edge: bow 1.5 to 3.0 outward in the middle to frame the face; add the facing or channel
   (2.5 to 3.5 for a drawcord).
6. Cut 2 (mirror) in shell and 2 in lining (a lining 0.3 smaller on the front edge to avoid the roll
   outward).
7. Check: the sum of the neck edges equals the neckline; the CB seam is the same length on both sides.

### 13.3 Three-piece hood (centre gusset)

Two side panels and one centre gusset (strip) from the front edge over the crown to the back neck.
Strip width: 6 to 10 at the front, 8 to 12 at the back (gives volume, allows a stiffer brim and a
peaked visor with a wire). Side seams: an approximately 45 cm curve each. Cut: side 2, gusset 1. The
gusset is the seam over the head, in waterproof garments taped.

### 13.4 Attachment

The hood neck edge is sewn to the garment neckline (the front ends at the front edge of the facing, so
the hood is open at the CF), and a facing or yoke covers the seam (neck tape 12 to 15). In zip jackets,
the hood front ends 1.0 short of the zip so the zip closes to the top, and the collar stand (funnel)
is a separate piece. Detachable hoods: a zip or buttons around the neck, or a stud placket.

### 13.5 Faults

| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hood falls backward, pulls the neck | lengthen the CB neck seam, check it equals the garment neck |
| Hood narrow, visor pinches | add 2 on the width and 3 on the height |
| Hood edge rolls out | cut the lining smaller by 0.3 |
| Hood collapses forward | add a gusset or a wired brim |

## 14. Parka

### 14.1 What it is, how to recognise it

A hip- to thigh-length cold-weather jacket with a hood (often with a fur or faux fur trim), large patch or
flap pockets, a drawcord at the waist, a zip with a storm flap, an inner cuff in rib or elastic, a
padded or fleece lining. Variants: M-51 fishtail (the back longer than the front, with a tail that
ties around the legs).

### 14.2 Pieces and cut counts

Front 2 (or 4 with a yoke), back 1 or 2, yoke 1, sleeve 2 (+ elbow darts or 2-piece), hood 2 (+
lining 2), hood trim 1, pocket patch 2 + flap 2, zip storm flap 2 (inner flap 1), facing 2, waist
channel 1 to 2 (4 wide, drawcord eyelets 8 mm), lining front 2, back 1, sleeve 2, wadding as a sheet
under the lining, inner cuff 2, fishtail tail (back extension) 1 (optional).

### 14.3 Drafting

1. Block as 1.3 (parka column): ease 24 to 32, underarm lowered 3 to 5.
2. Length 85 to 100; the waist channel at the waist line, 3.5 to 4 wide with a drawcord, eyelets or
   openings 8 mm at the front.
3. CF: a zip on the front edge (section 17), a storm flap 5 to 6 wide, buttoned or Velcro.
4. Pockets: patch with flap, 18 to 22 by 20 to 24, with a bellows pleat of 2 on the sides.
5. Sleeves: set-in 2 piece or 1 piece with a curve, bicep ease 16 to 24, cuff 14 to 16 flat, rib inner
   cuff 8 to 10 wide, cut at 85 percent of the circumference.
6. Hood 13.
7. Lining: quilted or fleece, the same pattern without the facing, 2 smaller on each seam than the
   shell, attached at the facing and hem.

### 14.4 Faults

Rain enters at the zip: add a storm flap and a chin guard. The shell drags on the lining: lining larger
by 1 to 2 on every seam. The hood sags: add a stay tape on the neck seam.

## 15. Puffer and quilted jackets

### 15.1 What it is, how to recognise it

A jacket made of shell + filling (down or synthetic wadding) + lining, with horizontal or shaped
channels (baffles) stitched across. Recognise by the repeating raised channels, a high collar,
hard-wearing zip, elasticated cuffs.

### 15.2 Channel width, take-up and fill allowance

Sew-through quilting (stitched through shell, wadding and lining) and baffle construction (inner walls
that separate the fill) behave differently.

Take-up: when a channel of stitched width `w` is filled so that each face has a rise (sagitta) `s`, the
arc length of the shell is about

`arc = w + 8 x s^2 / (3 x w)`

For `w` = 10 and loft total 5 (each face s = 2.5): arc = 10 + 8 x 6.25 / 30 = 11.7, 17 percent
more than the stitched width. For `w` = 6 and total loft 3 (s = 1.5): arc = 7.0, 17 percent. So the
shell panel must be cut longer, across the channels, by a percentage; the panel is measured in the
direction perpendicular to the stitch lines.

| Construction | Take-up (cut length vs. finished length across the channels) |
|---|---|
| Sew-through, synthetic 60 to 100 g/m2, channels 8 to 12 | 5 to 8 percent |
| Sew-through, synthetic, diamonds 6 to 8 | 6 to 10 percent in length and in width |
| Baffle box, down, channels 8 to 12 | 10 to 17 percent on the shell, depends on the baffle height |
| Baffle, thick loft 4 to 6 | 15 to 20 percent |

These are working values to confirm on a swatch; also measure shrinkage after the first wash and
pre-shrink the shell and the lining by the care method used. Sew the swatch with the real shell,
lining and fill, and measure the stitched width, the loft and the shrinkage.

Channel width (stitch to stitch, finished): down 8 to 14 (narrower for cold or for a fit-close sleeve),
synthetic wadding 6 to 12 (the wadding needs a stitch line at least every 10 to hold it in place).
Sleeve channels may be narrower (6 to 9) to avoid droop. The baffle wall height equals the loft
wanted: 2.5 to 3.5 for a jacket, 4 to 6 for expedition.

Fill weight for down: `fill (g) = V / (fp x 0.578)` where V is the volume of the channels (cm3), fp the
fill power in cubic inches per ounce (a fill power of 700 gives about 405 cm3 per g, 800 gives
about 462), and a margin of +10 to 15 percent for overfill (a baffle must be full to loft, not
compressed). Example: surface 1.2 m2 (12 000 cm2) x loft 3 = 36 000 cm3; 800 fp gives 78 g; with 12 percent
overfill about 87 g. Synthetic wadding: weight per square metre: 40 to 60 g (light), 80 to 100 (mid), 120
to 200 (heavy).

Ease: the girth increases by `6.3 x t` for each layer (1.1), see 1.2. The pattern is drawn at the
thickness the wearer has in the garment; the take-up in the vertical direction shortens the garment
(compensate in the length and in the sleeve length).

### 15.3 Pieces and cut counts (baffled down jacket)

Shell front 2, shell back 1, sleeve 2 (2-piece, with a gusset, or one-piece), hood 2 + lining 2,
collar 1, facing 2, pocket bag 2 pairs, baffle wall (inner netting or fabric) for each channel (front
2, back 1, sleeve 2), lining front 2, back 1, sleeve 2, zip, storm flap 2.

### 15.4 Drafting steps

1. Block as 1.3 (puffer): ease 30 to 45 (or add 6.3 x loft). Underarm lowered by 4 to 6, shoulder drop
   1 to 3, neck widened 2 and raised 1.
2. Mark the channel lines: horizontal on the body, at the same level front and back for continuity at
   the side seams; the side seams are located at the end of a channel or on the stitch line.
3. Seam allowance: 1.0 to 1.5 (taped seam 1.0); stitch the seams before the fill.
4. Sleeve: channel lines at right angles to the sleeve axis or in a spiral; sleeve cap ease 1 to 2;
   cap height reduced 1.5 (the sleeve is not tight).
5. Pattern the channel panels flat by cutting them as strips and joining them with a seam allowance,
   or cutting the panel longer by the take-up, drawn lines at the finished spacing.
6. Collar: a funnel collar of 10 to 14, a zip to the top.

### 15.5 Sewing order (baffled)

1. Sew the baffle walls to the shell and the lining along the channel lines.
2. Join the shell channels to the baffle wall, leaving the side ends open.
3. Fill each channel through its ends; weigh the fill per channel.
4. Close the channels, sew the side seams and shoulder seams through all layers.
5. Set sleeves, collar, hood, zip, hem and cuffs.
6. Seal the stitching holes if the garment is waterproof (section 17).

### 15.6 Faults

| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Down migrates, cold spots | narrower channels, a more tightly woven shell (down-proof), baffles |
| Garment too short after fill | add the take-up (15.2) in the length |
| Seams burst | seam allowance too small, stitch length 2.5 to 3.0, double stitch |
| Sleeves tight | channels in the sleeve too wide, add bicep ease 2 x loft |
| Cold lines at the stitch | use baffles instead of sew-through |

## 16. Bomber and denim jacket

### 16.1 Bomber

Short, with a straight body that blouses over a rib band at the hem and cuffs, a rib collar, a front
zip without a facing or with a narrow one, a nylon or satin shell, MA-1 sleeve pocket. Recognise by:
the rib on three edges, the blousing, the zip, the short length.

Pieces: front 2, back 1 or 2, sleeve 2 (set-in, often 2-piece), rib collar 1 (2 wide), rib cuffs 2,
hem rib 1 (cut as a band, joined at the side) or 2, facing 2 (3 to 4 wide) or a zip tape, welt
pockets with or without zip 2 + bags 4, sleeve pocket 1, lining front 2, back 1, sleeve 2, zip.

Draft: block with ease 22 to 32 (nylon, knit worn under); underarm lowered 3 to 5; shoulder 1.5 to 3
dropped. Length: nape to hem 50 to 62, hem sweep = body hip + 8 to 14 for the blouse (the rib is
smaller than the body hem).

Rib bands:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Finished rib depth | hem 6 to 8, cuff 6 to 8 (folded double, cut 12 to 16 plus a seam), collar 6 to 8 |
| Length of the rib band | 75 to 85 percent of the edge it is attached to for a rib knit with 100 percent stretch; 85 to 90 percent for a rib with elastane |
| Hem blouse ratio | body edge / rib circumference = 1.15 to 1.30 |
| Pre-test | pin a loop of rib around the body; do not guess the stretch; two published rules are: neckline 0.75 of the edge, hem or sleeve hem 0.90 of the edge (rib dependent) |

Example: hem of the body 100 round, rib length at 80 percent: 80 round, a blouse ratio 1.25.

Sewing order: assemble the body, sleeves and lining; sew the rib bands (joined at the ends) and attach
them with the stretch equally distributed (quarter marks), with zip stops at the ends of the front
rib; attach the lining; finish with the collar rib; the zip is set before the hem rib.

### 16.2 Denim (trucker) jacket

Recognise by: pointed front yoke with seams, chest flap pockets, two vertical back seams, a
cinch strap at the back waist (waist tabs), button front, 2-piece sleeves with a cuff, flat-felled
seams with double top-stitching, a straight waistband.

Pieces (typical): front 2, front yoke 2, back panel centre 1, back side panels 2, back yoke 1,
waistband 2 (with adjuster tabs), sleeve 2 (with a placket) + cuff 2 + placket 2, collar 2 + undercollar,
chest pocket 2 + flap 2, button placket on the front edge (cut on the front, folded double 3 to 4
wide), side pockets (optional) 2.

Draft: block with ease 12 to 20 (rigid denim). Front yoke at 8 to 10 below the shoulder; back yoke
12 to 14 deep at the CB, curved. Waist at the waistband: 4 to 5 high. Sleeve 2-piece, bicep ease 10 to
14, cuff 6 to 7 wide with 2 buttons. Seam allowance: 1.0 on the felled seams, with the 0.6 felled
width.

Sewing: fell the seams: first seam with a 1.5 allowance, trim one side to 0.5, fold and top-stitch
through. Denim stitches: 3 to 3.5 mm stitch length for top-stitching, two parallel rows 0.6 apart,
bartacks at the pocket corners. Order: yokes, front, pockets and flaps, shoulder and side seams, sleeves,
collar, waistband, cuffs, buttonholes, buttons (rivet or jeans button).

### 16.3 Hoodie and sweatshirt in short

Covered by the knits sheet. For the outerwear case: ease 10 to 20 for a regular sweat (stretch 20 to
50 percent), oversize 30 to 45, drop shoulder 2 to 6; the hood per 13 with the knit values (hood
height 36 to 40, no neck ease); kangaroo pocket (a rectangle 28 to 34 wide, 20 to 24 high,
slanted openings 14 to 16); rib at the hem and cuffs per 16.1 (85 percent); drawcord channel in the
hood facing 3.5 wide with eyelets 8 mm; neck tape 12 to 15 at the hood seam. Zip hoodie: front tape or
zip with a double jersey facing of 3 wide; hem rib ends square.

## 17. Zip fronts, facings, wind flaps and seam taping

### 17.1 Zip front with facing

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Zip type | separating (open-end) metal or coil, number 5 for most jackets, number 3 for light, 8 to 10 for heavy |
| Zip length | CF length (neck seam to hem) minus 1.5 at the hem and minus the collar zone; sold in 5 cm steps |
| Zip tape | 1.5 to 1.8 wide each side |
| Facing | 3.5 to 4.5 wide on the front edge, interfaced (woven or knit), cut on the same grain; back neck facing joined |
| Front edge seam allowance | 1.0 (the teeth are 0.3 to 0.5 from the edge of the tape) |
| Top end | the zip top stop 1.0 below the neck edge, or under the collar band |
| Bottom end | the box pin 1.0 above the hem fold |
| Fix | baste the zip tape right side to the garment edge, teeth outward, stitch at 0.6 from the teeth, understitch the facing |

Sewing: attach the zip to the front edges before the hem and before the facing, then fold the facing in,
topstitch 0.5 to 0.7 from the edge. At the hem, unzip fully and stitch the hem allowance, then the
zip bottom stop is trimmed.

### 17.2 Wind flap and storm flap

An inner flap (placket) 4 to 5 wide cut on the left front, inserted behind the zip, filling the gap
between the teeth and the body; it stops the air and the rain passing at the zip. Outer storm flap: 3
to 4.5 finished, on the zip front, closed by snaps, buttons or Velcro 2 squares. Cut the flap
double on the fold, interface, topstitch 0.6 from the edge, fix on one side only.

### 17.3 Seam taping for waterproof garments

| Item | Typical |
|---|---|
| Seam allowance | 1.0 to 1.2 (minimum, so the tape covers the allowance) |
| Tape | 16 to 22 wide, 3-layer or 2.5-layer seam sealing tape, compatible with the membrane |
| Stitch | 2.5 to 3.0 mm, needle 70 to 80 (fine, to minimise holes) |
| Process | hot-air or hot-wedge taping at the supplier's temperature, pressure and speed; test on a swatch |
| Seam types | prefer single-needle plain seams; flat-felled seams do not tape well |
| Curves | tape cut on the bias or a stretch tape for curved seams |

Rules: tape every seam through which water can pass; tape the seam before it is crossed by another seam
(shoulder before sleeve, sleeve seam before attach, side before hem); avoid darts; avoid cross seams
with large step differences; no pockets through the membrane unless inside or taped. Zips of waterproof
garments are PU-coated and covered by a storm flap. Hems: stitched, then taped on the inner side, or
welded.

## 18. CLO3D notes (short)

Rules shared with `clo3d.md` apply: added collision thickness 0 on every piece unless a reason is stated;
mesh size as for the fit (about 8 mm for tailored pieces, 10 to 20 mm for loose coats, 4 to 6 mm for
small pieces and quilting channels).

| Case | Note |
|---|---|
| Layers | shell, interlining, lining: separate pieces. Lining on the inside, with the same outline minus the facing. Layer order: underwear, shirt, jacket lining, jacket shell, coat. Put the lining sewn to the facing. |
| Canvas and interlining | a separate piece with a stiffer fabric property (thicker, higher bending stiffness), or the fusing option of the shell fabric; no collision thickness is added |
| Collar and lapel | a lapel roll cannot be sculpted by the seam only: use the roll line as an internal line with a fold angle (a folded edge in the 2D window) and a stiff fabric |
| Quilting and puffer | draw the channel lines as internal lines; use the fabric's pressure or padding setting so the channel rises, with a mesh of about 5 mm; do not use added collision thickness to fake loft |
| Pleats and vents | the CB pleat and the vent overlap must be drawn as extra width in the pattern; pleats are missing from the API (see `clo3d.md`) |
| Pockets | flap, patch: separate pieces sewn on the front; jetted: an internal line plus a bag piece |
| Hood | sew to the neckline; a front edge drawcord is hard to simulate; fix the mesh at 8 to 10 mm |
| Seam allowances | not exported to the API; keep the 3D pieces net of the allowances |
| Buttons, buttonholes | by the JSON export (`ButtonHeadList`, `ButtonHoleList`); check the position |
