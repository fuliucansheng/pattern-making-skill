# Reference analysis

Before drafting anything from a photo or a drawing, work out how the garment is built. A pattern copied
from the silhouette alone is wrong as soon as the construction differs: a two-panel glove is not a trank
glove, a gored skirt is not a circle skirt. Do this analysis every time, write it down, and draft from it.

## Protocol

1. **Collect every view.** Flat front, flat back, worn photos, details. Worn photos show fit, length,
   ease and how the fabric behaves; flat photos show seams and pieces.
2. **Zoom zone by zone.** Crop each zone (opening, edges, every seam crossing, fingertips, collar, hem)
   at two to four times, and boost the contrast: seams on dark fabric only show as thin light or dark
   lines. Look at both long edges of every piece.
3. **List every line**, and classify each one with its evidence:
   - **seam**: two raw edges joined; a thin line, often a slight ridge, sometimes a step at the end;
   - **fold**: a soft rounded edge with no line;
   - **topstitching**: a row of stitch holes or a parallel line at a fixed distance from an edge;
   - **crease or wrinkle**: a line that changes with the pose, absent on the flat view;
   - **gathers, shirring, elastic**: small regular pleats along a line.
4. **Deduce the pieces.** Each closed region bounded by seams is a piece; a fold joins two halves of the
   same piece. Count the pieces and name the construction from the catalogue below.
5. **Decide each ambiguous point with an evidence table**: for each candidate construction, write what
   it would show in each view, then what the photos actually show. Keep the candidate whose predictions
   match. Give a confidence. If it stays low and the choice changes the pattern, ask the person.
6. **Cross-check with the worn photos**: length relative to the body, ease, where the garment wrinkles,
   which parts are stretched.
7. **Write the construction sheet** before drafting: pieces with how many to cut, seams (which edge to
   which edge), folds, grain, finishes (hems, topstitching, elastic), closures, materials, estimated
   dimensions with what they were estimated from, and the confidence of each conclusion.

## Catalogues

The protocol applies to every garment and accessory. The tables below list, by family, the variants that
change the pattern and what tells them apart on a photo. When a garment is not listed, build the same
kind of table for it before drafting.

### Skirts

| Part | Variants | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Body | Straight with darts, A-line, gored (4, 6, 8 or more panels), half or full circle, wrap, tiered, pleated | Count the vertical seams around the skirt. Darts stop short; panel seams run to the hem. A circle skirt flutes evenly all round with no darts; a gored skirt flutes from each seam |
| Waist | Waistband (straight or contoured), facing, yoke, elastic | A band shows a seam line below the waist edge; a facing shows nothing outside but topstitching or a clean edge |
| Closure | Invisible zip (seam only), lapped zip (visible flap), buttons, wrap tie | Look at center back and the sides |
| Hem | Turned and topstitched, blind, rolled, raw | Row of stitches parallel to the hem, or none |

### Trousers

| Part | Variants | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Legs | Straight, tapered, wide, flared; side seam or no side seam | Follow the outside of the leg for a seam line |
| Front | Flat front, pleats, darts | Folds starting at the waistband |
| Rise and waist | High, mid, low; waistband, yoke at the back (jeans), elastic | Where the waistband sits on the body in worn photos |
| Pockets | Slant, on-seam, welt, patch, jeans scoop with coin pocket | Opening shape and topstitching |
| Fly | Zip fly with visible J-stitch, button fly, side zip | Curved topstitching at center front |

### Tops, shirts, dresses

| Part | Variants | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Shaping | Darts (bust, waist, shoulder), princess lines (from shoulder or armhole), side seams only (knits, boxy) | Darts end in a point; princess seams run the full length through the bust point |
| Sleeve | Set-in, dropped shoulder, raglan, kimono or dolman, cap | Armhole seam at the shoulder point (set-in), below it (dropped), diagonal neck to underarm (raglan), none (kimono) |
| Neckline and collar | Facing, binding, rib band, mock neck, stand, shirt collar with stand, flat collar, notched lapel | A stand shows a seam between collar and stand; a flat collar lies on the shoulders |
| Front | Plain, placket (concealed or visible), button extension, wrap | Count the rows of topstitching on the front edge |
| Back | Plain, yoke, center back seam, box pleat | Horizontal seam across the shoulders (yoke) |
| Cuffs and hems | Cuff with placket, rib, turned hem, curved shirt-tail hem | Shape of the hem on the side view |

### Jackets and coats

| Part | Variants | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Body | Side panels, princess lines, back seam, side seams | Count the vertical seams on front, side and back views |
| Sleeve | One-piece, two-piece (seams at the front and back of the arm), raglan | A two-piece sleeve shows a seam on the back of the arm towards the elbow |
| Collar | Notched lapel, shawl, stand, hood | The notch where collar meets lapel; a shawl collar has no notch |
| Closure | Single or double breasted, zip, toggles, hidden placket | Button rows and overlap width |
| Inside | Lined, half lined, unlined (bound seams) | Visible at the opening on flat views |

### Knits

| Part | Variants | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Seams | Side seams, raglan, tubular (no side seam) | Seam lines on the sides or none |
| Bands | Rib at neck, cuffs, hem; self-fabric band; turned hem with twin needle | Ribbed texture, or two parallel rows of stitches |
| Fit | Negative ease (stretched on the body), zero, loose | Worn photos: the knit stretches, the rib opens |

### Gloves

| Part | Variants | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Body | **Trank**: back and palm in one piece, folded on one side, one side seam on the other; **two panels**: back and palm cut separately, seams on both sides | A trank shows one long edge with a seam line and the other edge soft, with no line. Two panels show seam lines on both edges |
| Side seam | Little finger side (usual on a trank), index side | Follow the seam line from the opening to the finger it ends on |
| Thumb | **Bolton thumb**: one piece, set into a drop-shaped hole in the palm, one seam along the inner side of the thumb; **quirk thumb**: thumb plus a small diamond gusset (quirk) at the crotch; **two-piece thumb**: upper and under thumb, seams on both sides | Bolton: the visible face is unbroken and a single seam runs along one side. Quirk thumb: a small diamond at the base, between thumb and palm. Two-piece thumb: a seam on each side of the thumb, like a finger |
| Fingers | **Fourchettes**: narrow strips forming the sides of the fingers; **quirks**: small diamonds at the finger crotches; no fourchettes (simple two-panel glove) | Fourchettes show as two close parallel seams running up the side of each finger, from the crotch to the tip |
| Wrist | Elastic shirring on the palm, a slit with a button or a press stud, plain | Small regular gathers across the palm, usually just below the thumb |
| Opening | Turned hem with topstitching, faced, raw | A row of stitches parallel to the top edge |
| Length | Short (wrist), gauntlet, opera (above the elbow) | Worn photos: where the opening sits. A glove longer than the arm ruches on the forearm |

Glove fit: the hand part is tight, with zero or negative ease in leather. The thumb hole and the thumb
are drafted from the hand: thumb base round, thumb length, angle of the thumb towards the fingers.

## Example: long leather gloves

What the flat and worn photos of a pair of long black leather gloves showed, and what was concluded:

- **Back edge, little finger side**: a seam line along the whole length, ending as a small step at the
  opening. **Index side**: a soft edge with no line. So the body is a **trank** (one piece folded on the
  index side, side seam on the little finger side), not two panels.
- **Thumb**: separate, set into a drop-shaped hole on the palm, angled towards the fingers. The visible
  face is unbroken, and the worn photos show a single seam along one side of the thumb. So it is a
  **Bolton thumb (one piece)**, medium to high confidence. A two-piece thumb would show a seam on both
  sides.
- **Fingers**: two close parallel seams up the sides of each finger, on the back and the palm:
  **fourchettes** between index and middle, middle and ring, ring and little finger.
- **Wrist**: **elastic shirring** across the palm, just below the thumb.
- **Opening**: turned hem, topstitched about 1 cm from the edge.
- **Length**: opera, about 60 cm. Worn, it ruches on the forearm and reaches above the elbow.
- **Pieces**: trank, Bolton thumb, fourchettes: **3 piece types** per glove.
