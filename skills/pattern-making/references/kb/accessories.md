# Accessories: gloves, hats, bags, belts, ties, scarves, masks and small goods

Entry-per-item database (format in `FORMAT.md`). Methods and worked numbers are in `../techniques/accessories.md`
(gloves part 1, hats part 2, bags part 3, belts part 4, ties part 5, scarves part 6, headbands part 7, masks part 8);
this file is the lookup layer: what each item is, which pieces it has, which edge sews to which.

Reading conventions used in every entry:

- Notation: H head circumference (above brows and ears), G hand girth (round the palm at the knuckles, thumb
  excluded), W wrist girth, SA seam allowance, [T] typical value to confirm on a toile. Metric. Net outline (at the
  stitching line) unless stated.
- Honesty rule: the glove trank, fourchette and Bolton thumb data come from published glove patterns (The Shapes of
  Fabric, Koch in Threads, RCMP specification). Everything else here is either **derived geometry** (cone unfolding,
  cylinder, circle, rectangle: exact by construction) or **trade practice** with typical ranges. Where an outline was
  not taken from a drawn pattern or a documented cutting layout, "Shape" says "unverified".
- Glove sheet consistency: glove entries agree with technique sheet section 1.8 (thumb hole in the palm half along the
  fold, V flap freed by a curved slit, Bolton thumb a wide oval with two lobes sewn together as the tip, a slit in the
  thumb taking the flap, notches bounding the part sewn to the flap). The Bolton thumb entry is the reference.
- Glove anchors (trank, drawn flat, wrist line y = 0, fingers up): C0 index-to-index centre crotch (end of the centre
  slit), I|M, M|R, R|L crotch points, T thumb hole, V tongue point of the thumb hole, Ti fingertip heights.

# GLOVES

## Trank Glove with Bolton Thumb {#trank-glove}

- **Aliases**: classic fitted leather glove, dress glove, English-thumb glove, one-piece trank glove; fr: gant a trank, gant pouce Bolton
- **Family**: glove, fitted, leather or stretch fabric
- **Recognize**: no seam across the palm; seams along both sides of each finger (fourchettes); one closing seam on the little-finger side; the thumb joins the palm at a V-shaped corner with a short seam along the thumb; optional three rows of points on the back.
- **Pieces**: trank (x1 per hand: back half and palm half in one piece, fold on the thumb side), Bolton thumb (x1 per hand), fourchette arms (12 per pair: index 2, middle 4, ring 4, little 2), cuff facing or hem strip (x1 per hand), optional opening reinforcement; lining pieces if lined (lining shell patterns reduced by 1.5 to 2.5 mm at the sides and 3 mm at the tips). Per pair: 2 tranks, 2 thumbs, 12 arms.
- **Shape**: Trank = two halves side by side joined by the fold below the centre crotch C0. Back half (left): fingers L R M I; palm half (right): I M R L. Fingers are straight, parallel, vertical, with zero-width slits between them (no fanning). Slits end at crotch points; back slits are about 5 mm deeper than palm slits. The centre slit (index to index) ends at C0 and the two index halves are sewn together on their outer (thumb) side, never drawn as one paddle across the fold. Sides: one straight line from the little finger crotch to the glove end, no kink at the wrist. Thumb hole (gouch) in the palm half, inner side 1 to 6 mm from the fold, top about 40 mm under the palm crotch points, about 35 x 57 mm for a size 7, round bottom with a notch; the top edge is a V pointing down into the hole, the triangle above the V is a flap, freed by a curved slit that runs from the top corner of the hole away from the fold up to a dot about 10 mm above the hole (V depth about 9, slit about 17 mm for size 7). Bolton thumb = see #bolton-thumb. Fourchette arm = tapered strip, back edge about 5 mm longer than palm edge. Source: drawn pattern: The Shapes of Fabric (trank, fourchettes, thumb hole); published numbers from Koch (Threads) and the RCMP specification. The flap and thumb slit geometry follows technique sheet 1.8 (corrected from real patterns).
- **Drafting**: 1. Trank width W = G + 0 to 4 (fine stretch leather), G + 10 to 20 (suede), G + 25 to 30 (no stretch); back Wb = W/2 + 4, palm Wp = W/2 - 4. 2. Mid-finger fourchette width fw_mid = (sum of the four finger girths - W)/6 (example 6 mm); trank width of finger i = Cf_i - n_i x fw_mid with n = 1, 2, 2, 1 for I, M, R, L; split back : palm in the ratio Wb : Wp. 3. Fingertip height Ti = crease height + palm-side finger length + 3 to 6 (leather); use palm-side lengths on the back too. 4. Slit depth = lower neighbouring crease height + 4 on the palm, 5 mm deeper on the back; C0 at the thumb web height (about 80 for size 7). 5. Round the tips (corner radius 0.35 to 0.45 x finger width). 6. One straight side line to the glove end; cuff width = forearm girth at the end + 0 to 10 (stretch leather) or 10 to 20; ensure the fist passes (about G + 25). 7. Thumb hole and Bolton thumb as in #bolton-thumb. 8. Arms: base width 1.3 x fw_mid, tip 0.6 x fw_mid (see #fourchette). 9. Allowance: 0.8 to 1.5 outseam, 3 to 4 inseam, 6 self hem. All numbers size 7 worked example, scale with G.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Cut slits and thumb hole exactly; silk the points on the back. 2. Make up each fork (two arms joined at the base). 3. Make up the thumb (tip seam, lobe to lobe). 4. Set the thumb into the hole starting at the V, flap into the thumb slit. 5. Sew the forks to the fingers, index to little. 6. Close fingertips, index outer seam, little finger edge and side seam. 7. Hem or face the cuff, add closure. 8. Press on hand forms.
- **Finishes and notions**: outseam, prixseam, piqué or inseam; cuff self hem 6 mm; snap or button opening; optional silk lining.
- **Fabrics**: lambskin, kid, cabretta, nappa 0.45 to 0.6 mm (women), 0.8 to 1.0 (men); suede; stretch knit or stretch velvet in the knit version (W = G + 10, 3 mm allowances). Leather stretches mainly across the hand: fingers along the low-stretch direction, thumb circumference and trank width along the stretch.
- **Fit faults**: fingers fan out -> straight parallel fingers; two index fingers merge -> keep the centre slit to C0; thumb twists -> match thumb edge lengths to the hole, hole in the palm half; wrist kink -> one straight side line; tight on the back -> palm-side lengths on the back; webs tear -> add quirks, deepen slits.
- **Variants**: #two-piece-trank, #quirk-thumb, #french-thumb, #two-piece-thumb, #quirks, #fourchette, #gauntlet, #opera-glove, #driving-glove, #knit-glove.
- **CLO3D**: 2 trank pieces (or 1 with a fold line), 2 Bolton thumbs, 12 arms as separate pieces; segment sewing 1:1 between slit edges and arm edges; particle distance 4 to 6 mm, collision thickness 0; fingers rarely have avatar arrangement points, so the user places the glove around the hand from the wrist by hand and uses Pin or frozen avatar fingers while simulating; leather preset with low stretch.
- **Confidence**: trank, fourchette, thumb hole and Bolton thumb: verified (The Shapes of Fabric, Koch, RCMP). Finger-end tip rounding radius and the fourchette taper ratios: trade practice. Per-size numbers are a size 7 worked example, confirm on a toile.

## Bolton Thumb {#bolton-thumb}

- **Aliases**: English thumb, thumb with its quirk in one piece; fr: pouce anglais
- **Family**: glove thumb construction
- **Recognize**: on the outside, a short seam from the thumb tip along the thumb, then a seam that turns round the base of the thumb into a V-shaped corner on the palm; no separate diamond at the thumb base.
- **Pieces**: Bolton thumb (x1 per glove, folded on its long axis), thumb hole in the palm half of the trank with its V flap (part of the trank, not a separate piece).
- **Shape**: drawn pattern: The Shapes of Fabric (corrected against real patterns; technique sheet 1.8). Thumb hole: in the palm half, inner side 1 to 6 mm from the fold, top about 40 mm under the palm crotch points, about 35 wide x 57 long (size 7), round bottom toward the wrist with a notch. The top edge is a V pointing down into the hole; the triangle of leather above the V is a flap. A curved slit leaves the top corner of the hole away from the fold and rises to a dot about 10 mm above the hole, freeing the flap so it stays attached on the fold side only and can lift (V depth about 9, slit about 17 mm). Thumb: a wide oval, about 0.72 times as wide as high, folded on its long axis, height about 1.7 x the thumb outer length (about 100 mm for size 7). Top: two rounded lobes separated by a cleft on the fold axis, cleft depth about 15 % of the height; the two lobes are sewn together and make the rounded thumb tip. The width at any height is the round of the thumb there, widest around mid-height. A slit leaves one side edge at about 40 % of the height from the top, curving inward and down to a dot: the flap of the trank goes into this slit and gives the thumb its movement. On the other side edge two notches (single and double) bound the part that is sewn to the two edges of the flap's V; a notch at the bottom of the oval matches the bottom notch of the hole.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take the thumb outer length T_o and girth T_g. 2. Oval height = 1.7 x T_o, width = 0.72 x height; verify the edge perimeter against T_g plus ease. 3. Cut the two-lobe cleft on the axis (15 % of height). 4. Mark the slit at 40 % from the top with its dot; mark the single and double notches and the bottom notch. 5. Thumb edges are 2 to 4 % longer than the hole edges they meet; re-measure every section after the slits are cut. 6. Expect one or two toiles. Scaling per half size: thumb girth +2 mm, hole length +2 mm, hole width +1 mm.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Fold the thumb on the axis and sew lobe edge to lobe edge: from the cleft down to the slit on one side and to the first notch on the other (the tip seam). 2. Sew the part between the single and the double notch to the two edges of the flap's V (flap goes into the thumb slit). 3. Sew from the double notch round the bottom to the slit, round the hole, bottom notch to bottom notch. 4. Sew the two lips of the thumb slit to the two lips of the trank slit (body side and flap side). Never sew to the extreme edge at the start and end of a seam.
- **Finishes and notions**: outseam or prixseam in leather; inseam in fabric; back tack at the V and at the slit dots.
- **Fabrics**: leather (thumb circumference along the stretch); knit works with 3 mm allowances. Thick low-stretch leather: consider #quirk-thumb.
- **Fit faults**: elliptical tip with straight sides, tulip with horns or a narrow V -> wrong, redraw as two lobes on a wide oval; thumb twists -> thumb edges not 2 to 4 % longer than the hole edges, or hole not in the palm half; tear at the V -> back tack, flap not freed by the slit; thumb pulls the palm -> slit too short.
- **Variants**: #quirk-thumb, #french-thumb, #two-piece-thumb. Sources differ on the definition: Yeovil's glossary says the quirk is cut with the back and palm; Dreamstress and Cornelia James say thumb and quirk in one piece. This base uses "thumb with its quirk".
- **CLO3D**: draw the thumb oval with the fold line, the slit as an internal cut (two edges), notches as sewing points N1 to N3; sew the thumb to the hole with segment sewing, lobe edges first, then slit lips; check each pair has 2 to 4 % ease in length.
- **Confidence**: verified for the structure (drawn pattern: The Shapes of Fabric; Koch, Threads; technique sheet 1.8). Numeric ratios 0.72, 1.7, 15 %, 40 %, 9 and 17 mm are measured from a published size-7 pattern, confirm on a toile.

## Two-Piece Trank (Separate Back and Palm) {#two-piece-trank}

- **Aliases**: back and palm glove, two-panel glove, seamed-side glove; fr: gant deux pieces
- **Family**: glove, body construction
- **Recognize**: seams run the whole length of both sides of the hand (little-finger side and thumb side); the back and palm are visibly separate panels; common in work, sport and fabric gloves.
- **Pieces**: palm (x1 per hand), back (x1 per hand), thumb piece(s) (see #two-piece-thumb or a straight thumb), fourchette arms if used (12 per pair) or none (see Variants), cuff pieces.
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic: the trank of #trank-glove is cut along the fold into two panels, a back (Wb) and a palm (Wp), each with four fingers; there is now an open edge on the thumb side as well as on the little-finger side, so the two panels are sewn together on both sides. The thumb hole of the palm then either stays an interior hole (as in #trank-glove) or becomes an open notch in the thumb-side seam and the thumb is set into it. Back is slightly wider than palm (W/2 + 4 and W/2 - 4 in the knit pattern). Which of the two thumb layouts a given pattern uses must be read from the drawn pattern; do not assume.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft the trank per #trank-glove. 2. Split on the fold: add seam allowance to the cut edge of each panel (3 to 4 mm). 3. Check the length of the thumb-side seam: back edge and palm edge must match, with the back 0 to 3 mm longer if the thumb flap is part of the seam. 4. Where fourchettes are omitted, finger widths must carry the full finger girth: Cf_i/2 + ease per panel.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Silk or decorate the back. 2. Set thumb(s). 3. Sew fingers between panels (fourchettes first if used). 4. Close thumb-side seam, tips, little-finger side. 5. Cuff.
- **Finishes and notions**: inseam (turned), outseam or overlocked seams in sport fabrics; 3 mm allowance in knit.
- **Fabrics**: stretch fabrics and leather; with fewer seams across the fingers, stretch must come from the fabric (20 to 25 % across the hand).
- **Fit faults**: fingers too stiff or tight -> without fourchettes add width or switch to #fourchette; thumb-side seam twists the hand -> shift the seam to the thumb edge line.
- **Variants**: #trank-glove (one-piece trank), #work-glove-gunn-cut, #work-glove-clute-cut.
- **CLO3D**: two panels, sewn along both sides; arrange around the hand with the thumb side pointing to the thumb; use fabric preset with 4-way stretch for knit.
- **Confidence**: trade practice, shape unverified.

## Quirk Thumb {#quirk-thumb}

- **Aliases**: thumb with separate quirk, gusseted thumb (loosely); fr: pouce avec fourchette de pouce
- **Family**: glove thumb construction
- **Recognize**: thumb piece plus a small diamond or triangle at the base of the thumb in the crotch between thumb and hand.
- **Pieces**: thumb (x1 per hand), quirk (x1 per hand, diamond), thumb hole in the trank.
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Per technique sheet 1.8 [T]: the thumb is a plain tapered piece (straight sides, rounded tip, no two-lobe tip), width w_t at the base end; the hole is an oval without the tongue; the quirk is a separate diamond about 14 to 18 wide by 16 to 20 high, set at the base of the thumb in the crotch. Real drawn quirk-thumb patterns may differ in slit details; check against a drawn pattern before relying on it.
- **Drafting**: 1. Thumb length from T_o (outer) and T_p (palm side), width from T_g. 2. Hole oval about 0.17 x G wide and 0.25 x G long in the palm half. 3. Quirk: diamond 14 to 18 x 16 to 20 [T]. 4. Quirk long diagonal sewn into the thumb crotch, short sides to the thumb and hole edges.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Fold and sew the thumb. 2. Sew the quirk to the thumb base. 3. Set the thumb unit into the hole. 4. Continue as #trank-glove.
- **Finishes and notions**: outseam or inseam; back tack at the quirk points.
- **Fabrics**: thick or low-stretch leather, where the quirk gives the movement the stretch cannot.
- **Fit faults**: webs tear at the crotch -> enlarge quirk, back tack; thumb binds -> quirk too small.
- **Variants**: #bolton-thumb (quirk built into the thumb piece), #quirks.
- **CLO3D**: thumb tube plus a small diamond piece; sew the quirk edges with segment sewing at mesh 4 mm.
- **Confidence**: from technique sheet 1.8 [T]; shape unverified.

## French (Set-In) Thumb {#french-thumb}

- **Aliases**: set-in thumb, round thumb; fr: pouce rapporte, pouce francais
- **Family**: glove thumb construction
- **Recognize**: smooth round thumb sewn into a round or oval hole; no V corner on the palm and no separate quirk; seam is an oval ring at the thumb base.
- **Pieces**: thumb (x1 per hand, a single elongated oval folded on its axis), oval hole in the trank.
- **Shape**: unverified. Per technique sheet 1.8 [S: Yeovil, T]: oval hole in the palm half, width about 0.17 x G, length about 0.25 x G; thumb a single elongated oval folded on its axis, with no V and no quirk. Trade names vary by factory; some makers call other thumbs "French". Sources do not give a drawn outline in this base.
- **Drafting**: 1. Hole oval 0.17 G x 0.25 G. 2. Thumb perimeter at the base = hole perimeter + 2 to 4 % ease. 3. Thumb length from T_o and T_p. 4. Notches at the top and bottom of the hole matched on the thumb.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Fold and sew the thumb along its axis. 2. Set into the hole, matching notches, ease evenly. 3. Continue as #trank-glove.
- **Finishes and notions**: as #trank-glove.
- **Fabrics**: fine leather and stretch fabric; the lack of a quirk limits thumb motion in thick leather.
- **Fit faults**: thumb pulls at the hole -> ease too small; ring at the base binds -> enlarge the oval.
- **Variants**: #bolton-thumb, #quirk-thumb, #two-piece-thumb.
- **CLO3D**: round hole in the palm piece, oval thumb with a fold line, circular sewing at 4 mm particle distance.
- **Confidence**: from memory and technique sheet 1.8; shape unverified.

## Two-Piece Thumb (Upper and Under Thumb) {#two-piece-thumb}

- **Aliases**: thumb in two pieces, front and back thumb, upper thumb and under thumb (trade wording, not found in the glossaries consulted); fr: pouce en deux pieces
- **Family**: glove thumb construction
- **Recognize**: thumb with a seam on each side, running from the tip to the base (two seams along the thumb instead of one); the palm-side half and the back-side half are visibly distinct panels.
- **Pieces**: upper (back-of-thumb) piece (x1 per hand), under (palm-side) piece (x1 per hand), plus thumb hole in the trank; a quirk is optional.
- **Shape**: unverified. A patent literature description (US 1940s glove patents) describes a thumb made of a front thumb portion joined to the palm portion and a back thumb portion joined to the back portion of the glove, i.e. the thumb split front and back, each half continuous with the matching trank panel; whether a given factory piece is this or two free-standing halves must be read from the drawn pattern. Treat the term "upper thumb / under thumb" as a trade label without a verified outline. Half-width of each piece is about half the thumb girth plus allowance (derived geometry).
- **Drafting**: 1. Each piece is half the thumb round: width at the base about T_g/2 + 2 x SA, length T_o (back) and T_p (palm side) plus tip ease. 2. Rounded tip on each piece. 3. Base edges curve to the hole shape. 4. Notches at base and mid-thumb.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Sew the two thumb halves along both sides (or each to the trank panel in the continuous version). 2. Set into the hole or close with the trank seams. 3. Continue as #two-piece-trank.
- **Finishes and notions**: outseam or inseam; more seams so thinner allowances (1.5 to 3 mm).
- **Fabrics**: sport and work leather, and fabric gloves.
- **Fit faults**: thumb twists -> halves not matched on the notches; thumb tip seam bulky -> reduce allowance.
- **Variants**: #bolton-thumb (one piece), #french-thumb, #quirk-thumb.
- **CLO3D**: two thumb pieces sewn along both edges; sew base to the hole; mesh 4 mm.
- **Confidence**: from memory and patent description; shape unverified.

## Fourchette (One-Piece Fork and Two-Piece Arms) {#fourchette}

- **Aliases**: fork, forge, finger gusset, sidewall, finger side strip, continuous fourchette (one long strip); fr: fourchette, soufflet
- **Family**: glove, finger depth piece
- **Recognize**: a seam along both sides of each finger from the web to the tip, with a narrow strip (often 3 to 8 mm wide at mid-finger) between the back and the palm of the finger.
- **Pieces**: one arm per finger side: per hand index 1, middle 2, ring 2, little 1 (6 arms, 12 per pair). Two-piece construction: each arm cut separately and the two arms of a fork joined at the base. One-piece construction: the two arms of a fork cut as one piece folded at the web, or (continuous) one long strip run over each finger tip. The index outer side has no fourchette: the two index halves are sewn directly together.
- **Shape**: drawn pattern: The Shapes of Fabric and Koch (Threads) for the two-arm fork; the continuous strip follows Dreamstress, outline unverified. Each arm is a straight tapered strip, longer (by about 5 mm) on the edge that sews to the back finger edge than on the edge that sews to the palm finger edge, because back slits are about 5 mm deeper. Base end cut at a slant (or the back edge slightly curved) so both edges meet the finger edges; width at the base about 1.3 x fw_mid (knit sample: 15 mm at the base, 5 at the top), width at the tip about 0.6 x fw_mid (3 to 4 mm). Notch at the base (centre of the fork) and one notch at mid-finger. Tip: the arm ends where the tip rounding starts; cut across at the tip, shape one side to the finger contour and ease the other in a gentle slope from about 25 mm down (Koch). Worked example, size 7, back / palm edge: index 72 / 67, middle 87 / 82 (both sides), ring 75 / 70 (middle side) and 84 / 79 (little side), little 61 / 56.
- **Drafting**: 1. Edge length = fingertip height minus crotch height on the back and on the palm. 2. Draw the tapered strip, back edge longer. 3. Base width 1.3 x fw_mid, tip 0.6 x fw_mid, where fw_mid = (sum of finger girths - trank width)/6. 4. Notch base and mid. 5. Join the two arms of a fork at the base with a 4 to 6 mm seam, or cut as one piece folded at the web. 6. Check each arm edge against the slit edge within 0.5 mm. 7. Optional diamond quirk at the web (#quirks).
- **Sewing order**: 1. Make up each fork. 2. Align the fork centre with the crotch point, sew along both finger edges, index to little finger, in several short passes; do not stretch. 3. Close tips.
- **Finishes and notions**: outseam or inseam; 3 mm allowance in knit.
- **Fabrics**: leather or the same fabric; arms along the low-stretch direction. Two-piece fitted fourchettes keep the fingers from riding up the hand (patent literature).
- **Fit faults**: finger too tight -> arms too narrow or fw_mid mis-computed; web puckers -> arm base not centred on the crotch; fingers pull toward the palm -> back arm not longer.
- **Variants**: #quirks, #trank-glove, #two-piece-trank (no fourchettes in some work gloves).
- **CLO3D**: 12 thin pieces, segment sewing 1:1 to the slit edges; use the finest mesh (3 to 4 mm) there.
- **Confidence**: verified for two-arm forks (The Shapes of Fabric, Koch); one-piece continuous strip: from Dreamstress, outline unverified.

## Quirks {#quirks}

- **Aliases**: querks, finger-base diamonds, glove gussets; fr: quirk, losange
- **Family**: glove, movement piece
- **Recognize**: small diamonds or triangles at the base of each finger and at the thumb base (a pale diamond in a contrasting leather is a giveaway).
- **Pieces**: one diamond per web (3 per hand between the four fingers, plus the index-index centre if wanted) plus one at the thumb crotch if a quirk thumb. Optional on most gloves.
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Per technique sheet 1.9 [T]: diamond of 8 to 10 mm side set on a point, or triangle 10 x 12, at the web between two fingers; thumb quirk 14 to 18 wide x 16 to 20 high. Sources say they give movement and room mainly in thick or low-stretch leather and are rarely used by modern makers.
- **Drafting**: 1. Diamond at the crotch point. 2. Long diagonal along the slit; short points to the fork edges. 3. Notch both ends.
- **Sewing order**: sew the quirk to the slit end before the fourchette arms.
- **Finishes and notions**: back tack at the diamond points.
- **Fabrics**: thick leather, low stretch.
- **Fit faults**: webs tear -> add quirks and a back tack.
- **Variants**: #quirk-thumb, #fourchette.
- **CLO3D**: skip quirks in simulation unless the web fit is the goal; they are too small for the standard mesh.
- **Confidence**: from technique sheet [T]; shape unverified.

## Gauntlet Glove {#gauntlet}

- **Aliases**: gauntlet, riding gauntlet, welder's gauntlet, flared-cuff glove; fr: gant a crispin, crispin
- **Family**: glove, cuff variant
- **Recognize**: glove hand section ending at the wrist with a flared cuff over the sleeve, 100 to 180 mm long.
- **Pieces**: glove hand pieces as #trank-glove or #work-glove-gunn-cut; gauntlet cuff (x1 or 2 per hand): either extended trank sides or a separate frustum-shaped cuff; cuff lining optional, stiffener optional, strap or elastic closure.
- **Shape**: cuff outline is derived geometry; the join to the hand pieces is unverified. A separate cuff is a conical frustum (flat: a ring sector). With wrist circumference Cw (bottom), top circumference Ct (typically 230 to 280 mm round the cuff), slant length s (100 to 180): outer radius R = s x Ct/(Ct - Cw), inner radius r = R - s, angle = 360 x Ct/(2 pi R) degrees. Seam on the little finger side (outside) or the inner arm side. The cuff sews to the trank at the wrist line; the hand pieces end in a straight line at the wrist. Alternative: extend the trank side lines as one straight line (no kink) and add the flare per #trank-glove.
- **Drafting**: 1. Choose cuff length s and top circumference Ct (check the sleeve passes). 2. Compute R, r, angle. 3. Add a hem 6 mm or a facing 20 to 25 mm. 4. Join to the hand pieces at Cw = wrist girth + 25 (fist). 5. Mark the notches at the side seams.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Make the hand glove. 2. Seam the cuff. 3. Attach the cuff at the wrist. 4. Hem the cuff edge; add closure.
- **Finishes and notions**: outseam, binding, snap or strap with buckle, welt.
- **Fabrics**: heavy leather, split leather, canvas; the cuff may be stiffened.
- **Fit faults**: cuff pushes the sleeve up -> top circumference too small; cuff gapes -> wrist circumference too large.
- **Variants**: #opera-glove (long and snug), #ski-glove (gauntlet over the jacket), #driving-glove (short cuff).
- **CLO3D**: cuff as a separate piece sewn to the wrist; higher bending stiffness for stiffened cuffs.
- **Confidence**: geometry exact; cuff dimensions: technique sheet 1.7 step 6 [T].

## Opera Glove {#opera-glove}

- **Aliases**: long glove, evening glove, elbow glove (12 buttons), shoulder glove; fr: gant long, gant d'opera, mousquetaire (8 buttons)
- **Family**: glove, long
- **Recognize**: glove reaching above the elbow, tapering from the wrist to the upper arm, usually plain, in stretch satin or fine leather.
- **Pieces**: as #trank-glove up to the wrist (trank, thumb, fourchettes), forearm extension of the trank, optional wrist button slit, optional upper edge elastic or silicone grip.
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic from technique sheet 1.7 step 6: the side lines run from the little finger crotch as ONE straight line to the glove end; for long gloves the forearm is not linear, so add one intermediate point and use two straight segments, never a curve with a wrist kink. Glove end: 16 buttons about 406 mm from the thumb web (button length is about 1 inch per button, makers range from 1 to 1.33 inch). Typical size 7 forearm girth at the end about 250 mm (opera), 225 (elbow, 12 buttons), 190 (8 buttons) [T]. The cuff bottom edge is straight, square to the centre line.
- **Drafting**: 1. Measure arm girths at wrist, elbow and planned end. 2. Draw the two segments of the side line. 3. Cuff width at the end = A_end + ease 0 to 10 (stretch leather) or 10 to 20 (non-stretch). 4. Wrist opening: slit on the palm side about 75 mm with 2 to 4 buttons or loops for a mousquetaire. 5. Allowances as in #trank-glove.
- **Sewing order**: as #trank-glove; then close the long side seam, hem or elastic the top.
- **Finishes and notions**: self hem 6 mm, elastic, silicone grip; a row of buttons at the wrist.
- **Fabrics**: stretch satin, jersey, kidskin; stretch fabrics need negative ease in girth.
- **Fit faults**: slips down the arm -> add a silicone grip or a smaller top girth; wrinkles at the wrist -> two segments, not a kink.
- **Variants**: #gauntlet, #trank-glove.
- **CLO3D**: long tube; simulate on the avatar's arm; the avatar arm pose must allow the glove to be pulled on.

## Driving Glove {#driving-glove}

- **Aliases**: motoring glove, keystone-thumb driving glove, open-back driving glove; fr: gant de conduite
- **Family**: glove, leather, close fit
- **Recognize**: wrist-length leather glove, snug, thin, outseam stitching visible on the outside, often a snap at the wrist and perforations or an open back with a strap over the knuckles; the thumb is a wing or keystone-shaped insert.
- **Pieces**: palm (x1 per hand), back (x1), thumb piece(s), fourchette arms (often 0 to 6), wrist tab with snap, knuckle strap if open back.
- **Shape**: unverified. Sources disagree about the keystone thumb: some describe it as a wing-like piece cut with the palm that opens the thumb, others as a reinforced inset thumb that is double sewn, so a drawn pattern must be read before building the thumb. ## Cycling and Fingerless Glove {#cycling-fingerless-glove}

- **Shape**: unverified. Construction logic: the #trank-glove draft cut off at the finger base crease minus 5 mm (fingerless) or at the proximal joint (half-finger), with a hem of 10 mm (technique sheet 1.12). Seams are kept off the pressure zones: palm panel in one piece, seams moved to the sides, thumb hole an oval 20 x 35 at the thumb web height. The back panel is stretch fabric across the knuckles. ## Ski Glove {#ski-glove}

- **Aliases**: snow glove, insulated glove, gauntlet ski glove; fr: gant de ski
- **Family**: glove, sport, insulated, waterproof
- **Recognize**: bulky, pre-curved fingers, a long cuff that goes over the jacket sleeve, wrist strap, nose wipe on the thumb, reinforced palm and fingers.

## Work Glove, Gunn Cut {#work-glove-gunn-cut}

- **Aliases**: gunn pattern, gunn-cut leather palm glove; fr: coupe gunn
- **Family**: glove, work, cut-and-sewn
- **Recognize**: one-piece palm, a seamless back (leather version) or a back with a seam only at the base of the two middle fingers; common on leather work gloves.
- **Pieces**: palm (x1 per hand), back (x1), thumb (x1), separate middle-finger pieces (see Shape), cuff.
- **Shape**: unverified. Sources disagree: Kinco says a seamless back with one seam at the base of the two middle fingers, placed in the natural crease; Palmflex adds that the two middle fingers are sewn separately onto the palm piece. A gunn cut is therefore a palm panel with its fingers plus a back panel, with extra pieces for the middle fingers; the palm has few seams in the grip zone. Do not draw it as a trank without a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Start from a #two-piece-trank draft. 2. Move the seam lines to the back side of the finger bases, as the sources say. 3. Thumb either straight (in line with the index) or wing/keystone type. 4. Allowance 5 to 6 mm (work glove, outseam or inseam) [T].
- **Sewing order**: assemble the finger pieces on the palm, close the back, thumb, cuff.
- **Finishes and notions**: heavy thread, hem or knit wrist, reinforcement welts at the thumb crotch.
- **Fabrics**: cowhide, split leather, goatskin, with cotton or jersey backs.
- **Fit faults**: the seam at the base of the fingers bites -> move it into the crease; palm too tight -> enlarge the width.
- **Variants**: #work-glove-clute-cut, #two-piece-trank.

## Knit Glove (Sewn From Knit Fabric) {#knit-glove}

- **Aliases**: cut-and-sew knit glove, jersey glove, fabric glove; fr: gant en jersey
- **Family**: glove, stretch fabric
- **Recognize**: soft stretch fabric, seams with a 3 mm allowance, a rib or elastic wrist.
- **Pieces**: trank back and palm halves (x2 per hand), thumb (x1), fourchette arms (12 per pair; Shapes of Fabric version), cuff or rib.
- **Shape**: drawn pattern: The Shapes of Fabric (knit glove; W = 200: back I 28, M 26, R 24, L 26 summing to 104; palm I 26, M 24, R 22, L 24 summing to 96; fourchette arm 15 at the base, 5 at the top; thumb hole 3.5 cm). Fingers straight and parallel, thumb hole in the palm. Hand-knitted or machine-knitted gloves are shaped by knitting, not by cutting: do not use this entry for them.

# HATS

Hat notation: H head circumference measured above the eyebrows and ears, round the widest part (women 540 to 580,
men 570 to 620, children 480 to 540). Hat ease: +10 to +15 for a lined woven hat, 0 to -10 % for a knit. Arc F-B
(front to back over the crown) about 0.5 x H + 20. The head is close to an oval, not a circle: oval axes a and b = 0.82 a
with a = H/5.73 (Ramanujan perimeter, H = 570 gives a = 99.5, b = 81.6). Crown and brim geometry below is exact
(cone, cylinder, circle, sphere); styling proportions are trade practice.

## Bucket Hat {#bucket-hat}

- **Aliases**: fisherman hat, rain hat, bob; fr: bob
- **Family**: hat, soft, brimmed, unstructured
- **Recognize**: round flat top, straight slanted crown sides, brim of constant width all round (flat or drooping), seams round the crown base, often topstitched brim in rows.
- **Pieces**: crown top (x1 outer, x1 lining), crown side (x1 outer, x1 lining, seam at the back, or x2), brim (x2: top and under, each ring or ring sectors), interfacing for the brim (x1, optional), sweatband or bias binding, optional eyelets.
- **Shape**: derived geometry. Crown top = disc of radius Rt. Crown side = frustum, flat pattern = ring sector with outer arc length Cb (bottom circumference) and inner arc Ct, so the sector is the unrolled cone: slant s = sqrt(h^2 + (Rb - Rt)^2), outer radius Lout = s x Rb/(Rb - Rt), inner radius Lin = Lout - s, angle alpha = 360 x Rb/Lout. The straight back seam sews edge to edge; the inner arc sews to the top disc (check Ct = 2 pi Rt), the outer arc to the brim inner edge. Brim = flat ring, inner radius Rb, outer radius Rb + B, or, drooping at angle delta below horizontal, a ring sector of angle 360 x cos(delta) with inner radius Cb/(2 pi cos(delta)). Brim ring is cut in 2 or 4 sectors if the fabric width is short; sectors sew edge to edge. Source: technique sheet 2.1 (geometry); the sheet's printed example gave Lout 672, Lin 591 and alpha 49.4, which is slightly off; exact values for H = 570, Cb = 580, h = 80, theta = 8 degrees are Rb = 92.3, Rt = 81.1, Ct = 509.4, s = 80.8, Lout = 663.3, Lin = 582.5, alpha = 50.1 degrees. Outline proportions: unverified as a drawn pattern, geometry exact.
- **Drafting**: 1. Cb = H + 10. 2. Crown height h (vertical side) 70 to 90 [T]; slope theta 6 to 10 degrees [T] gives Ct = Cb - 2 pi h tan(theta). 3. Compute Rb, Rt, s, Lout, Lin, alpha. 4. Brim width B 40 to 60 (droopy 65 to 70) [T]; flat ring or sector formula above. 5. Add 10 SA on the crown seams, 6 on a topstitched brim edge. 6. Mark the back seam, 4 notches round the crown base and brim inner edge. A simple alternative to the cone: a straight band H + 10 long x 80 high, narrowed 15 mm per side at the top.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Crown side seam. 2. Crown to top. 3. Brim seam(s); layer the two brims right sides together and stitch the outer edge, turn, press, topstitch rows 6 to 8 mm apart (3 to 5 rows stiffen). 4. Attach brim to crown. 5. Lining crown, finish with sweatband or bias.
- **Finishes and notions**: topstitched brim, interfacing, optional eyelets, optional chin cord.
- **Fabrics**: cotton twill, denim, canvas, nylon, terry; the heavier the fabric, the fewer rows of stitching the brim needs.
- **Fit faults**: crown does not meet the brim (compare arc lengths Cb vs brim inner arc), brim collapses (interfacing or rows of stitching), too loose (reduce by 10 and add a tuck at the nape).
- **Variants**: #wide-brim-sun-hat (wider, wired brim), #sou-wester (back-weighted brim), #baseball-cap-6-panel.
- **CLO3D**: brim with higher bend stiffness; sew the brim sectors 1:1; mesh 5 mm; position the hat on the head with Pin to avoid slipping.
- **Confidence**: geometry verified by calculation; styling ranges trade practice.

## Baseball Cap, Six Panels {#baseball-cap-6-panel}

- **Aliases**: ball cap, 6-panel cap, dad hat (unstructured, curved bill); fr: casquette de baseball
- **Family**: hat, structured, peaked
- **Recognize**: six gores meeting at a button on top, two front panels stiffened, a curved visor (bill) at the front, a strap or band at the back, a sweatband inside.
- **Pieces**: panels (x6; 2 front panels interfaced), visor (x2 fabric + x1 stiff board), sweatband (x1), lining panels (x6, optional), covered button (x1), eyelets (x2, optional), back strap or closure (x1), optional centre-front seam (two front panels meet at the centre front).
- **Shape**: derived geometry. Each panel is a gore of a dome: width at distance s from the top w(s) = bw x sin(90 degrees x s/S), where bw = (H + 10)/6 and S = (H + 10)/4. For H = 570: bw = 96.7, S = 145; w = 0 at s = 0, 25.9 at 25, 49.8 at 50, 70.2 at 75, 85.4 at 100, 94.4 at 125, 96.7 at 145. Join the points with a smooth curve; the top is cut square at 12 to 15 mm width so the button covers it; the bottom edge is the head line. Real caps are shallower than the hemisphere (use S x 0.92 for a low profile). Visor: inner edge follows the head curve (chord about 2 x bw = 190, curve depth about 20 at the centre), length from the front about 65, outer edge an arc of radius 150 to 170; the stiffener is 6 mm smaller all round. The six panels sew edge to edge in a ring (side seam to side seam), the visor sews to the two front panels' lower edge, the sweatband to the bottom edge. Source: technique sheet 2.2 (geometry, [T] for the visor); visor outline unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. bw = (H + 10)/6. 2. S = (H + 10)/4 (x 0.92 for low profile). 3. Compute w(s) every 10 mm and draw the half-gore, mirror it. 4. Square the top at 12 to 15 mm. 5. Visor arc as above (two pieces plus stiffener). 6. Sweatband strip (H + 10) x 35 folded in half, finished length H. 7. Back opening: leave a 30 to 40 mm gap at the back seam for the strap. 8. SA 10; topstitch seams at 3 mm.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Interface front panels. 2. Sew panel pairs from the base to the top, join pairs, topstitch each seam. 3. Visor: two layers + stiffener, stitched in rows 8 mm apart. 4. Visor to the front panels. 5. Sweatband. 6. Button. 7. Back strap.
- **Finishes and notions**: fusible buckram or heavy interfacing on the front panels, plastic or board stiffener in the visor, eyelets, strap and buckle or snapback.
- **Fabrics**: cotton twill, canvas, wool; the front panels are stiffened.
- **Fit faults**: cap sits too high (panel too long), puckered tip (gore not tapered with a sin curve), visor flips up (too curved).
- **Variants**: #baseball-cap-5-panel, #trucker-cap, #flat-cap-newsboy.
- **CLO3D**: dome from 6 panels simulates well; mesh 5 mm; stiffen the front panels and visor with higher bending stiffness and weight.
- **Confidence**: geometry verified by calculation; visor and strap: trade practice.

## Five-Panel Cap (Camper Hat) {#baseball-cap-5-panel}

- **Aliases**: camper hat, 5-panel hat, cycling cap (similar); fr: casquette cinq panneaux
- **Family**: hat, structured or soft, peaked
- **Recognize**: no centre-front seam: the front is one wide panel; two side panels and two back panels, seams radiating from the top; flat or lightly curved visor.
- **Pieces**: front panel (x1), side panels (x2, one per side), back panels (x2, meeting at the centre-back seam), visor (x2 fabric + x1 stiffener), sweatband (x1), button optional, back strap.
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic: the same sphere gore as #baseball-cap-6-panel, but the circumference H + 10 is split unequally at the base: front panel widest (about 0.30 x (H + 10) at the base, typical, unverified), two sides about 0.20 each, two backs about 0.15 each; each panel width at distance s from the top is its base width x sin(90 degrees x s/S) with S = (H + 10)/4, exactly as for the 6-panel, so the panels still meet at one point. The front panel is one piece (no centre seam), which is why the front fabric can take a print or logo without a seam.
- **Drafting**: 1. Choose the five base widths so they sum to H + 10. 2. Use the sin formula per panel. 3. Square the top. 4. Visor and sweatband as the 6-panel. 5. SA 10.
- **Sewing order**: sides to the front, backs to the sides, close the centre back, topstitch, visor, band.
- **Finishes and notions**: as 6-panel; unstructured: no buckram.
- **Fabrics**: cotton twill, nylon, corduroy; unstructured versions use soft interfacing.
- **Fit faults**: as 6-panel; uneven panel widths twist the crown: check that the sum is H + 10.
- **Variants**: #baseball-cap-6-panel, #trucker-cap.
- **CLO3D**: as 6-panel with five panels.
- **Confidence**: geometry verified; the split of widths is unverified.

## Trucker Cap {#trucker-cap}

- **Aliases**: mesh back cap, foam front cap, snapback; fr: casquette trucker
- **Family**: hat, structured, peaked
- **Recognize**: front panel(s) in solid fabric lined with foam, back and sides in open mesh, plastic snap-back band, curved visor.
- **Pieces**: front panel (x1 or x2 foam-backed), mesh panels (x2 to x4), visor (x2 fabric + x1 stiffener), plastic snap back (x1 pair), sweatband (x1), button optional.
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic: same dome as #baseball-cap-6-panel or #baseball-cap-5-panel, with the front panel(s) in foam-backed twill and the other panels in mesh, plus a strap opening at the centre back for the plastic snap band.
- **Drafting**: 1. Dome per the 6-panel or 5-panel formula. 2. Leave the back open: back panels end with a 30 to 40 mm gap for the snap band, ends reinforced. 3. Foam: laminated; seam allowance 6 for foam-backed panels. 4. Mesh panels need a stay tape on the seams.
- **Sewing order**: front assembly, mesh panels, join, visor, band, snap back.
- **Finishes and notions**: bound seams, plastic snap strap, visor board.
- **Fabrics**: foam-backed cotton/polyester, polyester mesh.
- **Fit faults**: mesh bags out -> stay tape; front collapses -> thicker foam.
- **Variants**: #baseball-cap-6-panel, #baseball-cap-5-panel.
- **CLO3D**: assign a mesh-like light fabric to the back panels and a stiff fabric to the front.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; shape unverified.

## Beret {#beret}

- **Aliases**: Basque beret, French beret; fr: beret
- **Family**: hat, soft, brimless
- **Recognize**: flat circular disc, slight dome, a small stalk (tail) at the centre, an inner band, sits tilted on the head.
- **Pieces**: top (x1, circle diameter D_o), under (x1, ring with the same outer diameter and a centre opening D_i), band (x1, bias or knit strip), optional lining, optional stalk.
- **Shape**: derived geometry (technique sheet 2.3). Top is a plain circle. Under piece: circle of the same outer diameter with a circular opening D_i. The two sew right sides together along the outer circle, then turn through a gap; the band is sewn to the opening. Traditional berets are knitted or felted in a single seamless disc; this is the cut-and-sew version.
- **Drafting**: 1. D_i = (H + 10)/pi (H = 570: 184). 2. D_o = 260 to 290 for an adult (the larger, the slouchier), typical 270. 3. Band strip 25 wide x (H + 10), folded in half, sewn to the opening. 4. Optional stalk: a roll of 25 x 25.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Stitch top and under right sides together along the outer edge leaving a gap. 2. Turn, press, topstitch near the edge. 3. Attach the band to the opening. 4. Tack the stalk.
- **Finishes and notions**: bias band or elastic, leather trim at the opening.
- **Fabrics**: felted wool (traditional), wool jersey, velvet; heavy fabric needs a smaller D_o.
- **Fit faults**: beret sags (outer circle too large), tight at the opening.
- **Variants**: #pillbox-hat (structured), #beanie.
- **CLO3D**: set the opening to a head circumference; low stiffness; let it drape on the head.

## Tote Bag (Flat) {#tote-bag}

- **Aliases**: shopping bag, flat tote, canvas tote; fr: sac cabas plat, tote bag

- **Pieces**: body (x2, or x1 folded at the bottom), handles (x2), lining (x2, 10 shorter), interfacing (x2, fusible fleece or stabiliser), optional inner pocket (x1).
- **Shape**: derived geometry. Body panel = rectangle W + 20 wide x Ht + 20 high (10 SA each side). Handles are placed 70 to 120 from the side seams on the right side of the front and back panels and stitched with a box-X. ## Gusseted Tote {#gusseted-tote}

- **Aliases**: tote with side gussets, shopper, structured tote, bucket bag (when round); fr: sac cabas a soufflet
- **Family**: bag, soft or structured
- **Recognize**: a distinct bottom and side panel, a flat base, straight sides, handles.
- **Pieces**: front and back (x2), gusset (x1 strip, or x2 sides + x1 bottom), bottom board (x1, optional), handles (x2), lining (same pieces), interfacing.
- **Shape**: derived geometry (technique sheet 3.2). Front and back panels W x Ht. Gusset strip width D and length = 2 (Ht - r) + (W - 2 r) + pi r for a U-shaped path with corner radius r (r = 0 gives W + 2 Ht). Lower panel corners are rounded with radius r = 20 to 40 [T] when the gusset follows the same path length. The gusset sews all round the panels (edge to edge), the front and back are not seamed to each other. Alternative: a boxed corner (#boxed-corner-pouch) without a gusset piece.
- **Drafting**: 1. Panel size. 2. Gusset width D and length from the formula (measure the panel perimeter to confirm). 3. Corner radius. 4. Lining as above 10 shorter, patch pocket 150 x 180 [T]. 5. SA 10.

- **Finishes and notions**: bottom board, feet, piped seams, zipper.
- **Fabrics**: canvas, leather, waxed cotton; stiff fabric with high bend.
- **Fit faults**: gusset length mismatch (measure the panel perimeter), baggy corners (interface the bottom).
- **Variants**: #tote-bag, #boxed-corner-pouch, #duffel-bag.
- **CLO3D**: stiff fabric with high bending; add pressure or use pins to inflate the form.
- **Confidence**: geometry exact; ranges technique sheet [T].

## Necktie, Three-Fold {#necktie}

- **Aliases**: tie, standard lined tie, four-in-hand tie, silk tie; fr: cravate
- **Family**: neckwear
- **Recognize**: long strip widening to a blade, a narrow neck section, a tail, stripes running diagonally, a bar tack at the back, a keeper loop, a slip-stitched back seam.
- **Pieces**: blade (large end, x1), neck/centre section (x1, optional), tail (small end, x1), all cut on the bias and joined by 45 degree seams; interlining (x1, wool or cotton on the bias); tipping (x2, lining at both ends); keeper loop (x1).
- **Shape**: derived plus trade practice (technique sheet part 5.1). Finished length 1450; blade width 80 to 95 (slim 50 to 65), neck width 30 to 40, tail end 50 to 60. Profile [T]: width at the blade end 90, constant to 200, taper to 35 at 800 from the blade tip, constant 35 to 1150, widen to 55 at the tail end (1450). Cut with the length axis on the true bias (45 degrees to the grain). The pieces end in cuts that follow the grain, so that adjacent pieces join with a diagonal (45 degree) seam and the stripe pattern continues. Interlining is the finished shape minus 3 mm all round, also on the bias. The edges fold to the centre in three layers across (the "three-fold") and are slip-stitched loosely with a continuous thread, leaving slack so the tie stretches. Outline of the real three-piece cutting layout: unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Profile on a long strip. 2. Cut line: bias direction. 3. Separate into pieces at the 45 degree seam lines. 4. Add SA 10 on the joins and 6 to 10 on the edges that fold. 5. Interlining slightly smaller.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Join the pieces by 45 degree seams, press open. 2. Attach the tipping. 3. Place the interlining. 4. Fold the edges to the centre. 5. Slip-stitch loosely. 6. Add the keeper and the bar tack.
- **Finishes and notions**: tipping, keeper, bar tack, wool interlining (slip stitch thread).
- **Fabrics**: silk twill, wool, cotton, knit; bias cut keeps the tie from twisting.
- **Fit faults**: tie twists (not true bias), neck width too wide, tie too short for a large neck (lengthen by about twice the extra neck girth).
- **Variants**: #seven-fold-tie, #bow-tie, #cravat-ascot.
- **CLO3D**: set the grain at 45 degrees; mesh 5 mm; model the tie as a tapered strip, folds are not simulated.
- **Confidence**: profile and dimensions technique sheet [T]; piece layout from the sheet; hand construction details from trade practice.

The seam sews the two short edges together, right sides together; for a tube-lined version the long edges are sewn first, turned, then the ends closed.
- **Drafting**: 1. Width 280 (one layer), length 1500. 2. SA 10. 3. Seam position inside.
- **Sewing order**: join the short ends, turn, press.
- **Finishes and notions**: hidden seam, optional lining.
- **Fabrics**: jersey, wool knit, fleece, silk.
- **Fit faults**: twist of the loop (half twist for Moebius), too bulky (single layer).
- **Variants**: #scarf, #balaclava.
- **CLO3D**: tube; knit preset.
- **Confidence**: technique sheet [T].

## Face Mask (Fabric) {#face-mask}

- **Aliases**: community mask, cloth mask, pleated mask, fitted mask; fr: masque en tissu
- **Family**: accessory, fabric face covering (not a medical device)
- **Recognize**: pleated or shaped panel covering nose and mouth, with ear loops or ties, optional nose wire.
- **Pieces**: outer (x1), inner (x1), filter pocket (optional), ear loops (x2, 5 mm flat elastic, 170 to 190 each, or ties 2 x 450), nose wire (x1, 90 to 100 mm).
- **Shape**: technique sheet part 8 [T]. Pleated mask: finished 175 wide x 95 high; three pleats of depth 12.5 each use 75 of height; cut height = 95 + 75 + 20 = 190, cut width = 175 + 20 = 195, as two layers. Fitted (shaped) mask: centre seam with a curve of 30 to 40 depth, finished 160 x 100. The nose wire sits in a casing at the top edge; pleats face downward on the outside. Rectangles: exact; shaped version: unverified as a drawn pattern. The numbers follow common community masks; verify with local regulation if filtering is required.
- **Drafting**: 1. Pleated rectangle. 2. Fitted: centre seam curve. 3. Add 10 SA. 4. Elastic loops.
- **Sewing order**: outer and inner right sides together, sew top and bottom edges, turn, press, pleat, topstitch the sides folding in the elastic.
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic, wire, filter pocket.
- **Fabrics**: tightly woven cotton, 2 to 3 layers.
- **Fit faults**: gaps at the cheeks (wire too short), tight on the ears (loop too short).
- **Variants**: #balaclava, #snood.
- **CLO3D**: not meaningful.
- **Confidence**: technique sheet [T]; shape of fitted mask unverified.

## Slippers {#slippers}

- **Aliases**: house shoes, moccasin slippers, felt slippers, mules; fr: chaussons, pantoufles
- **Family**: footwear, soft
- **Recognize**: sole with an upper in soft fabric, often with a seam over the toes and a heel seam, no hard heel.
- **Pieces**: sole (x2 per slipper: outer, insole), upper (x1 to x2), lining, foam, heel counter optional.
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic: sole = foot outline plus 3 to 5 SA; upper = a vamp (toe cover) and a quarter (heel part) or a single wrap; the upper's lower edge is longer than the sole's edge by the amount of the ease, and is sewn to the sole's edge (stitched through or overlocked). Generic approaches (felt slipper): sole cut from the foot tracing; upper from the foot's girth at the instep and ball.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace the foot standing. 2. Offset 5 to 8 for the sole. 3. Upper length from the tracing: ball girth + 10 and instep girth + 10. 4. SA 6 to 10. All numbers unverified.
- **Sewing order**: close the upper, attach to the sole, turn.
- **Finishes and notions**: non-slip suede sole, foam insole, binding.
- **Fabrics**: felt, fleece, terry, leather.
- **Fit faults**: heel slips (quarter too low), toe cramped (add ease).
- **Variants**: #espadrille-upper.
- **CLO3D**: limited for footwear; not recommended.
- **Confidence**: from memory; unverified.

## Espadrille Upper (Basics) {#espadrille-upper}

- **Aliases**: espadrille, rope-sole shoe, alpargata; fr: espadrille
- **Family**: footwear, soft
- **Recognize**: canvas upper stitched onto a braided jute sole, ties around the ankle optional.
- **Pieces**: vamp (x1), quarter or heel (x1 or x2), lining (same), toe reinforcement, ankle ties optional, rope sole (bought).
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic: the upper is a vamp plus a back quarter whose lower edges sew to the sole's top edge (stitched through the braided jute, whipstitched, or glued); the upper's lower edge must match the sole's outline plus ease. The jute sole determines the size; trace the sole to find the upper's base outline.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace the sole outline. 2. Draw the vamp from the toe to the instep and the quarter around the heel. 3. SA 10 at the lower edge for lasting. 4. Lining the same.
- **Sewing order**: sew vamp and quarter, line, stitch to the sole.
- **Finishes and notions**: jute sole, heavy thread, ties.
- **Fabrics**: canvas, linen, cotton duck.
- **Fit faults**: upper gapes at the side (add width), heel slips (raise the quarter).
- **Variants**: #slippers.
- **CLO3D**: limited.
- **Confidence**: from memory; unverified.

## Detachable Collar {#detachable-collar}

- **Aliases**: separate collar, shirt collar (stiff), wing collar, stand collar, Eton collar, collar and cuffs; fr: col amovible, faux col
- **Family**: accessory, shirtmaking
- **Recognize**: a stiff collar sold separately, attached to the shirt neckband with studs through button holes at the front and back.
- **Pieces**: collar band (x2 layers + interlining), collar leaf or points (x2 + interlining), buttonholes (front and back), neck stud holes (x2).
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic: like a shirt stand collar (band) but sized to the neck in inches; the collar has two layers with a stiff interlining (starched) and button holes at the centre front and back of the band. Wing collars have folded points. Neck sizes in steps of half an inch (e.g. 14, 14.5, 15 in) [T, unverified]; the finished collar sits above the shirt neckband, so its length is the neckband length + about 10 to 15 mm.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take the shirt neckband length. 2. Stand height 25 to 38 [T]. 3. Collar leaf per the shirt collar. 4. Buttonholes: one front at each side, one back.
- **Sewing order**: interface, sew the layers right sides together, turn, press, topstitch, buttonholes.
- **Finishes and notions**: starch, stiffener, buttonholes, collar studs.
- **Fabrics**: cotton poplin or pique with fusible stiffener or traditional starch.
- **Fit faults**: neck too tight (size up by half), collar gapes (studs too far).
- **Variants**: shirt collars (see #necktie for the neckwear).
- **CLO3D**: stiff fabric; sew onto the neckband with pins.
- **Confidence**: from memory; unverified.

## Arm and Leg Warmers {#arm-leg-warmers}

- **Aliases**: arm warmers, leg warmers, knit sleeves, boot cuffs, gaiters (loosely); fr: manchettes, guetres, jambieres
- **Family**: accessory, knit tube
- **Recognize**: tapered tube of knit worn on the forearm or leg, no foot or hand, often with a thumb hole for the arm warmer.
- **Pieces**: tube (x1 per warmer: a trapezoid with a side seam), optional thumb hole, rib cuffs (x2).
- **Shape**: derived geometry plus trade practice. A trapezoid whose bottom edge equals the wrist (or ankle) circumference x 0.9 and top edge equals the upper arm (or calf) circumference x 0.9, length 300 to 400 for an arm warmer and 400 to 600 for a leg warmer [T, unverified]. Side seam joins the long edges. The thumb hole on an arm warmer is an oval or a gap in the side seam, 20 to 30 long, about 70 to 80 from the lower edge. Fabrics with 40 to 60 % stretch use negative ease of 10 %.
- **Drafting**: 1. Measure the girths at the start, middle and end. 2. Take 90 % of each. 3. Trapezoid with the side seam on the straight grain. 4. Rib cuff or hem at both ends. 5. SA 8.
- **Sewing order**: sew the side seam, make the thumb gap, attach the cuffs or hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: stretch stitch, rib, elastic.
- **Fabrics**: rib knit, jersey, wool, cashmere knit.
- **Fit faults**: slips down (elastic at the top), too tight at the elbow or knee (add width).
- **Variants**: #knit-glove, #cycling-fingerless-glove.
- **CLO3D**: knit preset, tube from a trapezoid, pin at the top.
- **Confidence**: geometry exact; sizes unverified.
