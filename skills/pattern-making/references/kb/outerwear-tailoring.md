# Outerwear and tailoring knowledge base

Entry-per-garment database for jackets, coats, capes, sport outerwear and the components they share
(lapels, sleeves, pockets, flaps, hoods, linings, canvas). Format: `FORMAT.md`. Methods and number tables
live in `../techniques/tailoring-outerwear.md` (cited as TO section) and `../techniques/sleeves-collars.md`
(cited as SC section); this file does not repeat them in full, it fixes the construction of each garment.

How to read the evidence:
- Piece lists marked "FreeSewing" come from the published cutting lists of the open FreeSewing designs
  Jaeger (blazer), Carlton (coat) and Wahid (waistcoat). Their outlines were not examined.
- No entry in this file was taken from a fully drawn pattern outline. Every Shape field is therefore
  "unverified" as an outline: it states the usual trade construction (pieces, anchors, which edge sews to which)
  so that an agent can build the pieces, and it must be checked on a toile before it is trusted.
- Numbers are typical working values in cm (full round unless "half"), taken from the two technique sheets
  or from trade practice. "Typical" means a range to confirm on a toile in the real fabric.
- Anchors used throughout: NP neck point (shoulder/neck), SP shoulder point, UP underarm (armhole bottom),
  CF centre front, CB centre back, WL waist line, BP break point (first working button, where the lapel
  folds), AH armhole.

## Blazer, women's {#blazer-womens}

- **Aliases**: fitted blazer, women's tailored jacket; fr: blazer femme, veste tailleur femme
- **Family**: tailoring, jackets
- **Recognize**: notch lapel with a visible roll, princess seams (or waist darts plus side back seam), welt or flap or patch pockets, two-piece sleeve with 3 to 4 cuff buttons, lining, 1 to 3 buttons, hip length
- **Pieces**: centre front panel (2), side front panel (2), back (2, CB seam), side back (2), top sleeve (2), under sleeve (2), top collar (1), undercollar (2, CB seam, bias), front facing (2), back neck facing (1), pockets as chosen (welt/flap/patch 2), chest piece and canvas (2), lining front (2), lining back (2), lining sleeve (4), pocket bags (4)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction; piece set cross-checked with the FreeSewing Jaeger cutting list: fronts, front facings, backs, side panels, top/under sleeves, collar, collar stand, undercollar, hip pockets, chest welt, canvas fronts and chest pieces).
  Centre front panel: bounded by the front edge (CF plus wrap 2.0 to 2.5, lapel above BP), the neck/gorge line, the shoulder seam from NP to a point about 1/3 of the shoulder length from NP (princess starts here) or, for an armhole princess, the shoulder is whole and the seam starts on the AH about 5 to 7 in from the shoulder point. The princess seam runs from that start, convex through the bust point, to WL and down to the hem. Side front panel: the mating concave edge, plus the lower AH and the side seam. Side seam of the side front sews to the side back; the side back top sits on the back AH about 7 to 9 below the shoulder line (see #suit-jacket-single-breasted). Back: CB seam shaped 1.0 to 1.5 in at WL. Back shoulder is 1.0 to 1.5 longer than the front shoulder and eased to it.
  Both halves of a princess seam are equal in length on the pattern (the bust shaping is steamed in), the front shoulder dart is absorbed into the princess seam.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Adapt the women's block per TO 1.3 (E_target 8 to 12 on bust 92, underarm lowered 1.0 to 1.5, shoulder raised by pad thickness, neck widened 0.5). Close the shoulder dart into the princess seam.
  2. Waist: jacket waist = body waist + 4 to 8 (typical). Distribute the suppression over CB (1.0 to 1.5), side back seam, princess seam and side seam.
  3. Draw the princess seam as one smooth curve with no corner, 0.5 to 1.0 hollow on the side front waist.
  4. Front edge, break point, lapel and collar per #notched-lapel; hem per TO 1.5 (58 to 70 from nape).
  5. Sleeve per #two-piece-sleeve with women's values (cap height 12.5 to 14.5, cap ease 2.5 to 4.0 in wool).
  6. Check each seam pair for equal length, then the armhole against the sleeve cap.
- **Sewing order**:
  1. Fuse hem, back stay, facing; cut notches.
  2. Sew princess seams and press over a ham.
  3. Pockets, then canvas/chest piece, then side back and CB seams.
  4. Shoulder seams (ease back to front), collar, facing, turn, sleeves, pads, lining, hem, buttonholes (TO 2.8).
- **Finishes and notions**: 1.0 allowance on princess seams, 1.5 on side seams, hem 4 to 5, sleeve hem 3.5 to 4.0, shoulder pads 5 to 15 mm, sleeve heads, tapes (TO 5.3), buttons 18 to 22 mm front, 15 to 18 sleeve
- **Fabrics**: wool crepe, gabardine, tweed, stretch wool (reduce ease by 2 to 4), linen (needs interfacing and a lining, add cap ease 1.5 to 2.0 only); nap or stripe: match at CF and princess seams
- **Fit faults**: bust pulls across -> add width at the princess seam, not at the side; collar gaps at the back neck -> shorten the collar neck edge; hem flares -> deepen waist suppression; drag lines from the bust to the AH -> release the princess seam at the armhole end
- **Variants**: #blazer-mens, #suit-jacket-single-breasted, #double-breasted-jacket, #chanel-jacket, #tailored-coat
- **CLO3D**: one piece per panel, sew princess seams with equal lengths, roll line as a fold line, lining as separate pieces on the inside, mesh about 8 mm; place shoulder pad and sleeve pitch by hand on the avatar
- **Confidence**: piece set verified in part (FreeSewing Jaeger cutting list); outlines and numbers trade practice (TO 2, 4); not verified on a drawn pattern

## Blazer, men's {#blazer-mens}

- **Aliases**: sports jacket, navy blazer, odd jacket; fr: blazer homme, veston sport
- **Family**: tailoring, jackets
- **Recognize**: like a suit jacket but unmatched and slightly more casual: often three patch pockets or flap pockets, metal or crested buttons (2 or 3 single-breasted, 6x2 double-breasted), soft shoulder, side vents or centre vent
- **Pieces**: front (2), side body (2), back (2, CB seam), top sleeve (2), under sleeve (2), top collar (1), undercollar (2), front facing (2), patch/flap pockets (2 to 3 plus flaps), breast pocket (1), canvas and chest piece (2), lining pieces, pocket bags (FreeSewing Jaeger list: fronts, front facings, backs, sides, top/under sleeves, collar, collar stand, pocket, chest welt, canvas fronts, chest pieces, lining incl. inner pocket welts and bags)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction; piece list from the FreeSewing Jaeger cutting list).
  Same body layout as #suit-jacket-single-breasted: front with dart or front side seam, side body between front and back, back with CB seam. Differences are surface: patch pockets are rectangles sewn on the front (hip pocket 16 to 19 wide by 18 to 22 deep, corners radius 1.5 to 2.5, top hem 3 to 4, breast pocket 10 to 12 by 12 to 14), sleeve cuffs often carry 3 to 4 buttons at 2.0 to 2.2 spacing (kissing or working). The collar is cut with a separate stand in the FreeSewing set (collar plus collar stand plus undercollar).
- **Drafting**:
  1. Men's block per TO 1.3 (E_target 12 to 16 on chest 100, underarm lowered 1.0 to 1.5).
  2. Length about 0.41 x height (72 to 78 from nape), vent per TO 2.7 (centre 24 to 30 or double 20 to 25).
  3. Patch pockets: mark on the front with a template at the pocket line, front end at least 5 behind the front edge, parallel to the hem.
  4. Lapel, collar, sleeve as #notched-lapel, #two-piece-sleeve.
- **Sewing order**: as the jacket (TO 2.8); patch pockets are sewn on the flat front before the side seams
- **Finishes and notions**: patch pocket corners bar-tacked or turned with a template, metal shank buttons 20 to 25 mm front and 15 to 18 sleeve, half or full canvas or fused, lining, shoulder pad 5 to 10 mm
- **Fabrics**: navy hopsack, flannel, tweed, cotton twill, linen; stripe match on patch pockets means cutting the pocket from the same stripe position as the front
- **Fit faults**: as TO 2.9; patch pockets gape -> reduce depth or cut 0.3 narrower at the mouth; pockets sag -> interface the full bag area
- **Variants**: #suit-jacket-single-breasted, #double-breasted-jacket, #blazer-womens, #field-jacket-m65 (for utility pockets)
- **CLO3D**: patch pockets as separate pieces sewn on the front (edges sewn with 0 gap); lapel roll via fold line; mesh 8 mm
- **Confidence**: piece list verified in part (FreeSewing Jaeger); outline and numbers trade practice

## Suit jacket, single-breasted {#suit-jacket-single-breasted}

- **Aliases**: lounge jacket, business suit jacket, sack coat (historic); fr: veste de costume, veston
- **Family**: tailoring, jackets
- **Recognize**: matching trousers, notch lapel, 2 or 3 buttons (2 or 3 roll 2), flap hip pockets and a left chest welt, two-piece sleeve with working or decorative buttons, centre or double vents, shoulder pad
- **Pieces**: front (2), side body (2), back (2), top sleeve (2), under sleeve (2), top collar (1), undercollar (2), front facing (2), back neck facing (1), chest welt (1), hip flaps (4), jets (2 sets), pocket bags (4), chest piece/canvas (2 each), lining front (2), lining back (2), lining sleeve (4), inner pocket bags (2 to 3) (TO 2.2)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction, TO 2.3.1 and 2.5).
  Front: edges are the front edge (CF + 2.5 wrap, square or rounded or cutaway hem), the lapel/gorge/neck line, the shoulder seam (sewn to the back shoulder, back 1.0 to 1.5 longer and eased), the front armhole (down to just in front of the underarm point UP), the front side seam (from the AH near UP to the hem, sewn to the front edge of the side body) and the hem. The front dart (belly or fish dart, 1.2 to 2.0 wide) sits on the pocket line from about 3 to 5 below the pocket mouth to a point 10 to 12 below the chest line, or the dart is replaced by a front side seam.
  Side body: a long narrow piece, top edge on the armhole around UP, two long edges: the front edge sews to the front side seam, the back edge sews to the back side seam; waist 1/2 back width; flares 1.5 to 2.5 each side at the hem.
  Back: CB edge (spine curve 1.5 to 2.0 in at WL, out 1.5 to 2.0 at the hem for the vent), the back neck, the back shoulder, the back armhole down to the side-back seam start (about 7 to 9 below the shoulder line, at least 6 above UP), the side back seam sewn to the side body back edge, the hem with vent extension (right overlap 3.5 to 4.5).
  Top sleeve and under sleeve per #two-piece-sleeve.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Block adapted per TO 1.3 (chest E_target 14 to 18 classic), waist line, length 72 to 78 from nape.
  2. Waist suppression S total 8 to 14 (fitted) split: CB 1.0 to 1.5, back side seam 1.0 to 1.5, side body to front 1.0 to 1.5, front dart or front side seam 1.5 to 2.5 (per half).
  3. Front edge and button stance: TO 2.4 (2 buttons: top at WL, second 9 to 10 lower; 3 buttons: top 8.5 to 9.5 above WL).
  4. Lapel and collar #notched-lapel; pockets #jetted-pocket, #flap-pocket, #welt-pocket; vent per TO 2.7.
  5. Sleeve: #two-piece-sleeve; check the AH against cap length (AH + ease).
- **Sewing order**: TO 2.8 (steps 1 to 17)
- **Finishes and notions**: canvas (full, half or fused, #interfacing-canvas-plan), shoulder pad 8 to 10 mm, sleeve head 3.0 to 4.5 wide, roll and edge tapes, hem canvas 4 to 6 deep, lining with CB pleat 1.5 to 2.0 (#lining-patterns), horn or corozo buttons 22 to 25 front / 15 to 18 sleeve
- **Fabrics**: worsted wool (cap ease 2.5 to 4.0), flannel, tweed; firm or synthetic: cap ease 1.5 to 2.0; stripes and checks matched at CF, pockets and collar
- **Fit faults**: TO 2.9 (collar stands away, gaping lapel, hem flare, pad ridge, cap puckers)
- **Variants**: #double-breasted-jacket, #tuxedo-dinner-jacket, #blazer-mens, #blazer-womens
- **CLO3D**: panels separate, fold line at the lapel roll, lining and canvas as extra layers, mesh 8 mm, collision thickness 0
- **Confidence**: construction logic and numbers trade practice (TO 2, aligned with Aldrich/Cabrera as cited in TO); not verified on a drawn outline

## Double-breasted jacket {#double-breasted-jacket}

- **Aliases**: DB jacket, 6x2, 4x1 DB, 6x1; fr: veste croisée, croisé
- **Family**: tailoring, jackets
- **Recognize**: two columns of buttons (4 to 6 buttons plus often a hidden inner button), wide overlap, usually peak lapels (notch also exists), no front dart on the pattern ("full chest drape"), often side vents
- **Pieces**: as the single-breasted jacket (front, side body, back, sleeves, collar, facings) with a wider front; front (2), side body (2), back (2), top/under sleeve (2+2), top collar (1), undercollar (2), facing (2), welt and flaps, lining
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction, TO 2.4).
  Front: the front edge is CF + 7.5 to 9 (wrap) for the overlapping side; the underlap side carries the same width (the garment is symmetric in the pattern, only buttonholes differ). Button columns 10 to 11 apart (each 5 to 5.5 from CF), the outer column 2.5 to 3 from the edge. BP at the top button of the working pair. The lapel edge runs from BP up and out; peak lapel gorge higher (6 to 8 above the shoulder, TO 2.6). The front hem is square or slightly rounded; the front dart is replaced by a front side seam or the suppression is taken at the side body.
- **Drafting**:
  1. As #suit-jacket-single-breasted, then wrap CF + 7.5 to 9.
  2. Button spacing: 6-button (2 to close) pairs at 10 to 12 intervals; 4-button (1 to close) 12 to 14.
  3. Lapel width 8 to 10, gorge per #peak-lapel; collar and rolling point at the top working button.
  4. Add 0.5 to the front length for hem balance because a wide front drops.
  5. Inside button/loop to secure the underlap (trade; position unverified).
- **Sewing order**: as TO 2.8; buttonholes on the correct front (men: right front outer column, women: left), working holes only on the closing pair
- **Finishes and notions**: buttons 22 to 25 mm (dummy buttons same size), canvas through the wide lapel, facing 8 to 10 wide at the chest
- **Fabrics**: as suit jacket; firm cloth holds the wide overlap; avoid sheer fabrics because the overlap shows through
- **Fit faults**: overlap gapes at the hem -> add ease at the side body; front drops/flares -> raise front hem 0.5 to 1.0, shorten the front; lapel rolls too low -> raise BP
- **Variants**: #suit-jacket-single-breasted, #reefer-jacket, #pea-coat, #peak-lapel, #blazer-mens
- **CLO3D**: the extra wrap is part of the front piece; place buttons by hand; both button columns are decoration except the working pair
- **Confidence**: dimensions trade practice (TO 2.4); button logic standard; inner button detail unverified

## Tuxedo / dinner jacket {#tuxedo-dinner-jacket}

- **Aliases**: dinner jacket, black-tie jacket, smoking jacket (shawl), DJ; fr: smoking, veste de smoking
- **Family**: tailoring, evening jackets
- **Recognize**: shawl collar or peak lapel faced in silk satin or grosgrain, one button (sometimes two), jetted pockets (no flaps), covered buttons, usually no vents, sleeve cuffs with 3 or 4 buttons or none
- **Pieces**: as the single-breasted suit jacket, with a facing-collar combination: front (2), side body (2), back (2), sleeves (2+2), shawl upper collar and facing in one (2, CB seam) or peak lapel with silk-faced lapel (2), undercollar (2, felt), jets (2 sets) plus silk jets/besom, chest welt optional, pocket bags, canvas, lining (Minerva 6080 lists shawl collar cut with the front and facing, usually satin or silk, jetted/welt pockets)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction; shawl facing logic from SC 2.6).
  Shawl: front edge continues in one curve from the single button to the neck, no gorge seam; the roll line runs from BP (button) to a point 2 to 2.5 beyond NP on the extended shoulder line; the lapel edge is 8 to 11 wide at the shoulder, curving round; the upper collar is the lapel reflected across the roll line, so front + collar is a single piece with the front facing and a CB seam at the back neck; undercollar has a CB seam and attaches to the body back neck. Facing silk is cut on the same shape to the roll line and covers the lapel face. Peak variant: see #peak-lapel with a silk lapel face.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Jacket body as #suit-jacket-single-breasted without vents; front edge CF + 2.0 to 2.5; one button at WL.
  2. Shawl collar per #shawl-collar (spring 2 to 3 at CB, neck edge = back neck length).
  3. Jetted pockets only, 15.5 to 17 mouth for the hip (#jetted-pocket).
  4. Silk facing: cut to the roll line plus 0.3 to 0.5 turn of cloth.
  5. Sleeve as #two-piece-sleeve, vent optional.
- **Sewing order**: as TO 2.8, but the silk facing is applied after the canvas; buttons covered in silk (turn the jacket buttons last)
- **Finishes and notions**: silk satin or grosgrain facing and jets, covered buttons 22 to 25 mm, half or full canvas, lined in satin
- **Fabrics**: barathea, wool mohair, midnight blue or black; silk faces are slippery and bias-sensitive, interface the facing, do not stretch the silk on the roll
- **Fit faults**: shawl collar stands away from the neck -> shorten the neck edge 0.5, increase spring; silk faces ripple -> underlay fusible or hair canvas; lapel pulls -> add turn of cloth
- **Variants**: #suit-jacket-single-breasted, #shawl-collar, #peak-lapel, #opera-coat
- **CLO3D**: facing as a separate silk-property piece under the front; fold line at the roll; mesh 8 mm
- **Confidence**: shawl and facing logic from SC 2.6 (trade practice); vents/button style from retail and pattern descriptions (Minerva 6080, Gentleman's Gazette); outline unverified

## Nehru jacket {#nehru-jacket}

- **Aliases**: mandarin jacket, Mao jacket, achkan-style, band-collar jacket, bandhgala (fr: veste Nehru, veste col Mao)
- **Family**: tailoring, jackets (stand collar)
- **Recognize**: close-fitting, hip to thigh length, stand (band) collar 3 to 4 high, front buttoned from neck to hem or waist, no lapel, usually 5 to 7 buttons, two-piece or one-piece sleeve
- **Pieces**: front (2), side front or side panel (2, optional), back (2, CB seam), sleeve (2, or top/under 2+2), stand collar (2 outer + 1 or 2 interfacing, see SC 2.2), front facing (2), pockets (welt or slash 2), lining
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction; collar from SC 2.2).
  Body: tailored front without lapel; the front edge runs straight from the neck at CF (plus a button extension of 1.5 to 2.0) to the hem. Neckline is raised at the front (about 0.5) because the collar stands. Collar: a curved band of length N (neckline, CB to CF), lower edge curved up 1.5 to 2.5 at the CF end, height 3 to 4 (low 1.5 to 2.5), front corners rounded 1.5 to 2.0; sewn to the neckline along its lower edge, the lining collar to the facing.
  Princess or dart and side seams as in a fitted jacket; hem straight or with two side slits (10 to 15).
- **Drafting**:
  1. Block adapted per TO 1.3 (fitted jacket ease), neck width 0.5 wider, front neck raised 0.5.
  2. Straight front edge with extension 1.5 to 2.0; buttons evenly spaced (n buttons: s = distance / (n - 1)).
  3. Collar: SC 2.2 mandarin steps (N, rise 1.5 to 2.5, height 3 to 4, top edge shorter).
  4. Sleeve: two-piece (#two-piece-sleeve) for tailored versions.
  5. Pockets: slanted welt at the pocket line, 14 to 17 mouth.
- **Sewing order**: darts/panels, pockets, side and shoulder seams, collar, facing, sleeves, lining, hem, buttonholes
- **Finishes and notions**: interfaced collar (hair canvas for a firm band), front facing 3.5 to 4.5, covered or metal buttons 15 to 20 mm
- **Fabrics**: wool, silk dupioni, cotton; embroidered or brocade fronts need extra stabilising
- **Fit faults**: collar pops away -> increase rise 0.5 and shorten the top edge; collar chokes -> lengthen N 0.5 to 1.0; gaping at the neck front -> raise the front neck 0.5
- **Variants**: #chanel-jacket (collarless), #safari-jacket, #suit-jacket-single-breasted
- **CLO3D**: collar sewn to the neckline, check no penetration at the neck; mesh 8 mm
- **Confidence**: collar numbers from SC 2.2 (trade practice); body construction trade practice; unverified outline

## Chanel-type jacket {#chanel-jacket}

- **Aliases**: Chanel cardigan jacket, collarless tweed jacket, boxy braid-trimmed jacket; fr: veste type Chanel, veste en tweed
- **Family**: couture tailoring, women's jackets
- **Recognize**: collarless round or V neck, fringed or braid-trimmed edges, patch pockets (often four), gilt buttons, lining quilted to each fabric piece, chain weight in the hem, three-piece sleeve in the couture version (Threads/Shaeffer), princess or panel seams
- **Pieces**: centre front (2), side front (2), back (2, CB seam), side back (2), sleeve pieces (2 sets, three-piece in the quilted version, two-piece also used), pockets (2 to 4 patch), lining pieces cut as the shell (each quilted to its shell piece), braid trim, chain
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade/couture construction; chain, quilting and three-piece sleeve reported in Threads/Shaeffer descriptions).
  Each body piece is a shell panel with a lining panel of the same outline quilted to it; the quilting lines are vertical, parallel to the grain, spaced by the lining/fabric pairing (spacing not known, to confirm). The jacket opens at CF with a faced or braid-trimmed edge and no lapel; neckline round, 0 to 1 below the neck. The hem carries a flat chain weight stitched into the seam allowance of the hem.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Fitted women's jacket block with moderate ease (TO 1.2: 8 to 12), neckline lowered 0.5 to 1.0 and widened, no collar.
  2. Princess or panel seams as #blazer-womens.
  3. Quilting: cut shell and lining the same size, baste, quilt in lines; trim after quilting. Lining panel cut slightly longer than the shell in vertical direction (take-up 1 to 2 percent, estimate to confirm on a swatch).
  4. Hem chain: weight about 2 to 3 g per cm of hem (typical, not verified).
  5. Sleeve: two-piece with a lining quilted to each piece, or the three-piece sleeve of the Threads/Shaeffer course.
- **Sewing order**: quilt each piece, sew the seams by hand-finished or machine seams with the allowances pressed open, apply braid by hand, set sleeves, finish the lining by hand, attach the chain, make the buttonholes (faux bound on the inside)
- **Finishes and notions**: braid or self-fringe trim, chain weight, gilt buttons, hand stitching at hem and edges
- **Fabrics**: loosely woven bouclé or tweed (do not fuse), silk lining quilted to the shell
- **Fit faults**: shell twists (fabric loose) -> cut on exact grain, interface only the edges; hem rolls -> chain too heavy or too light; neck gapes -> shorten the neckline edge, stay with tape
- **Variants**: #blazer-womens, #nehru-jacket, #spencer-jacket
- **CLO3D**: shell plus lining as a double layer; no real quilting channels (use shell stiffness); mesh 6 to 8 mm
- **Confidence**: couture elements reported by Threads magazine/Shaeffer; all numbers trade/estimate; outline unverified

## Spencer jacket {#spencer-jacket}

- **Family**: jackets, women's short jackets
- **Recognize**: short fitted jacket ending at or just below the bust or at the waist, long close sleeves, often a small collar or standing neck, front closed with buttons or hooks, back with centre seam and side back seams
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (2, CB seam), side back (2), sleeve (2, one- or two-piece), collar (1), facing (2), lining
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction).

- **Drafting**:
  1. Fitted bodice block with 4 to 8 ease at the bust; shorten at hem (spencer: under bust to waist).
  2. Back with CB and side back seams for the waist shape.
  3. Sleeve: one-piece fitted or two-piece (#two-piece-sleeve) with a close cuff.
  4. Neckline: round or small collar; front edge straight.
- **Sewing order**: darts, side and shoulder seams, collar, facing, sleeves, lining, closure

- **Variants**: #bolero, #shrug, #blazer-womens

## Bolero {#bolero}

- **Aliases**: bolero jacket, matador-style jacket, cropped open jacket; fr: boléro
- **Family**: jackets, women's short jackets
- **Recognize**: short open-front jacket, rounded or pointed fronts that do not meet, no closure or a single hook, optional short or three-quarter sleeves, cut-in armholes
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (1 on fold or 2), sleeve (2, optional), front facing (2) or full lining, hem binding
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (trade construction).
  Back is a short panel hemmed at or above WL with a shaped hem (curve rising 2 to 4 at the sides is typical). Fronts end at the bust or waist and curve away toward the underarm so the front edge is a continuous curve from the neck (or lapel/shawl) to the hem; there is no CF overlap. Shoulder seams as the bodice block. If sleeves are included, a short set-in cap sleeve or a three-quarter sleeve.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Fitted bodice block, close the shoulder dart, shorten to the chosen hem (bolero hem often at or above WL).
  2. Draw the front edge as a curve from the neck to the hem, fronts not meeting (gap 0 to 8 at CF is typical, to confirm on the toile).
  3. Lower the armhole 1 to 2 if worn over a dress.
  4. Add facing the full shape of the curved front edge, 3 to 4 wide.
- **Sewing order**: darts, shoulder seams, side seams, facing, hem, sleeves, finish edge with binding or topstitch
- **Finishes and notions**: curved front edges bound or faced and understitched, lightweight interfacing at the neckline
- **Fabrics**: velvet, brocade, wool, lace over lining; stiff fabrics need a facing, soft fabrics a binding
- **Fit faults**: front edges roll out -> understitch and ease the facing; shoulders slip -> add 0.5 shoulder slope
- **Variants**: #spencer-jacket, #shrug, #capelet
- **CLO3D**: no closure, check the front edge sits on the body; use a stiffer property for lined versions
- **Confidence**: trade practice; outline unverified

## Shrug {#shrug}

- **Aliases**: cropped cardigan, bolero shrug, sleeves-only cardigan; fr: boléro tricoté, cache-épaules à manches
- **Family**: knits and soft jackets
- **Recognize**: a back panel with attached sleeves and short or no front panels, front edges rolled or banded, worn open
- **Pieces**: body-sleeve panel (1 on fold, rectangle or T shape) or back (1) + sleeve (2) + front band (2); optional cuffs (2)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (common home-sewn rectangle construction).
  Rectangle version: one rectangle, the long dimension runs from wrist to wrist across the back (arm span, with the cuff ends), the short dimension is the length of the shrug at the centre (hem to neck line). The short edges are folded and sewn to make the sleeve tubes, leaving a central gap for the body. Fitted version: a back panel with raglan or set-in sleeves cut in one with the sides, fronts ending at the bust.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Measure wrist to wrist across the back with arms slightly bent, add ease (typical 4 to 8 total in knit).
  2. Choose height (body part) and sleeve circumference (upper arm + 4 to 8 for a knit).
  3. Fold the rectangle along its length, sew the sleeve seams from each short edge for sleeve length, leave the body part open.
  4. Add front bands (rib) of 4 to 6 wide cut at 85 to 90 percent of the edge.
- **Sewing order**: sew sleeve seams, hem or rib cuffs, attach front bands and neck finish
- **Finishes and notions**: rib or self-fabric bands, narrow elastic at the cuff optional
- **Fabrics**: jersey, rib, sweater knit; drape depends on knit stability
- **Fit faults**: shrug rides up -> lengthen the back; drag at the armpit -> increase the bicep circumference or add an underarm gusset
- **Variants**: #bolero, #cocoon-coat, #kimono-jacket
- **CLO3D**: tube sleeves simulate well in knit; use a pin at the neck
- **Confidence**: from memory (common home-sewing construction); no drafted source; outline unverified

## Notched lapel and collar {#notched-lapel}

- **Aliases**: notch lapel, step collar, notched collar; fr: revers cranté, col cranté, col tailleur
- **Family**: tailoring components, collars
- **Recognize**: a V-shaped notch where the collar (fall) meets the lapel at the gorge seam; the lapel turns back along a roll line that ends at the top working button; the collar stands at the back neck and folds over
- **Pieces**: front with integral lapel area (2), front facing with lapel face (2), top collar (1, CB seam optional), undercollar (2, CB seam, bias or straight with felt), canvas/chest piece with lapel stay (2), roll tape (2), edge tape (2)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (geometry and numbers from TO 2.5 and SC 2.7, which agree within about 1 cm).
  Front: break point B on the front edge (CF + wrap) at the top working button. Roll line: straight line from B to R, a point on the shoulder line extended beyond NP by the stand height (2.0 to 3.0). Lapel edge: parallel to the roll line at the lapel width (men 8 to 10, women 6.5 to 9), belly 0.5 to 1.5 convex for a soft lapel. Gorge seam: starts on the neckline at G, runs to the lapel edge at roughly 90 to 100 degrees to the roll line; gorge height 8 to 10 measured vertically from the shoulder line (high gorge 7 to 8 is classic). Notch: cut into the lapel edge and the collar, 2.5 to 3.5 deep and 3 to 4 wide. Collar: neck edge = neckline from G to CB on the seam line, drawn parallel to the roll line at the stand height; the neck edge is curved (spring 2 to 3.5 at CB); collar height at CB = stand 2.5 to 3.0 (men) plus fall 3.5 to 4.5, total 6.5 to 8. The neck edge of the collar sews to the body neckline G to CB (undercollar) and to the facing neckline (top collar); the gorge seam joins the top collar to the facing along the gorge line, the undercollar to the body along the gorge line.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Mark CF, wrap, B, R (stand height 2.5 to 3.0 beyond NP).
  2. Draw the roll line B to R; offset the lapel edge at the lapel width, shape the belly.
  3. Draw the gorge seam and the notch; check the lapel width at the shoulder 8 to 10.
  4. Collar: draw a parallel to the roll line at the stand height, measure the neckline length on it, square the CB, add spring 2 to 3.5, draw the outer edge to the notch.
  5. Turn of cloth: undercollar 0.3 to 0.5 smaller on the outer edge (0.5 to 0.8 in thick coat cloth); facing 0.3 to 0.5 larger at the lapel edge and roll line.
  6. Facing 8 to 10 at the chest (coats 10 to 12), reaching 4 to 6 from NP on the shoulder.
- **Sewing order**:
  1. Tape the roll line 0.8 to 1.0 short over 20 to 24 cm (TO 5.3) and pad stitch the lapel.
  2. Stretch the outer edge and shrink the neck edge of the undercollar on a ham.
  3. Join the facing to the front along the front edge and lapel, turn, press with the roll.
  4. Sew the undercollar to the body neckline and the top collar to the facing neckline.
  5. Close the gorge by hand (or machine) and sew the outer collar edge.
- **Finishes and notions**: hair canvas for lapel and undercollar, roll tape 10 to 13 mm, edge tape on the lapel curve (1.0 to 1.5 short), felt undercollar
- **Fabrics**: wool and wool blends roll best; thick melton needs larger turn of cloth; fused versions roll flatter
- **Fit faults**: collar stands away from the neck -> raise the neck-edge curve, shorten the undercollar neck edge, increase spring 0.5 to 1; lapel gapes -> shorten the tape 0.8 to 1.0, move B up 1; collar breaks low at the back -> raise the stand 0.3, reduce the fall; collar twists -> undercollar off grain
- **Variants**: #peak-lapel, #shawl-collar, #suit-jacket-single-breasted, #tailored-coat
- **CLO3D**: lapel facing and body as separate pieces joined at the front edge; roll line as a fold line with a fold angle; collar sewn at the neckline; stiff fabric property on lapel and undercollar
- **Confidence**: numbers verified against two own sheets (TO 2.5, SC 2.7), trade practice (Aldrich-type); outline unverified; drafted end to end with all seam lengths checked in examples/notched-lapel-jacket (outline still not compared to a published draft)

## Peak lapel {#peak-lapel}

- **Aliases**: peaked lapel, pointed lapel; fr: revers à pointe, revers pointu, revers en pointe
- **Family**: tailoring components, collars
- **Recognize**: the lapel edge carries an upward-pointing projection that goes beyond the collar; no notch, the gorge seam meets the lapel at the peak; usual on double-breasted jackets, dinner jackets and overcoats
- **Pieces**: as #notched-lapel (front, facing with peak, top collar, undercollar, canvas)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 2.6, SC 2.7).
  Draw as #notched-lapel up to the lapel width. The lapel edge continues above the gorge line as a pointed projection 3 to 5 long, pointing upward and outward at 20 to 30 degrees above horizontal (TO 2.6); SC 2.7 gives the lapel angle as 55 to 70 degrees for a peak, versus about 90 for a notch. Gorge height is higher than for a notch (6 to 8 above the shoulder line); lapel width 8 to 10. The end of the top collar is cut at an angle to meet the base of the peak; collar and lapel are joined along the gorge seam to the peak point. The facing carries the peak: facing and body are joined along the front edge and lapel edge, the point is turned and pressed.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Steps 1 to 3 of #notched-lapel; lapel width 8 to 10.
  2. Raise the gorge to 6 to 8 and extend the lapel edge above it as the peak (3 to 5 long, 20 to 30 degrees).
  3. Cut the collar end to meet the peak base; keep the collar length equal to the neckline.
  4. Turn of cloth as for the notch.
- **Sewing order**: as #notched-lapel; the peak point is turned with a fine corner and pressed with the point turned towards the shoulder
- **Finishes and notions**: canvas into the point, optional lapel buttonhole on the left lapel (boutonniere)
- **Fabrics**: as for the notch; silk-faced peaks (dinner jacket) need interfacing under the satin
- **Fit faults**: peak droops -> stiffen the point with canvas, shorten the lapel edge; collar end shows -> recut the collar end angle
- **Variants**: #notched-lapel, #shawl-collar, #double-breasted-jacket, #tuxedo-dinner-jacket
- **CLO3D**: same as #notched-lapel; the peak is part of the front outline
- **Confidence**: values from TO 2.6 and SC 2.7 (trade practice); outline unverified

## Shawl collar {#shawl-collar}

- **Aliases**: shawl lapel, roll collar (tailoring), tuxedo collar (shawl form); fr: col châle
- **Family**: tailoring components, collars
- **Recognize**: continuous rounded curve from the neck over the lapel to the single button, no gorge seam and no notch; the collar and lapel are one piece, with a seam only at CB
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (1 or 2), top collar with facing in one (2, CB seam), undercollar (2, CB seam), interfacing on fronts, undercollar and facing
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (SC 2.6, used in #tuxedo-dinner-jacket).
  Break point at the first button (waist or bust level), roll line from the break point to a point 2.0 to 2.5 beyond NP on the extended shoulder line. Lapel edge parallel to the roll line at 8 to 11 wide at the shoulder, curving into a rounded shape and continuing to CB. The shape on the front is reflected across the roll line to give the collar; the upper collar is cut in one with the front facing and has a seam at CB. The collar neck edge equals the back neckline length measured from the extended shoulder point to CB, spring 2 to 3 at CB. The undercollar is a separate piece with a CB seam attached to the body neckline; the upper collar/facing is joined to the body along the front edge and the neck.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Mark CF, wrap 1.5 to 2.5, break point.
  2. Roll line from break point to R (2.0 to 2.5 beyond NP).
  3. Lapel edge parallel at 8 to 11; round the curve.
  4. Reflect across the roll line to form the collar; set the CB seam.
  5. Collar neck edge length = back neck; spring 2 to 3 at CB.
  6. Facing turn of cloth 0.3 to 0.5 at the roll line.
- **Sewing order**: interface fronts, tape the roll line short 0.5 to 1.0, sew the undercollar CB seam, attach to the neckline between the SNPs, sew the upper collar/facing around the outer edge, turn, press the roll over a ham
- **Finishes and notions**: cotton twill roll tape 10 mm, interfacing on the facing, silk or velvet faced versions need an underlay
- **Fabrics**: wool, velvet, satin facings; slippery faces need interfacing
- **Fit faults**: collar stands away from the neck -> shorten the neck edge 0.5, increase the spring; lapel pulls -> add turn of cloth 0.3 to 0.5
- **Variants**: #notched-lapel, #peak-lapel, #tuxedo-dinner-jacket, #wrap-coat
- **CLO3D**: undercollar and upper collar as separate pieces, roll line as a fold; lapel facing connected to the front
- **Confidence**: SC 2.6 (trade practice); outline unverified

## Two-piece sleeve {#two-piece-sleeve}

- **Aliases**: tailored sleeve, coat sleeve (two-piece); fr: manche deux pièces, manche tailleur
- **Family**: tailoring components, sleeves
- **Recognize**: two seams on the sleeve, one at the forearm (front) and one at the hindarm (back, nearer the elbow), the sleeve hangs with a slight forward curve; often a vent with 3 or 4 buttons at the cuff
- **Pieces**: top sleeve (2), under sleeve (2); optional sleeve head (2), cuff facing, vent underlap
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (SC 1.4 and TO 4.3 describe the draft; the two sheets differ on the seam offsets, both are quoted).
  The top sleeve is the larger piece and carries the cap top and the axis; it extends over the underarm region at the front by 1.5 to 2.5 so the forearm seam is hidden on the underside. The under sleeve is a narrow piece (28 to 33 percent of the bicep width, TO 4.3) formed from the front and back strips of the one-piece sleeve joined along the original underarm line; its upper edge is the lower third of the cap (underarm area) on the armhole. Forearm seam: from the front notch on the cap to the hem at 4 to 5 from the front hem corner (5 to 6 at the bicep line). Hindarm seam: starts 1.5 to 2 above the back notch, 6 to 7 at the bicep line and 5 to 6 at the hem. The hindarm edge of the top sleeve is longer than the under sleeve edge by 1.0 to 1.5 (elbow ease, TO) or 1.5 to 2.5 (SC), eased between the elbow notches. The forearm seam edges have equal length. The hem is curved: front corner lifted 1, back corner dropped 0.5 to 1; pitch (hem forward) 1.5 to 2.5.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Draw the sleeve block with the adapted cap (jacket cap height 14 to 16 men, 12.5 to 14.5 women; cap ease 2.5 to 4.0 in wool, 1.5 to 2.0 firm cloth).
  2. Mark the elbow line and move the cuff forward by the pitch p (1.5 to 2.5).
  3. Draw the forearm and hindarm lines (values above).
  4. Separate the pieces: top sleeve centre, under sleeve from the two outer strips, rejoin the strips along the underarm line.
     The one-piece base must have parallel underarm sides for this: take the taper to the hem in the forearm and hindarm
     seams, never in the underarm sides, or the two strips of the under sleeve end up on different grains (worked example:
     examples/notched-lapel-jacket).
  5. Shift the top sleeve front edge 1.5 to 2.5 over the under sleeve (seam lengths stay equal).
  6. Elbow ease by dart or a slash and spread at the elbow line.
  7. Hem allowance 3.5 to 4.0 (coats 5 to 6); vent extension on the top sleeve 3 wide, 10 to 12 long with a mitre.
  8. Check cap length = AH + ease; forearm seams equal within 0.3.
- **Sewing order**: interface hems; sew the hindarm seam (top sleeve upwards, ease to the under sleeve), press open; make vent and hem; sew the forearm seam; set into the armhole; sleeve head
- **Finishes and notions**: sleeve head 3 to 4.5 wide, 4 working or kissing buttons spaced 2.0 to 2.2 starting 1.0 to 1.5 from the hem, vent 10 to 12
- **Fabrics**: wool and tailoring cloths; firm and synthetic fabrics reduce cap ease; stripes: match at the hindarm and forearm seams
- **Fit faults**: sleeve twists forward -> shift the hem 1 back; pulls at the elbow -> add elbow ease on the hindarm; seam visible -> push the top sleeve further under; under sleeve too wide at the hem -> narrow 1 each side; puckered cap -> reduce cap ease
- **Variants**: #raglan-coat-sleeve, #suit-jacket-single-breasted, #tailored-coat
- **CLO3D**: two pieces; sew the forearm seam first, then the hindarm with an ease ratio; set the avatar arm relaxed to check pitch
- **Confidence**: SC 1.4 and TO 4.3 (trade practice), differences between them noted; outline unverified

## Raglan coat sleeve {#raglan-coat-sleeve}

- **Aliases**: raglan sleeve (coat), Balmacaan sleeve; fr: manche raglan, manche raglan de manteau
- **Family**: sleeve components
- **Recognize**: a diagonal seam from the neck to the underarm on both front and back; no shoulder-point seam; sometimes a shoulder dart in the sleeve or a two-piece raglan with a shoulder seam
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (2 or 1), sleeve (2, or 2 + 2 with shoulder seam), underarm gusset optional
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 12.6 summarises the Aldrich method; SC 1.5 has a longer version).
  The sleeve cap reaches the neckline. Front raglan line: from the neckline 3.5 to 4.5 from NP, curving down (about 1.0 hollow) to the front underarm. Back raglan line: from 4.0 to 5.0 from NP to the back underarm. The shoulder dart of the block (about 1.5 to 2.0) is closed and given to the raglan seam, or the sleeve keeps a dart over the shoulder. The sleeve edge that sews to the front raglan line of the bodice equals it in length with 0 ease; the same for the back. Sleeve centre line angled 20 to 30 degrees from the vertical for a close fit, 45 degrees for a low sleeve (TO 12.6).
- **Drafting**:
  1. Overlap the adapted block and sleeve, sleeve top at the shoulder point, centre line angled as above.
  2. Draw the raglan lines, front and back.
  3. Close the shoulder dart into the raglan seam; separate body and sleeve.
  4. Bicep ease per TO 4.2 (coat 14 to 20); the sleeve width at the underarm equals the lowered underarm of the body.
  5. Check front and back raglan lengths, sleeve versus body: equal.
- **Sewing order**: sew raglan seams (front and back) to the sleeve, then sleeve underarm and side seams in one pass
- **Finishes and notions**: stay tape on the raglan curve, no pad (Balmacaan) or a thin raglan pad
- **Fabrics**: wool, gabardine, waterproofs (tape the raglan seams)
- **Fit faults**: diagonal drag lines -> release the raglan line at the shoulder slope; neck gapes -> shorten the neckline end of the raglan seam; underarm tight -> lower the underarm curve
- **Variants**: #two-piece-sleeve, #trench-coat, #raincoat-mac, #cocoon-coat
- **CLO3D**: sleeve and body as pieces, raglan seams sewn at equal lengths; shoulder dart in the sleeve piece
- **Confidence**: TO 12.6 (trade practice, Aldrich summary); outline unverified

## Welt pocket {#welt-pocket}

- **Aliases**: single-welt pocket, breast welt, barchetta welt (curved, Italian); fr: poche à un passepoil large, poche poitrine, poche à rabat sans rabat
- **Family**: pocket components
- **Recognize**: one wide strip (the welt) lying over the opening, a hidden second edge; typically the left chest pocket of a jacket, slightly tilted, 10 to 11.5 wide
- **Pieces**: welt (1, folded, interfaced), top pocket bag (facing piece in shell, 1), bottom pocket bag (pocketing, 1), opening stay (fusible, 1), optional jet strip for the lower edge
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 8.1 and 8.2 give sizes and positions; the pocket layout is trade practice).
  Opening: 10 to 11.5 long. Welt finished 1.5 to 2.0 deep, same length as the opening, sewn at the lower edge of the opening line and turned up over the opening (typical breast welt; the orientation varies by maker, unverified). One jet is stitched at the upper edge. Behind the opening the bag is two pieces joined at the lower edge: shell-fabric facing piece on the upper side, pocketing piece below; the bag hangs from the opening, never from the stitched corners. Position (jacket, left chest): mouth at the chest line (0 to 3 above), inner end 5 to 6 from the lapel roll and 7 to 9 from the armhole; the outer end 1 to 2 higher than the inner end (TO 8.2).
- **Drafting**:
  1. Mark the opening line on the front; fuse an opening stay 2 to 3 wide, 3 longer than the opening.
  2. Welt: rectangle opening length + seam allowance by 2 x finished depth + 2 x allowance, interface, fold.
  3. Bag: depth 12 to 14 for a breast pocket (typical), width opening + 2.
  4. Mark corner points on the stay; cut the opening with two triangles at each end.
- **Sewing order**:
  1. Stitch the welt to the lower seam line and the jet (with the top bag) to the upper seam line, same length.
  2. Cut the opening with triangles at the ends.
  3. Pull the welt and bags to the wrong side, press, close the triangles.
  4. Sew the bag edges; hand-tack the welt ends.
- **Finishes and notions**: bar tack or hand stitch at the ends, opening stay, pocketing 100 percent cotton
- **Fabrics**: stripes and checks matched with the welt cut from the same position on the fabric
- **Fit faults**: welt gapes -> the stay is too light or the opening too long; the corners pucker -> triangles too short; bag sags -> interface the opening, shorten the bag
- **Variants**: #jetted-pocket, #flap-pocket, #suit-jacket-single-breasted
- **CLO3D**: internal line for the opening plus a separate welt piece sewn on; the bag is usually omitted or a small piece behind
- **Confidence**: dimensions from TO 8.1 to 8.3 (trade practice; Permanent Style and FreeSewing double-welt pages for the vocabulary); the welt orientation and bag depth are not verified

## Jetted (double-welt) pocket {#jetted-pocket}

- **Aliases**: double-welt pocket, besom pocket, piped pocket, jetted pocket; fr: poche à double passepoil, poche passepoilée, poche en besace (besom)
- **Family**: pocket components
- **Recognize**: an opening closed between two narrow jets (strips), no flap; found on dinner jackets, trousers (back pockets), inside pockets, zipped jacket pockets
- **Pieces**: top jet (1), bottom jet (1), top bag (shell facing piece, 1), bottom bag (pocketing, 1), opening stay (fusible, 1); optional zip
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline; construction from TO 8.3, FreeSewing double-welt docs.
  The opening length equals the jet length (men's hip 15.5 to 17, breast 10 to 11.5, women 12.5 to 14). Each jet finished 0.5 to 0.6 wide (total 1.0 to 1.3), cut as strips 2.5 to 3 wide and opening length + 3 long. The two stitching lines are exactly the opening length and 1.0 apart (for 0.5 jets). The cut between the stitch lines stops 1 cm from the ends and splits into triangles (to the stitching corners). The upper bag is the shell-fabric facing piece sewn to the top jet seam, the lower bag in pocketing sewn to the bottom jet seam; the triangles are sewn to the ends of the jets.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Mark the opening line and the jet lines 0.5 to 0.6 each side.
  2. Cut the jets and bags: upper bag shell facing, lower bag pocketing, depth 18 to 22 for a hip pocket (typical).
  3. Fuse an opening stay on the wrong side, 3 to 4 deep.
- **Sewing order**: stitch the jets to the pocket line (rectangle of stitching), cut the opening with triangles, push the jets through, press, sew the triangles to the jet ends, attach and close the bags
- **Finishes and notions**: opening stay, optional bar tack, pocketing cotton or viscose
- **Fabrics**: stripe or check: cut the jets from the same position; slippery fabrics need extra interfacing
- **Fit faults**: lips gape -> the jets are too narrow or the stay too light; corners fray -> clip too close to the stitching
- **Variants**: #welt-pocket, #flap-pocket, #bellows-pocket, #tuxedo-dinner-jacket
- **CLO3D**: internal line for the opening and a bag piece; flap and jets are decorative
- **Confidence**: TO 8.3 (trade practice, cross-checked with FreeSewing docs); outline unverified

## Flap pocket {#flap-pocket}

- **Aliases**: jetted pocket with flap, hip pocket with flap, ticket pocket (small version); fr: poche à rabat, poche à rabat passepoilée, poche ticket
- **Family**: pocket components
- **Recognize**: a flap hanging over the opening, rounded or square lower corners; on suit jackets, blazers, coats, trench coats
- **Pieces**: flap (outer, 1, interfaced), flap (under, 1), bottom jet (1), top bag (shell facing, 1), bottom bag (pocketing, 1), opening stay (1)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 8.1, 8.4 and 8.6; Permanent Style pocket guide).
  Flap: 4.5 to 5.5 deep and as wide as the opening plus 0.3 per side (jackets, hip 15.5 to 17 men, 13 to 15 women); ticket flap 3.0 to 3.5 deep with an opening 14.5 to 16 (hip pocket 16.5 to 17). Lower corners rounded (radius 1.0 to 1.5) or square. Under flap 0.3 smaller so that the seam rolls underneath. The flap is sewn into the top seam of the opening (it replaces the top jet), and the bottom jet is a single strip 0.5 to 0.6; the bag is as #jetted-pocket. Position: 8 to 10 below WL, centred on the front dart, front end at least 5 behind the front edge, opening parallel to the hem or tilted 1 forward; the ticket pocket sits 3 above the right hip pocket (TO 8.2). A hacking style is slanted by 2.5 (direction to check on the model).
- **Drafting**:
  1. Mark the opening line on the front.
  2. Cut flap outer and under: depth + allowances, width = opening + 0.6 + allowances.
  3. Cut a bottom jet and bags; fuse the stay.
- **Sewing order**: sew the flap (turn, understitch, press), attach the flap to the top seam line and the bottom jet to the lower seam line, cut the opening, pull through, make the bags and close the ends
- **Finishes and notions**: interfacing on the outer flap, optional button and loop for a flap pocket on a trench, the flap hangs down 0.5 to 1 below the opening line
- **Fabrics**: check/stripe match: cut the flap from the same position as the front
- **Fit faults**: flap curls upward -> the under flap is too large or not trimmed; the pocket gapes -> shorten the flap, stay the opening; flap too short -> cover the opening plus 0.3 per side
- **Variants**: #jetted-pocket, #welt-pocket, #bellows-pocket, #field-jacket-m65
- **CLO3D**: flap as a separate piece sewn to the front; opening as an internal line
- **Confidence**: TO 8.1 to 8.4 (trade practice), Permanent Style guide for the vocabulary; outline unverified

## Bellows pocket {#bellows-pocket}

- **Aliases**: pleated patch pocket, box-pleat pocket, cargo pocket, accordion pocket, safari pocket; fr: poche à soufflet, poche cargo, poche à pli creux, poche safari
- **Family**: pocket components
- **Recognize**: a patch pocket with expanding gussets at the sides (inverted or box pleats), usually with a flap; on safari jackets, field jackets, parkas, cargo trousers
- **Pieces**: pocket body (1, with pleat allowances), flap (1 + under flap), optional separate side gussets (2)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 8.5 mentions the pleated patch pocket with a box pleat of 2 to 3; TO 14.3 gives a parka pocket 18 to 22 by 20 to 24 with a bellows pleat of 2 on the sides).
  Front panel is the visible width W (e.g. 16 to 22) plus two side pleats; the cut width = W + 2 x (pleat depth x 2), with a pleat depth 2 to 3 per side (box pleat taken as 2 x depth folded away). The pleat folds are pressed inward (inverted box pleat) so that the pocket can expand by the pleat width; the bottom corners are sewn with a continuous line round the pleat. Top hem 3 to 4, bar tack at the top corners of the stitch line. The patch is stitched to the garment along its three sides; a flap is added 1 above the pocket mouth.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Decide the finished visible width W and depth D.
  2. Cut width = W + 4 x p (p = pleat depth 2 to 3 per side: pleat folds are twice p), height = D + top hem 3 to 4 + 1 for the bottom turn.
  3. Mark the pleat folds at each side, press, topstitch the pleat at the top 2 to 3.
  4. Flap 4.5 to 6 deep, width = pocket + 0.6.
- **Sewing order**: hem the top, form and press the pleats, turn the other three edges, stitch to the garment (before side seams), bar tack, flap above
- **Finishes and notions**: bar tacks, snap or button on the flap, optional drain stitch hole at the bottom corner
- **Fabrics**: cotton twill, canvas, nylon, ripstop; thick cloth makes the pleats bulky, reduce p to 1.5 to 2
- **Fit faults**: pocket sags -> interface the back of the pocket area; flap won't close over a full pocket -> flap 0.5 to 1.0 deeper
- **Variants**: #flap-pocket, #field-jacket-m65, #safari-jacket, #parka-fishtail-m51
- **CLO3D**: pocket body flat with the pleat edges sewn as separate seams, or a plain patch with decorative lines; limited expansion in 3D
- **Confidence**: dimensions from TO 8.5 and 14.3 (trade practice); pleat arithmetic geometric; outline unverified

## Hood, two-piece {#hood-two-piece}

- **Aliases**: two-panel hood, hood with CB seam; fr: capuche deux pièces, capuche à couture dos
- **Family**: hood components
- **Recognize**: hood with a single seam over the head from front to back (CB seam), a pointed or rounded top
- **Pieces**: hood side (2 mirrored), lining (2)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 13.1 and 13.2, SC 2.10).
  A rectangle D wide (depth, front edge to CB, 27 to 31 women, 29 to 33 men) by H high (neck seam to crown, 34 to 38 women, 36 to 40 men): left edge = front edge, right edge = CB seam. SC 2.10 gives somewhat lower values (H 33 to 36, D 28 to 32). Neck edge: base line of length N = garment neckline from CF to CB; curved concave 0.5 to 1.5 and rising 1.5 to 2.0 at the back (nape). Crown: round the back-top corner with a radius of 10 to 14 and the front-top corner with a radius of 6 to 8. Front edge: bow 1.5 to 3.0 outward in the middle to frame the face; facing or channel 2.5 to 3.5. The two side pieces are sewn along the CB seam (top and back) and the neck edge joins the garment neckline; the face edge is finished with a facing, binding or a drawcord channel.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Measure N on the garment seam line.
  2. Draw the rectangle D by H; base line N with the concave curve; round the corners.
  3. Add the front edge bow; add a facing or channel.
  4. Cut 2 shell, 2 lining (lining 0.3 smaller on the front edge).
  5. Check that the sum of the neck edges equals the neckline and the CB seam length is the same on both sides.
- **Sewing order**: sew CB seam of shell and lining, join at the face edge, turn, topstitch, attach to the neckline
- **Finishes and notions**: drawcord, eyelets 8 mm, neck tape 12 to 15 over the seam, wire in the brim optional
- **Fabrics**: wool melton (duffle), nylon (parka), jersey (hoodie, hood height 36 to 40, no neck ease)
- **Fit faults**: hood falls backward -> lengthen the CB neck seam, check it equals the garment neck; narrow visor pinches -> add 2 to the width and 3 to the height; edge rolls out -> lining 0.3 smaller; sags forward -> add a gusset or wired brim
- **Variants**: #hood-three-piece, #duffle-coat, #parka-fishtail-m51, #anorak
- **CLO3D**: hood sewn at the neckline, mesh 8 to 10 mm, hang with the head of the avatar; drawcord is hard to simulate
- **Confidence**: dimensions from TO 13 and SC 2.10 (trade practice, Minerva hood article); outline unverified

- **Family**: hood components
- **Recognize**: two side panels and a strip over the crown from the front edge to the nape, giving a hood that stands off the head; common on technical shells with a peaked brim
- **Pieces**: side panel (2), centre panel (gusset, 1), lining (3)

  2. Remove 4 to 6 along the head curve from each side piece.

## Lining patterns {#lining-patterns}

- **Aliases**: lining derivation, jacket lining, coat lining, bagged lining; fr: doublure, patron de doublure
- **Family**: tailoring components, linings
- **Recognize**: the inside of a jacket or coat; a pleat at the back, lining joined to the front facings, sleeve lining, inside pockets in the lining front
- **Pieces**: lining front (2, including inner pockets), lining back (1 on fold with CB pleat or 2), lining sleeve (2 top, 2 under, or 2 one-piece), pocket bags (2 to 3), jump pleat at the hem
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline; construction from TO 7.2 and 7.3 (Minerva lining article, Liz Haywood, Threads jump pleat).
  Lining front: shell front without the facing area (remove the facing width at the front edge and add 1.0 at the facing edge). Lining back: shell back with a CB pleat of 1.5 to 2.0 depth (pleat value 3 to 4 at the fold) from the nape to the waist or the hem, held with a catch stitch at the neck and waist. Armhole lowered 0.5 to 1.0 and 0.5 to 1.0 added across the back; back neck lowered 0.3. Hem: lining finished 1.0 to 2.0 above the shell hem edge, with a jump pleat (cut length = shell length - hem allowance - facing offset + 3 to 4 with a fold of 1.5 to 2.0). Sleeve lining: cap eased 1 to 1.5 less than the shell, 1 to 1.5 added at the elbow, jump hem 2.0 at the cuff (length = shell sleeve length - hem depth + 3 to 4). Width: lining roomier than the shell; a single survey of menswear brands reports 5.6 at the chest, 3.8 at the waist, 2.7 at hem and bicep in suit jackets (2.4, 1.3, 1.3, 1.1 in casual), indicative only.
- **Drafting**:
  1. Trace the shell pieces minus the facing; add 1.0 at the facing edge.
  2. Add the CB pleat (1.5 to 2.0 deep) and the back width; lower the armhole 0.5 to 1.0.
  3. Add length: jump pleat 3 to 4 at the hem; sleeve lining jump 2.0 at the cuff.
  4. Cut the inside pockets in the front: breast (left) 14 to 15 wide with a double jet 1.0, 8 to 10 below the armhole bottom line.
  5. For coats: lining hangs from the facings, CB pleat 2.0 and a hem pleat; quilted or interlined versions cut 1 smaller per seam.
- **Sewing order**: sew pockets to the lining front, join front and back lining, sleeves to the lining body, bag the lining to the facings (machine) and set to the shoulder, hem and sleeve edges (hand or machine, turned through the sleeve)
- **Finishes and notions**: lining acetate, viscose, cupro or polyester 70 to 100 g/m2, hanging loop at the neck, label
- **Fabrics**: lining must never be tighter than the shell; stretch-woven linings need less ease
- **Fit faults**: lining drags -> add a CB pleat 1.5 to 2.0 and length 2 to 3; the lining shows at the hem -> shorten the lining hem 1 to 2 above the shell; sleeve lining restricts -> add 1 to 1.5 elbow ease
- **Variants**: #interfacing-canvas-plan, #suit-jacket-single-breasted, #tailored-coat
- **CLO3D**: lining as separate pieces inside the shell, sewn to the facing; layered with the shell via layer order
- **Confidence**: dimensions from TO 7.2 (trade practice); survey figures from one study (db.koreascholar.com), treat as order of magnitude; outline unverified

- **Fabrics**: lightweight shells: fused or half canvas; firm wool: full canvas; sheers: no fusing

- **Variants**: #lining-patterns, #notched-lapel, #suit-jacket-single-breasted
- **CLO3D**: canvas as a stiffer piece beneath the shell or use the fusing option on the shell; collision thickness 0

## Cape {#cape}

- **Aliases**: circular cape, half-circle cape, shoulder cape, poncho cape, opera cape; fr: cape
- **Family**: capes and cloaks
- **Recognize**: sleeveless outer garment hanging from the shoulders, open at the front, with arm slits or openings; falls in folds from a shoulder curve; may be a circle or half-circle cut, or fitted at the shoulder with darts

## Kimono jacket {#kimono-jacket}

- **Aliases**: kimono cardigan, haori-style jacket, wrap jacket with kimono sleeves, Japanese-style jacket; fr: veste kimono, veste style haori
- **Family**: jackets, kimono-sleeve garments
- **Recognize**: T-shaped or rectangular body with sleeves cut in one with the body, a straight open front with a wide front band (collar band running round the neck and down the front), no closure or a tie
- **Pieces**: body panel (front 2 and back 1 on the fold or with CB seam), sleeve extension (if cut separate: 2), front band/collar (1 or 2, long strip), side gussets (optional 2), pockets in the seam (optional)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified for the jacket; see the traditional #haori and #kimono entries in world-historical.md for the documented straight-cut construction.
  Rectangular panels: the back panel on the fold, wide as the chest plus ease; the sleeve is a rectangle extending from the shoulder line; the fronts are two rectangles meeting at CF (or overlapping), the neck opening is a curve or a straight slit. The front band is a long strip folded double, sewn round the neck and down both fronts (width 6 to 10 finished, typical). Sleeve slope 0 to 15 degrees depending on the shape (SC 1.6: kimono sleeve, with or without gusset).
- **Drafting**: SC 1.6 for the kimono and dolman sleeve; length of the front band = 2 x front length + back neck length; underarm gusset 8 to 12 square to give arm lift
- **Sewing order**: shoulder/sleeve seam, underarm and side seams, band attached round the neck, hem
- **Finishes and notions**: band interfacing, inside tie optional
- **Fabrics**: silk, cotton, linen, light wool; kimono silk is narrow (36 cm), so panels are joined
- **Fit faults**: sleeves lift the body -> add a gusset; neck gapes -> shorten the band 0.5 to 1
- **Variants**: #shrug, #cocoon-coat, #wrap-coat, #haori, #kimono
- **CLO3D**: panels sewn flat, drape with stiffness low; band as a separate strip
- **Confidence**: from memory plus SC 1.6; outline unverified
