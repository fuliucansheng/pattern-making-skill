# Lingerie, corsetry, nightwear, swimwear, babies and children

Knowledge-base entries in the format of `FORMAT.md`. Method background (stretch reduction, band and
elastic ratios, bra sizing, wires, negative ease, Laplace pressure) is in
`references/techniques/knits-lingerie-swimwear.md`; entries here refer to it as "techniques 5.x / 6.x".

Reading rules for this file:

- **Shape** is a construction description (pieces, anchors, which edge joins which). No entry below was
  taken from a drawn, graded pattern, because none was available online. Each Shape field says what its
  basis is: "text source" (a published description of the construction, named in Sources), "trade
  practice" (standard industry construction, consistent across makers) or "unverified". Anchors are named
  inside each entry and are not coordinates.
- Numbers are "typical" unless a source is given. "Not known" means no reliable number was found; the
  engine must measure the real garment or toile instead of guessing.
- Always confirm on a toile in the real fabric and elastic. Fit-critical constructions (cups, corsets,
  crotch lengths) are never final from a table.

## Underwire Full-Cup Bra (three-piece cup) {#underwire-full-cup-bra}

- **Aliases**: full-coverage bra, full-cup underwire bra, three-piece cup bra, dream cup; fr: soutien-gorge à armatures couvrant, soutien-gorge emboîtant (3 pièces)
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: cup rises close to the strap line and covers the full breast; high gore (CF) of about 4 to 6 cm; two cup seams visible (one roughly horizontal or diagonal across the cup, one running from the bust point down to the wire); wire channel along the cup bottom; straps set at the outer third of the cup top; side sling or power bar; wing with 2 or 3 hook rows.
- **Pieces**: upper cup (2), lower cup inner (2), lower cup outer (2), cup lining or stabiliser per piece (2 sets), cradle (2), wing / side band, outer power net (2) + lining (2), power bar or side sling (2), wire channel (2), strap (2), strap elastic, top-edge elastic, under-band elastic, hook-and-eye tape (1 pair), underwires (2), rings and sliders (2 each).
- **Shape**: text source for the principle (Cloth Habit, three-piece variation): the main cup seam must cross the bust point; the third piece is made by a line from the bust point (B) down to the bottom seamline, ending anywhere along it, splitting the lower cup in two; the new seam joins the main seam to the bottom seam with a smooth, subtle curve. Trade practice for the rest, other makers split the upper cup instead, so the position of the third piece is a design choice, not fixed. Anchors: W1 = wire end at the gore, W2 = wire end at the side, G = top of the gore, S = strap point at the cup top, N1 = inner top corner of the cup, B = apex (bust point). Upper cup U: bottom edge = main seam M (concave), top edge = neckline from N1 to S (convex, finished with elastic), ends at N1 and S. Lower cup inner LI: from W1 along the wire line to the vertical seam foot V on the wire, up the vertical seam to B, then along M back to N1 side. Lower cup outer LO: from V along the wire to W2, along M to the side end, back down to B along the vertical seam. Sew: U.M to LI.M and LO.M (U is sewn to both lower pieces, M must match their combined length); LI to LO along the vertical seam V-B; lower cup bottom edge (wire line W1-V-W2) to the cradle top edge; cradle lower edge to the wing; wing to wing at CB through the hook tape. Wire line length = actual traced wire length plus seam allowances at ends. Basis for pieces and anchors: trade practice, shape of each outline unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take underbust, bust, breast base, depth, inner and outer curve (techniques 5.1). 2. Choose and trace the real wire; draft it 5 mm open at the bottom (techniques 5.2). 3. Draft the cradle and the band (techniques 5.5 steps 3 and 4): gore 5 to 6 cm for full cup, side height 7 to 9 cm, CB 4.5 to 8 cm. 4. Draw the two-piece horizontal cup first: wire line at the bottom, M convex by 5 to 10 mm on the lower cup. 5. Add the vertical seam through B, ending on the wire in the lower part of its curve. 6. Cut apart, re-draw each seam as a smooth curve, check that lengths of paired seams are equal ("walk" the seams). 7. Add 0.6 to 1.0 cm seam allowance (6 mm on cup seams is common), 1.0 to 1.2 cm on the wire channel. 8. Toile and move volume between pieces rather than resizing (techniques 5.5 step 6). One commenter cites pushing the lower-cup curve out by about 4 mm as a start (unverified).
- **Sewing order**: lower cup pieces together (LI to LO); upper cup to lower cup along M; cup lining; cup to cradle, seam allowances toward the cup; gore; wire channel; side seams; power bar; under-band and top-edge elastic; straps; hook-and-eye; insert wires; bartack the wire ends (techniques 5.5).
- **Finishes and notions**: stable cup fabric (tricot, non-stretch lace with stabiliser), power net for wing, wire channel 12 mm typical, wires, 2 rings, 2 sliders, 10 to 18 mm strap elastic, hook-and-eye tape with 2 to 4 rows (techniques 5.5 step 4).
- **Fabrics**: cup fabrics should be stable or have little stretch; a stretch fabric loses support and fits differently from the drawing; band in power net (stretch 60 to 100 percent). Lace needs a stabiliser layer in the cup.
- **Fit faults**: cup wrinkles at the top: volume too large, shorten the top edge 2 percent; gaping at the top: reduce depth or add a power bar; wire digs: larger wire or check tilt; quadraboob (spill): add a size or a deeper upper cup; band rides up: shorten the band and check cup volume (techniques 5.5).
- **Variants**: balconette #balconette-bra; demi #demi-bra; plunge #plunge-bra; soft-cup version without wire #soft-cup-bra; moulded #tshirt-moulded-bra.
- **CLO3D**: build each cup piece, cradle and wing as separate patterns, sew with the real seam lengths; mesh 4 to 6 mm; wire as a narrow stiff strip or high-bending internal line along the wire line; sew the elastics with the elastic ratio; the user must place cups on the avatar breast and check strain on the cup seams.
- **Confidence**: three-piece principle verified (text source); piece list, wire joining, band proportions trade practice; outline of each piece unverified.

## Balconette Bra {#balconette-bra}

- **Aliases**: balcony bra, shelf bra with wire; fr: soutien-gorge balconnet
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: wide, low-cut cup that ends in a straight or gently curved top edge; straps set wide apart, often at the outer third; a horizontal or slightly diagonal seam; wire with low gore (3 to 4 cm) and low outer height; cup shows the upper breast "on a shelf".
- **Pieces**: upper cup (2), lower cup (2), cradle (2), wing (2 outer + 2 lining), power bar (2), wire channel (2), strap (2), top elastic, under elastic, hook-and-eye (1 pair), wires (2).
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified outlines. Two-piece horizontal cup: lower cup LC (wire line W1-W2 at the bottom, convex seam M on top), upper cup UC (concave bottom M, nearly straight top edge T from inner corner N1 to outer corner N2, N1 near the wire gore end, N2 at the strap). The straps attach at N2 region; because the neckline is wide, the strap points sit further apart than in a full cup. Seams: UC.M to LC.M; LC.wire line to the cradle; wing to cradle; wing to wing at CB. Neck edge T is a straight to concave line of about the same length as the cup edge it bridges, finished with elastic at 95 percent (techniques 5.5 step 7) or lace.
- **Drafting**: 1. Wire: balconette/demi style, low outer end (techniques 5.2). 2. Gore 3.5 to 4.5 cm. 3. Draw the cup with the wire line as the bottom; horizontal seam through the bust point region. 4. Top edge T lowered by 1 to 2 cm below the full-cup top edge at the centre (typical, not from a source). 5. Strap position wide: outer third. 6. Remaining steps as #underwire-full-cup-bra.
- **Sewing order**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra with a two-piece cup.
- **Finishes and notions**: lace or elastic on T, picot elastic on the cradle edge, 12 mm channel.
- **Fabrics**: stable cup fabric, lace on a net base; nonstretch upper cup holds the shape.
- **Fit faults**: spillage above the cup: upper cup too shallow; gaping at the outer top: shorten T at the outer end by 5 to 10 mm and move the strap in; straps slide: move the straps inward.
- **Variants**: demi #demi-bra, plunge #plunge-bra.
- **CLO3D**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra; the horizontal seam is easy to simulate.
- **Confidence**: from trade practice; outlines unverified.

## Plunge Bra {#plunge-bra}

- **Aliases**: deep plunge bra, push-up plunge; fr: soutien-gorge décolleté plongeant
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: very low gore (about 2.5 to 3 cm at CF), cups slanting toward the sides in a V, wires wide-set with an angled inner end, straps wide or convertible, often a diagonal seam on the cup, frequently with padding at the outer lower cup.
- **Pieces**: upper cup (2), lower cup (2), cradle (2), gore (part of cradle), wing (2+2), power bar (2), padding (2, optional), channel (2), straps (2), elastics, hook-and-eye, wires (2).
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified outlines. A plunge wire has a lower inner end that sits near the sternum; the cup inner edge slopes from the gore top G (low) outward and upward to the neckline outer point S. The cup seam M is diagonal, running from the wire near the inner-lower point toward the strap side; the upper cup is a triangle-like piece whose top edge T leans toward the outer side. The gore is low (the CF piece of the cradle is lower than in a full cup), and the wire inner ends meet at about 2 to 3 cm apart. Seam sequence as #underwire-full-cup-bra.
- **Drafting**: 1. Plunge wire (techniques 5.2). 2. Gore height 2.5 to 3 cm (techniques 5.3). 3. Inner cup edge slopes outward at an angle (value not known); keep the wire inner end angled outward. 4. A diagonal main seam; the lower cup must carry enough volume at the outer-lower breast. 5. Check that the neckline edge length is about equal to the elastic length ratio 0.95. 6. Remaining steps as #underwire-full-cup-bra.
- **Sewing order**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra; for convertible straps, make strap attachments at the cup top outer edge and at the CB.
- **Finishes and notions**: 12 mm channel; clear straps and J-hooks for convertible styles; optional removable padding.
- **Fabrics**: stable fabrics for the upper cup; stretch lace needs a net backing.
- **Fit faults**: gore floats off the sternum: gore too tall for the shape or wire too wide; replace with a plunge wire and lower the gore; inner cup gaps: shorten the inner edge 3 to 5 mm; strap slides: move inward.
- **Variants**: balconette #balconette-bra, push-up (adds pad pieces, not described here).
- **CLO3D**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra; test with an avatar whose sternum is flat; a floating gore appears as a gap.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; numbers of inner edge angle not known.

## Demi Bra {#demi-bra}

- **Aliases**: demi-cup bra, half-cup bra, demi balconette; fr: soutien-gorge demi-corbeille
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: cup covering about half of the breast; straight or slightly curved top edge low on the breast, low-cut wire, medium gore, wide-set straps.
- **Pieces**: as #balconette-bra (cup is two-piece or one-piece with a dart): upper cup (2), lower cup (2), cradle (2), wing (2+2), power bar (2), channel (2), straps (2), elastics, hook-and-eye, wires (2).
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified outlines. Same as #balconette-bra with a lower neck edge T. The upper cup is shallow (a thin strip above M), so the seam M lies close to the top of the cup; the cup depth is therefore mostly in the lower cup. Some demi cups are one piece with a vertical dart from the upper edge toward the bust point; the dart legs are sewn to each other and the cup shape then needs the wire to be exact.
- **Drafting**: as #balconette-bra with T lowered further; strap points at the outer third; gore 3.5 to 4.5 cm.
- **Sewing order**: as #balconette-bra.
- **Finishes and notions**: picot elastic on T; 12 mm channel.
- **Fabrics**: as #balconette-bra; stable fabric keeps the half-cup shape.
- **Fit faults**: breast slides out above the edge: raise T by 5 to 10 mm; edge gapes: shorten T by 2 to 3 percent.
- **Variants**: balconette #balconette-bra (more upper coverage), plunge #plunge-bra.
- **CLO3D**: as #balconette-bra.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; outlines unverified.

## T-Shirt Moulded Bra {#tshirt-moulded-bra}

- **Aliases**: seamless moulded bra, moulded cup bra, spacer bra; fr: soutien-gorge moulé, bonnet moulé, soutien-gorge sans couture
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: smooth cup surface with no cup seams, rounded uniform dome; edges cut or bonded; often a stitched seam only at the neckline and the wire channel; spacer or foam cups.
- **Pieces**: moulded cup (2), outer fabric over cup (optional), cradle (2), wing (2+2), channel (2), strap (2), elastics, hook-and-eye, wires (2). Moulded cups are not cut from flat patterns: the cup is formed by heat on a mould from foam, spacer fabric or tricot, and arrives as a finished shape with a flat trim edge.
- **Shape**: text source (manufacturing practice): cups are moulded. The patternmaker draws the cup trim line (the edge of the moulded shell after the flat is trimmed) and the cradle and wing around it. Trim line anchors: wire edge (lower), neck edge (upper, with notches at S and N1), side edge. The cradle top edge is drawn to the wire line and is sewn to the cup wire edge; the cup neck edge is finished with elastic or a bonded edge. Mould size and shape are specified by the supplier per cup size; the patternmaker supplies the wire, the trim-line and the sizes. Everything outside the cup (cradle, wing) is trade practice and unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Fix wire and cup size; request or pick a mould (volume and depth by size, not drawn here). 2. Draw the cradle and wing to the wire line. 3. Match the cup trim edge to the wire length. 4. Specify the strap attachment point on the cup neck edge. 5. Cup lining or cover fabric is cut as a flat blank (pieces cut shapes like a 2-piece lined cup) before moulding; sizes come from the mould supplier (not known).
- **Sewing order**: cup bonded or stitched to the cradle; wire channel; wings; elastics; straps; hook-and-eye; wires.
- **Finishes and notions**: bonded edges, no seams on the cup surface, spacer or foam lining, 12 mm channel.
- **Fabrics**: nylon tricot or spacer over polyurethane foam; cup rigidity is part of the foam, not of the pattern.
- **Fit faults**: dents in the cup: cup too small, go up a size; gap at the neckline: wrong mould shape for the breast, not fixable by pattern changes; edge shows through: trim line too sharp.
- **Variants**: seamed full cup #underwire-full-cup-bra, soft cup #soft-cup-bra.
- **CLO3D**: moulded cups are not cut; model the cup as a single rigid-ish piece from a 3D shell or as a foam-like fabric with high bending stiffness; sew the cup to the cradle along the wire; the user supplies or traces the mould shape.
- **Confidence**: construction principle trade practice; no measured cup blank data.

## Bralette {#bralette}

- **Aliases**: triangle bralette, longline bralette, wireless bra, bra top; fr: bralette, brassière sans armatures
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: no wire, little or no padding; triangle or soft two-piece cups; elastic under the bust; strap and band often in lace or elastic; longline versions extend to the lower rib cage.
- **Pieces**: cup (2, triangle or two-piece), cup lining (2), band (1 or 2 with CB seam), straps (2), under-band elastic, top elastic, hook-and-eye or pullover; longline adds a band panel (1 front, 1 back).
- **Shape**: techniques 5.6 gives a triangle cup base = bust width/2 + 0.5 cm and a height from the bust point to the top; the rest is unverified. Triangle cup: three corners: A (inner, near CF at the band), B (outer, at the side), C (top, strap point); bottom edge AB attaches to the band (curved convex downward by a few mm), edges AC and BC are finished with elastic or lace. Band: a strip of the underbust width x 0.80 to 0.85, 5 to 8 cm high, with the cups sewn along the top edge. Longline: the band panel is a tall rectangle-like shape with a curved upper edge following the cup base, and a bottom edge at the lower rib cage; boning optional at the side seams.
- **Drafting**: 1. Band length: underbust x 0.80 to 0.85, from techniques 5.6. 2. Triangle cup per techniques 5.6; add 0.5 to 1 cm fullness at the cup base for roundness (not a source value). 3. Elastic 85 to 95 percent on AC and BC. 4. Straps from C. 5. Longline: band height depends on style, not known.
- **Sewing order**: cup lining to cup, cup to band, elastics, straps, closure or pullover seam.
- **Finishes and notions**: fold-over elastic, plush-back elastic 15 to 25 mm under the cup (techniques 5.6), hook-and-eye or none.
- **Fabrics**: stretch lace, mesh, jersey with elastane; need stabiliser if lace is soft.
- **Fit faults**: cup gapes at the top: shorten AC/BC by 3 to 5 percent; bust spills at the bottom: base too narrow; band rides up: shorten the band.
- **Variants**: soft cup #soft-cup-bra, sports bra #sports-bra.
- **CLO3D**: cup triangles with edge elastic seams; mesh 4 to 6 mm; model the band as a power net rectangle.
- **Confidence**: base measurements from techniques 5.6 (trade practice); shapes unverified.

## Soft-Cup Bra {#soft-cup-bra}

- **Aliases**: wire-free bra, wireless soft cup, lounge bra; fr: soutien-gorge souple, sans armatures
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: wire-free with fabric cups shaped by seams and darts; band with side support but no cradle wire; often a two-piece or three-piece cup.
- **Pieces**: cup pieces (2 or 4 or 6), cup lining, band (1 or 2), side sling (2), straps (2), elastics, hook-and-eye.
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified outlines. Same cup geometry as #underwire-full-cup-bra without the wire channel; the lower cup edge (formerly the wire line) is joined directly to the band top edge with a stabilised seam (stay tape or elastic) and the band edge may be shaped as a slight concave curve. Support comes from a wide band with a side sling.
- **Drafting**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra with the wire replaced by the band edge; stabilise the lower cup seam with elastic at 90 to 95 percent.
- **Sewing order**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra without the wire and channel.
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic at the underbust, 15 to 25 mm; straps 10 to 18 mm.
- **Fabrics**: stable or lightly stretch jersey, lace with stabiliser, cotton spandex; need firmer lining than the outer.
- **Fit faults**: cup collapses: add a lining layer; band rides: shorten and add a wider elastic; side spill: add a side sling.
- **Variants**: bralette #bralette, wired #underwire-full-cup-bra.
- **CLO3D**: as #bralette.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; outlines unverified.

## Sports Bra (compression and encapsulation) {#sports-bra}

- **Aliases**: sport bra, crop top bra, racerback sports bra; fr: brassière de sport
- **Family**: lingerie, activewear
- **Recognize**: compression: pullover, one wide front panel with no individual cup seams, racerback; encapsulation: two separate moulded or seamed cups, often with a wire or a firm band and adjustable straps.
- **Pieces**: techniques 6.5: front (1, or 2 with CF seam), back (1), front lining (1), back lining (1), straps (2), under-band elastic (1), top elastic (1), optional cups (2). Encapsulation adds cup pieces (2 or 4), cradle or band (2), closure.
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified outlines. Compression: front piece FP is a bib shape: bottom edge (band edge) a straight or slightly curved line at the underbust, side edges as armhole curve from the side seam up to the strap point, top edge T neckline (front depth 8 to 14 cm from techniques 6.5); back BP is the same with a racerback shape, back neckline 3 to 5 cm deep; FP and BP are sewn at the side seams; straps join the top corners to the back (Y or T meeting point 8 to 12 cm below the neck for racerback). Encapsulation: each cup is a two- or three-piece cup as #soft-cup-bra, sewn into a band that wraps the rib cage and closes at CB with hooks or at CF with a zip.
- **Drafting**: techniques 6.5: band length underbust x 0.80 to 0.85, bust width reduced by 10 to 15 percent on a double layer, strap width 15 to 25 mm (low and medium) or 30 to 40 mm (high), strap length reduced by 5 to 10 percent, edge elastics 0.85 to 0.90.
- **Sewing order**: lining to outer, shoulders and side seams, elastics (under band, top, armhole), straps.
- **Finishes and notions**: flatlock or coverstitch, plush elastic, bonded edges; mesh insert possible.
- **Fabrics**: nylon or polyester with elastane 15 to 25 percent; moisture-wicking; at least 50 percent stretch.
- **Fit faults**: bounce: more compression or wider band; straps dig: widen or pad; band rides up: shorten and use higher modulus elastic (techniques 6.5).
- **Variants**: bralette #bralette, soft cup #soft-cup-bra.
- **CLO3D**: for compression, model as a double layer, strong negative ease, check strain map; cups of an encapsulation bra as separate pieces.
- **Confidence**: numbers from techniques 6.5 (typical); shape unverified.

## Nursing Bra {#nursing-bra}

- **Aliases**: maternity bra, breastfeeding bra, drop-cup bra; fr: soutien-gorge d'allaitement
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: each cup opens without taking the bra off: a clip at the strap to drop the upper cup (drop-cup), a zip or flap, or crossover panels; a soft or wire-free base; often a wider band with 3 hook rows for size change.
- **Pieces**: drop-cup type: lower cup (2), upper cup flap (2), strap clip assembly (2), side sling (2), band (2+2), straps (2), elastics, hook-and-eye; crossover type: two crossing front panels.
- **Shape**: unverified, two common types without a drawn pattern. Drop-cup: the cup is cut at the horizontal seam M as for a two-piece cup, but the upper cup UC is not sewn to the lower cup along its whole length; it is attached at the neck end and fastened to the strap clip, so it folds down to expose the breast. Crossover (wrap): left and right cup panels are cut as triangles that cross at CF and attach to the band, each panel sewn on the outside edge and fastened with a snap or clip near the strap.
- **Drafting**: as #soft-cup-bra with a cup area increase of one cup size allowed for milk volume (a trade practice, no number found); closure positions marked in the drafting notes. Strap clips are set at the upper cup top outer corner S.
- **Sewing order**: cups as #soft-cup-bra; attach clips; bar-tack the stress points.
- **Finishes and notions**: nursing clips, plush elastic, extra hook rows, soft fabrics.
- **Fabrics**: cotton jersey with elastane, stable lining; need stretch recovery.
- **Fit faults**: leakage at the clip: overlap too small; clip rubs: pad or cover; band rides up: shorten.
- **Variants**: soft cup #soft-cup-bra, sports bra #sports-bra.
- **CLO3D**: closures are not simulated; model the closed state.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; unverified outlines.

## Strapless Bra {#strapless-bra}

- **Aliases**: bandeau bra, multiway bra, strapless underwire bra; fr: soutien-gorge sans bretelles, bustier bra
- **Family**: lingerie, bras
- **Recognize**: no straps; a taller, firmer band; internal boning at the sides and CF; silicone gripper along the top edge; cup edge reaches the top of the band.
- **Pieces**: cup (2) as #underwire-full-cup-bra or #demi-bra, band-cradle (2) taller than usual, wing (2 outer + 2 lining), bones (4 to 6), silicone gripper elastic, hook-and-eye (3 to 4 rows), wires.
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified outlines. Wire and cup as #underwire-full-cup-bra; the band is taller (value not known; the usual side height 7 to 9 cm from techniques 5.5 step 3 is not enough); the cup top edge T and the band top edge are a continuous straight or gentle curve; boning channels run vertical at CF, side seams and between. Gripper elastic is sewn to the top edge.
- **Drafting**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra with a taller band, bone channels 8 to 12 mm (techniques 5.9), CB with 3 to 4 hook rows; the top edge is cut at 100 percent, not stretched, for grip.
- **Sewing order**: as #underwire-full-cup-bra; insert bones before the band is closed.
- **Finishes and notions**: bones 6 mm flat steel or plastic, silicone-backed elastic, extra hook-and-eye.
- **Fabrics**: stable, firm, with power net lining; nonslip finish.
- **Fit faults**: slides down: increase boning, gripper, or band tension; digs at the sides: shorten bones or use plastic.
- **Variants**: balconette #balconette-bra, bandeau top in swimwear #bikini-bandeau.
- **CLO3D**: model the bones as stiffer narrow strips; friction of gripper cannot be simulated.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; band heights not known.

## Basic Brief {#basic-brief}

- **Aliases**: full brief, classic brief, panties, knickers, mid-rise brief; fr: culotte, slip (femme), culotte taille mi-haute
- **Family**: lingerie, briefs (knit)
- **Recognize**: three-piece stretch garment: front, back and a lined crotch gusset; side seams; waist and leg openings finished with elastic or a turned edge; leg curve moderate, mid rise.
- **Pieces**: front (1), back (1), gusset outer (1), gusset lining (1), waist elastic (1), leg elastic (2). Front and back are single pieces (the side seams are on the pattern edge); gusset lining often cotton jersey.
- **Shape**: trade practice, consistent with techniques 5.7; no drawn pattern. Front FP and back BP are cut on the fold at CF and CB. Anchors: W = waist corner at the side seam, H = hip point on the side seam, L = leg point (where the leg curve meets the side seam), C = crotch end of the leg curve. Side seam W-H-L straight to slightly curved, about 10 to 14 cm tall in a full brief (unverified). Leg curve L to C concave toward the body; the back BP curve is deeper than the front FP curve, and BP is wider at the crotch (back width at the gusset 9 to 11 cm, front 7 to 8 cm, techniques 5.7). Gusset: a long rounded rectangle 7 to 8 cm wide, 12 to 14 cm long, rounded at both ends. Joins: FP side seam to BP side seam; the gusset front end sews to the front crotch edge of FP, the gusset back end to the back crotch edge of BP; the lining layer of the gusset encloses one of these edges (sandwich); leg elastic runs around the whole leg opening L-C-gusset-C-L.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take hip, waist, rise from waist to crotch; choose the reduction R (10 to 15 percent for 50 to 80 percent stretch, techniques 5.7). 2. Draw half-hip width x (1 - R) and the rise: front rise 15 to 20 cm, back rise 22 to 28 cm for a full brief (techniques 5.7; typical). 3. Draw the waist line straight CF to W, slightly curved at the hip. 4. Draw the leg curve from C to a point 5 to 8 cm above the hip line on the side seam. 5. Draw the gusset 7 to 8 cm x 12 to 14 cm, rounded. 6. Leg elastic at 0.85 to 0.95 of the leg edge, waist elastic 0.85 to 0.90 (techniques 5.7). 7. Seam allowance 0.6 to 1.0 cm (overlock), none if edges are elastic-bound.
- **Sewing order**: assemble the gusset (outer plus lining stitched at the front end) and attach to the back first, then the front; sew side seams; leg elastics; waist elastic (techniques 5.7).
- **Finishes and notions**: 6 to 12 mm leg elastic (plush-back or picot), 10 to 25 mm waist elastic, 3-step zigzag or coverstitch, lace trim optional.
- **Fabrics**: cotton jersey with 5 to 8 percent elastane, microfibre, stretch lace. Higher stretch means a larger reduction; a lace brief needs a stabilised crotch.
- **Fit faults**: leg elastic cuts: lengthen elastic 3 percent; leg gapes: shorten 3 percent; back sags: raise back rise 1 to 2 cm; crotch rides: shorten the gusset (techniques 5.7).
- **Variants**: #bikini-brief, #hipster-brief, #boyshort, #high-waist-brief, #thong, #french-knickers.
- **CLO3D**: front, back and gusset patterns; mesh 4 to 6 mm; sew the gusset to the crotch edges, leg and waist edges with the elastic ratio; collision thickness 0.
- **Confidence**: proportions typical (techniques 5.7, trade practice); outlines unverified.

## Bikini Brief {#bikini-brief}

## Modern Corset (underbust and overbust, steel-boned) {#corset-modern}

- **Aliases**: underbust corset, overbust corset, waist-training corset, steel-boned corset; fr: corset (moderne), corset buste long
- **Family**: corsetry
- **Recognize**: rigid garment made of vertical panels joined by long seams, flat steel bones in the seams and spiral steel at curved seams, a steel busk at CF (two flat steel plates with loops and pins), CB lacing through grommets, a waist tape, a modesty panel behind the lacing; the top is cut above or below the bust (overbust or underbust).
- **Pieces**: panels per side: 4 to 8 (11-panel and 13-panel patterns exist, see below), outer fabric and strength layer (coutil or canvas) and lining; busk (1 pair), lacing strips (2, each with a flat bone either side of the grommets), modesty panel (1), waist tape (1), binding strips (top and bottom), bones (flat and spiral), grommets/eyelets.
- **Shape**: text source (FreeSewing Cathrin underbust corset): 11 panels with the centre panel (panel 1) cut once on the fold and panels 2 to 6 cut twice; each panel has a core layer and an outer layer; the drafting method uses underbust, waist and hip circumference, waist-to-underbust and waist-to-hip lengths. The position of panel 1 (CF or CB) is not stated in the source (unverified). Trade practice for the structure: each panel is a long vertical piece whose width changes along its height: narrowest at the waist, widening toward the bottom (hip spring) and slightly toward the top, so each seam is a curved line that is concave at the waist and convex at the hip; a bust gusset or cup is added in overbust styles. All panel seams are sewn along these curves; the grommet strips are straight. Panels are numbered from CF or CB, notches mark the waist, the hip line and the underbust line. Waist reduction: starter 5 to 10 cm, trained 10 to 15 cm (techniques 5.9, 7.2); lacing gap (closed) 2 to 5 cm.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take underbust, waist, high hip, hip, waist-to-underbust and waist-to-hip lengths (as in the Cathrin measurements). 2. Decide the reduction R (5 to 10 cm, up to 15 cm for a trained wearer) and a lacing gap G (2.5 to 5 cm). 3. Total panel width at the waist (all panels, both sides) = W - R - G; per panel at the waist (W - R - G) / (2 n), n = panels per side (techniques 7.2). 4. Hip: corset hip circumference = H - 5 to 10 cm, not smaller than the waist (techniques 7.2). 5. Height: 12 to 17 cm above the waist (to under the bust) and 8 to 12 cm below for a classic length (techniques 7.2). 6. Draw each panel as a vertical strip whose edges are smooth curves through the waist, hip and top widths; pair seams must have equal length (walk them). 7. Bone channels 8 to 12 mm for 6 mm steel (techniques 5.9). Add 1.0 to 1.5 cm seam allowance; for strength use a strength layer plus a fashion layer.
- **Sewing order**: lay the strength layer and fashion layer together (flatlined) per panel or sew separate; sew panels from the centre back panel; press seams; insert busk and lacing strips; bone channels; boning (flat at busk and lacing, spiral at curves); waist tape through all layers; bind top and bottom; set grommets 1.5 to 2 cm apart on the lacing strips (techniques 7.2); lace.
- **Finishes and notions**: coutil (strength layer), flat steel bones 6 to 8 mm, spiral steel 4 to 5 mm, busk 10 to 20 cm for an underbust, waist tape 2 to 2.5 cm, binding, size 2 grommets, about 3 m of lacing (Cathrin).
- **Fabrics**: non-stretch coutil, cotton canvas, denim for the core, cotton, linen, silk or satin for the outer; no stretch.
- **Fit faults**: top gapes: reduce the top edge circumference; digs into the hip: raise the lower edge or add hip spring; wrinkles at the waist: panel too wide at the waist; boning pokes out: bone too long, shorten by 2.5 to 3.8 cm from the channel length (techniques 7.2).
- **Variants**: #corset-victorian, #corset-edwardian, #waist-cincher.
- **CLO3D**: use very stiff panels (bending stiffness high, stretch zero), pieces as panels, sew the seams with the real lengths; make bones as stiffer strips or narrow internal lines; fit at a fixed avatar waist with a negative ease; the user must check the closed gap at CB.
- **Confidence**: text source (Cathrin description, techniques 7.2); outlines of individual panels unverified.

## Victorian Corset {#corset-victorian}

- **Aliases**: Victorian corset, 1880s corset, hourglass corset; fr: corset victorien
- **Family**: corsetry, historical
- **Recognize**: rounded hourglass silhouette with a curved front (bust and abdomen swell, waist dips), a curved busk at CF (12 to 14 inch typical), CB lacing, gussets at the bust and hip, many panels, bones in all seams, a long hip line; the front edge is sometimes busk-backed with a stud closure.
- **Pieces**: panels per side (6 is documented for an 1886 pattern reproduction), gussets at bust (1 per side, in many 1880s styles) and hips (1 per side), busk (1 pair), lacing strips (2), bone casings, lining, binding.
- **Shape**: text source for the principle: an 1886 corset reproduction is a 6-panel pattern from a Dutch fashion magazine with a busk at CF and CB lacing (Amazon Dry Goods listing); other documented structures vary. The extra rounded shape comes from gussets: a gusset is a small, long triangle or lozenge-shaped piece inserted in a slit cut in two adjacent panels at the hip or bust, widening the circumference at that level; the gusset base lies on the cup or the hip line, its two long edges are sewn to the two slit edges of the neighbouring panels. Unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take bust, underbust, waist, hip and the lengths. 2. Reduce the waist by 5 to 10 cm for a comfortable wear (techniques 5.9, 7.2); the lacing gap 2 to 5 cm. 3. Panels per side 5 to 8; the hip gussets add 6 to 10 cm total to the hip circumference (derived from the 4 to 8 cm spring in techniques 5.9, typical). 4. The front curve for the busk is longer than the straight line: curved busk 13 to 14 inch typical. Then follow #corset-modern drafting. Keep unverified shapes confirmed on a toile.
- **Sewing order**: sew panels, insert gussets (stitch the slit edge, then the gusset along the slit), channels, busk in the CF pockets, bones, lacing grommets, binding.
- **Finishes and notions**: coutil or twill with cotton lining, steel bones or substitute (modern makers use steel), curved busk, eyelets 36 (one 1880s reproduction), lacing about 5 m (Fashion Through Her Story reproduction), cord piping optional.
- **Fabrics**: coutil, cotton twill, satin outer; period corsets sometimes use cording.
- **Fit faults**: bust gapes: reshape the gusset; hips pinch: add width in the gusset; busk pulls at the front: the front is too short.
- **Variants**: #corset-edwardian, #corset-modern.
- **CLO3D**: panels rigid, gussets as separate small triangles sewn into slits; check strain; pose avatar with a lower waist circumference.
- **Confidence**: construction principle text source; panel count example only; shapes unverified.

- **Fit faults**: feet too short: lengthen the sole; zip pulls up the neck: add a guard; sleeves rub: lengthen cuff.

## Children's Dress {#childrens-dress}

- **Aliases**: girl's dress, toddler dress, yoke dress, party dress; fr: robe d'enfant, robe fillette
- **Family**: childrenswear
- **Recognize**: a bodice with no darts, a gathered or A-line skirt, a yoke or waist seam, short sleeves, a CB zip or button closure.
- **Pieces**: bodice front (1), bodice back (2), skirt front (1), skirt back (1 or 2), sleeve (2), neck facing (1), lining, closure, optional sash or pockets.
- **Shape**: text source (techniques 11.5): use a block with no darts; ease 8 to 12 cm at the chest; neck width = neck / 5 (+0.5 for toddlers); front neck depth = width + 1.5; armhole slightly lower (+1 cm); hem allowance 4 to 6 cm for growth; openings at CB or at the shoulder (envelope neck overlap 2.5 cm) for babies. Neck width = neck/5 (+0.5 toddler), front depth = width + 1.5 (techniques 11.5). 4. Armhole +1 cm. 5. Skirt width = hip + gathering ratio of 1.5 to 2 (typical). 6. Hem allowance 4 to 6 cm; consider growth tuck (see #children-growth-allowances-grading). 7. Safety: no cords at the neck for under 7 years (EN 14682, techniques 11.5).

- **Variants**: #chemise, #childrens-elastic-waist-trousers.

## Children's Elastic-Waist Trousers {#childrens-elastic-waist-trousers}

- **Recognize**: pull-on trousers with an elastic waist (no fly), straight legs, often a cuff at the ankle or a growth cuff, optional side pockets and drawstring.
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (2), waistband casing (cut-on or separate, 1), waist elastic, cuff (2, optional), pocket bags (2 to 4).
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified numbers. A simple trouser block with a shallow crotch curve: the crotch depth of children is lower (rise short; the child's rise is measured); waist and hip are almost the same size in young children so the pattern is nearly straight from waist to hip; back crotch curve deeper than front. Rise = measured sitting rise + 2 cm ease (typical). 4. Waist: elastic at 0.80 to 0.90 of the waist (typical for children), casing 2 cm above elastic. 5. Growth: add 4 to 6 cm to the hem or a deep cuff (techniques 11.5). 6. Drawstrings: EN 14682: cords and drawstrings at the waist: free ends of cords at the chest or waist not longer than 140 mm (secondary source), no cords in the hood and neck area for under 7 years; check the standard before use.

- **Variants**: #kids-dungarees, #pajama-trousers.

## Growth Allowances and Grading for Children {#children-growth-allowances-grading}

- **Aliases**: kids' pattern grading, children's size charts, growth tucks; fr: gradation vêtements enfants, marge de croissance
- **Family**: childrenswear technique
- **Recognize**: size labels by height (not by chest like adults); growth devices: deep hems, folded cuffs, adjustable straps, elastic waists, raglan sleeves, tucks.
- **Pieces**: not applicable (technique); applies to all pattern pieces.
- **Shape**: not applicable; this is a technique entry.
- **Drafting**: 1. Size by height: Ottobre uses 6 cm height steps, starting at 50 cm (pattern adjustment guide). 2. Grading increments from Müller & Sohn (children's coat size 122 to 140): body height 6 cm per size and chest 3 cm per size; pattern-level chest width increment 21 mm and in a jacket 128 to 152 chest width 28 mm and jacket length 17 mm (their stated pattern increments; do not mix with the body increments). 3. Ottobre (when a child grows one size) chest, waist and hip increase only 2 to 3 cm. 4. EN 13402 gives preferred numbers for body dimensions for children but the intervals do not cover all body shapes (a study found 22 percent of bust measurements for 7 to 10-year-old girls were covered); treat tables as a framework, check against current standard text (not obtained). 5. Children's grading is driven by height more than girth (practitioner view, secondary). 6. Never scale adult patterns proportionally (techniques 11.5); use charts for the age group. 7. Growth allowances typical: hem allowance 4 to 6 cm (techniques 11.5); sleeves and trousers: deeper hem 3 to 5 cm that can be released (typical); adjustable elastic waists and straps. 8. Safety: EN 14682 (cords and drawstrings) limits: no cords or drawstrings in the hood and neck area for children up to 7 years (height about 1.34 m), free ends of cords at the chest or waist not over 140 mm, front-tied belts no more than 360 mm untied from the tie point (figures from consumer and regulator summaries; verify in the standard text); armscye and neck shaped to avoid choking.
- **Sewing order**: not applicable.
- **Finishes and notions**: turn-up cuffs, hem tucks (stitched but releasable), button-adjust straps, snap-adjust waists, elastic with adjustable buttons inside.
- **Fabrics**: allow for pre-wash shrinkage (typical 2 to 4 percent in cotton); stable fabric with stretch for longer wear.
- **Fit faults**: sleeves too short after months: add a deep cuff; trousers drag: let down the hem; chest tight: raglan or stretch.
- **Variants**: #childrens-dress, #childrens-elastic-waist-trousers, #kids-dungarees.
- **CLO3D**: the grading tool changes size on the avatar of a given size; create the size table from a child's size chart; check with a child avatar rather than scaled adult.
- **Confidence**: increments are from Ottobre and Müller & Sohn (verified published values); EN 13402 and EN 14682 figures are from secondary summaries; typical growth allowances are trade practice.
