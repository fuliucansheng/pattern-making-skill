# Bottoms: skirts, trousers, shorts, jumpsuits

Entry-per-garment database (format in `FORMAT.md`). Methods are in `../techniques/skirts-trousers.md`; this file
is the lookup layer: what each garment is, which pieces it has, where its anchors are, which edge sews to which.

Reading conventions used in every entry:

- Notation: W waist, H hip (full circumferences), L finished length, C = W + ease (circle waist circumference),
  a ease, SA seam allowance, d pleat depth, n count. Metric. Half-body unless stated.
- Skirt anchors (front piece, drawn with CF on the left): A waist at CF, B waist at side seam (raised 0.7 to 1.2 cm
  above the waist line), C hip at side seam, D hem at side seam, E hem at CF. Back piece mirrors it with CB.
  Side seam B-D sews to the back B-D. Notch the hip level on both.
- Trouser anchors (front piece): A waist at CF, B waist at side seam, C crotch point (where crotch curve meets
  inseam), K knee, Hm hem. Crease line (grain) runs waist to hem through the centre of the leg. Inseam C-K-Hm sews
  to back inseam, side seam B-K-Hm to back side seam, front crotch curve A-C to back crotch curve (below the fly
  stop). Back piece: CB higher and inclined, crotch extension wider than the front.
- No entry here comes from a drawn pattern read by the author. "Shape" therefore states the construction logic
  and marks outlines "unverified" unless a specific drawn source is named. Numbers are typical textbook or
  workshop ranges (several textbooks differ by 0.5 to 1 cm) and must be confirmed on a toile.
- Ids `#kilt` and `#coverall-boiler-suit` are defined in other KB files (kilt in the traditional-dress file, coverall in
  workwear) and are only linked from here.

# SKIRTS

## Straight Skirt {#straight-skirt}

- **Aliases**: slim skirt, block skirt, tube skirt (when very close); fr: jupe droite
- **Family**: skirt, fitted, from the skirt block
- **Recognize**: near-parallel side seams from hip to hem, hem width roughly equal to hip width; 1 or 2 darts per side at front and back; CB zip; hem sweep often too small to walk without a vent or slit.
- **Pieces**: front (x1 on fold), back (x2, CB seam for zip), waistband (x1) or facings front x1 / back x2, interfacing for band or facings. Optional lining with the same cut.
- **Shape**: Front: A at CF on fold; waist edge A-B with the dart(s) (typical 1.5 to 3 cm intake, 9 to 10 cm long at front); B-C-D side seam, slight curve in over the waist-to-hip, then straight vertical C-D (hem width = hip width, or up to 2 cm less per quarter); hem D-E horizontal (square to CF); E-A on fold. Back: same, CB edge A-E straight (or with a 0.5 to 1 cm shaped CB), darts 12 to 14 cm long, waist lowered 0.5 to 1 cm at CB. Hip quarter = (H + 4 to 6)/4, +0.5 on the front, -0.5 on the back. Side seam front sews to back side seam (equal length); CB seam sews back to back below the zip stop. Source: construction logic from the block, unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draw the block on the measured waist-to-hip depth (typical 18 to 22 cm) and length L. 2. Hip quarter (H + 4 to 6)/4 (+0.5 / -0.5 front/back). 3. Waist-hip difference per quarter shared between side seam (1 to 1.5 cm, 3 max) and darts (1.5 to 3 cm each). 4. Raise side waist 0.7 to 1.2 cm, lower CB 0.5 to 1 cm, redraw the waist as a curve square to CF and CB. 5. Hem: vertical from hip to hem; allowance 3 to 4 cm. 6. Check hem sweep; below about 85 to 90 cm full circumference add a vent or slit (see #pencil-skirt). All values typical.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Darts. 2. CB seam from zip stop to hem. 3. Zip. 4. Side seams. 5. Waistband or facing. 6. Hem. 7. Hook and bar.
- **Finishes and notions**: invisible or lapped zip 18 to 22 cm, overlocked seams pressed open, blind or catch-stitched hem, hook and bar, waistband interfacing.
- **Fabrics**: wovens 150 to 300 g/m2 (wool crepe, twill, gabardine, linen). Stretch wovens allow 2 to 4 cm less hip ease. Stripes and checks: match at CB and side seams, dart placement must not cut the motif.
- **Fit faults**: horizontal hip wrinkles -> add 1 to 2 cm total ease at the side seams and check the hip line depth; smile wrinkles under the seat -> lengthen the back / add 0.5 to 1 cm at the hip; side seams swing -> rebalance front : back as (H/4 + 0.5) : (H/4 - 0.5); gap at CB waist -> lower CB 1 cm and enlarge the CB dart; hem lifts at the front -> lengthen the front.
- **Variants**: #pencil-skirt (tapered, vent), #a-line-skirt, #yoke-skirt, #skirt-lengths.
- **CLO3D**: sew line to line, keep darts as sewn darts, particle distance 5 to 10 mm, woven 150 to 300 g/m2; seat ease below 4 cm shows pull wrinkles at the thighs in the avatar pose.
- **Confidence**: trade practice and the techniques sheet (A1). Hip distribution and dart sizes typical; not tied to one source.

## Pencil Skirt {#pencil-skirt}

- **Aliases**: wiggle skirt (when very close), fitted skirt with vent; fr: jupe crayon, jupe fourreau
- **Family**: skirt, fitted
- **Recognize**: straight skirt whose side seams taper inward below the hip so the hem is narrower than the hip; always a back vent, back kick pleat or front/side slit for walking; CB zip; usually knee length.
- **Pieces**: front x1 on fold, back x2 (one with a vent extension, one with underlap), waistband x1 or facing, interfacing; kick-pleat variant adds an underlay x1 (self or lining); lining front x1, back x2.
- **Shape**: As #straight-skirt down to the hip C; below C each side seam leans inward: D is 2 to 3 cm inside the vertical through C (hem circumference = H - 6 to H - 12). Vent version: at the back CB, below the zip stop the CB edge continues straight to E; on the left back piece (overlap) an extension of 3.5 to 4.5 cm is added beyond CB from the vent top (15 to 25 cm above hem) down to the hem; the right back piece (underlap) has a normal 1.5 to 2 cm SA. Kick pleat version: the back panels are cut with a CB slit; a separate underlay rectangle (12 to 20 cm wide, length vent + SA) is stitched into the two slit edges and pressed to form an inverted pleat; the top of the pleat is bar-tacked or stitched in a square. Slit version: a side or front seam left open for 15 to 35 cm from the hem, SA 1.5 to 2.5 cm each edge. The taper line is straight from C to D (or from the knee for a narrower pencil). Source: unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft as #straight-skirt. 2. At hem take in 2 to 3 cm per quarter at each side seam. 3. Mark vent length 15 to 25 cm and depth 3.5 to 4.5 cm (minimum vent 15 cm if hem width about 90 cm). 4. Check walking width: full hem circumference at least 85 to 90 cm or a vent is required. 5. Hem allowance 3 to 4 cm (1 to 1.5 cm if the hem is curved). 6. Lining: 2 to 4 cm shorter than shell, add a 2 to 2.5 cm ease pleat at CB, leave a 2 to 3 cm "box" open at the vent.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Darts. 2. CB seam to the vent top, bar-tack the top. 3. Zip. 4. Side seams. 5. Press the vent flap, miter the corner, hem. 6. Waistband or facing. 7. Lining.
- **Finishes and notions**: interface the vent / slit seam allowances, bar tack at the vent top, 1.5 to 2 cm SA at the zip, hook and bar, mitred hem corner at the vent.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, gabardine, stretch wool, ponte. Very stretchy fabrics allow a smaller vent or none; stiff cloth needs the full vent. Directional prints must match across the vent.
- **Fit faults**: vent gapes or flips -> widen the extension to 4 to 4.5 cm and interface; hem restricts walking -> raise vent / add 4 to 6 cm to the hem sweep; rides up -> reduce the taper; lining drags the vent -> leave the lining open box.
- **Variants**: #straight-skirt, #tulip-skirt, #trumpet-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: build the vent as an overlap of two back pieces on different layers (underlap lower); sew the CB seam only to the vent top; mesh 5 mm near the vent.
- **Confidence**: trade practice, techniques sheet (A1, vent and kick pleat sub-sections). Dimensions typical, vent geometry from memory.

## A-Line Skirt {#a-line-skirt}

- **Aliases**: trapeze skirt, light flare; fr: jupe trapèze, jupe évasée légère
- **Family**: skirt, flared (light)
- **Recognize**: smooth waist and hip, side seams straight from the hip to a hem wider than the hip, outline forms a capital A; no gathers; darts or a yoke at the waist.
- **Pieces**: front x1 on fold, back x2 (or on fold), waistband or facings, interfacing.
- **Shape**: Front: A, B, C as the straight skirt; side seam leaves C and goes straight to D, which lies out by the flare amount (2 to 6 cm per quarter for a light A-line, 8 to 15 cm for a pronounced one); hem D-E curves slightly down toward the side seam so it meets the side seam at 90 degrees. Front and back side seams must be equal length. Dart(s) at the waist shortened or kept; any dart partly closed by pivoting to increase hem width. Source: unverified as a drawn pattern. The dresspatternmaking.com A-line pages exist as a drawn reference (diagram not read for this entry).
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace the block. 2. Extend the hip line side seam straight to the hem and add flare at D. 3. Option: close part of each dart by slash-and-pivot from hem to dart point, which moves the dart intake into the hem sweep. 4. Square the hem at the side seam and blend the hem curve between front and back. 5. Hem allowance 3 to 4 cm, 1 to 1.5 cm on a curved hem. 6. Cut the side seam at least 1 to 3 cm longer than the final if cut on the bias (fabric drops).
- **Sewing order**: darts, CB seam, zip, side seams, waistband, hang 24 h on a stand, level, hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: invisible zip 18 to 22 cm, narrow baby hem on a curved edge, optional lining 2 to 4 cm shorter.
- **Fabrics**: medium wovens (twill, wool, denim). Heavy cloth keeps a stiff A silhouette; drapey cloth collapses the angle and drops on the bias at the side seams.
- **Fit faults**: hem dips at the side -> side seam bias drop, re-level after hanging; rippling hem -> unequal hem curve lengths front and back; side seam swings -> straighten grain.
- **Variants**: #flared-skirt (more spread), #gored-skirt, #straight-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: check the hem outline equals the flat pattern; a corner at the side seam shows as a point.
- **Confidence**: trade practice, techniques sheet (A2). Flare amounts typical.

## Flared Skirt {#flared-skirt}

- **Aliases**: swing skirt, trumpet-free flare (informal); fr: jupe évasée
- **Family**: skirt, flared by slash-and-spread
- **Recognize**: fitted waist and hip, then spreading to a pronounced hem with visible folds but no gathers; hem sweep typically 2 to 3 x H, below a circle.
- **Pieces**: front x1 on fold (or x2 with CF seam), back x2, waistband or facings.
- **Shape**: Block with the darts closed by pivoting to the hem. Slash lines run from the hem to the waist (to each dart point, plus lines between them). One text-described example (dresspatternmaking.com, Flared Skirt 2, 1-dart block): each of front and back is divided into four panels with three openings; each opening spread to 12.5 cm and a 7.5 cm extension added at the side; the dart value is removed from the side seam if the dart does not fully close. Outline of the finished piece: waist as the block (smaller, darts removed), side seams flared out, hem a smooth convex curve; ends of the hem curve square to the side seam. Outline source: the page text only (diagram not read), so the shape is unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace the block and draw slash lines hem to waist (through dart points, square from the hem). 2. Cut, leaving hinges at the dart point (centre) and the waist (others). 3. Spread each opening (typical 3 to 12.5 cm; spread several smaller openings rather than one). 4. Close the darts; if a dart will not close, hinge from the waist. 5. Add the side extension. 6. True the hem so front and back meet square at the side seam. 7. Re-level after hanging 24 h; cut 1 to 3 cm extra at bias points.
- **Sewing order**: seams, zip, waistband, hang, level, baby hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: narrow rolled or baby hem (3 to 6 mm up to 1.5 cm), stay tape on bias waist edges.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, light wool, poplin. Drapey fabrics drop and ripple more; stiff fabrics stand out.
- **Fit faults**: waist rippling -> waist stretched, stay-tape; hem points down at bias -> relevel; side seam bias stretches -> stay-tape or re-grain.
- **Variants**: #a-line-skirt, #godet, #half-circle-skirt, #gored-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: particle distance 5 mm at the hem; use a mid-weight drapey fabric preset; compare the hem curve with the flat pattern.
- **Confidence**: trade practice and the techniques sheet (A2); the 12.5 cm and 7.5 cm figures are from a text description of one site's draft (unverified outline).

## Gored Skirt (4, 6, 8, 12 panels) {#gored-skirt}

- **Aliases**: panel skirt, 4-gore, 6-gore, 8-gore, 12-gore skirt; fr: jupe à découpes, jupe à godets (gores)
- **Family**: skirt, panelled
- **Recognize**: vertical seams from waist to hem, panels equal in width, each panel narrow at the waist, widest at the hem; no darts when n >= 6.
- **Pieces**: n identical gore panels (4: front x2 + back x2, seams at CF, CB, both sides; 6: one panel x6; 8: one panel x8; 12: one panel x12), waistband or facings. When n is 6 or more, seams do not have to land on CF/CB.
- **Shape**: One gore drawn about a vertical grain line (panel centre). Waist edge width = (W + a_w)/n (a_w 1 to 2 cm). Hip level width = (H + a_h)/n (a_h 4 to 6 cm). Hem width = hem sweep / n. Side edges: waist-to-hip a slight outward curve (not straight; a straight line pops at the hip), hip-to-hem straight or slightly convex. Worked example (illustrative, W 70, H 96, a_w 2, a_h 5, hem sweep 192): waist/hip/hem per gore = 4 gores: 18 / 25.25 / 48; 6: 12 / 16.8 / 32; 8: 9 / 12.6 / 24; 12: 6 / 8.4 / 16 cm. Each side edge sews to the next gore's side edge; equal lengths; notch hip level. Source: arithmetic from the formulas, shape unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draw the grain line, set waist, hip, hem half-widths symmetrical about it. 2. Join waist to hip with a gentle curve, hip to hem straight (A-line) or curved (more flare). 3. Hip level at block depth. 4. Hem sweep typically 1.5 to 3 x H for a flared gore skirt. 5. Cut n times. 6. Bias: side seams of a straight-grain-centred gore lie on bias; stay-tape them.
- **Sewing order**: stay-tape bias seams, sew gores pressing seams open, zip at a seam, waistband, hang, hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: seams pressed open, stay tape, zip in one gore seam (CB), narrow hem.
- **Fabrics**: gores suit stripes (chevrons), lining-weight crepe, wool. Directional prints must be cut consistently. Stiff fabrics hold the panel shape; drapey fabrics drop at the seams.
- **Fit faults**: gores pop at the hip -> curve the waist-to-hip section; hem uneven -> hang and re-level (bias at each seam); seam pucker -> stretched bias seams, ease or stay-tape.
- **Variants**: #godet, #trumpet-skirt, #mermaid-skirt, #a-line-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: one panel pattern, n copies sewn edge to edge; particle distance 5 mm; check equal seam lengths.
- **Confidence**: formulas from the techniques sheet (A3, typical); worked numbers computed here as illustration only.

## Godet {#godet}

- **Aliases**: godet insert, gusset insert, pie insert, gore insert (not the same as a gore panel); fr: godet
- **Family**: skirt, insert; also dresses, trousers
- **Recognize**: a triangular or pie-shaped wedge set into a slit or a seam end at the hem; seams visible as a V; extra sweep at the hem and a slightly fluted fall.
- **Pieces**: skirt panels cut with a slit (or open seam end), godet x1 per slit.
- **Shape**: Sector with its apex at the slit top: two straight edges of equal length G (slit height, typical 15 to 40 cm), curved hem edge of arc length about G x theta (theta in radians; a 60 degree godet has a hem width of about 1.05 G). The two straight edges sew to the two edges of the slit; the apex sits exactly at the top of the slit and is the stress point. The hem edge matches the skirt hem curve. Source: geometry and published descriptions (threadsmagazine.com, thecreativecurator.com); no drawn pattern read, shape unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Choose slit height G and hem width. 2. Draw a vertical grain line and a perpendicular hem line; mark G. 3. Draw the sector with radius G and chosen angle (45 to 90 degrees). 4. Add SA on both straight sides and the hem. 5. Skirt slit length = G + SA with the slit edges cut to match the godet edges.
- **Sewing order**: reinforce the apex with a square of fusible on the wrong side; pin from the apex; sew each side starting at the apex, pivot at the apex with a short stitch length; press seams toward the skirt.
- **Finishes and notions**: apex fusible square, narrow hem, optional lining.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, satin, wool, silk. Godets cut from a contrast fabric are common.
- **Fit faults**: apex puckers or tears -> reinforce, stitch exactly to the apex; godet hangs unevenly -> cut on matched grain.
- **Variants**: #gored-skirt, #trumpet-skirt, #mermaid-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: sew with the apex at the slit top; fine mesh (3 to 5 mm) near the apex.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet (A3, typical); web check supports the sector shape, not the dimensions.

## Half Circle Skirt {#half-circle-skirt}

- **Aliases**: semicircle skirt, 180 degree circle skirt; fr: jupe demi-cercle
- **Family**: skirt, circle
- **Recognize**: waist smooth with no gathers, hem a wave of folds, two bias zones; one CB seam with a zip.
- **Pieces**: skirt x1 as a half annulus (cut on a fold or as two quarter pieces), waistband x1 or facing, zip.
- **Shape**: Half annulus: centre O, inner arc radius r (the waist), outer arc R = r + L + hem; the two straight radial edges both lie on lengthwise grain and are sewn together at CB with a zip; sector angle 180 degrees. The waist arc length equals C = W + 1 to 2 cm: r = C/pi = C/3.1416 (checked: the same factor is published at dresspatternmaking.com). Example C 72: r 22.9 cm. The cut waist edge sits at r - SA (towards the centre). The hem arc is concave-up and runs the full semicircle.
- **Drafting**: 1. r = C/3.1416. 2. Fold the paper into a quarter; mark r from the corner on both folds; quarter-arc. 3. R = r + L + hem allowance (1 to 1.5 cm narrow hem). 4. Cut the pattern as a quarter, place its straight edge to the fabric fold, cut on a full-width fold and open for the half circle. 5. Waist SA toward the centre.
- **Sewing order**: stay tape the waist (cut 0.5 to 1 cm shorter), CB seam with zip, waistband, hang 24 h (48 h for viscose or crepe), level, narrow hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: stay tape 1 cm, zip 18 to 22 cm, rolled or baby hem 3 to 6 mm.
- **Fabrics**: drapey wovens drop 2 to 5 cm on the bias zones; stiff cloth needs a lower hang time. Fabric size: R across and 2R along.
- **Fit faults**: hem uneven -> relevel after hanging; waist stretches -> stay tape; lampshade stand-out -> fabric too stiff for the radius.
- **Variants**: #three-quarter-circle-skirt, #full-circle-skirt, #skater-skirt, #asymmetric-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: 5 mm particle distance at the hem; rest the avatar pose 100 to 300 frames.
- **Confidence**: radius formulas verified (dresspatternmaking.com Circle Skirts, omnicalculator and others); bias drop figures and hang times trade practice.

## Three-Quarter Circle Skirt {#three-quarter-circle-skirt}

- **Aliases**: 270 degree circle skirt; fr: jupe trois quarts de cercle, jupe 3/4 de cercle
- **Family**: skirt, circle
- **Recognize**: as the half circle with a fuller hem and four bias zones when cut as two pieces; waist smooth.
- **Pieces**: skirt x1 as a 270 degree annulus sector, or front + back with side seams; waistband; zip.
- **Shape**: Sector angle 270 degrees; inner arc radius r = C/(1.5 pi) = C/4.712 (checked); outer radius R = r + L + hem. Example C 72: r 15.3 cm. The two straight edges sew together at CB with a zip. The pattern cut in two halves yields an angle of 135 degrees each with a side seam at each end.
- **Drafting**: 1. r = C/4.712. 2. Draw the quarter-circle repeatedly three times on the fabric fold, or draw a 135 degree wedge and cut two. 3. R = r + L + hem. 4. Mark CB/CF grain on the straight edge.
- **Sewing order**: as #half-circle-skirt.
- **Finishes and notions**: stay tape, 18 to 22 cm zip, narrow hem.
- **Fabrics**: as the half circle; more fabric (3/4 circle needs about 2R square if cut as one).
- **Fit faults**: as the half circle; more bias area means more hem drop.
- **Variants**: #half-circle-skirt, #full-circle-skirt, #double-circle-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: as half circle.
- **Confidence**: radius formula verified; practice values typical.

## Full Circle Skirt {#full-circle-skirt}

- **Aliases**: circle skirt, 360 degree skirt; fr: jupe cercle, jupe corolle
- **Family**: skirt, circle
- **Recognize**: very full, deep even folds at the hem, four bias zones; one seam or two plus a waist zip.
- **Pieces**: skirt x1 full annulus (cut as 4 quarters on two folds or two halves with side seams), waistband, zip.
- **Shape**: Full annulus, sector 360 degrees; inner radius r = C/(2 pi) = C/6.283 (checked); outer R = r + L + hem. Example C 72: r 11.46 cm; for L 60 R = 71.5 cm, fabric about 143 cm square. The waist edge is a closed circle; a radial slit to the waist (CB) provides the opening or the circle is cut in two halves with seams at the sides; each slit edge sews to its opposite edge with a zip.
- **Drafting**: 1. r = C/6.283 (C = W + 1 to 2). 2. Fold paper into a quarter; mark r from the corner and R = r + L + hem. 3. Cut the cut waist edge at r - SA. 4. Grain: straight edges of the quarter on lengthwise grain.
- **Sewing order**: as #half-circle-skirt.
- **Finishes and notions**: stay-tape 1 cm cut 0.5 to 1 cm short, rolled hem 3 to 6 mm, zip 18 to 22 cm.
- **Fabrics**: 1.4 to 1.5 m square fabric for L 60; ensure wide goods; lightweight fabrics for a soft fall.
- **Fit faults**: lampshade effect -> lighter fabric; hem points -> hang and level.
- **Variants**: #double-circle-skirt, #skater-skirt, #handkerchief-hem-skirt, #petticoat.
- **CLO3D**: 5 mm particle; test bias drop with stretch values from a measured fabric.
- **Confidence**: formula verified (several calculators, dresspatternmaking.com); hang time and hem advice trade practice.

## Double Circle Skirt {#double-circle-skirt}

- **Aliases**: 720 degree circle skirt, two-circle skirt; double-layer circle skirt; fr: jupe double cercle
- **Family**: skirt, circle
- **Recognize**: either (a) one circle sweep of 720 degrees (twice around) with huge hem sweep, or (b) two stacked circle skirts of different lengths worn together (overskirt shorter).
- **Pieces**: (a) two circle pieces joined at the waist seams (or one cut as two full circles joined) + waistband + zip; (b) top circle x1, under circle x1, shared waistband.
- **Shape**: (a) Total waist arc = C split between the two circles; each circle has waist circumference C/2, so r = C/(4 pi) = C/12.566 for each (derived from r = C/theta with theta = 4 pi). Hem edge of each circle is 2 pi R. Example C 72: r 5.73 cm (a very small waist hole), and R = r + L. Each circle has a radial slit to its waist edge; the slit edges form the openings; the two circles sew to each other at the waist hole edge-to-edge or to the waistband. (b) Two ordinary full circles (see #full-circle-skirt), the top shorter by 10 to 20 cm. Source: derived geometry, no drawn pattern read.
- **Drafting**: 1. For (a): r = C/12.566; R = r + L + hem. 2. For each circle, cut a pattern as a quarter. 3. Stay-tape the waist seam; ensure the small waist hole will hold the waistband stable. 4. For (b) use separate r = C/6.283 for each.
- **Sewing order**: sew each circle's seam; join the waist edges or sandwich both in one waistband; zip at CB; hang each separately 24 to 48 h; level each hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: stay tape 1 cm; rolled hem 3 to 6 mm; optional horsehair braid for a standing hem.
- **Fabrics**: lightweight, soft wovens; fabric consumption is double the full-circle skirt (two circles of 2R).
- **Fit faults**: heavy waist -> use lightest fabric and a wide waistband; hem uneven -> relevel each layer.
- **Variants**: #full-circle-skirt, #petticoat, #skater-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: 5 mm mesh at the hem; collision layers: inner lower than outer; heavy simulations.
- **Confidence**: geometry derived (r = C/theta); definition (a) vs (b) is usage-dependent. Use as trade practice.

## Skater Skirt {#skater-skirt}

- **Aliases**: circle mini, jersey flared skirt; fr: jupe patineuse
- **Family**: skirt, circle in knit
- **Recognize**: short (40 to 50 cm), flared like a full or 3/4 circle, stretch waist (elastic or foldover band), no zip.
- **Pieces**: skirt x1 (full) or x2 halves, waistband x1 (stretch), elastic.
- **Shape**: Circle annulus (full or 3/4) with the waist hole sized for knit stretch: C = W x 0.90 to 0.95 for elastic, C = W for a wide foldover band; hem R = r + L + 1 cm. Waistband is a rectangle circumference W x 0.85 to 0.90 stretch around. The waist edge of the skirt sews to the band's lower edge (band stretched evenly); side seam(s) join the half pieces. Source: unverified; sheet-based.
- **Drafting**: 1. r = C/(2 pi) (full) or C/4.712 (3/4). 2. R = r + L + 1. 3. Cut the band with the stretch around: length W x 0.85 to 0.9, width 2 x finished + SA. 4. Mark grain on the jersey's straight direction (stretch around the waist).
- **Sewing order**: seams on overlocker, waistband tube, quarter-pin, stretch the band to the skirt, coverstitch hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic 2.5 to 5 cm width; twin needle or coverstitch hem; hem tape on curling jerseys.
- **Fabrics**: ponte, jersey with 40 to 50 percent crosswise stretch; avoid very stretchy fabrics without a stable hem.
- **Fit faults**: waist twists -> band too long or grain off; hem curls -> hem tape or narrow hem.
- **Variants**: #full-circle-skirt, #three-quarter-circle-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: knit preset; shrink the waist band horizontally (0.85 to 0.9) to simulate stretch.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet (A4); ease factors typical.

## Yoke Skirt {#yoke-skirt}

- **Aliases**: hip-yoke skirt; fr: jupe à empiècement
- **Family**: skirt, yoked
- **Recognize**: horizontal or curved seam 8 to 14 cm below the waist; smooth yoke above, gathers/pleats/flare below.
- **Pieces**: yoke front x1 on fold (or x2), yoke back x2, skirt front x1, skirt back x2, facing or band.
- **Shape**: Yoke: top edge is the waist (darts closed), lower edge is the yoke seam, a straight or curved line (8 to 12 cm at the side seam), CF fold, CB seam. The lower piece: top edge is the yoke seam length multiplied by the fullness ratio (gathers 1.3 to 2.0 depending on fabric, flare per #flared-skirt); its top sews to the yoke's lower edge. The dart intake that lay above the yoke line moves into the yoke seam. Source: technique logic, unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draw the yoke line on the block. 2. Close the darts by pivoting into the seam if the dart point lies above the line; if below, close to the line and put the rest into ease/flare. 3. Trace the yoke. 4. Spread the lower piece by 1.5 to 3 times for gathers.
- **Sewing order**: yoke seams, gather the lower edge, join to yoke, press toward yoke, topstitch 2 to 6 mm, CB zip.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip, waistband or facing, topstitching.
- **Fabrics**: cotton, denim, lightweight wool.
- **Fit faults**: yoke gapes at the lower edge -> dart not fully closed, transfer more into the seam.
- **Variants**: #denim-skirt, #tiered-skirt, #dirndl-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: sew the yoke seam with a gather ratio.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet (A5), trade practice.


## Knife-Pleated Skirt {#knife-pleated-skirt}

- **Aliases**: side-pleated skirt, one-way pleated skirt, tennis skirt (short), school skirt; fr: jupe plissée (plis plats), jupe à plis couchés
- **Family**: skirt, pleated
- **Recognize**: a row of narrow folds all pressed in the same direction, each fold overlapping the next; smooth hip yoke or stitched-down section at the top, free folds below; hem circumference barely larger than the hip.
- **Pieces**: skirt front x1 (one wide panel, or on fold), skirt back x2 (CB seam with zip), yoke or waistband x1 (shaped yoke front x1 / back x2 if the pleats are stitched to a yoke), interfacing for the band.
- **Shape**: Each panel is a rectangle widened by the pleat take-up, side and hem edges straight. Per pleat: visible step s, depth d (fold line to underfold line); fabric per pleat = s + 2d (take-up 2d; consistent with the dresspatternmaking.com Skirt with Knife Pleats page, whose example has 5 central pleats of 1.5 in depth and a 3 in underlay). When d = s the pleats fully overlap and the flat width is 3 x the finished width. Fold lines F and underfold lines U run parallel from waist to hem (straight pleats) or taper toward the hem (tapered pleats). Pleats are stitched down for 12 to 18 cm from the waist (hip level) and are free below; in the published example the block dart is absorbed into the pleat next to the side seam. Edges: panel sides sew to the next panel, the pleated top edge (width W + ease) sews to the yoke or waistband, the hem is straight, with a slight hem flare to keep the folds closed. Outline otherwise unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Finished waist F = W + ease (1 to 2 cm), steps s = F/n (typical s 2.5 to 4 cm). 2. Choose d <= s. 3. Flat waist width = F + n x 2d (+ SA). 4. Mark F and U lines alternately along waist and hem (at the hem the F-to-F distance follows the hip width / n for a straight skirt). 5. Mark the stitch-down line at hip depth (12 to 18 cm). 6. Fold direction: toward the side seam at the front, toward CB at the back. 7. Absorb the block darts in the pleat nearest the side seam, or attach to a yoke. All values typical.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Mark F and U lines (tailor's tacks or chalk). 2. Fold and baste each pleat. 3. Press from the wrong side with a damp cloth. 4. Edge-stitch the stitch-down section. 5. Seams, zip. 6. Waistband or yoke. 7. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: topstitched pleat edges 2 to 3 mm, stay tape or lining to the hip, hem 3 to 4 cm, hook and bar, zip 18 to 22 cm.
- **Fabrics**: wool, wool blends, poplin, gabardine, heat-set polyester. Thick fabric makes the hip bulky: reduce d or stitch down only to a yoke. Plaids and stripes: motif repeat equal to the step lets the pleats match.
- **Fit faults**: pleats splay at the hip -> stitch down lower or increase d; pleats flip at the back -> check fold direction; bulk at the waist -> yoke or grade the underlays; hem sways -> add a slight hem flare.
- **Variants**: #box-pleated-skirt, #inverted-pleat-skirt, #accordion-pleated-skirt, #sunray-pleated-skirt, #kilt (deep traditional pleats, other file).
- **CLO3D**: fold arrangement (fold angle, fold strength 0.5 to 1) on a flat panel, or internal lines; particle distance 5 mm to resolve fold edges; sew the waist at the finished waist length.
- **Confidence**: take-up and vocabulary verified (dresspatternmaking.com, techniques sheet A6); stitch-down depth and step values trade practice; outline unverified.

## Box-Pleated Skirt {#box-pleated-skirt}

- **Aliases**: box pleat skirt; fr: jupe à plis creux
- **Family**: skirt, pleated
- **Recognize**: pairs of folds turned away from each other, forming a flat face with a hidden underlay on each side; repeated flat rectangles from waist to hem.
- **Pieces**: skirt front x1 (or x2), skirt back x2, waistband or yoke, interfacing.
- **Shape**: One box pleat = face b plus an underlay of depth d on each side (two knife pleats folded away from each other). Take-up 4d per pleat. When d = b/2 the two underlays meet behind the face (fully closed box pleat; flat width per pleat = b + 4d = 3b). Verified on a published textbook example: depth 12 cm, finished width 60 cm, pattern width 108 cm = 60 + 4 x 12. Fold lines F on both edges of the face, underfold lines U on the outer sides of the underlays, U lines d beyond each F. F and U run vertically, waist to hem; stitched down 12 to 18 cm from the waist or onto a yoke. Outline otherwise unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Finished waist F = W + ease. 2. Choose n pleats and face b. 3. Flat width = F + n x 4d (+ SA). 4. Mark F and U lines on each pleat, waist to hem. 5. Mark the stitch-down level. 6. Check the hip: the yoke or stitched section must be at least H + 4 around. All values typical.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Mark folds. 2. Fold, baste, press. 3. Stitch down at the top or to a yoke. 4. Side seams, zip. 5. Waistband. 6. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: edge-stitching of face folds, stay tape across the pleat set, bar tack at stitch-down end, zip 18 to 22 cm.
- **Fabrics**: wool crepe, flannel, heavy cotton, polyester. Four layers at the waist: use a yoke and cut underlays in lining to cut bulk.
- **Fit faults**: pleats pop open at the hip -> stitch lower or add 1 cm ease at the hip; bulk at the waist -> yoke and trimmed underlay.
- **Variants**: #inverted-pleat-skirt, #knife-pleated-skirt, #kilt.
- **CLO3D**: fold arrangement, or a face panel with two underlay panels sewn at the folds; 5 mm particles.
- **Confidence**: take-up verified (textbook example, techniques sheet A6); outline unverified.

## Inverted Pleat Skirt {#inverted-pleat-skirt}

- **Aliases**: inverted box pleat skirt, kick pleat skirt (single pleat); fr: jupe à pli creux inversé
- **Family**: skirt, pleated (or fitted with a single pleat)
- **Recognize**: two folds that meet at the face, the underlay opening between them; seen from the right side a seam-like vertical line that opens into a pleat on movement. Single version: at CB or CF of a straight skirt.
- **Pieces**: single pleat: front x1 on fold (or with a rectangular cut-out) plus a pleat backing x1; kick pleat at CB: back x2 plus underlay x1; repeated pleats: as #box-pleated-skirt.
- **Shape**: An inverted pleat is a box pleat seen from the wrong side: two knife pleats folded toward each other, folds meeting at the centre line, take-up 4d. Single pleat (dresspatternmaking.com Skirt with Inverted Box Pleat; page text read, diagram not read): a fairly straight skirt traced from the block; to avoid a seam inside the pleat, the pleat is cut on the fold or drafted as a full pattern piece with a rectangular cut-out, or a separate pleat backing piece is added. Finished pleat width typically 8 to 12 cm; top stitched down by a bar tack or stitched square, 15 to 25 cm above the hem for a kick pleat, or all the way to the waist. Repeated-pleat outline unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace the straight skirt block. 2. Choose the pleat centre (CB for a kick pleat, CF for a front pleat) and depth d. 3. Slash and spread by 4d. 4. Mark F lines (visible folds) and U lines (underfold). 5. Backing piece x1 width 2d + SA, length pleat length + SA if the pleat is not cut in one.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Stitch the centre seam to the pleat top. 2. Open and press the pleat so the folds meet. 3. Attach the backing. 4. Edge-stitch the folds. 5. Zip. 6. Hem pleat and skirt together.
- **Finishes and notions**: bar tack at the pleat top, edge-stitching on folds, backing in lining, zip 18 to 22 cm.
- **Fabrics**: crisp wools, gabardine, denim, polyester blends; not very soft jersey (pleat collapses).
- **Fit faults**: pleat gapes -> underlay too narrow or depth too small; pleat bulks -> lining backing.
- **Variants**: #pencil-skirt (kick pleat), #box-pleated-skirt, #knife-pleated-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: three layers with collision layers; 5 mm particles.
- **Confidence**: take-up and "box pleat from the wrong side" verified; kick pleat dimensions typical (techniques sheet A1).

## Sunray Pleated Skirt {#sunray-pleated-skirt}

- **Aliases**: sunburst pleated skirt, sunburst skirt, radial pleat skirt, fan-pleated skirt; fr: jupe plissée soleil
- **Family**: skirt, pleated (radial)
- **Recognize**: very fine radial pleats narrow at the waist and fanning wider to the hem; no gathers; usually thermoplastic chiffon, georgette or satin; waistband or elastic.
- **Pieces**: skirt as a circle section (half or full circle, or panels) x1, waistband x1, zip or elastic.
- **Shape**: The flat skirt is a circle section; fold lines run along radii, so pleat width grows from waist to hem. Take-up is a ratio k of the finished waist (2 to 3, techniques sheet A6): the flat waist arc = k x C. One or two radial seams (CB). The pleated waist arc sews to the waistband. Real sunray pleating is done on a pleating machine with a paper template and heat-set, holding only on thermoplastic fibres (polyester, nylon). Outline unverified as a drawn pattern; pleater templates not read.
- **Drafting**: 1. Finished waist C = W + ease; choose k (2 to 3). 2. Choose the sector angle theta (full circle 2 pi). 3. Inner radius of the flat circle r_f = k x C/theta. 4. Outer radius R = r_f + L + hem. 5. Pleat size at the hem 1 to 2 cm typical, set by the pleater template. All values typical.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Radial seam. 2. Narrow rolled hem (before pleating). 3. Pleat (pleater, or hand-pressed with a paper template). 4. Baste the pleats at the waist. 5. Waistband or elastic casing.
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic or flat waistband, hand tacks at pleat tops, rolled hem.
- **Fabrics**: polyester chiffon, georgette, satin, crepe de chine (poly). Natural fibres lose the pleats with wear.
- **Fit faults**: pleats do not hold -> fibre not thermoplastic; pleats overlap at the waist -> k too high; waist tight -> elastic too short.
- **Variants**: #accordion-pleated-skirt, #full-circle-skirt, #knife-pleated-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: lightweight fabric with a gather ratio at the waist on a circle section, or fold arrangement for a regular fan; 3 to 5 mm particles.
- **Confidence**: principle from the techniques sheet and trade practice; ratio typical; not verified against a pleater template.

## Accordion-Pleated Skirt {#accordion-pleated-skirt}

- **Aliases**: concertina skirt, micro-pleat skirt (very fine), plissé skirt; fr: jupe plissée accordéon
- **Family**: skirt, pleated (parallel)
- **Recognize**: very narrow parallel zig-zag pleats from waist to hem, equally wide, not radial; straight or gently flared; often an elastic waist.
- **Pieces**: pre-pleated fabric panel x1 (tube) or x2, waistband or elastic casing x1; no darts.
- **Shape**: Rectangle (or slight trapezoid) of fabric, pre-pleated. Flat width before pleating = ratio x finished width (ratio 2 to 3 per the techniques sheet A6), pleat size 0.5 to 1.5 cm typical. Pleats are parallel, so the hem is as wide as the waist unless the panel is cut as a trapezoid before pleating (pleats then fan slightly). The side edges sew in one seam, the waist edge to an elastic casing or band, the hem is a narrow hem done before pleating. Outline unverified as a drawn pattern; accordion pleats are machine-made (paper template, heat) and hold only on thermoplastic fibres.
- **Drafting**: 1. Finished hip circumference H + 4 to 10 for a pull-on, or waist + ease with a pleated yoke. 2. Flat width = ratio x finished. 3. Length L + hem + casing allowance. 4. Hem before pleating.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Hem the flat edge. 2. Close the side seam. 3. Pleat. 4. Casing and elastic, or waistband.
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic 2 to 3 cm, rolled hem; pre-pleated polyester sold by the metre may be cut directly.
- **Fabrics**: polyester chiffon, crepe, georgette, satin.
- **Fit faults**: pleats do not open over the hip -> elastic waist and a wider flat width; pleats lose their set -> fibre not thermoplastic.
- **Variants**: #sunray-pleated-skirt, #knife-pleated-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: use a gather ratio rather than modelling each pleat; 5 mm particles.
- **Confidence**: from memory and the techniques sheet (A6); not verified against a published draft.

## Tiered Skirt {#tiered-skirt}

- **Aliases**: layered skirt, gathered tiers, prairie skirt; fr: jupe à volants étagés, jupe étagée
- **Family**: skirt, gathered
- **Recognize**: horizontal bands joined at gathered seams, each tier wider and longer than the one above; top tier fitted or gathered to a waistband.
- **Pieces**: n tiers (each a rectangle x1, or x2 if side-seamed), waistband or elastic casing x1, optional lining.
- **Shape**: Each tier is a rectangle (or a curved ring if cut from a circle section). Width of tier k+1 = ratio x width of tier k (ratio 1.3 to 2.0; chiffon 2 to 3; heavy cloth 1.2 to 1.3; techniques sheet A7). Heights typically 30:35:35 percent of L, top tier shortest. The gathered upper edge of each tier sews to the lower edge of the tier above after gathering; SA 1 cm on the gathered edge. Side seams close each tier into a ring. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. T1 = H + ease (no gathers) or ratio x waist. 2. T2 = ratio x T1, T3 = ratio x T2. 3. Heights = L x tier percentage minus SA. 4. Add SA each edge and hem allowance. 5. Join several fabric widths when needed.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Close each tier into a ring. 2. Two rows of gathering stitches (stitch length 4 to 5 mm, at 6 mm and 1 cm from the edge). 3. Match quarter points to the tier above, stitch, overlock, press up. 4. Waistband or casing. 5. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: overlocked gathered seams, elastic or zip, narrow hem; lining for sheer cloth.
- **Fabrics**: lawn, voile, poplin, viscose, chiffon (higher ratio). Heavy cloth bulks at the gather lines.
- **Fit faults**: bulk at the waist -> flat top tier or yoke; tier sags -> ratio too high for the weight; uneven gathers -> distribute from quarter points.
- **Variants**: #dirndl-skirt, #yoke-skirt, #petticoat.
- **CLO3D**: sewing with a gather ratio at each tier seam; 5 mm particles on the gathered side.
- **Confidence**: ratios and heights from the techniques sheet A7 (typical).

## Dirndl Skirt {#dirndl-skirt}

- **Aliases**: gathered skirt, rectangle gathered skirt, peasant skirt; fr: jupe froncée
- **Family**: skirt, gathered
- **Recognize**: gathered rectangle at the waist, falling straight in soft folds; waistband or elastic; no darts; side-seam pockets common.
- **Pieces**: skirt rectangle x2 (or one width x1 with CB seam), waistband x1 (or elastic casing), pocket bags x4 optional, zip optional.
- **Shape**: Rectangle with width = ratio x (W + ease) and length = L + hem + waist SA; sides and hem straight; the top edge is gathered to the waistband length and sews to the waistband lower edge. Side seams close the sides, pockets in the side seams. Unverified (trivial shape); ratios from the techniques sheet A7.
- **Drafting**: 1. F = W + ease. 2. Skirt width = ratio x F (heavy 1.2 to 1.3, medium 1.3 to 1.5, light 1.5 to 2.0, chiffon 2.0 to 3.0). 3. Length L + hem + band seam. 4. If F < H add an opening (zip or elastic) so the skirt passes over the hip.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Side seams (leave pocket openings). 2. Gathering rows. 3. Waistband. 4. Zip or elastic. 5. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip 18 to 22 cm or elastic 3 to 4 cm, narrow hem, overlocked seams.
- **Fabrics**: cotton, linen, lawn, flannel (lower ratio).
- **Fit faults**: bulk at the hip -> lower ratio or add a yoke; waist twists -> uneven gathers.
- **Variants**: #tiered-skirt, #yoke-skirt, #paperbag-trousers.
- **CLO3D**: gather ratio at the waist seam; 5 mm particles.
- **Confidence**: trade practice and techniques sheet A7; ratios typical.

## Wrap Skirt {#wrap-skirt}

- **Aliases**: wraparound skirt, tie skirt; fr: jupe portefeuille
- **Family**: skirt, wrap
- **Recognize**: diagonal front edge where one panel overlaps another, closed by a tie, button or snap at the waist; no zip; the opening runs from waist to hem.
- **Pieces**: front overlap x1, front underlap x1, back x1 on fold or x2, waistband with ties x1 (ties x2), facings along the opening x2.
- **Shape**: Based on an A-line or circle section. The overlap panel extends beyond CF by 15 to 25 cm at the waist (more at the hip); the opening line is a straight or smooth curved line from the waist to the hem. The underlap extends at least 8 to 10 cm behind the overlap at the hip (techniques sheet A8). Opening edges carry a facing 4 to 6 cm wide. The waist edges sew to the waistband, which extends into ties; the side seams sew front to back. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace the A-line skirt front as a full front (no fold). 2. Extend CF by the overlap and shape the opening. 3. Underlap extends beyond the opening on the other side by the minimum underlap. 4. Waistband length W + overlap + ties (60 to 90 cm each side).
- **Sewing order**: 1. Darts. 2. Side seams. 3. Facings on opening edges, understitch. 4. Hem. 5. Waistband and ties.
- **Finishes and notions**: edge facings, snap or button and loop to stop gaping, narrow hem on curved edges.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, linen, viscose, cotton; bias areas drop on circle versions.
- **Fit faults**: opening gapes -> overlap too small or underlap too short, add 5 cm; wrap sags -> inner snap; hem uneven -> relevel.
- **Variants**: #sarong, #asymmetric-skirt, #a-line-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: two stacked pieces at the overlap with collision layers (overlap above underlap).
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet A8; overlap values typical.

## Sarong {#sarong}

- **Aliases**: pareo, lava-lava, wrap cloth; fr: paréo, sarong
- **Family**: skirt, wrapped cloth
- **Recognize**: a rectangle of cloth wrapped around the waist or hips and tied or tucked; no shaped pieces.
- **Pieces**: rectangle x1 (typical 100 to 115 cm wide x 150 to 180 cm long; adjust to the wearer), optional ties.
- **Shape**: Rectangle only. Around the body = hip circumference + overlap 40 to 60 cm or more for wrapping twice; length = waist to ankle or knee + hem. Edges hemmed or selvedge. Trivial shape; sizes typical. Tailored sarong skirts (curved waist, ties, facings) follow #wrap-skirt.
- **Drafting**: 1. Length L + hem. 2. Width hip + overlap. 3. Square corners; selvedges as edges.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Hem the raw edges (1 cm double fold or rolled hem). 2. Optional ties on one corner.
- **Finishes and notions**: rolled hem, optional ties, hook or button.
- **Fabrics**: cotton, rayon, silk, batik; any light drapey fabric.
- **Fit faults**: slips -> tie or snap; too short -> increase length.
- **Variants**: #wrap-skirt, #handkerchief-hem-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: model as a rectangle sewn or pinned to itself at the overlap; light fabric preset.
- **Confidence**: general knowledge; sizes typical.

## Bubble Skirt {#bubble-skirt}

- **Aliases**: balloon skirt, pouf skirt, puffball skirt; fr: jupe boule, jupe ballon
- **Family**: skirt, volume at the hem
- **Recognize**: rounded silhouette, hem gathered in and turned under so the skirt puffs out above it; hem narrower than the widest part.
- **Pieces**: outer shell front x1 and back x1 (or x2), inner skirt (pencil or straight) front x1, back x2, hem band or facing x1 (or an elastic hem casing), waistband or facing.
- **Shape**: Inner skirt is a fitted straight or pencil skirt with hem circumference about H or less. Outer shell: same waist as the inner; at the hip level width = inner x 1.15 to 1.4; the outer is cut long by 2 x the puff drop p (typically 8 to 20 cm), so its hem folds under and is gathered onto the inner hem or hem band; outer hem width about 1.3 to 2 x the inner hem width before gathering (techniques sheet A9). Outline unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft the inner skirt from the pencil block. 2. Draft the outer shell from the same block, widen at the hip and hem by slash and spread (width ratio above). 3. Outer length = L + 2p + SA. 4. Hem band or elastic casing at the inner hem.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Assemble inner and outer separately. 2. Gather the outer hem to the inner hem / band, folding the extra length up inside. 3. Join inner and outer at the waist. 4. Zip and waistband.
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic or stiffened band at the hem, optional horsehair or tulle inside for a firmer pouf, lining.
- **Fabrics**: crisp cottons, taffeta, light wool; limp fabrics collapse.
- **Fit faults**: pouf collapses -> outer too short or fabric too limp, add length or an inner tulle layer; hem band wrinkles -> interface the band.
- **Variants**: #tulip-skirt, #pencil-skirt, #petticoat.
- **CLO3D**: two layered pieces (outer layer 2, inner layer 1) joined at the hem with a gather ratio; 5 mm particles.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet A9 (typical values); not verified against a published draft.

## Tulip Skirt {#tulip-skirt}

- **Aliases**: petal skirt, overlap-front skirt; fr: jupe tulipe
- **Family**: skirt, fitted with curved wrap
- **Recognize**: hem narrower than the hip, front formed of two curved petals that overlap at CF and curve under; back is a normal pencil skirt with a vent.
- **Pieces**: front right petal x1, front left petal x1 (mirrored), back x2, waistband, facing along the curved edges.
- **Shape**: From the pencil block narrow the hem 3 to 5 cm per side; draw a curved line from near CF at the waist to the hem; the petal overlaps CF by 6 to 10 cm at the hem; the two petals cross at CF (techniques sheet A10). The curved overlap edge carries a facing; petals sew at the side seam to the back. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace the pencil skirt front without the fold. 2. Draw a convex curve from the CF waist to a point beyond CF at the hem. 3. Cut the front into two petals along the curve; add the overlap on the top petal. 4. Trace facings.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Darts. 2. Facing on curved edges, understitch. 3. Stack the petals and baste at the waist. 4. Join side seams and back. 5. Waistband. 6. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: understitched facing, zip at CB, vent at the back, hook and bar.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, stretch wool, ponte, structured cotton.
- **Fit faults**: petals gape -> add 1 to 3 cm to the overlap; hem restricts walking -> raise the vent or loosen the taper.
- **Variants**: #pencil-skirt, #wrap-skirt, #bubble-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: two front pieces stacked with collision layers; check the petal curve length equals the facing.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet A10; dimensions typical.

## Trumpet Skirt {#trumpet-skirt}

- **Aliases**: flute skirt; fr: jupe trompette
- **Family**: skirt, fitted-and-flared
- **Recognize**: close fit through the hip and thigh to the knee area, then a flare that opens to the hem; often 6 to 8 panels or a godet or flounce.
- **Pieces**: 6 to 8 gores x1 each (cut n), or front x1 on fold and back x2 with a flare; waistband or facing.
- **Shape**: Fitted section from the waist to the knee (straight from the hip line to the knee, knee width = knee circumference + 6 to 10 cm, techniques sheet A11), then slash and spread from the flare start (knee level, typically 25 to 30 cm below the hip line, or mid-thigh) to the hem, with a total hem sweep 2 x H up to 3 x H. When panelled, each gore is drawn like #gored-skirt but with the hip-to-knee edge close to the body and a curve flaring from the knee to the hem. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Start from the straight/pencil skirt block. 2. Mark the flare start. 3. Slash from the hem to the flare start at the dart lines and between them; hinge at the flare start; spread to give the hem sweep. 4. Redraw the hem as a smooth curve. 5. For gores, use the gore formulas (#gored-skirt) with hip/knee/hem widths from the target circumferences.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Stay tape the seams at the knee. 2. Seams, zip. 3. Waistband. 4. Hang, level, hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip 18 to 22 cm, stay at the flare start, narrow hem, optional lining to the knee.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, satin, stretch wool; drapey fabrics flare softly, stiff ones keep a defined trumpet.
- **Fit faults**: restricts walking -> add 4 to 6 cm total at the knee or open a vent; flare pops out too high -> lower the flare start; seam puckers at the flare start -> clip and stay.
- **Variants**: #mermaid-skirt, #godet, #gored-skirt, #pencil-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: 5 mm particles, panels sewn with equal seam lengths; bias at the flare drops the hem.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet A11; typical values, not tied to a drawn pattern.

## Mermaid Skirt {#mermaid-skirt}

- **Aliases**: fishtail skirt, fish-tail skirt; fr: jupe sirène
- **Family**: skirt, fitted-and-flared
- **Recognize**: very close fit from waist to the knee or below, then a sudden flare, often a flounce, godets or a long train; usually floor-length.
- **Pieces**: 6 to 8 gores (or front and back with godets), optional flounce x1 (circle section), waistband or facing.

## Handkerchief Hem Skirt {#handkerchief-hem-skirt}

- **Aliases**: pointed hem skirt, hanky-hem skirt, kerchief skirt; fr: jupe à pointes, jupe mouchoir
- **Family**: skirt, asymmetric hem
- **Recognize**: several points hanging lower than the rest of the hem, formed by a square or a square-cut flared hem; points fall at the sides, front or back.
- **Pieces**: skirt as a square (or two overlapping squares) with a waist hole, or a circle skirt with a point-shaped hem; waistband or elastic.
- **Shape**: A square of cloth with a circular waist hole at its centre (radius r = C/(2 pi)); the four corners become the points. For a square of side s: mid-side distance from the centre = s/2, corner distance = s/sqrt2 = 0.707 s, so the points hang 0.207 s (= 0.414 x half-side) lower than the mid-sides. The waist edge cut at r - SA; the hem is the square edge. For a two-point version, cut a rectangle. Unverified as a drawn pattern; the ratio is derived from the square geometry.
- **Drafting**: 1. Choose point length P (from waist to corner) and shortest length S (mid-side), P - S = 0.414 x half-side if cut as a square. 2. Fold the square in four; cut the waist hole with r = C/(2 pi). 3. Hem allowance 1 cm. 4. Cut a radial slit to the waist (CB) for a zip.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Slit and zip. 2. Waistband. 3. Rolled hem all round (before or after hanging).
- **Finishes and notions**: rolled hem, zip or elastic.
- **Fabrics**: chiffon, georgette, crepe; the hem drops on the bias so allow hanging time.
- **Fit faults**: points sag -> lengthen the sides; hem wavy -> stay-stitch and use a narrow hem.
- **Variants**: #full-circle-skirt, #asymmetric-skirt, #sarong.
- **CLO3D**: single piece with a hole at the waist; 5 mm particles; points hang by gravity.
- **Confidence**: geometry (square with a circular hole) is derived; styling variants differ between makers.

## Asymmetric and High-Low Skirt {#asymmetric-skirt}

- **Aliases**: high-low skirt, hi-lo skirt, mullet skirt, asymmetric-hem skirt, diagonal hem skirt; fr: jupe asymétrique, jupe courte devant longue derrière
- **Family**: skirt, hem variation
- **Recognize**: hem length varying between front and back (high-low), or higher on one side (diagonal); the waist and fit are normal.
- **Pieces**: as the base skirt (A-line, circle, gathered or straight): front x1 / back x2, waistband or facing.
- **Shape**: Hem curve drawn between two lengths: the short length L1 (typically at the front or side) and the long length L2 (at CB, typically L1 + 15 to 40 cm). The hem is a smooth curve that is not a straight line from L1 to L2, and passes through the side seam at the same height on front and back so that the side seams are equal length. Circle versions: shorten or lengthen along radii (R varies by angle). The base skirt outline is unchanged. Source: construction logic, unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft the base skirt (e.g. #a-line-skirt or #half-circle-skirt). 2. Mark the length difference at CF and CB, and the length at the side seam. 3. Draw a smooth curve between the marks, perpendicular to the seams. 4. Check that the front and back hem curve meet at 90 degrees at the side seam. 5. Re-level after hanging.
- **Sewing order**: as the base; hem with a narrow rolled hem or a facing.
- **Finishes and notions**: narrow rolled or baby hem, optional lining with the same hem.
- **Fabrics**: as the base; drapey fabrics lengthen on the bias.
- **Fit faults**: hem points at the sides -> uneven curve; back hem drags when sitting -> shorten L2.
- **Variants**: #handkerchief-hem-skirt, #half-circle-skirt, #wrap-skirt, #a-line-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: compare the hem outline to the flat pattern; the long section tends to wrinkle at the avatar's calves.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; lengths typical.

## Denim Skirt {#denim-skirt}

- **Aliases**: jean skirt, jeans-style skirt; fr: jupe en jean
- **Family**: skirt, casual with jeans details
- **Recognize**: flat-felled or topstitched seams in denim, curved yoke at the back, front scoop or jeans pockets, back patch pockets, button or zip fly, belt loops, rivets.
- **Pieces**: front x2 (left and right), back x2, back yoke x2, front pocket bags x2, pocket facings x2, back patch pockets x2, waistband x1 (or x2), belt loops x5 to x7, fly facing and shield if a fly is used.

Hem wide enough to walk: front slit or a hem width >= 90 cm. Unverified as a drawn pattern; yoke and pocket dimensions from the jeans entry (techniques sheet B3).
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft a straight or A-line skirt (#straight-skirt, #a-line-skirt). 2. Close the back darts into the yoke seam. 3. Add the front scoop pockets and patch pockets. 4. Fly or button placket at CF. 5. Waistband with belt loops. All values typical.
- **Sewing order**: as #jeans (pockets, fly, yoke, side seams, waistband, hem).
- **Finishes and notions**: topstitching, rivets and bar tacks at pocket corners, jeans button, zip 14 to 18 cm.
- **Fabrics**: denim 8 to 14 oz; stretch denim allows less hip ease.
- **Fit faults**: back yoke gapes -> reshape the yoke curve; pockets pop -> loosen the hip; skirt rides up -> lengthen.
- **Variants**: #jeans, #cargo-skirt, #a-line-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: denim preset; topstitch tool for the J-stitch; pocket pieces on a lower layer.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; the jeans details come from the techniques sheet B3.

## Cargo Skirt {#cargo-skirt}

- **Aliases**: utility skirt, pocket skirt, military skirt; fr: jupe cargo
- **Family**: skirt, utility
- **Recognize**: bellows or patch pockets with flaps on the front thigh or hip, topstitching, often an A-line or straight skirt in twill; belt loops.
- **Pieces**: front x1 (or x2), back x2, patch pockets with bellows x2, pocket flaps x2 (x4 cut with the facing), pocket gussets x2 (side pieces), waistband x1, belt loops, closures (zip, button).
- **Shape**: Base A-line or straight skirt with patch pockets as in #cargo-trousers: pocket body a rectangle, pocket gusset (bellows) a strip along the sides and bottom, flap a shaped rectangle 1 to 1.5 cm wider than the pocket and 5 to 7 cm deep. Placement on the front panel at the side. Dimensions typical, shape unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft the base skirt. 2. Draft each pocket (typical 14 to 18 cm wide, 16 to 20 cm deep, gusset 3 to 4 cm). 3. Flap pattern 1 cm wider than the pocket. 4. Place the pocket 3 to 5 cm below the hip line on the side seam side of each front.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Pockets and flaps (before sewing the skirt seams). 2. Darts, seams. 3. Zip. 4. Waistband and belt loops. 5. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: topstitching, snaps or buttons, bar tacks at pocket corners.
- **Fabrics**: twill, canvas, ripstop; heavy fabrics need larger ease at the hip.
- **Fit faults**: pockets bulge -> reduce the gusset depth; flap does not close -> widen the flap.
- **Variants**: #cargo-trousers, #denim-skirt, #a-line-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: patch pocket as separate pieces on a higher layer, sewn along the edge.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; dimensions typical.

## Culotte Skirt {#culotte-skirt}

- **Aliases**: divided skirt, split skirt, skirt-trousers; fr: jupe-culotte
- **Family**: skirt/trouser hybrid
- **Recognize**: looks like an A-line or wide skirt when standing but is sewn with a crotch seam and two legs; wide legs with fullness at the hem.
- **Pieces**: front x2, back x2 (as trousers), waistband x1, zip or fly facing, pocket bags optional.
- **Shape**: A trouser block lengthened or shortened to knee or calf, with the legs made wide: the side seam and the inseam both flare outward from the hip, hem width per leg 30 to 50 cm flat (techniques sheet B2), crotch extension kept normal or increased 1 cm. The inseam continues straight from the crotch point to the hem. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft the trouser block with +1 cm rise. 2. Extend side and inseam outward from hip and crotch to the chosen hem width. 3. Check the hem width per leg is more than half the thigh circumference. 4. Draw the hem as a curve square to the seams.
- **Sewing order**: as trousers: darts, pockets, fly or side zip, crotch, side seams, inseams, waistband, hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip, hook and bar, waistband, blind hem.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, linen, wool, twill; drapey fabrics hang like a skirt.
- **Fit faults**: legs swing apart -> reduce the flare; crotch pulls -> add rise.
- **Variants**: #culottes, #gauchos, #palazzo-trousers, #skort.
- **CLO3D**: build as trousers (crotch seam sewn), not as a skirt; avatar legs apart slightly.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B2; typical values.

## Skort {#skort}

- **Aliases**: skirt-shorts, tennis skort, golf skort; fr: jupe-short
- **Family**: skirt/shorts hybrid
- **Recognize**: front panel that looks like a skirt (A-line or pleated) over built-in shorts; inner shorts visible only when moving; elastic or flat waistband.
- **Pieces**: outer skirt front x1 and back x1 (or x2), inner shorts front x2 and back x2 (short trouser pattern), waistband x1.
- **Shape**: Outer skirt (A-line or pleated, mini length) sewn to the waistband at the top. Inner shorts (mid-thigh or shorter inseam of 5 to 12 cm, typical) hang free inside, with the waist sewn to the same waistband; the front of the skirt may overlap the shorts as a wrap-front panel. - **Sewing order**: 1. Assemble the skirt. 2. Assemble the shorts (stretch or woven). 3. Baste at the waist, sew the waistband through both layers. 4. Hem each separately.
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic waist 2.5 to 3.8 cm, optional pocket in the shorts, coverstitch hem on knits.
- **Fabrics**: knit, performance fabrics for sport, woven for casual; shorts are often in a lighter lining knit.
- **Fit faults**: shorts ride up under the skirt -> lengthen or widen; bulk at the waist -> use a thinner lining for the shorts.
- **Variants**: #culotte-skirt, #hot-pants, #a-line-skirt.
- **CLO3D**: two layers of cloth at the waist; shorts on layer 1, skirt on layer 2.
- **Confidence**: from memory; no drawn source read.

- **Fabrics**: tulle, net, stiff cotton, organza, taffeta.
- **Fit faults**: shows through -> lengthen or add a smooth layer; scratchy on thighs -> add a lining layer.
- **Variants**: #tiered-skirt, #full-circle-skirt, #double-circle-skirt.

- **Confidence**: construction typical; not read from a drawn pattern.

# TROUSERS

Common to all trouser entries: pieces and anchors as in the conventions at the top of this file; side seam and inseam equal front to back (back inseam may be 0.5 to 1 cm shorter if the front is eased); crotch curve lengths checked front and back on the pattern; SA 1.5 cm on side and inseam, 1 to 1.5 cm on the crotch curve, 2 to 4 cm hem. Flat widths below are half the leg circumference at the level named.

## Classic Trouser {#classic-trouser}

- **Aliases**: women's trousers, tailored trousers, pants, slacks; fr: pantalon classique, pantalon de tailleur
- **Family**: trousers, from the trouser block (see also #trouser-block, #dress-trousers, #mens-trousers)
- **Recognize**: two-leg garment with a fly or side zip, waistband with belt loops, front darts or pleats, back darts, side or slanted pockets, creased legs, straight to slightly tapered leg from hip to hem.
- **Pieces**: front x2, back x2, waistband x1 (or contour x2), fly facing x1 and fly shield x1 (zip fly), pocket bags (facing and back) x2 pairs, interfacing for waistband and fly, belt loops x5 to x7.
- **Shape**: Front: A at CF waist, B at the side-seam waist, I side hip point, C crotch point (front crotch extension about H/16 + 0.5 to H/20 beyond the vertical CF line), K knee, Hm hem; crease line straight and perpendicular to waist and hem through the centre of the leg at the crotch line level. Front waist: W/4 + 2.25 (including one 2 cm dart on the crease line, 10 cm long); front hip width H/4 + 0.5; crotch depth (body rise) typically 27 to 28 cm for an adult woman of W 70, H 96 (illustrative size, replace with measured values); knee width = hem width + 1.3 each side; hem flat 21.75 per leg for a straight leg (0.125 W + 13). Back: CB line inclined (raise 2 cm and move in 2 cm at the waist), back hip width H/4 + 1.5, back crotch extension about H/8 (12 to 13 cm for H 96, about twice the front), back waist W/4 + 4.25 with two 2 cm darts (12 and 10 cm long), back leg 1 cm wider each side than the front, hem lowered 1 cm at the crease on the back. Seams: front inseam C-K-Hm to back inseam; side seam B-I-K-Hm to back side seam; front crotch curve to back crotch curve below the fly stop. Source: published draft read online for the techniques sheet (checked, Method A in B1); other textbooks differ by 0.5 to 1 cm.
- **Drafting**: 1. Rise line at body rise, hip line at waist-to-hip depth. 2. Front: crotch reference E = A - (H/12 + 1.5); front extension H/16 + 0.5; hip H/4 + 0.5; waist 1 cm in at CF then W/4 + 2.25. 3. Front crotch curve: deepest point 2.75 to 3.5 cm from the diagonal (by waist size). 4. Back: back hip H/4 + 1.5; back extension about H/8; CB raised 2 cm and moved in 2 cm; waist W/4 + 4.25; back crotch curve depth 4.25 to 4.75 cm. 5. Knee halfway between the crotch line and the hem, moved up 5 cm. 6. Check crotch length: front about 25 to 28 cm, back about 32 to 36 cm for H 96. 7. Notch hip, knee, crotch, fly stop. All values from the techniques sheet B1; typical.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Darts and pockets. 2. Fly. 3. Front crotch seams to the fly stop. 4. Back crotch seam. 5. Side seams. 6. Inseams. 7. Waistband. 8. Belt loops. 9. Hem. 10. Buttons, hook and bar.
- **Finishes and notions**: overlocked seams pressed open, crease pressed with a damp cloth, hem 3 to 4 cm blind-stitched or 2 cm machined, zip 14 to 18 cm, fly stop bar tack, hook and bar or button.
- **Fabrics**: wool gabardine, crepe, twill, cotton drill, linen. Stretch wovens allow 2 to 4 cm less seat ease. Stripes: match at the crease; checks at CF and inseams.
- **Fit faults**: drag lines to the crotch -> lower the crotch line 0.5 to 1.5 cm or lengthen the back crotch; whiskers at the front crotch -> add thigh/hip width or lengthen the front rise; baggy seat -> shorten the back extension 1 cm or raise the crotch 0.5 to 1 cm; fish-eye fold at front crotch -> shorten the front rise 0.5 to 1 cm; gap at CB waist -> increase the CB angle by 1 cm; crease swings -> regrain; back thigh wrinkles -> add 1 cm at the back thigh; baggy knees -> reduce knee width.
- **Variants**: #chinos, #straight-trousers, #pleated-trousers, #high-waisted-trousers, #jeans, #culottes.
- **CLO3D**: sew side seams and inseams, then crotch; avatar in A pose to relax; mesh 5 to 10 mm; crotch curve segment lengths must match (CLO stretches them otherwise).
- **Confidence**: verified draft (Method A, techniques sheet B1, checked online) for numbers; Aldrich-style ratios typical; not read as a drawn pattern by the author.

## Slim Trousers {#slim-trousers}

## Pleated Trousers {#pleated-trousers}

- **Aliases**: single-pleat trousers, double-pleat trousers, forward-pleat trousers, reverse-pleat trousers; fr: pantalon à pinces, pantalon à plis
- **Family**: trousers, pleated front
- **Recognize**: one or two folds at the front waist on each side, falling from the waist toward the crease, stitched down 8 to 12 cm.
- **Pieces**: as #classic-trouser, with front pieces wider.
- **Shape**: The front dart is replaced by pleats. Slash the front from the waist to the hem along the crease line (or from the centre of the dart) and spread by the pleat intake: 3 to 4 cm for a single pleat, 2 x 2.5 cm for a double pleat (techniques sheet B2). Add 0.5 to 1 cm to the crotch width on the front. Forward pleats (toward the side seam) are the modern default, reverse pleats (toward CF) traditional. Pleat depth runs waist to hip, stitched 8 to 12 cm, then free. Unverified as a drawn pattern.

- **Finishes and notions**: stay tape at the waist, pleat edge-stitching optional.
- **Fabrics**: wool, flannel, linen; thick fabric makes bulk at the waist.
- **Fit faults**: pleats gape -> intake too large for the hip; pleats pull -> depth too small, add 0.5 to 1 cm.
- **Variants**: #tapered-trousers, #classic-trouser.
- **CLO3D**: fold arrangement at the waist or gather ratio; 5 mm particles.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B2.

## Sailor Trousers (Button Front) {#sailor-trousers}

- **Aliases**: broadfall trousers, drop-front trousers, fall-front trousers, navy bell-bottoms, cracker jack trousers; fr: pantalon marin, pantalon à pont
- **Family**: trousers, historical / uniform
- **Recognize**: high waist; a rectangular front flap (the fall) as wide as the front, buttoned at the waist and each side; no centre fly; a hidden fly or no fly; wide or bell leg; lacing or a gusset at the back waist in naval versions; side or slant pockets.
- **Pieces**: front legs x2 (cut with the waist raised and side-fastening seam edges), front fall panel x1 (flap), fall lining or facing x1, back legs x2 with a raised, shaped waist, waistband x1 (or x2), pocket bags x2 pairs, back lacing gusset x1 and tie cord (naval), buttons.
- **Shape**: Documented examples (read as descriptions, no drawn outline): an 1831 Royal Navy pair has a flap front with four buttonholes and two buttons each side of the front waistband plus one at the back (probably for braces) (Royal Museums Greenwich); c. 1800 to 1830 white linen fall-front trousers have a flap as wide as the front fastening with four buttons, a separate fly below it and slanting pockets flanking the fly, the back waist higher and shaped with triangular panels (University of Westminster archive); the modern navy type is described as a 13-button broadfall flap, high-waisted, with lace-up back and welt pockets (surplus listings; button counts historically 12 to 16 per Folkwear). Construction logic: the front is cut as two legs whose waist edges end a few cm each side of CF; the fall is a separate panel (or a part of the front piece) whose top edge sews to the waistband, with its two side edges buttoning down to the trouser fronts at the opening; the fall's lower edge is free or lined and hides the fly. Outline, flap depth and button spacing: unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Start from the high-waisted trouser block (#high-waisted-trousers) with the waist raised 5 to 10 cm, wide leg. 2. Remove the centre-front strip: mark the fall width (CF to each side seam, between the pockets, typical 20 to 30 cm each) and cut the front into a fall panel and two side pieces. 3. Fall panel: waist edge a straight line, side edges follow the pocket-opening lines, lower edge at the crotch fork or lower. 4. Add button extensions (1.5 to 2.5 cm each side). 5. Back: raise the waist at CB, add a lacing slit (optional) with a gusset. 6. Flare the leg for bell-bottoms (see #flared-trousers). All values typical or unknown; make a muslin before cutting.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Pockets and darts. 2. Side pieces and fall facings. 3. Join legs to the fall panel edges. 4. Buttonholes and buttons. 5. Crotch, side seams, inseams. 6. Back waist, gusset and lacing. 7. Waistband. 8. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: 7 to 13 buttons (flat navy buttons for naval), buttonholes in reinforced fabric, waist stay, optional braces buttons, lacing eyelets.
- **Fabrics**: wool melton or serge (navy), cotton twill, linen (historical).
- **Fit faults**: flap gapes at the waist -> lengthen the flap or add buttons; waistband stands away at CB -> adjust the lacing; crotch drags -> raise the rise at the gusset.
- **Variants**: #high-waisted-trousers, #flared-trousers, #dungarees (fall-front bib).
- **CLO3D**: build the flap as a separate piece on a higher layer; use buttons from the trim library.
- **Confidence**: construction logic from museum and retailer descriptions (not a drawn pattern); dimensions unknown or typical.

## Jodhpurs and Riding Breeches {#jodhpurs}

- **Aliases**: breeches, jods, riding pants; fr: culotte de cheval, jodhpur
- **Family**: trousers, equestrian
- **Recognize**: full over the hip and thigh (historic) or fitted stretch; fitted from the knee to the ankle (jodhpurs end at the ankle, breeches below the knee); inner knee or full-seat patches; front pockets; back seat seam.

- **Finishes and notions**: suede or silicone patches, topstitching, zip fly, belt loops, ankle strap or stirrup for jodhpurs.
- **Fabrics**: stretch twill or cotton drill (modern), wool twill (historic), suede or leather patches.
- **Fit faults**: knee patches bunch -> match the knee curve; bag at the thigh -> reduce the flare; crotch pulls on the saddle -> lengthen the rise.
- **Variants**: #classic-trouser, #leggings, #stirrup-pants.
- **CLO3D**: patches as separate pieces on the leg; stretch fabric preset.
- **Confidence**: the patent and museum descriptions verified online; flare dimensions unknown; no drawn pattern read.

- **Finishes and notions**: flat-felled seams (SA 1.5 cm wide side, 1 to 1.5 narrow side, trim to 3 to 4 mm), twin topstitching 5 to 7 mm, rivets at pocket corners, bar tacks, jeans button, YKK zip 14 to 18 cm.
- **Fabrics**: denim 150 to 400 g/m2 (8 to 14 oz); stretch denim with 1 to 3 percent elastane; selvedge denim for outer seams.
- **Fit faults**: back yoke gapes at CB -> curve the yoke seam less, bring CB down 1 cm; pocket gape at the hip -> loosen the hip or widen the pocket bag; honeycomb creases are wash effects, not pattern faults.
- **Variants**: #classic-trouser, #denim-skirt, #chinos.
- **CLO3D**: denim preset, topstitch tool for the J and pockets, pocket bag on a lower layer.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B3 (typical); dimensions from trade practice.

## Leggings {#leggings}

- **Aliases**: tights (outer), yoga pants, stretch trousers; fr: legging
- **Family**: trousers, knit, close-fitting
- **Recognize**: skin-tight knit trousers, no pockets or only slit pockets, elastic or wide stretch waistband, ankle-length.
- **Pieces**: leg x2 (front and back combined or separate), crotch gusset x1 (optional), waistband x1 (elastic or fold-over).
- **Shape**: Trouser block with ease removed (hip width H/4 instead of H/4 + 1.5), negative ease 10 to 15 percent horizontal for 4-way stretch of 40 to 50 percent; 15 to 25 percent for 70 to 100 percent stretch; vertical reduction 0 to 3 percent; fabric stretch at least 1.5 to 2 x the reduction (techniques sheet B5). No-side-seam version: front and back pieces joined along a straight side seam (pivot/overlay at the hip and knee); the leg then has only an inseam; the gusset is a diamond 12 to 15 cm long and 4 to 6 cm wide. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trouser block without ease. 2. Reduce width by the chosen percentage. 3. Redraw crotch curves keeping their measured length. 4. Hem = ankle + 0 to 2 cm. 5. Waistband length = waist x 0.85 to 0.90; fold-over cut 2 x height (12 to 20 cm folded) + SA. 
- **Sewing order**: 1. Crotch seams. 2. Inseams (and side seams if any). 3. Gusset. 4. Waistband. 5. Hem with coverstitch or twin needle.
- **Finishes and notions**: ballpoint/stretch needle 75/11 to 80/12, stretch stitch or 3-4 thread overlock, elastic 2.5 to 5 cm, differential feed 0.8 to 1.0.
- **Fabrics**: 4-way stretch cotton-lycra, nylon-spandex, ponte; check sheerness when stretched.
- **Fit faults**: sagging at the knee -> vertical reduction too small; wrinkles at the crotch -> gusset too small; sheer at seat -> ease too tight or fabric too light.
- **Variants**: #stirrup-pants, #jogger-trousers, #jodhpurs.
- **CLO3D**: high-stretch knit preset; apply horizontal shrinkage 0.9 for negative ease; 5 mm mesh.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B5; reduction figures typical.

## Stirrup Pants {#stirrup-pants}

- **Aliases**: stirrup leggings, footed leggings (without the foot); fr: pantalon à sous-pied, fuseau à étrier
- **Family**: trousers, knit or stretch, close-fitting
- **Recognize**: leggings or slim trousers ending in a band that passes under the arch of the foot to hold the leg in place; tapering leg.
- **Pieces**: leg x2 (as #leggings), stirrup band x2 (rectangular or shaped, often cut x4 and sewn in pairs), waistband.
- **Shape**: Leggings leg with the hem extended into the stirrup, or a separate strap. Published tutorials: a rectangular stirrup 2 to 3 inches (5 to 7.5 cm) wide, cut in four pieces, sewn into two tubes along the long edges, turned, and attached to the inseam and side seam (Seamwork); or a separate drafted stirrup centred on the hem and attached right sides together with a narrow zigzag at 3/8 inch SA (WeAllSew); or a peg-leg variant with a heel band sewn into a slit at the ankle edge (Patterns for Pirates, 4-way stretch of at least 50 percent). Length of the stirrup: not found in the sources read; fit to the foot (arch circumference minus about 10 percent). Outline unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Leggings draft. 2. Stirrup width 5 to 7 cm, length = under-arch distance + 2 SA, measured on the foot. 3. Taper the hem to the ankle.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Make stirrups. 2. Attach to the hem at the inseam and side seam. 3. Complete seams. 4. Waistband.
- **Finishes and notions**: stretch stitch, elasticated stirrup, coverstitch.
- **Fabrics**: 4-way stretch knit with 50 percent or more; the stirrup is usually cut in the same fabric.
- **Fit faults**: stirrup too tight -> lengthen; leg rides up -> shorten the stirrup by 1 cm.
- **Variants**: #leggings, #jodhpurs.
- **CLO3D**: stirrup as a loop under the avatar's foot; avatar foot geometry must match.
- **Confidence**: tutorials checked online (width, attachment); stirrup length unknown.

# SHORTS

Common to the shorts entries: drafted from the trouser block cut off parallel to the hem line (techniques sheet B4); hem flat width must be at least (thigh circumference at the hem level + 4)/2 so the leg opening does not bind; for a tapered look take the hem in 1 to 2 cm, for a loose one add 2 to 4 cm. Inseam lengths (typical): hot pants 0 to 3 cm, short shorts 5 to 8, mid-thigh 10 to 18, Bermuda 25 to 35, knee-length 40 to 45, capri 50 to 60.

## Tailored Shorts {#tailored-shorts}

- **Aliases**: city shorts, dress shorts, trouser shorts; fr: short de ville, short habillé
- **Family**: shorts, tailored (see also #mens-shorts)
- **Recognize**: trouser shorts with a crease, front darts or pleats, slant pockets, fly, waistband and belt loops; hem 10 to 18 cm inseam, plain or cuffed.
- **Pieces**: front x2, back x2, waistband x1, fly facing x1, shield x1, pocket bags x2 pairs, belt loops x5, interfacing.
- **Shape**: #classic-trouser block cut at the chosen length, parallel to the hem line; crease line kept; hem flat width 24 to 32 cm per leg for mid-thigh (typical; follows the leg width at that level plus ease, check against the thigh rule above). Front and back inseam lines stay parallel to the crease below the crotch point (crotch extension not changed). Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trouser block. 2. Mark the finished inseam below the crotch line; square the hem to the crease. 3. Hem allowance 2 to 4 cm. 4. Check the leg opening vs. thigh circumference.
- **Sewing order**: as #classic-trouser.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip fly, button, hook and bar, topstitching 6 mm, hem 3 to 4 cm blind.
- **Fabrics**: cotton twill, linen, lightweight wool.
- **Fit faults**: ride up the crotch -> widen the hem by 1 to 3 cm; gap at the leg opening -> reduce hem 1 cm.
- **Variants**: #bermuda-shorts, #cargo-shorts, #chinos.
- **CLO3D**: as #classic-trouser.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B4; typical.

## Bermuda Shorts {#bermuda-shorts}

- **Aliases**: knee shorts, walk shorts; fr: bermuda
- **Family**: shorts, long
- **Recognize**: shorts ending at or just above the knee (inseam 25 to 35 cm), straight leg, tailored or casual, with waistband, fly and pockets.
- **Pieces**: as #tailored-shorts.
- **Shape**: Trouser block (#straight-trousers) cut off at the knee level or 2 to 5 cm above, hem flat 26 to 34 cm per leg (follows the knee circumference + ease), crease kept. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Straight trouser. 2. Cut at 25 to 35 cm inseam. 3. Hem width = knee flat width + 2 to 4 cm so the knee can bend. 4. Hem 3 to 4 cm.
- **Sewing order**: as #classic-trouser.
- **Finishes and notions**: topstitching, belt loops, hem 3 to 4 cm.
- **Fabrics**: cotton, linen, seersucker, light wool.
- **Fit faults**: hem rides up when sitting -> widen 2 cm; binds at the back of the knee -> lengthen 1 to 2 cm.
- **Variants**: #tailored-shorts, #capri-pants, #cargo-shorts.
- **CLO3D**: as #classic-trouser.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B4; typical.

## Cargo Shorts {#cargo-shorts}

- **Aliases**: utility shorts, combat shorts; fr: short cargo
- **Family**: shorts, utility
- **Recognize**: knee-length or mid-thigh shorts with thigh patch pockets with flaps and bellows, belt loops, wide leg.
- **Pieces**: as #cargo-trousers with the legs shortened; pocket and flap pieces unchanged.
- **Shape**: Block as #bermuda-shorts, loose; cargo pockets as in #cargo-trousers (body 14 to 18 wide, 16 to 20 deep, bellows 3 to 4 cm, flap 5 to 7 deep) positioned on the outer thigh with the top edge 8 to 12 cm below the hip line; the pocket bottom stays 5 to 8 cm above the hem. Dimensions typical, unverified.
- **Drafting**: as #bermuda-shorts with hip ease +6 to 10 cm; place pockets with the hem clearance.
- **Sewing order**: pockets first, then as #classic-trouser.
- **Finishes and notions**: bar tacks, snaps or hook and loop at the flaps, topstitching.
- **Fabrics**: cotton twill, canvas, ripstop.
- **Fit faults**: pocket hits the hem -> move up; pocket pulls the leg -> reduce the gusset.
- **Variants**: #cargo-trousers, #cargo-skirt, #bermuda-shorts.
- **CLO3D**: pocket pieces on a higher layer, folded bellows via internal lines.
- **Confidence**: trade practice.

## Running Shorts {#running-shorts}

- **Aliases**: athletic shorts, split shorts, track shorts, gym shorts; fr: short de running, short de sport
- **Family**: shorts, sport, woven or light knit
- **Recognize**: very light shorts with an elastic or drawcord waist, short inseam (7 to 15 cm), side vents (split hem), internal brief or mesh liner, small key pocket.
- **Pieces**: front x2, back x2, waistband or casing x1, liner brief x1 (front and back, with gusset), elastic, drawcord.
- **Shape**: Elastic-waist block (techniques sheet B7): top width = hip + 2 to 6 cm, straight from waist to hip, no darts; legs wide enough to run (hem flat 26 to 34 cm per leg, typical); the side seam has a vent at the hem 4 to 8 cm (typical) with the front hem curved up to the vent. The liner brief is a close-fitting pattern like #leggings, sewn into the waist. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trouser block cut to 7 to 15 cm inseam. 2. Remove the darts and widen the top to hip + 2 to 6. 3. Add the side vent by leaving the side seam open 4 to 8 cm and rounding the hem corner. 4. Waist casing 2 x (elastic width + 0.3) + SA. 5. Liner: leggings draft, cut very short.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Liner brief seams. 2. Short seams (sides to the vent, inseams, crotch). 3. Waist casing, elastic, drawcord, liner attached at the waist. 4. Hem (rolled or coverstitched).
- **Finishes and notions**: elastic 3 to 4 cm, flat drawcord, rolled hem, bar tack at the vent top, optional reflective trim.
- **Fabrics**: nylon, polyester microfibre, light woven stretch; wicking knits for the liner.
- **Fit faults**: shorts ride up -> hem width too small or inseam too short; waist rolls -> elastic too narrow; liner rubs -> change the seam position.
- **Variants**: #tailored-shorts, #boxer-shorts (underwear shorts, defined in the lingerie file), #hot-pants.
- **CLO3D**: lightweight woven preset; liner on layer 1, shell on layer 2.
- **Confidence**: from memory and the techniques sheet B7; vent dimensions typical.

## Hot Pants {#hot-pants}

- **Aliases**: micro shorts, short shorts, booty shorts, daisy dukes; fr: mini short, hot pants
- **Family**: shorts, very short
- **Recognize**: shorts with an inseam of 0 to 3 cm (hem at the crotch level) to 8 cm, close fitting at the thigh, often high-waisted, with a fly or a side zip.
- **Pieces**: front x2, back x2, waistband x1 (or facing), zip, pocket bags optional.
- **Shape**: Trouser block cut at the crotch level or up to 8 cm below; thigh width maintained; the leg opening cut straight or slightly curved up toward the side seam; crotch curve kept; the back leg may be shortened at the hem curve to avoid showing the seat (hem curved up 1 to 2 cm at the side seam). Unverified as a drawn pattern; sheet value hem at crotch level (techniques sheet B4).
- **Drafting**: 1. Trouser block cut to the chosen inseam. 2. Keep the thigh width, do not taper. 3. Curve the hem up at the side seam. 4. Hem allowance 1.5 to 2.5 cm.
- **Sewing order**: as #classic-trouser.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip or fly, belt loops optional, narrow hem.
- **Fabrics**: stretch denim, twill, corduroy, stretch cotton.
- **Fit faults**: shorts ride up -> widen the leg opening or lengthen; seat shows -> lengthen the back.
- **Variants**: #running-shorts, #tailored-shorts, #skort.
- **CLO3D**: stretch fabrics preset; fine mesh near the leg opening.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B4; typical.

# JUMPSUITS AND ALL-IN-ONES

## Jumpsuit {#jumpsuit}

- **Aliases**: boiler suit (workwear, see also #coverall-boiler-suit), catsuit (fitted), all-in-one, utility jumpsuit; fr: combinaison-pantalon, combinaison
- **Family**: jumpsuit (bodice + trousers in one)
- **Recognize**: one-piece garment from shoulder or neckline to the leg hem; waist seam with or without a belt or elastic; often a CF or CB zip or button placket.
- **Pieces**: bodice front x1/x2, bodice back x1/x2, trouser front x2, trouser back x2, sleeves x2 (if any), waistband, casing or belt, facings or lining, pockets.
- **Shape**: Bodice block and trouser block placed with the waist lines level, joined at the waist seam; the torso length is increased: the body measurement from the nape over the shoulder through the crotch back to the nape plus total ease 4 to 6 cm (2.5 to 3 cm each at front and back at the waist seam), +2 cm for a wearer who sits a lot (techniques sheet B6). The waist can be bloused by 4 to 8 cm. The trouser top is raised 1.5 to 3 cm at CF and CB when the extra length is added there. Edge matches: bodice waist edge sews to the trouser waist edge (equal length); inseams, side seams and crotch as trousers; bodice side seams continue to the trouser side seams. Opening: full CF or CB zip 50 to 65 cm, side plus back zip, front placket or wrap; opening must reach below the hip when the waist is smaller than hip + 4. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take the girth measurement. 2. Place the blocks level at the waist. 3. Raise the trouser waist by the torso addition at CF and CB. 4. Add 4 to 8 cm blouson at the waist if wanted, plus a casing, belt or elastic. 5. Check the crotch length. 6. Choose the opening.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Make the bodice (darts, shoulders). 2. Make the trousers. 3. Join at the waist seam. 4. Zip or placket. 5. Sleeves, neck finish. 6. Waist casing or belt. 7. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip 50 to 65 cm, waist stay tape, interfacing for plackets, elastic casing for a blouson waist.
- **Fabrics**: crepe, twill, drill, linen, stretch jersey; stretch fabrics reduce torso ease.
- **Fit faults**: pulling at the shoulders when sitting -> torso too short, add 2 cm at the waist; crotch cuts -> lengthen the crotch curve; waist cut at the sides -> loosen the waist seam.
- **Variants**: #playsuit, #dungarees, #coverall-boiler-suit (other file), #classic-trouser.
- **CLO3D**: sew the bodice to the trousers at the waist seam; test sitting and arm raise poses to check torso length.
- **Confidence**: techniques sheet B6 (typical); not read from a drawn pattern.

## Playsuit {#playsuit}

- **Aliases**: romper, short jumpsuit, short all-in-one; fr: combishort, combinaison-short
- **Family**: jumpsuit, short legs
- **Recognize**: short-legged jumpsuit; bodice with straps, sleeves or halter; shorts or skort legs; elastic or tie waist; often an open or button back.
- **Pieces**: bodice front/back, shorts front/back (x2 each), waist casing or band, straps x2, facings, zip or placket, pockets optional.
- **Shape**: As #jumpsuit with legs cut as #tailored-shorts or elastic-waist shorts at the chosen inseam (hot pants to Bermuda); the torso length uses the same girth measurement plus 4 to 6 cm ease; the shorts crotch is lowered by 1 cm and widened if the waist is loose. Bodice waist edge sews to the shorts waist edge. For a loose playsuit the leg opening is wide (A-line shorts) to prevent binding. Unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft a short bodice (crop or fitted). 2. Short trouser block at the chosen length. 3. Add torso addition. 4. Include a waist casing or ties. 5. Choose the opening (CB zip 35 to 45 cm typical, side zip, front buttons).
- **Sewing order**: as #jumpsuit; lining the bodice is common.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip, elastic, bias binding at necklines and armholes, narrow hems.
- **Fabrics**: cotton lawn, viscose, linen, light crepe, jersey.
- **Fit faults**: crotch pulls when bending -> add torso 2 cm; legs ride up -> wider leg opening; waist cuts -> loosen elastic.
- **Variants**: #jumpsuit, #dungarees, #tailored-shorts.
- **CLO3D**: as #jumpsuit; simulate sitting pose.
- **Confidence**: from memory and techniques sheet B6 logic; not tied to a drawn source.

## Dungarees {#dungarees}

- **Aliases**: overalls, bib-and-brace overalls, pinafore trousers, dungaree dress (skirt version); fr: salopette
- **Family**: trousers with bib, workwear / casual (see also #coverall-boiler-suit)
- **Recognize**: trousers with a front bib covering the chest, shoulder straps crossing the back or fastened at the bib, side buttons or buckles, patch or hammer pockets on the bib and legs; trouser legs and waist seam.
- **Pieces**: front trouser x2, back trouser x2, bib front x1 (x2 with lining or facing), bib back x1 or yoke (or back bib) x1, straps x2 (each folded and stitched, cut 2 or 4), bib facing, bib pocket x1, hip or hammer pockets, side opening or button tab, waistband or waist seam. Published patterns list: front and back panels, front and back bib each with its own facing, straps cut as separate pieces folded lengthwise, optional hammer pocket, side button tabs (SQA children's dungaree instructions, Lyon pattern).
- **Shape**: Bib front: rectangle or slightly shaped piece (top width 20 to 30 cm, height 20 to 30 cm, typical for an adult, unverified) whose lower edge joins the trouser front at the waist; the top edge carries the straps; straps: strips about 4 to 6 cm wide finished (typical), long enough to cross the shoulder; fastened with buttons or buckles at the bib (or back). The trouser front and back as #classic-trouser or #straight-trousers with an easy fit (hip ease 6 to 10 cm) and the waist often cut with a back elastic or a back yoke; side openings at the waist (zip or buttons) because the bib prevents a front fly (unless a fall front). The bib facing is turned 1.5 cm on the lower edge (SQA). Outline unverified as a drawn pattern.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trouser block with ease. 2. Draw the bib on the front waist: width 20 to 30 cm centred at CF, height set from the waist to the chosen bib top. 3. Back: yoke or bib with straps. 4. Add side opening at the left side (waist to hip) with a placket. 5. Straps: length measured from the bib to the back over the shoulder plus 4 to 6 cm adjustable.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Pockets (bib, hip). 2. Make straps. 3. Bib assembled with facing and straps. 4. Trousers assembled (crotch, side seams, inseams). 5. Join the bib to the trousers at the waist seam. 6. Side opening, buttons or buckles. 7. Hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: strap buckles or buttons (covered buttons on straps in children's versions), topstitching 6 mm, bar tacks at stress points, rivets for workwear.
- **Fabrics**: denim, corduroy, twill, canvas, cotton drill.
- **Fit faults**: straps pull the bib -> lengthen the straps or the bib; waist gaps -> back elastic or darts; crotch drags -> lengthen the rise.
- **Variants**: #jumpsuit, #sailor-trousers, #coverall-boiler-suit (other file), #jeans.
- **CLO3D**: bib and trousers as separate pieces sewn at the waist; straps as long strips over the avatar's shoulder; denim preset.
- **Confidence**: component list from published patterns (SQA, ikatee/clothkits listings, checked online); dimensions typical or unknown; no drawn outline read.
