How to read the numbers in this file: "typical" = textbook or trade starting value, not a standard; confirm on a toile. "Source" in a Shape field says where the outline comes from. No drawn pattern from a published book was available online for any entry in this file, so most Shape fields say "unverified" for the proportions; the piece list, the seam join order and the numbers marked "verified" were checked against the cited sources (see `## Sources`). Convention: men's shirts lap right front over left as worn (buttons on the wearer's right); men's trouser flies conventionally lap left over right as worn (opposite to the shirt). ## Men's Dress Shirt {#mens-dress-shirt}

- **Aliases**: formal shirt, business shirt, Oxford dress shirt (fr: chemise homme classique, chemise de ville); (de: Herrenhemd)
- **Family**: men's shirts, woven tops
- **Recognize**: separate collar stand and collar fall, back yoke seam across the shoulders, button-through front with a placket or grown-on fold, long sleeve with a tower placket and a barrel (or French) cuff, curved shirt-tail hem that is longer at CF and CB than at the sides, often a box pleat or two side pleats at the back yoke.
- **Pieces**: front right (1), front left (1), back (1, on fold), yoke outer (1), yoke inner (1), sleeve (2), sleeve placket over and under (2 each side), cuff outer and inner (2 each, 4 total), collar stand outer and inner (2), collar fall upper and under (2), front placket strips (2, only if the placket is separate), chest pocket (0 or 1), collar stays (2, sewn pockets or loose), interfacing for stand, fall, cuffs, plackets.
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern; construction below is trade practice. Front: a vertical piece with a straight CF edge extended by the button stand (finished stand width typical 2.5 to 3 cm; grown-on fold = 2x stand plus the CF extension), a rounded neckline (front neck seamline length is half of the stand-neck edge), a straight shoulder seam that is attached to the yoke, an armhole deeper than a women's block (technique sheet: +2 to 3 cm for men), a straight side seam and a hem curved up toward the side (the shirt-tail). Back: a rectangle-ish piece from the yoke seam to the hem, whose top edge is straight (yoke seam, length equals the yoke lower edge plus the pleat intake); a box pleat of 2 to 2.5 cm total at CB or two 1.5 to 2 cm side pleats placed near the armhole ends of the yoke. Yoke: a band whose lower edge is straight or slightly curved, as long as the back width, whose upper edge is the back neck curve and shoulder slopes (see #mens-shirt-yoke). Sleeve: low cap (technique sheet 1.3), straight underarm seams, a vertical slit on the back-arm side near the wrist for the placket. Which edge sews to which: front shoulder edge -> yoke shoulder edge (sandwiched between yoke outer and yoke inner); back top edge -> yoke lower edge (back sandwiched); stand lower edge -> neckline; fall neck edge -> stand upper edge; sleeve cap -> armhole (sewn flat before the side seam closes); cuff -> sleeve hem. Source for the order: trade practice; the neck/collar/cuff numbers come from `techniques/sleeves-collars.md` (textbook ranges).
- **Drafting**: 1. Shirt block from the chest, neck N (measured on the seamline), shoulder, sleeve length; chest ease for a regular fit typical 16 to 20 cm, slim 8 to 12 cm (typical, varies by brand). 2. Armhole depth +2 to 3 cm over a jacket block; shoulder extended 1 to 2 cm; sleeve cap 10 to 13 cm, cap ease 1.5 to 2.5 cm (sheet 1.3). 3. Draw the yoke line (see #mens-shirt-yoke) and close the back shoulder dart into it. 4. Add back pleat intake. 5. Collar stand and fall per #mens-shirt-collar-stand. 6. Sleeve: length from the shoulder point to the wrist, minus cuff height, plus 3 to 4 cm for droop over the cuff (typical); the wrist width is wrist plus 6 to 8 cm, absorbed by 1 or 2 small pleats at the cuff (#mens-shirt-cuff). 7. Hem: shirt-tail curve, side seam shorter than CF by about 8 to 12 cm (typical), hem allowance 0.6 to 1 cm narrow rolled. 8. Pocket: patch, 10 to 12 cm wide x 12 to 14 cm tall (typical), 1.5 to 2 cm hem.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Pocket, interface collar parts, stand, cuffs, plackets. 2. Front plackets (fold or sew strip). 3. Yoke to back (sandwich). 4. Fronts to yoke (the "burrito" or sandwich method; outer yoke to fronts, inner yoke closed by hand or edge-stitch). 5. Collar assembled, collar to stand, stand to neck. 6. Sleeves set flat into the open armhole and flat-felled or double-needle stitched. 7. Sleeve plackets before the underarm seam. 8. Close side seam and sleeve underarm in one run. 9. Cuffs. 10. Hem. 11. Buttonholes and buttons.
- **Finishes and notions**: flat-felled or French side seams, 0.5 cm topstitch on collar and cuffs, interfacing (fusible, light woven) on stand/fall/cuffs/plackets, 7 to 11 mm shirt buttons (typical 10 to 11 mm for the front), collar-stay pockets 6 to 7 cm, tail gussets at the side hem optional.
- **Fabrics**: cotton poplin, twill, oxford, end-on-end, Sea Island; woven stretch (2 to 3 percent elastane) needs lower cap ease. Stripes and checks: match at yoke, pocket, placket, sleeve; the yoke is often cut on the cross grain for stripes.
- **Fit faults**: collar gaps at CF -> increase collar spring or shorten the stand; collar choking -> add 0.5 to 1 cm to N; drag lines from the shoulder blade -> more back-pleat intake or a wider yoke dart; sleeve bunching under the arm -> lower cap/ deeper armhole is needed; sleeve too short with raised arm -> add length at the cuff and check the cap height.
- **Variants**: casual shirt #casual-shirt-mens, button-down #button-down-shirt, work shirt #work-shirt, Western #western-shirt, tuxedo shirt (pleated bib, wing collar, studs) not detailed here.
- **CLO3D**: build stand and fall as separate layered pieces, sew the collar to the stand by segment, set the fall interface as a heavier fabric, sleeve sewn with a small ease ratio, mesh 8 to 10 mm, drag the cuff pleat via a fold arrangement. Place the sleeve pitch by hand with the avatar arms in the A pose.
- **Confidence**: construction order and piece list: trade practice. Collar/cuff/placket numbers: from the technique sheet (textbook ranges, not a drawn pattern). Yoke and tail numbers: from memory/typical, unverified.

## Men's Shirt Yoke {#mens-shirt-yoke}

- **Aliases**: shoulder yoke, back yoke, split yoke, double yoke (fr: empiècement, piqûre d'épaule)
- **Family**: shirt construction element
- **Recognize**: horizontal (or slightly curved) seam across the upper back, with the shoulder seams running along the yoke edges; on the front of Western shirts the yoke may be pointed or have a second front yoke.
- **Pieces**: yoke outer (1, often on fold at CB or 2 with a CB seam), yoke inner (1), optionally front yoke (2, Western) or a split yoke (2 pieces with a CB seam, cut on opposite bias for checks).
- **Shape**: Unverified; typical geometry. The yoke is a band as wide as the back block at the yoke line. Its top edge is the back neckline from the CB to the shoulder neck point (SNP), then the back shoulder line to the shoulder point (the shoulder seam runs along this edge, so the yoke shoulder equals the front shoulder length). Its lower edge is the yoke line, drawn from CB (typical 7 to 9 cm below the nape) to the armhole (typical 4 to 6 cm below the shoulder point). The back shoulder dart of the block (typical 1 to 1.5 cm intake) is closed by pivoting, which opens the lower yoke edge by the same amount; this opening is absorbed by the back body as a box pleat or side pleats. Sew rule: back top edge (including the pleat intake) -> yoke lower edge; front shoulder edge -> yoke shoulder edge.
- **Drafting**: 1. Mark the yoke line on the back block. 2. Close the shoulder dart in the yoke (pivot at the shoulder point side). 3. Re-true the yoke lower edge as a smooth line. 4. Add the intake to the back body by slash-and-spread (box pleat at CB, 2 to 2.5 cm total, or two side pleats of 1.5 to 2 cm). 5. For a split yoke, cut with a CB seam and the grain rotated 45 degrees only if a stripe or check chevron is wanted. 6. Check the lower yoke edge equals the back top edge minus the pleat intake.
- **Sewing order**: back pleat folded and basted, back placed between yoke outer and inner (right sides together), stitched, yoke pressed up, then fronts sandwiched between the yokes at the shoulders.
- **Finishes and notions**: topstitch 0.5 cm, optional hanging loop at CB, light interfacing only on a heavy fabric.
- **Fabrics**: stripes and checks are matched across the yoke seam; a bias-cut yoke is a style cue on striped shirts.
- **Fit faults**: back pulls across the shoulder blades -> widen the yoke dart intake or lower the yoke; yoke line shows horizontal drag -> yoke too deep or shoulders too sloped.
- **Variants**: Western pointed yoke #western-shirt, workwear yoke #work-shirt.
- **CLO3D**: yoke as two layered pieces with the back sewn between; one seam line only is needed in CLO if the inner yoke is drawn as a lining layer.
- **Confidence**: trade practice for the sandwich method; yoke depths are typical values from memory, unverified.

## Men's Shirt Collar and Stand {#mens-shirt-collar-stand}

- **Aliases**: two-piece shirt collar, collar with band, collar and neckband (fr: col chemise avec pied de col, col tailleur de chemise)
- **Family**: collars
- **Recognize**: a band that stands at the neck (stand) plus a collar that folds over it (fall); the stand has a rounded front end with a button; the fall has visible points and a seam between stand and fall at the neck.
- **Pieces**: stand outer (1), stand inner (1), fall upper (1), fall under (1, 2 to 3 mm smaller), interfacing for stand and fall (2 to 4), collar stays (2).
- **Shape**: drawn pattern: not available; the shape below follows the numeric construction in `techniques/sleeves-collars.md` 2.3, which is textbook-based. Stand: a long curved strip of length N plus 1.5 to 2 cm (button overlap), height typical 3 to 3.5 cm at CB tapering to 2.5 cm at the front ends; the lower edge curves up about 1.5 cm at the front; the front end is rounded (radius 1.5 to 2.5 cm); the upper edge is slightly shorter than the lower. Fall: a strip whose neck edge equals the stand upper edge (CB to about 1 to 1.5 cm short of the stand end); CB height = stand + 1 to 1.5 cm (typical 4 to 4.5 cm); the neck edge is curved upward at the front by 1.5 to 2.5 cm (spring); the points are 7 to 8 cm long, point angle 75 to 95 degrees; the outer edge is about 3.5 to 5 cm longer per half than the neck edge. Under collar: 2 to 3 mm smaller on the outer edge and points. Sew rules: upper fall -> under fall at the outer edge and points (right sides together), turned; collar neck edge -> stand upper edge between stand outer and stand inner; stand outer lower edge (or inner, per method) -> shirt neckline.
- **Drafting**: as the technique sheet 2.3: N, stand height, spring, point length, spread (8 to 12 cm between points at the neck); neck size is the measured neck circumference plus 1.5 to 2 cm ease for dress shirts (typical).
- **Sewing order**: interface fall and one stand layer; sew upper to under fall, trim, turn, press seam to the under side; insert stays; sandwich between stand layers; stitch the top; stand to neck; close the stand ends; buttonhole on the stand end.
- **Finishes and notions**: 0.5 cm topstitch; 6 to 7 cm collar-stay pockets; button 10 to 11 mm; buttonhole horizontal on the stand.
- **Fabrics**: poplin and twill need a fusible; for soft collars use less interfacing; for starched collars add a sew-in layer.
- **Fit faults**: stand shows above fall -> raise fall CB height 0.5 to 1 cm; points curl -> bigger turn-of-cloth on the under collar, add stays; gap at CF -> more spring; strangles -> N + 0.5 to 1 cm.
- **Variants**: button-down #button-down-shirt (same stand, buttons on the points); spread, cutaway, club, pin collars differ in the point angle and spread only; camp collar #camp-collar-shirt (no stand); band collar (stand only).
- **CLO3D**: stand and fall as separate pieces sewn by segment; fall curvature is set by the arrangement; keep the stand's closing overlap by placing the right stand end over the left.
- **Confidence**: numbers: from the technique sheet (textbook ranges, unverified here). Piece list and sewing order: trade practice. Retail specs found online give a collar point length of about 80 mm for a straight point and 88 mm for an Italian spread, and a casual band collar height of about 30 mm (supplier specs, cited in Sources).

## Men's Shirt Sleeve Placket {#mens-shirt-sleeve-placket}

- **Aliases**: tower placket, shirt sleeve opening, continuous lap placket (fr: poignet pattelette, fente de manche)
- **Family**: sleeves, closures
- **Recognize**: a vertical slit on the back-arm side of the sleeve above the cuff, with a narrow strip (tower: pointed at the top end) on one side and a plain strip on the other.
- **Pieces**: placket over (1, pointed or squared end), placket under (1), or a single continuous strip (1) for the continuous lap; interfacing optional.
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. Slit on the sleeve: 9 to 12 cm for men (technique sheet 1.20), cut in the lower sleeve about 4 cm in from the back underarm seam, parallel to the grain. Under placket: a straight strip (cut width typical 2.5 to 3.5 cm, length slit + 3 to 4 cm) sewn to the back edge of the slit. Over (tower) placket: a longer strip (cut width 3.5 to 4 cm, pointed end) sewn to the front edge of the slit; the pointed end sits at the top of the slit and is stitched with a box or X anchor. Sew rules: under strip edge -> slit back edge; over strip edge -> slit front edge; both strips fold over the raw edges to the right side; the over placket laps the under at the wrist end where the cuff covers it. Continuous lap: one strip wrapped around both slit edges, the end folded in a triangle bar tack.
- **Drafting**: 1. Mark the slit on the sleeve block and cut the slit into the hem edge. 2. Draft the strips from the technique sheet 1.20. 3. Add pleats at the wrist: 1 or 2 pleats of 1 to 1.5 cm each, positioned near the placket. 4. Transfer the cuff buttonhole position (center of the cuff end).
- **Sewing order**: interface strips; stitch strips to the slit; press and topstitch; pointed end anchored; sleeve underarm seam closed; cuff set.
- **Finishes and notions**: 0.5 cm topstitch; sometimes a small button on the placket; pleat is edge-stitched.
- **Fabrics**: any shirting; checks should be matched across the sleeve.
- **Fit faults**: placket gapes -> overlap too small, add 0.5 cm; placket puckers -> grain not parallel to the slit; point sags -> not interfaced.
- **Variants**: continuous lap (women's blouse), faced slit (jacket), no placket with a zip (sport).
- **CLO3D**: model the strips as separate pieces layered over the sleeve, with sewn lines; the slit must be drawn as an internal cut line.
- **Confidence**: dimensions: technique sheet (typical). Layout: trade practice, unverified drawn pattern.

## Men's Shirt Cuff {#mens-shirt-cuff}

- **Aliases**: barrel cuff, single cuff, French cuff, double cuff, convertible cuff, mitred cuff (fr: poignet mousquetaire, poignet simple)
- **Family**: cuffs
- **Recognize**: a band at the sleeve end; barrel cuff fastened with one or two buttons; French cuff turned back and fastened with cufflinks; corners can be square, rounded, or mitred (cut diagonally).
- **Pieces**: cuff outer (1 per arm) and cuff inner (1 per arm) or one folded piece; interfacing; for French cuff, one piece with a fold line.
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. Barrel cuff: a rectangle of length = wrist + 3 to 4 cm ease + overlap 2.5 to 3 cm and a folded height (typical 6 to 7 cm for men, technique sheet 1.19). The front corner can be rounded (radius 1.5 to 2 cm) or mitred. French cuff: cut height = 4x folded height + seam allowances (technique sheet), folded in half and again, no button, four holes in the overlap for the link. Sew rule: the cuff outer edge (long edge) -> sleeve hem; inner edge turned and stitched on the sleeve seamline; pleats at the sleeve wrist are absorbed to equal the cuff length.
- **Drafting**: 1. Wrist measurement plus ease (3 to 4 cm) plus overlap. 2. Height 6 to 7 cm finished. 3. Button 1.5 cm from the end, second button 1.5 to 3 cm further for adjustable cuffs. 4. Interface the outer layer.
- **Sewing order**: interface; fold; sew outer to sleeve hem; wrap inner; topstitch 0.5 cm; buttonholes and buttons.
- **Finishes and notions**: fusible (light) or sew-in; cuffs often have a 10 to 12 mm button; French cuff uses cufflinks.
- **Fabrics**: heavy poplins for stiff cuffs; stripe direction can be cut across for a visual effect.
- **Fit faults**: cuff too tight -> add 1 to 2 cm overlap; too loose -> second button; cuff crawls up -> sleeve too short.
- **Variants**: see names in Aliases; sport polo knit cuff #polo-shirt-knit.
- **CLO3D**: cuff as a rectangle sewn to the sleeve end; the pleat gather can be simulated with a sewing ratio on the sleeve edge.
- **Confidence**: numbers: technique sheet. Construction: trade practice.

## Casual Shirt (Men's) {#casual-shirt-mens}

- **Aliases**: sport shirt, everyday shirt, flannel shirt, overshirt (fr: chemise décontractée, chemise de week-end)
- **Family**: men's shirts
- **Recognize**: like the dress shirt but with a softer collar (single layer of interfacing or none), a roomier body, a patch pocket (one or two), a straight hem or a short curved tail, often a chest pocket with flap.
- **Pieces**: front right and left (2), back (1 on fold), yoke (2), sleeve (2), cuff (4), collar stand (2), collar fall (2), placket (grown-on or separate), pocket (1 or 2), optional back loop.
- **Shape**: Unverified. Same layout as #mens-dress-shirt with: a shorter hem (a straight or only slightly curved edge, side seam shorter than CF by 5 to 8 cm, typical), a wider body, a dropped-shoulder option, a back box pleat or none. Seam join: yoke sandwiches back and fronts exactly as the dress shirt.
- **Drafting**: as the dress shirt with chest ease of 20 to 28 cm (typical for a relaxed fit), lower interfacing, no collar stays; shoulder extended 1 to 3 cm for a relaxed fit.
- **Sewing order**: as the dress shirt.
- **Finishes and notions**: flat-felled side seams, 0.5 to 0.7 cm topstitch, buttons 12 to 15 mm, hem 1 to 1.5 cm.
- **Fabrics**: brushed cotton flannel, chambray, linen; check the shrink of brushed cottons (pre-wash).
- **Fit faults**: as the dress shirt; flannel bulk under the collar -> single-layer interfacing.
- **Variants**: #mens-dress-shirt, #button-down-shirt, #work-shirt, #camp-collar-shirt.
- **CLO3D**: use a softer drape fabric; a loose body needs a larger mesh (10 mm) only if the garment has few folds.
- **Confidence**: from memory/trade practice; numbers typical, unverified.

## Button-Down Shirt {#button-down-shirt}

- **Aliases**: BD shirt, Oxford button-down, American collar (fr: col boutonné, chemise à col boutonné)
- **Family**: men's shirts
- **Recognize**: collar points fastened to the shirt body with small buttons, usually a rolled collar with no stays; Oxford cloth; often a back box pleat with a locker loop.
- **Pieces**: as #mens-dress-shirt, plus two collar-point button holes (in the points), two small buttons on the body (sewn into the front), a back locker loop (optional).
- **Shape**: Unverified. Collar fall: points shorter (point length typical 6.5 to 7.5 cm), spread narrower, the roll line softer (less interfacing or a lightly fused fall), neck edge with a bigger roll; the point holes are placed 1.5 to 2 cm from the end of the point. The buttons on the shirt front are sewn to the body 1.5 to 2 cm from the shoulder seam, in line with the collar points when the collar is closed; the position is checked on the finished collar. Sew rule: same as the dress shirt, with buttons attached through the facing/placket.
- **Drafting**: as the dress shirt; mark the button placement after the collar is set; use a lighter fusible (or none) on the fall.
- **Sewing order**: as the dress shirt; buttons last.
- **Finishes and notions**: 8 to 9 mm small buttons on the collar points; locker loop of self fabric.
- **Fabrics**: oxford cloth, pinpoint, chambray.
- **Fit faults**: collar lifts at the point button -> button too far out; points do not reach the button -> shorter than expected, adjust the button position.
- **Variants**: dress collar with stays; polo collar with buttons (sport, #golf-polo).
- **CLO3D**: add small buttons using the button tool; set the collar to a soft fabric to allow the roll.
- **Confidence**: from memory/trade practice.

## Camp Collar Shirt (Cuban, Revere, Hawaiian-style) {#camp-collar-shirt}

- **Aliases**: Cuban collar shirt, camp shirt, bowling shirt collar, revere collar, loop collar; Hawaiian shirt (fr: chemise à col cubain, col chemisier ouvert)
- **Family**: men's shirts, collars
- **Recognize**: a one-piece open collar with no stand, falling in a V; typically short sleeves, a straight boxy hem, button-through front, often no pocket or one patch pocket.
- **Pieces**: front right and left (2), back (1 on fold, or yoke + back), sleeve (2), collar upper (1), collar under (1; same as the upper), facings (grown-on placket or separate front facings, 2), pocket (0 or 1), optional loop on the back.
- **Shape**: drawn pattern: not found; the shape follows dresspatternmaking.com "Basic collar (camp collar)": a one-piece convertible collar drafted from the front and back neck curves of the bodice block (the web example sums to a half neckline of 7.81 inches); width typical 2.5 to 4 inches (6.4 to 10 cm) including stand and fall. The under collar equals the upper collar (no separate pattern piece). Our technique sheet 2.4 draws the roll line from the first button through a point 2 to 2.5 cm beyond SNP, neck edge curved with spring 2.5 to 3.5 cm and points 7 to 9 cm. Sew rule: collar neck edge -> neckline (collar between shirt and neck facing); the front opening has a facing or placket.
- **Drafting**: 1. Measure N from the bodice. 2. Draw collar neck edge N with spring. 3. Height 7 to 8 cm at CB (stand portion 3 to 3.5 cm). 4. Point 7 to 9 cm. 5. Front V neckline lowered to the first button, typical 18 to 22 cm from SNP. 6. Cut straight boxy hem (no tails), side vents 5 to 8 cm.
- **Sewing order**: collar assembled, turned, collar basted to the neckline, facing sandwiched, understitch, turned and topstitched; sleeves; side seams; hem; buttons.
- **Finishes and notions**: loop or button at the neck; 0.5 cm topstitch; weight on the collar with a light fusible; buttons 12 to 15 mm.
- **Fabrics**: rayon, viscose, linen, light cotton; soft drapey fabrics need a light fusible on the under collar only.
- **Fit faults**: collar stands away at the neck -> more spring; collar flops -> interface the stand portion; neck gape -> shorten N by 0.5 cm.
- **Variants**: Hawaiian #hawaiian-shirt (print placement), Revere (lapel-like) collar, band collar.
- **CLO3D**: collar by segment sewing to the neckline; set the roll line with a fold arrangement if needed.
- **Confidence**: collar width and no-stand/one-piece: verified (dresspatternmaking.com). Other numbers: technique sheet, typical.

## Hawaiian Shirt {#hawaiian-shirt}

- **Aliases**: aloha shirt, tiki shirt, resort shirt (fr: chemise hawaïenne)
- **Family**: men's shirts, printed shirts
- **Recognize**: camp collar, boxy cut, short sleeves, all-over print (often matched across the front), coconut or plastic buttons, straight hem with small side vents, often a left chest pocket with a matching print.
- **Pieces**: as #camp-collar-shirt, plus a matched pocket (cut from the same print position as the front).
- **Shape**: Unverified. Same layout as the camp collar shirt; the difference is print matching: the left front and right front are laid with the same pattern repeat so that the print lines up across the closed front, the pocket is cut from the front with the same repeat position, and the sleeve is aligned to the body at the shoulder line. Pattern pieces need cutting notches at pattern-match points, and fabric is cut single layer for matched pieces.
- **Drafting**: as the camp collar shirt; add 2 to 5 percent extra fabric for matching; mark the match points at the pocket and front.
- **Sewing order**: as the camp collar shirt.
- **Finishes and notions**: coconut shell buttons (typical), 12 to 15 mm; rolled narrow hem; side vents 5 to 8 cm with a stay stitch.
- **Fabrics**: rayon challis, viscose, rayon crepe, silk, light cotton; a bold print needs single-layer cutting.
- **Fit faults**: print mismatch at the pocket -> cut the pocket last, from the matching position of the front.
- **Variants**: #camp-collar-shirt, with a loop collar.
- **CLO3D**: apply the print to the pattern pieces as a texture with matching coordinates; use the Fabric Print mapping to align across the front.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; no drawn pattern verified.

## Western Shirt {#western-shirt}

- **Aliases**: snap shirt, cowboy shirt, rodeo shirt, pearl snap shirt (fr: chemise western, chemise cowboy)
- **Family**: men's shirts
- **Recognize**: a pointed (V or "one-point") yoke across the front and back, pearl snaps instead of buttons (front, cuffs and pocket flaps), two chest pockets with pointed flaps (often with a snap), a spread collar, a curved shirt-tail hem, often decorative piping or embroidery along the yoke. Per Langston's glossary: piping outlines the yoke and front placket; snaps run along the front placket and cuffs.
- **Pieces**: front right and left (2), back (1 on fold, with a pleat or two), front yoke (2, pointed), back yoke (1 or 2, pointed), sleeve (2), cuff (4), sleeve placket (2 to 4), collar stand (2), collar fall (2), pocket (2), pocket flap (4 pieces with lining, or 2 folded), snap tapes or snaps.
- **Shape**: Unverified. Shape inferred from retail description (one-point front and back yokes with a point at CF/CB; snaps on the front placket and cuffs; pocket flaps with snaps). The front yoke is a separate piece sewn to the front body along a V line: the V peak is at the CF or at the center of the yoke, the arms of the V rise toward the armhole; the back yoke is a straight band with an inverted V lower edge or an arch. Sew rule: yoke point lower edge -> front body; front body gathers or pleats to the yoke lower edge in some styles; shoulder seam sandwiched; snaps on the placket in place of buttonholes.
- **Drafting**: as #mens-dress-shirt, with the yoke line drawn as a V: from the armhole at about 8 to 12 cm below the shoulder point, to the center of the yoke at about 4 to 6 cm lower (typical, unverified). Pockets: 11 to 13 cm wide, 13 to 15 cm tall, flaps 4.5 to 6 cm.
- **Sewing order**: yokes first with piping, then fronts and back; pockets and flaps; snaps set after the shirt is hemmed.
- **Finishes and notions**: snaps are set with a snap press, 11 to 13 mm snap caps (typical), front stand 3 to 4 cm; the placket stand is interfaced to carry the snap.
- **Fabrics**: poplin, twill, denim, gabardine; plaid yokes are cut on the bias for contrast.
- **Fit faults**: snaps pop open -> stand too narrow or interfacing too light; yoke point sags -> grain not centered on the point.
- **Variants**: #mens-dress-shirt (formal), denim work shirt #work-shirt.
- **CLO3D**: yoke as a separate piece with an angular seam; snap trims added with the button tool.
- **Confidence**: yoke and snap features: verified from retail descriptions (Langston's glossary, retailer listings); all dimensions: typical/unverified.

## Work Shirt {#work-shirt}

- **Aliases**: utility shirt, shop shirt, chambray work shirt, mechanic shirt (fr: chemise de travail)
- **Family**: workwear, men's shirts
- **Recognize**: heavy fabric, two flap or patch chest pockets, a pen slot or a small utility pocket, a double-needle (flat-felled) seam, a straight hem, a pointed collar, sometimes a pleated back or an action pleat.
- **Pieces**: front right and left (2), back (1), yoke (2), sleeve (2), cuff (4), collar stand (2), collar fall (2), pocket (2), pocket flap (2 or 4).
- **Shape**: Unverified. Layout as the dress shirt; a deeper armhole (extra 1 to 2 cm, typical) and a sleeve cap of 10 to 13 cm for mobility; a back yoke straight across or slightly arched; hem straight with side vents or a gentle curve of 2 to 4 cm rise at the side.
- **Drafting**: as #casual-shirt-mens with chest ease 20 to 28 cm and shoulder extended 1 to 2 cm; flap pocket 11 to 13 cm wide x 13 to 15 cm tall, flap 4.5 to 5.5 cm.

## Wetsuit (Neoprene Panels) {#wetsuit-neoprene}

- **Aliases**: full suit, shorty, steamer, spring suit, long john (fr: combinaison de plongée, combinaison néoprène); (de: Neoprenanzug)
- **Family**: swimwear and water sports, technical garments
- **Recognize**: a close-fitting neoprene suit made from many panels, with glued and blind-stitched seams, a back zip, chest zip or zipperless entry, collar seal, wrist and ankle seals, thicker neoprene on the torso than on the limbs (a notation such as 4/3 means torso/limb thickness).
- **Pieces**: chest panel (1), back panel (1 or 2, with zip), shoulder or yoke panels (2), arm panels (inner and outer, 4), underarm gussets (2), leg panels (thigh, knee, calf, front and back, 8 to 10), crotch panel (1), neck collar (1), cuffs and ankle seals (4), knee pads (2, optional), lining (jersey on the inside, laminated), tape for seams (internal tape, strip).
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. The source search says a single suit can have 15 to 30 panels that fit together like a jigsaw, cut from neoprene sheets with a template that also sets the seams; designers vary thickness by zone, thinner neoprene in flexible areas such as the armpits and thicker on the chest, back and upper legs; extra pieces include an armpit gusset, mesh-lined chest and back panels and zip positions (see Sources). The arm and leg panel shapes were not described in the sources found. General approach (trade practice): the body is a stretch block (neoprene stretch not measured here: test the actual sheet with the technique sheet 1.1 stretch test) with horizontal reduction 10 to 15 percent and vertical reduction 5 to 8 percent (technique sheet 6.1, 2.2) so that the suit is close to the skin; panels are placed where the body flexes (underarm, knee, back of knee) so that seams do not cross the stress points; seam allowance is small (0.5 to 0.7 cm, typical), and panel edges are butted, glued, blind stitched with a curved needle that does not penetrate fully, and taped on the inside. Sew rule: panel edges are butted (not overlapped) and glued, then stitched from the inside; flatlock seams overlap the panel edges and are not watertight.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft a close-fit block for the body in the entry position, using the full stretch of the fabric for the entry and a lower reduction at the cuffs. 2. Split into panels along the body contours and place the seam where the body bends. 3. Add a zip position (back or chest). 4. Mark glue seam allowance (typical 5 mm). 5. Check the entry circumference at shoulders and hips.
- **Sewing order**: heat-press the panels; apply rubberized glue to the edges; press the seams together and let them cure (about 24 hours for glued suits in one source); blind stitch from the inside; tape the back of the seams; add the zip, seals and pads.
- **Finishes and notions**: glued and blind-stitched (GBS) seams for cold water; flatlock for warm water; liquid rubber seam seal on high-end suits; internal tape; knee pads; zip.
- **Fabrics**: closed-cell neoprene 2 to 6 mm laminated with nylon or jersey; thickness differs by zone.
- **Fit faults**: water flushes in the neck -> collar seal too loose; chest wrinkles -> torso too long; knee panel binds -> too little stretch, move seam.
- **Variants**: #rash-guard, #sport-leggings.
- **CLO3D**: use a thick, low-drape fabric with high stretch; each panel as a separate pattern; seams as sewing lines; avatar in the entry position; verify the stretch of the suit in the simulation.
- **Confidence**: panel counts, thickness zones and seam methods: web sources (Sources). Shape of arm and leg panels: not known. Reductions: typical.

## Martial Arts Gi (Judo, Karate, Jiu-Jitsu) {#martial-arts-gi}

- **Aliases**: kimono, keikogi, dogi, uniform (fr: kimono de judo, kimono de karaté, judogi, karategi); (de: Kampfsportanzug)
- **Family**: sportswear, uniforms
- **Recognize**: a wide, cross-fronted jacket (uwagi) with a thick collar and a belt, wide straight sleeves, plus loose trousers with a drawstring and a reinforced knee.
- **Pieces**: jacket body (front left and right 2, back 1, usually with a CB seam), sleeve (2), collar (lapel strip, 2 or 1 long strip), skirt reinforcement (2), shoulder reinforcement (2), sleeve cuff reinforcement (2), trousers front and back (2 pairs), knee reinforcement (2), crotch gusset (1, often), drawstring (1), belt (1).
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. General shape (trade practice): a kimono-type jacket whose fronts cross left over right as worn, with a wide collar strip running along the front edges from the hem across the neckline to the opposite hem (a continuous lapel strip stitched on each front), reinforced with quilting; sleeves are wide and straight with a short cap and sewn into a deep armhole (kimono-style gusset, see `kimono-from-block-gusset` in methods-cutting); trousers are straight with a drawstring waist and a gusset at the crotch. - **Drafting**: 1. Wide jacket block, chest ease 25 to 40 cm (typical), length to the mid-thigh. 2. Add the crossing: each front extends past CF to the opposite side by 25 to 30 cm (typical). 3. Trouser: wide straight leg with inseam ending near the ankle bone, crotch gusset.
- **Sewing order**: collar and lapel assembly; reinforcements; shoulder seams; sleeves; side seams and skirt; trousers; drawstring.
- **Finishes and notions**: heavy rows of stitching (3 to 5 cm apart) for reinforcement, cotton drawstring.
- **Fabrics**: heavy cotton weave (pearl weave, ripstop, canvas), pre-shrunk; sizes allow for shrinkage.
- **Fit faults**: sleeves too long for the competition rule -> shorten to the limits above; lapel flips -> thickness inadequate; trouser gusset binds -> reduce rise.
- **Variants**: #mens-trousers, #chef-jacket, #boxing-shorts.
- **CLO3D**: heavy cotton; collar as a thick layer; fronts overlap with proper layering.
- **Confidence**: IBJJF measurements: web sources (Sources; sleeve and trouser tolerances differ between sources). Layout: trade practice, unverified.
